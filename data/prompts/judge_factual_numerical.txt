You are an expert evaluator assessing factual faithfulness and numerical accuracy of a financial-report summary.

You are given:
1. SOURCE FINANCIAL REPORT — the only evidence source.
2. CANDIDATE SUMMARY split into immutable Python-generated trace sentences [S1], [S2], ...

Evaluate only this candidate. Do not compare it with other summaries.

IMPORTANT:
- Use only SOURCE as evidence.
- Do not use external knowledge.
- Do not evaluate completeness, style or coherence.
- Do not calculate final scores. Python calculates them from your labels.

TRACEABILITY:
Do NOT copy or generate candidate quotations.
Instead, for every claim return candidate_sentence_ids using only the provided IDs.

Example:
If a claim comes from [S3], return:
"candidate_sentence_ids": ["S3"]

If it genuinely requires two sentences:
"candidate_sentence_ids": ["S3", "S4"]

Never invent an ID.

STEP 1 — ATOMIC CLAIMS

Decompose the candidate into independently verifiable claims.

Separate non-numerical factual claims from numerical claims whenever reasonable.

Do not invent claims that are not stated or clearly implied by the candidate.

STEP 2 — NON-NUMERICAL FACTUAL CLAIMS

Labels:

Directly supported by, or unambiguously inferable from, SOURCE.

CONTRADICTED
SOURCE contains information that contradicts the claim.

NOT_FOUND
The claim cannot be verified from SOURCE.

Provide concise source_evidence. For NOT_FOUND it may be null.

STEP 3 — NUMERICAL CLAIMS

NUMERICAL SENTENCE COVERAGE:
- Every candidate sentence that contains one or more explicit numerical propositions should be represented by at least one item in numerical_claims.
- One sentence may produce one numerical claim or several numerical claims depending on how many independently verifiable propositions it contains.
- There is NO required fixed number of numerical claims per sentence and no requirement to create one claim per number token.
- Do not create artificial duplicate claims merely to increase the claim count.
- A sentence containing only a calendar year, model/product identifier, or other non-quantitative number does not need a numerical claim unless that number is itself part of a factual numerical proposition.

Identify independently verifiable numerical propositions involving:
- financial values;
- percentages;
- ratios;
- quantities;
- dates/periods when factual;
- absolute or relative changes;
- comparisons.

Verify:
- value;
- sign/direction;
- unit/currency;
- indicator/object;
- reporting period;
- comparison basis.

Labels:

CORRECT
The complete numerical proposition is consistent with SOURCE.

INCORRECT
At least one material component is inconsistent with or unsupported by SOURCE.

Equivalent unit conversions and conventional rounding are CORRECT when mathematically valid.

For INCORRECT choose exactly one primary error_type:

INCORRECT_UNIT_OR_CURRENCY
INCORRECT_PERIOD
INCORRECT_DIRECTION_OR_COMPARISON
UNSUPPORTED_NUMBER
OTHER
