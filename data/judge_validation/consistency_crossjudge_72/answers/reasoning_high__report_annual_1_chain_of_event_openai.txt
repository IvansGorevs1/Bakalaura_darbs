AAC Clyde Space’s 2025 financial performance weakened compared with 2024, mainly because of programme delays and timing effects. Net sales fell 16% to SEK 295.3 million from SEK 352.9 million, while total revenue declined to SEK 337.8 million from SEK 408.8 million. EBITDA remained positive for the third consecutive year but decreased to SEK 17.5 million from SEK 46.7 million. EBIT deteriorated to a loss of SEK 40.0 million from SEK 4.0 million, and the loss after tax widened to SEK 46.6 million from SEK 5.7 million. Earnings per share declined to SEK -7.63 from SEK -1.00.

The deterioration was concentrated in Products & Missions, where net sales fell to SEK 245.0 million from SEK 346.0 million and EBITDA to SEK 16.8 million from SEK 51.6 million. Data & Services developed positively, with sales increasing by 80% to SEK 80.1 million and EBITDA rising to SEK 27.8 million from SEK 21.1 million. The Group stated that two programme-related delays materially affected revenue, cash flow and order intake. Technical discussions involving an end customer and subcontractor shifted approximately SEK 32 million of milestone revenue into 2026, while political and procurement developments postponed the start of the EPS-Sterna programme. Longer-than-planned commissioning of the Sedna satellites and launch-provider rescheduling for the first VIREON satellites also affected timing.

Operating cash flow fell sharply to an outflow of SEK 72.6 million from an inflow of SEK 56.8 million. Cash and cash equivalents declined to SEK 30.1 million from SEK 49.7 million, although the available overdraft facility increased from SEK 30 million to SEK 50 million and was undrawn at year-end. The equity ratio improved to 80% from 71%, supported by two directed share issues that raised approximately SEK 114.8 million before issue costs. No dividend was proposed.

Net financial items worsened to a loss of SEK 10.8 million from SEK 1.3 million, reflecting higher foreign-exchange losses and the remeasurement of contingent purchase considerations. The order backlog decreased to SEK 351.8 million from SEK 641 million at year-end, consistent with delayed programme activity and the timing of customer orders.

Operationally, Sedna-1 and Sedna-2 became fully operational, supporting recurring maritime data revenues, while assembly began on the VIREON Earth Observation constellation and the INFLECION Phase 1 contract was signed. After year-end, AAC received its largest order to date: a EUR 76.3 million, approximately SEK 820 million, contract for 20 EPS-Sterna microwave radiometers. This increased the order backlog to approximately SEK 1.1 billion by the end of March 2026 and improved the Group’s forward financial visibility.
