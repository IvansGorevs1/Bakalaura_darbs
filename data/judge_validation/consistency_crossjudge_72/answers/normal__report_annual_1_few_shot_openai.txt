AAC Clyde Space’s financial performance weakened in 2025, primarily because programme delays reduced revenue recognition and cash generation. Net sales decreased 16% to SEK 295.3 million from SEK 352.9 million, while total revenue declined to SEK 337.8 million from SEK 408.8 million. EBITDA fell to SEK 17.5 million from SEK 46.7 million, although the Group remained EBITDA-positive for the third consecutive year. After depreciation, amortisation and impairment, the operating result declined to a loss of SEK 40.0 million, compared with a loss of SEK 4.0 million in 2024. The loss after tax widened to SEK 46.6 million from SEK 5.7 million, and earnings per share declined to SEK -7.63 from SEK -1.00.

The main pressure came from the Products & Missions segment, where net sales fell to SEK 245.0 million from SEK 346.0 million and EBITDA declined to SEK 16.8 million from SEK 51.6 million. Two programme-related delays materially affected the year: approximately SEK 32 million of revenue from a delayed customer milestone shifted into 2026, while political and procurement developments postponed the start of the EPS-Sterna programme. Sedna commissioning also took longer than planned, and the launch of the first VIREON satellites was delayed by the launch provider. In contrast, Data & Services continued to expand, with sales rising 80% to SEK 80.1 million and EBITDA increasing to SEK 27.8 million from SEK 21.1 million. Sedna-1 and Sedna-2 became operational, supporting greater maritime-data capacity and recurring customer revenues.

Cash flow deteriorated significantly. Operating cash flow was negative SEK 72.6 million, compared with positive SEK 56.8 million in 2024, while cash and cash equivalents declined to SEK 30.1 million from SEK 49.7 million. The Group nevertheless reported an equity ratio of 80%, up from 71%, had no utilised bank borrowing at year-end, and increased its available overdraft facility to SEK 50 million. Two directed share issues raised approximately SEK 114.8 million before issue costs, supporting investment in satellite constellations and strategic programmes.

Despite the weaker annual results, the Group’s outlook strengthened after year-end. It received an EUR 76.3 million order, approximately SEK 820 million, for 20 EPS-Sterna microwave radiometers, increasing the order backlog to approximately SEK 1.1 billion by the end of March 2026. Additional Sedna and VIREON satellites are being developed, while INFLECION Phase 1 was completed. No dividend was proposed.
