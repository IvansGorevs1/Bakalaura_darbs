During the first three months of 2026, AS Amber Latvijas balzams experienced a substantial deterioration in financial performance. Sales volume declined 36.1% year on year to 517,600 9-liter cases from 810,079 cases. Net revenue fell 29.6% to EUR 11.1 million, compared with EUR 15.8 million in the corresponding period of 2025. The decrease was primarily attributed to lower sales volumes and a high prior-year comparison base, which had benefited from a revenue adjustment recognized under contractual terms.

Gross profit fell 66.5% to EUR 1.2 million from EUR 3.5 million. Management attributed the decline mainly to changes in product mix and adjustments to the pricing methodology. Although the Company reduced costs during the period, these savings were largely offset by those factors. The Company reported an operating loss of EUR 1.6 million, compared with an operating profit of EUR 0.6 million a year earlier, and its operating margin declined to negative 14.5% from positive 3.7%. The net result changed from a profit of EUR 0.9 million to a loss of EUR 1.4 million, while earnings per share decreased from EUR 0.11 to negative EUR 0.19.

Cash flow also weakened materially. Operating activities used EUR 1.8 million, and cash and cash equivalents fell from EUR 1.0 million at 31 December 2025 to EUR 38,000 at 31 March 2026. The Company stated that the combined effect of weaker market conditions and other external challenges created significant cash-flow difficulties and led to delayed excise tax payments.

A key development was the January 2026 Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceeding involving related company Stoli Group (USA), LLC, following which a trustee was appointed and the Group lost control of that company’s U.S. operations. The event directly affected the solvency of the Company’s largest customer and its ability to meet creditor obligations. Additional pressure came from Russian actions against Group companies after the Group’s public support for Ukraine and peace in Europe, including the confiscation of the Group-owned Talvis distillery. The Company also cited a global spirits-market downturn, declining export volumes, changing consumer habits, pressure on discretionary spending, and distributor inventory adjustments.

In response to these pressures, the Company applied for legal protection proceedings on 30 January 2026; the Riga City Court approved the application on 5 February. The proceedings provide a framework for restructuring liabilities and stabilizing operations after the State Revenue Service froze the Company’s bank accounts over overdue excise liabilities. The action-plan deadline was extended to 5 October 2026. Separately, the principal shareholder entered Luxembourg judicial reorganization proceedings in April, with a moratorium until 24 August 2026. Management is reviewing governance, controls, efficiency, costs, and commercial performance to support business continuity and restore financial stability.
