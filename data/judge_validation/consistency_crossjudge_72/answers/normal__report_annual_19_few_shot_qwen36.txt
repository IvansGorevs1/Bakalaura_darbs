Talkpool reported a period of significant financial improvement and strategic transition in 2025, marking the end of its multi-year cost-reduction phase and the beginning of organic growth. Net sales increased by 12.5 percent to EUR 17.8 million, up from EUR 15.8 million in the previous year. This revenue growth was driven by strong performance in the Americas, which remained the largest market, and stabilization in Pakistan, the group’s main production base. Despite currency headwinds from a weakening US dollar and Pakistani Rupee in the first half of the year, the company achieved double-digit revenue growth for the first time since its consolidation phase began.

Profitability metrics showed mixed but generally positive trends. EBITDA rose slightly to EUR 1.8 million, maintaining an EBITDA margin of 10.3 percent, down marginally from 10.8 percent in 2024. Gross margins stood at 26.4 percent. Net earnings after tax increased substantially by 42 percent to EUR 0.9 million, with the net profit margin improving to 4.8 percent from 3.8 percent in the prior year. The improvement in net earnings was supported by cost efficiencies and the stabilization of operations in key markets, although management noted that temporary margin pressure was expected due to initial investments in future growth initiatives.

The company’s balance sheet strengthened considerably during the period. Equity improved by EUR 4.6 million, moving from a negative position of EUR 2.5 million in 2021 to a positive EUR 2.1 million in 2025. Net debt was reduced further, resulting in a net cash position of EUR 0.3 million at year-end, a significant turnaround from the net debt of EUR 5.7 million in 2021. This financial stability was reinforced by an independent valuation of the German subsidiary at EUR 20.5 million by BDO, which helped offset reductions in the carrying value of Pakistani assets.

Strategically, Talkpool launched the “Vision 2030” plan in April, setting targets for 16.8 percent annual revenue growth, a 14.0 percent EBITDA margin, and zero net debt by 2030. The company shifted its geographic focus toward developed markets, particularly Western Europe and the United States, while maintaining its role as a service provider to major vendors and operators. Key developments included the launch of a new Swedish affiliate for defense communications, a partnership with Nokia USA for 5G testing, and the re-entry into the US market through a contract with MasTec. Additionally, the company began integrating AI-powered tools into its remote service delivery model, aiming to enhance efficiency and offer technology-enabled solutions to clients. Management expects near-term pressure on cash flow and margins due to these investments, but anticipates that new business activity will drive performance to higher levels in the coming year.
