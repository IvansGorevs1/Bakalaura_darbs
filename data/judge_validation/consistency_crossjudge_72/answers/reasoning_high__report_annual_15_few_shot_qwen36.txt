The company reported a substantial decline in revenue and profitability during the 2025 reporting period compared to the prior year. Consolidated revenue fell by 42.3 percent, dropping to EUR 310.9 million from EUR 539.0 million in 2024. This decrease was primarily driven by the completion of several large-scale construction contracts awarded in previous periods, which had elevated prior-year results. Despite the top-line contraction, gross profit margins remained relatively stable at 18.0 percent, while operating profit decreased to EUR 34.3 million, reflecting a normalization of margins following exceptional project completions in 2024. Net profit attributable to equity holders stood at EUR 39.9 million, yielding a net margin of 12.8 percent. Return on equity declined to 15.6 percent from 28.4 percent in the previous year, largely due to the reduced revenue base.

The construction services segment accounted for the majority of the revenue drop, falling to EUR 237.6 million as major infrastructure and defense-related projects reached completion. Conversely, the real estate development segment showed resilience, with revenue rising to EUR 73.4 million and apartment deliveries increasing to 358 units. The group’s secured order book grew substantially to EUR 466.9 million, up from EUR 340.6 million, indicating strong future revenue visibility. New construction contracts worth EUR 362.8 million were secured during the year, including significant engagements related to national defense and transport infrastructure.

Macroeconomic conditions across the Baltic states presented mixed dynamics in 2025. While Estonia and Latvia experienced subdued private investment and cautious consumer sentiment, Lithuania maintained stronger economic momentum and higher apartment sales activity. The broader market environment was characterized by shifting procurement patterns toward public sector initiatives, particularly in defense and railway infrastructure, which helped stabilize workload despite weak private-sector demand. Additionally, investments in joint ventures operating under the Connecto brand contributed meaningfully to consolidated earnings.

Financially, the group maintained a conservative capital structure, with equity expanding to EUR 260.6 million and the equity ratio improving to 62.8 percent. Interest-bearing liabilities represented a modest 8.0 percent of total assets, supporting robust liquidity with a current ratio of 2.9 times. Management proposed a dividend distribution of EUR 1.25 per share, aligning with the group’s long-term payout policy. Overall, the reporting period reflected a transitional phase marked by lower recognized revenues from completed projects, yet it was underpinned by a strengthening order backlog, disciplined cost management, and a solid balance sheet positioning the group for steady operations in the coming years.
