During the 2025 financial year, Tallink Grupp reported consolidated revenue of EUR 765.3 million, a decline from EUR 785.8 million in 2024. Passenger volumes fell 0.9 percent to 5.53 million, and cargo transported dropped 19.2 percent to 245,004 units. These headwinds compressed annual EBITDA to EUR 130.1 million from EUR 175.2 million, while net profit contracted to EUR 17.3 million, or EUR 0.023 per share, against EUR 40.3 million previously. The deterioration primarily reflected economic uncertainty in Finland, where subdued consumer confidence, inflation, and geopolitical tensions restrained leisure travel and commercial freight. Weakness was concentrated on the Finland-Sweden and Estonia-Sweden routes, although the Estonia-Finland corridor preserved 1.8 percent passenger growth.

Fourth-quarter results demonstrated a clear recovery. Quarterly revenue advanced 2.4 percent to EUR 188.0 million, EBITDA rose to EUR 27.6 million, and net profit rebounded to EUR 12.2 million from a EUR 5.2 million loss. This improvement coincided with seasonal demand normalization and moderating cargo volume declines. Operational restructuring further bolstered margins. The Group divested three vessels to optimize its fleet, a move that complemented a retrospective alignment of cruise and passenger vessel useful lives to forty-five years. Combined, these accounting and structural adjustments reduced annual depreciation and amortisation to EUR 79.8 million. Concurrently, financing costs declined by EUR 6.8 million to EUR 21.4 million, reflecting sustained deleveraging and favorable interest rate movements.

Financial prudence remained central to the period. The Group settled EUR 113.5 million in principal and interest repayments alongside a EUR 44.6 million dividend distribution plus associated taxes. Despite these outflows, the equity ratio strengthened to 56 percent, and net debt stabilized at EUR 432.4 million, yielding a net debt to EBITDA ratio of 3.3. Supplementary charter agreements for three ferries provided alternative revenue streams, while capital expenditures totalled EUR 33.0 million, focused on essential maintenance and technology upgrades. Amid regulatory shifts like expanded CO2 allowance mandates and regional tax adjustments, management emphasized route flexibility and ongoing transition toward biomethane fuels to curb future compliance costs. Building on stabilized operations, the board intends to propose maintaining the shareholder return framework with a planned dividend of six euro cents per share for 2026.
