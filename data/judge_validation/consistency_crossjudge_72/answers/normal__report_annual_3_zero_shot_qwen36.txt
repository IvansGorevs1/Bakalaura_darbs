Coop Pank reported a net profit of 28.7 million euros for the financial year 2025, representing an 11% decrease compared to the 32.2 million euros recorded in 2024. This decline in profitability was primarily driven by a lower-interest economic environment, which reduced net interest income despite significant growth in business volumes. Net interest income fell to 73.3 million euros from 77.6 million euros in the prior year, reflecting the impact of declining interest rates on the bank's asset yields. Total net operating income decreased slightly to 79.4 million euros from 81.9 million euros, while net fee and commission income saw a modest increase to 4.5 million euros from 4.3 million euros. Operating expenses rose to 41.5 million euros from 40.6 million euros, leading to a deterioration in the cost-to-income ratio to 52.3% from 49.6%. Credit loss allowances decreased to 3.3 million euros from 4.6 million euros, and income tax expense increased to 5.8 million euros from 4.5 million euros.

Despite the drop in profits, the bank demonstrated strong volume growth aligned with its strategic expansion goals. The loan portfolio grew by 19% to reach 2.11 billion euros, marking the first time the portfolio exceeded this threshold. Business loans were the fastest-growing segment, increasing by 27% to 986 million euros, followed by home loans which grew by 17% to 863 million euros. The customer base expanded by 9%, adding 19,000 clients to reach a total of 227,000. Customer deposits increased by 9% to 2.05 billion euros, with demand deposits rising by 11% and term deposits growing by 8%. The net interest margin compressed to 3.0% from 3.8% in 2024, and return on equity declined to 12.9% from 16.2%.

Several key business developments influenced the bank's performance and capital position in 2025. Coop Pank issued its first covered bonds on the Irish stock exchange, raising 250 million euros with a four-year maturity, which provided stable long-term funding and allowed the bank to reduce reliance on more expensive term deposits. The bank also entered into a synthetic securitization transaction with the European Investment Bank Group, transferring credit risk on part of its loan portfolio to obtain capital relief, enabling the issuance of up to 249 million euros in new loans for projects promoting gender equality and environmental sustainability. Additionally, a subordinated loan of 5 million euros was secured from the European Energy Efficiency Fund. Moody’s affirmed Coop Pank’s deposit rating at Baa2 with a positive outlook, citing strong profitability and solid capitalization. The bank maintained robust capital adequacy ratios, with a CET1 ratio of 14.15% and a total capital adequacy ratio of 18.54%, both exceeding regulatory requirements. The bank distributed 7.2 million euros in dividends to shareholders during the year.
