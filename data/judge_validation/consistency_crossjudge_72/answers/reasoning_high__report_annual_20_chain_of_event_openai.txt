AS Tallink Grupp’s financial performance weakened in 2025 amid low consumer and business confidence, ongoing economic challenges in its core markets and heightened geopolitical tensions. Revenue declined 2.6% to EUR 765.3 million from EUR 785.8 million in 2024, while revenue from core route operations fell EUR 13.7 million to EUR 625.2 million. Passenger volumes decreased 0.9% to 5.5 million, cargo units fell 19.2% to 245,004, and passenger vehicles declined 2.2% to 760,473.

Profitability deteriorated significantly. Gross profit decreased to EUR 143.2 million from EUR 153.6 million, EBITDA fell EUR 45.1 million to EUR 130.1 million, and EBIT declined to EUR 50.3 million from EUR 77.4 million. Net profit dropped 57.1% to EUR 17.3 million, reducing earnings per share to EUR 0.023 from EUR 0.054. The EBITDA margin declined from 22.3% to 17.0%, while the net profit margin fell from 5.1% to 2.3%. Restaurant and shop sales, the largest revenue source, decreased to EUR 366.4 million from EUR 376.0 million, while cargo revenue fell to EUR 77.9 million from EUR 88.6 million and charter revenue to EUR 54.9 million from EUR 62.0 million.

The Estonia-Finland route generated higher revenue of EUR 314.5 million, supported by a 1.8% increase in passengers, but its result fell EUR 10.9 million to EUR 70.4 million as cargo units declined 20.0%. Finland-Sweden revenue decreased to EUR 225.5 million and its result to EUR 1.3 million, with maintenance works on Silja Serenade and Baltic Princess affecting operations for 68 days. Estonia-Sweden revenue fell to EUR 85.3 million, although its loss improved to EUR 1.2 million. The Other segment’s result remained broadly stable at EUR 28.5 million.

Operating costs were pressured by higher personnel expenses, which rose 4.3% to EUR 201.0 million, and fuel and emission costs, which increased to EUR 96.5 million. These effects were partly offset by continued cost-efficiency measures, vessel chartering and lower depreciation. Depreciation and amortisation fell EUR 18.0 million to EUR 79.8 million, including a EUR 16.7 million reduction from extending the estimated useful lives of most cruise and passenger vessels to 45 years. Net finance costs also declined EUR 6.8 million to EUR 21.4 million as debt and interest rates fell.

The Group sold three vessels during the year and had no vessels in lay-up by the end of the third quarter, supporting fleet optimisation and generating EUR 78.8 million of disposal proceeds. Net debt declined to EUR 432.4 million from EUR 537.7 million, and interest-bearing liabilities fell 19.9% to EUR 445.9 million, although net debt to EBITDA increased to 3.3 because of the lower EBITDA. Operating cash flow remained positive at EUR 127.1 million, while cash fell to EUR 13.5 million after EUR 44.6 million of dividends and EUR 113.5 million of loan and interest payments. The Group paid EUR 0.06 per share in dividends and plans to propose the same amount for 2026.
