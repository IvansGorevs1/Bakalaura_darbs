Kvika banki reported a Q2 2026 pre-tax profit of ISK 1,545 million, down ISK 480 million, or 23.7%, from Q2 2025 and 14.6% from Q1 2026. After-tax profit fell to ISK 903 million from ISK 1,439 million a year earlier. The quarterly decline reflected lower operating income, higher impairment charges and increased special bank taxes.

Net operating income decreased 7.3% year on year to ISK 4,756 million. Net interest income remained resilient, increasing 1.7% to ISK 3,013 million, as higher income from customer lending offset a smaller liquidity portfolio and increased funding costs. However, it fell ISK 253 million from Q1, mainly because lower inflation reduced the contribution from Kvika’s positive ISK 6.1 billion inflation balance, while the ISK 10.2 billion dividend paid in June and recent senior preferred and AT1 issuances also reduced net interest margin. The margin declined to 4.0% from 4.5% in Q1.

Net fee and commission income fell 26.8% year on year to ISK 1,416 million, reflecting subdued capital-markets activity, lower transaction volumes and weaker client activity. Other net operating income increased ISK 96 million to ISK 327 million, supported by stronger net financial income and other operating income. Administrative expenses declined 1.1% to ISK 2,947 million despite a one-off ISK 112 million restructuring charge. Efficiency measures reduced employee numbers to 232 and are expected to lower the annual cost base by ISK 300–400 million. Net impairment increased to ISK 269 million from ISK 122 million, reflecting a conservative loan-book review and greater caution amid economic uncertainty.

For the first six months, performance was stronger: pre-tax profit from continuing operations rose 23.0% to ISK 3,354 million. Net interest income increased 6.8% to ISK 6,279 million and administrative expenses decreased 1.8% to ISK 5,961 million, while fee income declined 16.0%. After-tax profit from continuing operations reached ISK 2,277 million, although total profit for the period fell from ISK 3,525 million because Q2 2025 included ISK 1,901 million from discontinued operations.

Balance-sheet growth continued, with loans to customers up 7.6% from year-end 2025 to ISK 223.3 billion and deposits up 16.5% to ISK 201.2 billion. Capital and liquidity remained strong: CAR was 23.0%, 530 basis points above regulatory requirements, LCR was 276%, and NSFR was 137%. Straumur’s sale process is underway, the CEO plans to step down by year-end 2026, and management expects funding-cost improvements, continued lending growth and strategic asset realisations to support future returns.
