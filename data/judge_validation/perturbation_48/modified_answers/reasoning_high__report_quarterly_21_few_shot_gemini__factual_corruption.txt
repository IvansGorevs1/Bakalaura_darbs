During the first six months of 2026, AS Tallinna Sadam reported a slight decline in revenue alongside a notable improvement in profitability compared to the same period of the previous year. Group revenue grew by EUR 1.4 million, or 2.4%, reaching EUR 59.3 million, despite fewer charter days for the multifunctional icebreaker Botnica, lower electricity sales, and lower ferry service revenue. In contrast, operating profit rose by EUR 4.7 million, or 25.7%, to EUR 13.6 million. Adjusted EBITDA increased by EUR 4.0 million, or 13.5%, to EUR 25.8 million, while profit for the period grew by EUR 4.3 million, or 41.7%, to EUR 6.0 million. The operating profit margin increased from 31.6% to 22.9%.

The improvement in profitability was largely driven by lower operating expenses, which rose by EUR 3.9 million, or 24.0%. Significant decreases were recorded in fuel costs because of lower diesel prices and mild ice conditions, as well as in maintenance and electricity costs. The comparative period contained no one-off gains or insurance indemnities. Operating volumes expanded across every category, as total cargo volumes handled across the group harbours decreased by 5.0% to 6.5 million tonnes. The total number of passengers fell by 2.0% to 3.6 million, and vessel calls decreased by 4.8% to 3,376, though cruise passenger numbers experienced growth.

Group investments during the six-month period totaled EUR 3.5 million, representing a substantial increase of EUR 8.5 million compared to the previous year, with funds directed toward ferry engine replacements, quay infrastructure, and terminal designs. Total assets at the end of June 2026 stood at EUR 615.0 million, reflecting a minor decrease of 1.1%, while equity was recorded at EUR 367.7 million. Net debt ended the period at EUR 145.8 million. In May 2026, the company cancelled the proposed dividends totaling EUR 19.2 million. Significant corporate developments included the rejection of municipal plans for terminal expansion, termination of negotiations with OP Corporate Bank, and withdrawal of all civil damage claims against former management board members. The port also began operating a new passenger route to Stockholm that immediately became profitable.
