During the 2025 financial year, TKM Grupp AS experienced a weakening of financial performance amid a challenging economic environment characterized by low consumer confidence, high price sensitivity, and domestic tax changes in Estonia, notably the introduction of a motor vehicle tax. Consolidated sales revenue declined by 2.6 percent to 919.6 million euros compared to 944.6 million euros in the previous year. Profitability contracted more significantly, with profit before tax falling by 31.4 percent to 24.3 million euros and net profit decreasing by 36.1 percent to 17.5 million euros. Net profit per share dropped to 43 cents from 67 cents. Effective cost control helped mitigate pressures from rising expenses, while net profit also benefited from lower borrowing interest costs.

Performance varied across the Group's segments. The supermarkets segment generated revenue of 611.9 million euros, a modest increase of 0.3 percent, while net profit increased by 38.6 percent to 9.5 million euros due to a lower share of promotional products and stronger consumer purchasing power. The department stores segment saw revenue decrease by 1.1 percent to 103.0 million euros, resulting in a net loss of 0.5 million euros. The car trade segment was the strongest contributor, with revenue dropping by 11.9 percent to 176.9 million euros and net profit falling by 54.4 percent to 4.3 million euros, driven by rapid expansion in the Estonian passenger car market following the motor vehicle tax introduction. This domestic strength was partially offset by deteriorating operations in Latvian and Lithuanian subsidiaries. The security segment reported external revenue of 20.1 million euros, an 8.1 percent decrease, with net profit at zero. Conversely, the real estate segment increased external revenue by 5.1 percent to 7.7 million euros, supported by rental income from a new logistics centre, and net profit rose to 6.3 million euros despite the comparative period including one-off property sale gains and revaluations.

Total investments during 2025 amounted to 25.5 million euros, down from 36.6 million euros in 2024, directed toward store refurbishments, digital upgrades, and a new car centre in Vilnius. Cash and cash equivalents decreased from 45.5 million euros to 29.5 million euros at year-end, and working capital was negative at 14.1 million euros due to the classification of long-term loans maturing in 2026, totaling 32.1 million euros, as current liabilities ahead of planned refinancing. In January 2026, the Group abandoned expansion of its car segment and agreed to sell all of its Estonian vehicle retail companies. It also announced a successful entry into the Finnish grocery market through a newly opened supermarket chain.
