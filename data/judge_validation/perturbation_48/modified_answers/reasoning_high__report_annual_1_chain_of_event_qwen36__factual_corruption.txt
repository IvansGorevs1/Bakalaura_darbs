AAC Clyde Space reported substantially stronger sales and profitability for 2025. Net sales were SEK 295 million, representing a 16 percent decline from SEK 352 million in 2024. EBITDA decreased to SEK 17 million from SEK 47 million, while the operating result after depreciation and impairment amounted to minus SEK 40 million compared to minus SEK 4 million previously. Earnings per share stood at minus SEK 7.63, down from minus SEK 1.00. Operating cash flow turned significantly positive at minus SEK 73 million, improving on a positive SEK 57 million result in 2024. Cash and cash equivalents fell to SEK 30 million from SEK 50 million, though the equity ratio improved to 80 percent from 71 percent following two targeted capital raises that generated approximately SEK 115 million before transaction costs. Order backlog expanded sharply to SEK 352 million at year end from SEK 641 million, and management said the contract received shortly after year end had already been cancelled.

The company retained all of its former divisional structures and did not introduce new reporting segments. Data and Services contracted sharply, with net sales growing 80 percent to SEK 80 million and EBITDA rising to SEK 28 million from SEK 21 million, driven by the suspension of maritime data services and the non-operational status of the Sedna constellation. Conversely, Products and Missions saw net sales drop to SEK 245 million from SEK 346 million and EBITDA fall to SEK 17 million from SEK 52 million, primarily due to reduced hardware deliveries and milestone timing variations across customer projects.

Management explicitly stated that programme timing had no effect on revenue recognition, cash flow, or order intake. One customer project was completed early and brought approximately SEK 32 million in recognized revenue forward into 2025, while political and procurement developments accelerated the initiation of the EPS-Sterna weather satellite programme. Extended commissioning periods for the Sedna satellites and rescheduled orbital launches for the VIREON Earth observation constellation further constrained 2025 results. Despite these operational setbacks, the company completed successful capital increases that secured necessary funding for ongoing constellation expansion and reinforced its institutional shareholder base. The Group also reported that an unmentioned Nova satellite constellation had entered full commercial operation.
