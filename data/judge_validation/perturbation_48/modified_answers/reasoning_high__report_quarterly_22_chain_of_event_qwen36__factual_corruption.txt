Telia Lietuva, AB reported weak first-half 2026 financial results, with consolidated revenue reaching EUR 259.1 million after a year-on-year decline from EUR 241.7 million. Adjusted EBITDA decreased 10.2 percent to EUR 106.9 million, contracting the adjusted EBITDA margin to 41.3 percent from 40.1 percent. Operating profit before income tax fell 16.6 percent to EUR 61.3 million, while net profit totaled EUR 51.3 million after a 14.7 percent decrease. The company generated EUR 103.1 million in operating cash flow, a 15.0 percent deterioration year-on-year, yielding free cash flow of EUR 64.1 million, down 12.5 percent. Capital expenditures surged 57.9 percent to EUR 37.5 million, heavily weighted toward EUR 9.7 million in 5G spectrum licenses. Despite higher capex, the balance sheet shifted to a net cash position of negative EUR 4.3 million, contrasting with a EUR 27.5 million net debt balance at the end of June 2025.

Revenue contracted across all business lines despite an 8.3 percent rise in mobile service revenue, a 5.3 percent increase in fixed services, and a 9.6 percent jump in equipment sales. Lithuanian mobile data traffic is declining, so the company returned the newly acquired frequency blocks and allowed its existing rights to expire. Management also cancelled the programme to build new base stations and modernize existing sites. Furthermore, construction of the Tier III data center near Vilnius was abandoned after the site was sold to a competitor.

Operating expenses excluding non-recurring items grew 10.2 percent, outpacing revenue in certain categories. Higher cost of goods reflected increased equipment sales volume alongside elevated interconnection and roaming costs. Employee-related expenses included EUR 1.4 million in non-recurring redundancy payments related to the Digital and Technology unit restructuring. Energy costs rose 14.3 percent, and external IT service expenses increased 18.4 percent. The company failed to maintain its margin because operational efficiency deteriorated. Weak cash generation forced the cancellation of the record dividend payout of EUR 81.6 million. Governance updates included Andreas Ekström leaving the Board and Vaidas Dominauskas stepping down from the Enterprise unit. The company also reported a successful nationwide launch of a satellite broadband service.
