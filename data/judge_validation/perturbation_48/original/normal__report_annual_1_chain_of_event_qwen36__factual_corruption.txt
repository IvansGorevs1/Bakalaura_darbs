AAC Clyde Space reported a decline in financial performance for the year 2025, driven by operational delays and a strategic shift in revenue recognition timing. Net sales decreased by 16 percent to SEK 295.3 million, down from SEK 352.9 million in 2024. This reduction was primarily caused by delays in the Products & Missions segment, where technical discussions and procurement postponements shifted approximately SEK 32 million in revenue to 2026. Specifically, the EPS-Sterna programme initiation was delayed due to political and procurement developments, and a separate customer project milestone was postponed. Despite the lower top line, the Data & Services segment demonstrated strong growth, with net sales increasing by 78 percent to SEK 80.1 million from SEK 44.9 million in the prior year. This growth reflects the company’s expanding capability to design, build, and operate its own satellite systems, generating recurring revenue streams.

The Group’s profitability metrics reflected the impact of these delays and increased investment activities. EBITDA fell significantly to SEK 17.5 million from SEK 46.7 million in 2024. The operating result after depreciation and impairment was a loss of SEK 40.0 million, compared to a loss of SEK 4.0 million in 2024. The loss after tax amounted to SEK 46.6 million, resulting in earnings per share of negative SEK 7.63, compared to negative SEK 1.00 in the previous year. Operating cash flow also deteriorated, moving from a positive SEK 56.8 million in 2024 to a negative SEK 72.6 million in 2025, largely due to the timing of revenue recognition and working capital changes associated with project delays.

Key business developments during the period included the signing of a Phase 1 contract with the European Space Agency for the INFLECION programme, aimed at developing a satellite constellation for maritime intelligence. The company also received an order for long-lead components for the EPS-Sterna weather constellation and began assembly of its first VIREON™ Earth Observation satellites. The Sedna-1 and Sedna-2 satellites became fully operational, strengthening the maritime data network. To support these investments and ensure liquidity, the company completed two directed share issues, raising approximately SEK 114.8 million before transaction costs. Notably, Bonnier Capital became a major shareholder through a strategic investment. The equity ratio improved to 80 percent from 71 percent, indicating a strengthened balance sheet despite the operational losses. Order backlog stood at SEK 352 million at year-end, though the CEO noted that a major contract awarded in early 2026 would significantly increase this figure. The company maintained a strong focus on capital discipline while expanding its constellation capacity to meet growing demand for space-based data services.
