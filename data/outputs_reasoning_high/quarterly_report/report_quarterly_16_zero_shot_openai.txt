Nordic Fibreboard AS reported higher revenue but significantly weaker profitability in the first quarter of 2026. Consolidated revenue increased 7.3% to €1.861 million from €1.735 million in Q1 2025, with the fibreboard business accounting for almost all sales. Growth was supported mainly by Finnish DIY stores, where order volume increased by €241 thousand to €855 thousand. European Union revenue rose to €1.781 million from €1.639 million, while sales to Africa declined to €62 thousand from €87 thousand.

Despite the revenue growth, costs increased substantially. Cost of goods sold rose to €1.966 million from €1.608 million, producing a gross loss of €105 thousand compared with a gross profit of €127 thousand a year earlier. Electricity, heat and water costs increased to €1.048 million from €725 thousand. Distribution costs rose to €261 thousand from €228 thousand and administrative expenses to €154 thousand from €111 thousand. EBITDA deteriorated to a negative €395 thousand from a negative €85 thousand, and the EBITDA margin declined to negative 21% from negative 5%. Operating loss widened to €530 thousand from €217 thousand, while the operating margin fell to negative 28% from negative 13%.

The Group attributed the weaker result primarily to the scheduled factory shutdown in early January during the transition to a new boiler house. Commissioning, calibration and start-up of the new system took approximately two months. Cold weather also increased normal energy consumption. In late February, the report states that a US military attack on Iran caused gas market prices to nearly double; although prices subsequently fell, they remained approximately 50% above the beginning-of-year level. The upgraded boiler system was completed in January and is intended to improve production reliability and energy efficiency.

Net loss more than doubled to €570 thousand from €228 thousand. Financial expenses decreased to €41 thousand from €51 thousand, but Q1 2025 had also benefited from €40 thousand of financial income related to the sale and revaluation of Trigon Property Development shares, which did not recur. Labour costs declined to €411 thousand from €471 thousand, alongside a reduction in employees to 57 from 69.

Operating activities generated €13 thousand of cash, compared with a €528 thousand outflow in Q1 2025. Investing activities used €169 thousand, mainly for production assets, versus a €471 thousand inflow a year earlier that included proceeds from selling financial assets. Financing activities provided €152 thousand, including a €343 thousand related-party loan. Net cash decreased by €4 thousand, leaving €1 thousand at quarter-end.

At 31 March 2026, assets were €9.694 million and equity €3.892 million, down from €4.462 million at year-end after the quarterly loss. Liabilities were €5.802 million and the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 60% from 52%. The Group also signed a preliminary agreement to sell ten Pärnu properties by 30 June 2026, while continuing development planning for the Admirali Quarter.
