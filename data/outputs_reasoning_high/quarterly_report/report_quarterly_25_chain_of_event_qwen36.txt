During the first quarter of 2026, VILVI Group reported consolidated revenue of 86.97 million euros, a 22.4 percent increase compared to the prior year. Top-line growth was driven by expanded production volumes and the consolidation of new segments following recent corporate transactions. Revenue from fresh milk products rose 73.4 percent, dry milk products increased 126.9 percent, and the newly consolidated canned milk segment contributed 11.92 million euros. Geographic sales showed accelerated momentum, with Lithuania surging 72.9 percent and other countries advancing 46.6 percent, while European Union sales remained flat. The group procured 155,954 tons of basic indicator milk, up 54.2 percent, reflecting heightened processing capacity utilization.

Operational profitability contracted significantly despite the revenue expansion. EBITDA declined 50.2 percent to 3.00 million euros, reducing the margin to 3.5 percent from 8.5 percent. Gross profit fell to 5.42 million euros from 7.46 million euros, while distribution and administrative expenses more than doubled as the company scaled operations and integrated new assets. Conversely, net profit increased 25.7 percent to 5.09 million euros, delivering earnings per share of 0.43 euros. This divergence between declining EBITDA and rising net earnings is explicitly attributed to a preliminary negative goodwill gain of 5.5 million euros stemming from the January acquisition of Marijampolės pieno konservai UAB. The report specifies that this acquisition-related gain is excluded from EBITDA metrics but flows directly into net profit, where management plans to realize future operational synergies.

Capital investment and financing activities materially altered the financial position. Net financial debt surged 89.7 percent to 83.43 million euros, primarily funded through increased borrowings that accompanied 4.52 million euros in capital expenditures. These investments coincide with the official commencement of commercial cheese production at the new Baltic Dairy Board SIA facility in Bauska, Latvia. The new factory targets an annual capacity of 18,000 tons for export to over forty countries and recently secured third place in the 2025 Latvian Construction Awards. Consequently, total assets expanded to 220.31 million euros, while cash reserves contracted to 951 thousand euros amid active infrastructure deployment and working capital demands. The reporting period fundamentally reflects a strategic shift toward scaled manufacturing and portfolio consolidation, where volume-driven revenue growth and acquisition accounting benefits currently support headline profitability even as near-term operational margins and leverage face headwinds from expansion costs.
