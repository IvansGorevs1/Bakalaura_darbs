The company reported strong profitability growth despite flat revenue in the first half of 2026. Consolidated revenue remained broadly unchanged at EUR 945.9 million compared with the prior year, while EBITDA rose 31 percent to EUR 111.2 million and operating profit nearly doubled to EUR 51.1 million. Second-quarter revenue declined to EUR 440.9 million from EUR 504.5 million, driven mainly by lower energy sales volumes as wholesale trading activity contracted. Gross profit improved to EUR 59.4 million from EUR 55.7 million, reflecting tighter cost management and favorable product mix shifts across operations. The EBITDA margin expanded to 11.8 percent for the six-month period, up from 8.9 percent last year.

Performance diverged materially between business segments. Maritime transport revenues stabilized at EUR 207.0 million in the second quarter, though EBITDA moderated to EUR 34.3 million from EUR 37.4 million. The reduction resulted from an EUR 8.4 million surge in fuel expenses and expanded EU Emissions Trading System compliance requirements that now cover all CO2 equivalent emissions. Additionally, the temporary chartering of the St Patrick vessel reduced fleet utilization. The energy segment saw a 34 percent decrease in sales volumes due to milder weather and reduced wholesale exposure, but maintained stability through dedicated distribution networks and regulated tariffs. The most pronounced improvement occurred in supporting businesses, which reversed a EUR 4.4 million quarterly loss into a EUR 5.6 million profit by integrating new agricultural operations and closing unprofitable lines.

Financial positioning stayed robust, with total equity rising to EUR 1.20 billion and net debt settling at EUR 829.9 million. Liquidity measures strengthened effectively, raising cash reserves to EUR 253.2 million and improving the current ratio to 1.0 from 0.8 year-end levels. During the period, the group advanced key structural initiatives, including purchasing additional shares that raised its Tallink Grupp holding to 68.76 percent, commissioning the Halinga biomethane facility, and continuing the EUR 67.2 million Rail Baltica contract. The energy division also secured a EUR 60 million credit for Polish infrastructure expansion and activated new battery storage systems while navigating forward market pricing trends.

Overall, the company’s results were defined by operational efficiency and strategic execution rather than sales expansion. Successful margin recovery across multiple divisions, combined with disciplined capital allocation and steady cash generation, has fortified the group’s capacity to navigate geopolitical uncertainties and sustain long-term operational growth.
