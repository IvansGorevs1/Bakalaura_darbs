AS Pro Kapital Grupp delivered a strong and profitable performance in 2025, marking a significant turnaround from the net loss recorded in the prior year. Total revenue surged to EUR 53.2 million in 2025, up from EUR 18.2 million in 2024, driven primarily by the handover of completed apartments in the Kalaranna and Uus-Kindrali developments in Tallinn and the City Villas project in Vilnius. Fourth quarter revenue also expanded substantially to EUR 18.9 million from EUR 7.6 million in the same period last year. Gross profit rose to EUR 18.8 million, improving the gross margin to 35 percent from 30 percent, while operating profit reached EUR 14.7 million, lifting the operating margin to 28 percent from just 1 percent. The group reported a net profit of EUR 12.0 million for the full year compared to a net loss of EUR 3.9 million in 2024, resulting in earnings per share of EUR 0.22 versus negative EUR 0.06 previously. Cash generated from operating activities turned positive at EUR 10.8 million in 2025, reversing EUR 10.4 million used in the prior year. Return on equity improved sharply to 21.0 percent from negative 7.0 percent, and the debt-to-equity ratio declined to 0.97 from 1.32. Net asset value per share increased to EUR 1.12.

The financial improvement aligns with key operational milestones and strategic actions throughout the year. Residential project completions in Estonia and Lithuania heavily boosted revenue recognition, with the Estonian segment accounting for 75 percent of total sales, up from 27 percent in 2024. In Riga, construction advanced on the Blue Marine residential building within the Kliversala Quarter, supported by a new construction loan from BluOr Bank. Financially, the group partially redeemed EUR 9.405 million of its senior secured callable fixed rate bonds in August 2025, reducing the outstanding secured bond nominal amount to EUR 10.545 million. The group also increased its ownership in its Italian subsidiary, Preatoni Nuda Proprietà S.r.l., to 77.5 percent following a EUR 280 thousand acquisition. While the Italian and German hotel segments faced subdued market conditions, with hotel occupancy softening to 65 percent, the broader portfolio demonstrated resilience. Management further streamlined operations by committing to sell the Brivibas Business Quarter commercial property to refocus on core residential development. Overall, the group successfully converted stable execution into sustainable growth, strengthening its balance sheet, generating positive operating cash flows, and delivering robust profitability across its Baltic markets.
