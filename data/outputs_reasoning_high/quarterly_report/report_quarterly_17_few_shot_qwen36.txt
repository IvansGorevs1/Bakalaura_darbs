The company executed a strategic pivot toward profitability during the twelve-month period ended 31 December 2025, deliberately reducing service volumes while achieving measurable improvements in operational efficiency and margins. Consolidated revenue fell by 16.1 percent year on year to EUR 168.48 million, reflecting a 20 percent decline in passengers carried to 191,000 travelers. This contraction stemmed from a managed compression of the travel programme and the discontinuation of unprofitable routes amid intense industry competition and excess travel supply. Despite the reduced top line, gross profit contracted by a smaller 5.3 percent to EUR 19.65 million, lifting the gross profit margin by 1.3 percentage points to 11.66 percent.

Operating and bottom-line results strengthened substantially as the group shifted away from volume-driven growth. The annual net loss narrowed sharply by 39 percent to EUR 1.17 million, largely benefiting from the absence of a EUR 5.7 million non-cash goodwill impairment that weighed heavily on the prior year. EBITDA reversed course to post a positive EUR 420 thousand, marking a decisive improvement from a negative EUR 6.1 million result in 2024, while adjusted EBITDA similarly recovered to EUR 420 thousand from a negative EUR 449 thousand. The turnaround was underpinned by stricter supply discipline, optimized destination mix, and an aircraft load factor that climbed by 1.5 percentage points to 97.2 percent. Momentum building into the next cycle was evident through early summer bookings, which surged 59 percent compared with the previous year.

External headwinds, including heightened market capacity, autumn pricing pressure, and temporary flight disruptions at Vilnius Airport due to unauthorized drone activity, curtailed a faster demand recovery. Nevertheless, the organization successfully rebalanced its commercial approach, prioritizing yield over scale. Management expenses remained controlled, and the firm outlined a continuation of its efficiency-focused strategy for the coming year, complemented by planned expansions in competitively priced accommodation offerings. On the capital side, total borrowings increased to EUR 12.87 million from EUR 9.25 million, raising the debt-to-equity ratio and compressing the equity buffer. Overall, the reporting period demonstrated a successful corrective realignment, establishing a more resilient and margin-oriented business model despite a structurally challenging travel market.
