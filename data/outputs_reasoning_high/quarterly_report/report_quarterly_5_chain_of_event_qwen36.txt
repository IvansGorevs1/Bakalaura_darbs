Arco Vara AS reported a marked turnaround in financial performance for the first quarter of 2025, transitioning from a net loss to a net profit of 103 thousand euros compared to a loss of 349 thousand euros in the same period last year. This improvement was accompanied by a substantial rise in sales revenue, which reached 1,706 thousand euros, an increase of 716 thousand euros from the 990 thousand euros recorded in Q1 2024. Operating profit also reversed its trajectory, rising to 259 thousand euros from an operating loss of 208 thousand euros previously. Earnings per share improved to 0.01 euros, up from negative 0.03 euros.

The positive financial shift was primarily driven by strong demand in Estonia’s real estate market, which continued a moderate recovery that began in late 2024. The continuous decline of the Euribor, alongside stabilizing inflation, bolstered consumer confidence and transaction activity, particularly in the residential segment. Within this backdrop, the group successfully marketed its active development projects. In the Rannakalda project, four additional apartments were sold, bringing total sales to 91 out of 113 units. Pre-sales for the Soodi 6 project advanced to ten out of sixty-six apartments, while the Bulgarian Botanica Lozen development saw nine out of sixteen houses sold amid ninety-five percent construction readiness. Franchise and service revenues also contributed, rising to sixty-five thousand euros from fifty-five thousand euros.

Despite the profitability turnaround, the company’s cash position contracted as operating activities consumed two thousand eight hundred thirty-eight thousand euros, reflecting ongoing development expenditures and land acquisition costs. To fund these investments, including newly acquired sites in Tallinn and construction phases for upcoming projects, the group expanded its debt footprint. Net loans increased by one thousand nine hundred sixty thousand euros to reach fifteen thousand four hundred fifty-five thousand euros at quarter-end. The expansion was largely facilitated by refinancing completed developments with bank loans and securing new financing for active construction sites. Consequently, total interest-bearing liabilities grew by one thousand three hundred fifty-three thousand euros during the quarter. However, the cost of borrowing moderated slightly, with the weighted average annual interest rate decreasing by half a percentage point to eight point two percent, a change explicitly linked to the broader decline in Euribor rates.

Management anticipates continued market stability supported by improving financing conditions and a robust pipeline of pre-sales. The strategic focus remains on liquidating completed inventory to generate stable cash flows, supporting the launch of larger-scale developments scheduled for construction throughout 2025. Personnel costs were managed effectively, with staff reducing to thirteen employees, contributing to leaner administrative overhead. Overall, the first quarter demonstrated successful execution of sales and financing strategies within a cautiously optimistic environment.
