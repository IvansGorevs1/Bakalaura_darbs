Arco Vara AS reported a clear improvement in its financial performance during the first quarter of 2025, transitioning from prior-year losses to profitability. Group revenue rose to 1,706 thousand euros, an increase of 716 thousand euros compared to 990 thousand euros in the same period last year, driven primarily by higher volumes of sold residential properties in Estonia. Operating profit turned positive at 259 thousand euros against a loss of 208 thousand euros previously, while net profit reached 103 thousand euros compared to a loss of 349 thousand euros. This turnaround was supported by stronger sales realization in active development segments and disciplined expense management, which collectively improved operating margins despite persistent fixed costs.

Cash generation remained constrained by the capital-intensive nature of ongoing construction projects. Outflows from operating activities widened to 2,838 thousand euros, partially financed by new borrowing that expanded total interest-bearing liabilities to 16,568 thousand euros. Net debt increased by 1,960 thousand euros to 15,455 thousand euros. Nevertheless, the group benefited from favorable financing conditions, as the weighted average annual interest rate on loans declined by 0.5 percentage points to 8.2 percent, reflecting broader declines in benchmark lending rates. Shareholders equity stood at 19,842 thousand euros, resulting in an equity ratio of 46.4 percent.

Operational results were shaped by a gradual recovery in the Estonian real estate market, characterized by stabilizing price levels and renewed buyer activity following prolonged periods of elevated borrowing costs. Continued reductions in the Euribor and moderating inflation have restored transaction momentum, particularly within the residential segment. Arco Vara advanced its development pipeline across multiple locations, securing additional pre-sales at the Rannakalda and Soodi 6 projects and acquiring a new residential site in Tallinn. The company also progressed detailed planning for larger future phases while carefully aligning construction schedules. In Bulgaria, the Botanica Lozen project neared completion with stable pre-sales, supporting planned handovers in the second quarter. Management emphasized that current sales efforts remain concentrated on finished units to sustain cash flow and fund upcoming launches.

Overall, the first quarter marked a transitional phase where early signs of market stabilization translated into improved earnings and steady operational execution. While long development cycles and refinancing requirements continue to influence liquidity dynamics, the combination of lower borrowing costs, an expanding project portfolio, and cautious market recovery positions the group for measured growth as it moves forward with scheduled deliveries.
