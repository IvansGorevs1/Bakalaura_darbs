AS LHV Group reported a consolidated net profit of EUR 24.7 million for the second quarter of 2026, an increase of EUR 5.0 million from the first quarter but a decrease of EUR 6.1 million compared to the same period last year. Total net income rose 7.3 percent sequentially to EUR 79.0 million and grew 7.0 percent against Q2 2025, reaching EUR 73.9 million. Basic earnings per share amounted to EUR 0.07, while return on equity stood at 13.1 percent, up from 10.7 percent in the prior quarter yet 4.3 percentage points below the 17.4 percent recorded in Q2 2025. Operating expenses decreased by EUR 2.0 million quarter-on-quarter to EUR 43.6 million, though they remained 8 percent higher than in Q2 2025. Loan and bond provisions increased to EUR 5.8 million in the quarter, reflecting forward-looking impairment adjustments, whereas income tax expenses fell to EUR 5.0 million. The capital adequacy ratio declined slightly to 20.8 percent at quarter-end.

The loan portfolio expanded by 3 percent to EUR 5,768 million over the quarter, driven by residential mortgages and corporate lending, particularly in real estate. Customer deposits contracted by EUR 245 million sequentially, mainly due to reduced volumes from financial intermediaries and deliberate platform deposit reductions. Net interest income grew 5 percent quarter-on-quarter to EUR 62.5 million, benefiting from elevated market interest rates. Net service fee income remained stable at EUR 14.6 million but underperformed plans due to subdued client activity. Other financial income improved significantly, supported by bond portfolio revaluations. Management noted that while costs were slightly above forecast, the core driver of net interest income exceeded internal expectations.

Strategically, the quarter featured major operational and technological transitions. The bank completed the migration of its core systems to a cloud-based architecture on Amazon Web Services, improving scalability and development speed. Leadership shifted to a product-based management model and adjusted board composition accordingly. The group obtained Estonia’s first MiCA-compliant crypto-assets service provider license and initiated preparations to rebrand LHV Pank as LHV Bank AS to support international identity. Applications for cross-border European Economic Area banking authorization were also advanced. Macroeconomic conditions included persistent geopolitical tensions, fluctuating energy prices, and continued European Central Bank rate hikes. Despite cautious corporate investment in Estonia, household consumption and lending activity remained resilient. The group maintained strong asset quality while proactively adjusting impairment models, positioning itself for sustained profitability and structural modernization.
