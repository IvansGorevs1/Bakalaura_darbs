AS Tallink Grupp’s 2025 performance was weaker year on year, although results improved notably in the fourth quarter. The Group carried 5.53 million passengers, a 0.9% decline from 2024, while cargo volumes fell 19.2% to 245,004 units and passenger vehicles decreased 2.2% to 760,473. Revenue declined by EUR 20.5 million to EUR 765.3 million. Gross profit fell to EUR 143.2 million from EUR 153.6 million, EBITDA decreased to EUR 130.1 million from EUR 175.2 million, and EBIT declined to EUR 50.3 million from EUR 77.4 million. Net profit was EUR 17.3 million, compared with EUR 40.3 million in 2024, reducing earnings per share from EUR 0.054 to EUR 0.023.

Results were affected by low consumer and business confidence, economic uncertainty in Finland and the other core markets, geopolitical tensions, restrained spending and weaker cargo demand. Revenue from route operations decreased to EUR 625.2 million, with the segment result falling to EUR 70.5 million from EUR 81.5 million. Estonia–Finland revenue increased to EUR 314.5 million as passenger volumes rose 1.8%, but its segment result declined by EUR 10.9 million. Finland–Sweden revenue fell to EUR 225.5 million and its result declined to EUR 1.3 million, partly reflecting 68 days of maintenance on Baltic Princess and Silja Serenade. Estonia–Sweden revenue decreased to EUR 85.3 million, although its segment loss narrowed to EUR 1.2 million. The Other segment generated EUR 145.5 million of revenue and a broadly stable EUR 28.5 million result.

Operating costs from operations increased 1.4% to EUR 640.9 million, while depreciation and amortisation fell 18.4% to EUR 79.8 million following the change in estimated vessel useful lives and the sale of three vessels. Net finance costs declined by EUR 6.8 million to EUR 21.4 million because of lower debt balances and interest rates. Capital expenditure increased to EUR 33.0 million from EUR 22.4 million, mainly for vessel maintenance and refurbishment, with planned maintenance lasting 73 days.

The Group sold Star I, Regal Star and Sailor and ended the year with no vessels in lay-up, while three vessels remained chartered out. Net debt was EUR 432.4 million, down from EUR 537.7 million at the end of 2024, and the net debt-to-EBITDA ratio was 3.3. Cash and unused credit lines formed a EUR 110.4 million liquidity buffer after substantial debt repayments, dividends and related taxes.

Fourth-quarter revenue rose 2.4% to EUR 188.0 million, EBITDA increased to EUR 27.6 million, and net profit recovered to EUR 12.2 million from a EUR 5.2 million loss. Management intends to propose another dividend of EUR 0.06 per share for 2026, while continuing to manage economic, regulatory, fuel-cost and emissions-related pressures.
