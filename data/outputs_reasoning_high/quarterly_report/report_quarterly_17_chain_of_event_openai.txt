AB Novaturas reported improved profitability in 2025 despite a significant reduction in business volume. The Group served 190.9 thousand travellers, down 20.3% from 239.5 thousand in 2024, while revenue declined 16.1% to EUR 168.483 million. Gross profit decreased by only 5.3% to EUR 19.650 million, increasing the gross margin by 1.3 percentage points to 11.66%.

Operating performance improved substantially. The operating loss narrowed to EUR 274 thousand from EUR 6.555 million, while EBITDA reached EUR 420 thousand compared with negative EUR 6.132 million in 2024. Adjusted EBITDA, excluding the EUR 5.7 million goodwill impairment recorded in 2024, also improved from negative EUR 449 thousand to positive EUR 420 thousand. EBITDA was positive in the second half of 2025 at EUR 674 thousand, although adjusted EBITDA was lower than the EUR 1.116 million recorded in the second half of 2024. The annual net loss fell to EUR 1.169 million from EUR 7.604 million, and earnings per share improved from negative EUR 0.97 to negative EUR 0.15. Excluding the prior-year goodwill impairment, management reported a 39% reduction in the annual loss compared with the comparable 2024 result.

The improvement was supported by lower selling expenses, which fell 11.3% to EUR 15.170 million, and general and administrative expenses, which declined 53.1% to EUR 4.867 million. Novaturas deliberately reduced volumes on loss-making routes, optimized its travel programme, emphasized higher-margin destinations and applied stricter supply discipline. The aircraft load factor consequently increased from 95.7% to 97.2%; long-haul load factor rose to 98.5%, while ski destination load factor increased from 90.5% to 97.2%.

Flight package tours remained the core business. Their revenue decreased 15.0%, but segment sales profit increased 18.0% to EUR 12.162 million, with the margin rising by 7.8 percentage points. In contrast, sightseeing tours by plane generated 84.4% less segment sales profit, and the loss from other sales widened 12.4% to EUR 2.515 million.

Performance was constrained by intense competition, excess travel supply and autumn price pressure. Disruptions at Vilnius Airport linked to illegal cross-border activity involving unmanned balloons also reduced last-minute demand. The Group ended the year with EUR 1.691 million in cash, while total borrowings increased to EUR 12.867 million from EUR 9.250 million and the equity ratio fell to 16.96% from 20.33%. Looking ahead, early bookings for summer 2026 were 59% higher by December 2025, and the Group plans a similar or slightly larger travel programme focused on further efficiency and profitability improvements.
