During the first three months of 2026, AS Amber Latvijas balzams experienced a significant decline in financial performance amid severe operational, market, and legal challenges. Total sales volume dropped by 36.1 percent to 517,600 nine-liter cases compared to 810,079 nine-liter cases in the corresponding period of 2025. Unaudited net turnover decreased by 29.6 percent to EUR 11.1 million, down from EUR 15.8 million in the first three months of 2025. This reduction in revenue was primarily driven by lower sales volumes and a high comparison base from the previous year, which had benefited from a contractual revenue adjustment. Gross profit fell by 66.5 percent, or EUR 2.3 million, to EUR 1.2 million compared to EUR 3.5 million in the prior year period, impacted by changes in the product mix and pricing methodology adjustments.

The company reported an operating loss of EUR 1.6 million for the reporting period, representing a sharp deterioration from an operating profit of EUR 0.6 million in the first three months of 2025, resulting in an operating profit margin of -14.5 percent compared to 3.7 percent previously. Unaudited net results for the first three months of 2026 showed a net loss of EUR 1.4 million, compared with a net profit of EUR 0.9 million in the corresponding period of 2025. Earnings per share declined to negative EUR 0.19 from EUR 0.11 in the prior year period.

These financial declines were heavily influenced by several major external and structural events. In January 2026, Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceedings commenced for related group company Stoli Group USA, leading to a loss of control within the U.S. jurisdiction and directly impairing the solvency of the company's largest customer. Additional severe pressures stemmed from geopolitical fallout, including Russian authorities designating related entities as extremist and confiscating the Talvis distillery following the principal shareholder's public support for Ukraine, as well as a broader downturn in the global spirits market affecting export sales. These combined pressures created critical cash flow difficulties, resulting in delayed excise tax payments and the subsequent freezing of bank accounts by the Latvian State Revenue Service.

To protect creditors and ensure business continuity, the company filed for legal protection proceedings, which were approved by the Riga City Court in February 2026 to restructure liabilities. Furthermore, the legal protection filing automatically triggered events of default for the principal shareholder under its bond and bank financing arrangements, leading to subsequent judicial reorganization proceedings and a four-month moratorium granted by a Luxembourg court. Despite these severe operational and financial headwinds, the company paid EUR 8.4 million in taxes to the state budget during the reporting period, including EUR 6.4 million in excise tax, while management continues to evaluate measures to improve production efficiency and stabilize operations.
