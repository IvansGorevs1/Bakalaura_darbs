AB Novaturas reported a smaller operating scale but improved profitability in the twelve-month period ended 31 December 2025. The Group served 190.9 thousand travellers, a 20.3% decline from 2024, while revenue decreased 16.1% to EUR 168.5 million. Despite the lower volumes, gross profit declined by only 5.3% to EUR 19.7 million, and the gross profit margin improved by 1.3 percentage points to 11.66%. The improvement reflected tighter control of the travel programme, the removal of loss-making routes and greater emphasis on higher-margin destinations.

Operating performance improved substantially. The operating loss narrowed to EUR 274 thousand from EUR 6.6 million in 2024, while EBITDA turned positive at EUR 420 thousand compared with negative EUR 6.1 million. Adjusted EBITDA, which excludes the EUR 5.7 million goodwill impairment recorded in 2024, improved from negative EUR 449 thousand to positive EUR 420 thousand. The reported net loss narrowed to EUR 1.17 million from EUR 7.60 million, and earnings per share improved to negative EUR 0.15 from negative EUR 0.97. Excluding the prior-year goodwill impairment, management reported a 39% reduction in the annual net loss. Selling expenses fell 11.3% to EUR 15.2 million and general and administrative expenses decreased 53.1% to EUR 4.9 million.

Flight package tours remained the main business and generated EUR 145.1 million in sales, down 15.0%, but segment sales profit increased 18.0% to EUR 12.2 million. Its sales profit margin rose to 8% from 1%, supported by a higher gross margin and lower commission expenses. By contrast, sightseeing-tour sales declined, with sales profit from plane tours falling 84.4% to EUR 135 thousand. Other sales generated a EUR 2.5 million loss, compared with a EUR 2.2 million loss in 2024.

The Group operated amid intense competition and excess travel supply. Management intentionally reduced volumes and optimized capacity; the aircraft load factor nevertheless improved by 1.5 percentage points to 97.2%. Long-haul and ski-tour load factors also increased materially. Autumn market-wide supply growth pressured prices, while disruptions at Vilnius Airport associated with illegal cross-border balloon activity reduced last-minute demand. Early bookings for the 2026 summer season were 59% higher by December 2025, and the Group planned a similar or slightly larger programme, including new Morocco and Mallorca offerings.

Financial leverage increased: borrowings rose to EUR 12.9 million, equity declined to EUR 6.9 million, and the debt-to-equity ratio increased to 191.4% from 121.2%. Cash and cash equivalents increased to EUR 1.7 million, partly supported by EUR 2.2 million of net financing cash inflows, while operating cash flow was negative EUR 656 thousand. The Group had EUR 21 million of travel guarantees and EUR 4.5 million of related cash deposits, and reported compliance with some, but not all, financial covenants.
