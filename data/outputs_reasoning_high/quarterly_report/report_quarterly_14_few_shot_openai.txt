AS Merko Ehitus’ financial performance weakened in the first half of 2026, despite a stronger second quarter and a substantial increase in its secured order book. Revenue for the six months was EUR 149.9 million, down 10.7% from EUR 167.9 million a year earlier. Second-quarter revenue, however, increased to EUR 92.7 million from EUR 82.6 million as major projects entered more active construction phases and apartment deliveries increased.

Profitability declined significantly year on year. Gross profit fell to EUR 17.4 million from EUR 29.1 million, reducing the gross margin from 17.3% to 11.6%. Operating profit decreased to EUR 8.4 million from EUR 18.6 million, while the operating margin declined from 11.1% to 5.6%. Pre-tax profit was EUR 10.8 million, compared with EUR 23.5 million in the first half of 2025, and net profit attributable to shareholders fell to EUR 12.8 million from EUR 21.7 million. The net profit margin decreased from 12.9% to 8.6%, and EBITDA declined to EUR 10.3 million from EUR 20.1 million. In the second quarter, net profit was EUR 8.5 million, compared with EUR 11.2 million a year earlier.

Construction services generated EUR 118.3 million of revenue, broadly in line with the prior-year period, but operating profit fell by 65.5% to EUR 3.0 million. Management attributed the decline to the expiration of successful contracts reported in earlier periods and a return to more typical margins in a highly competitive market. Real estate development revenue decreased by 33.9% to EUR 31.6 million, while operating profit fell 40.8% to EUR 7.0 million. Apartment sales declined to 120 from 222, although second-quarter apartment-sale revenue remained broadly stable at EUR 20.6 million.

The most positive development was the order position. The secured order book reached a record EUR 835.7 million at 30 June, approximately 88% above the prior year, supported by EUR 486.4 million of new contracts signed during the first half. New second-quarter contracts included projects in Tallinn’s Krulli Quarter. Construction of the Rūdninkai defence force campus also commenced at full scale after the reporting date; the two phases have a combined construction cost exceeding EUR 370 million. Public-sector, defence-related and Rail Baltica projects continued to support demand, while private-sector customers remained cautious.

Cash and cash equivalents increased to EUR 39.6 million from EUR 25.9 million, but operating cash flow worsened to negative EUR 31.6 million, primarily reflecting higher inventories and construction-contract balances. Additional borrowing supported development activity, raising the debt ratio to 15.8% from 6.1% and net debt to EUR 29.6 million. The company invested EUR 42.6 million in development projects and paid EUR 22.1 million in dividends. Results remain exposed to elevated energy, labour and construction-input prices, geopolitical uncertainty and price-sensitive residential demand, although management expects moderate growth in Baltic construction markets.
