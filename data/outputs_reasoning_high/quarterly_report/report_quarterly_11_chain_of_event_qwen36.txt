Kvika Banki hf. reported a Q2 2026 pre-tax profit of ISK 1,545 million, a decrease of 23.7 percent year-over-year and 14.6 percent from Q1 2026. After-tax profit fell to ISK 903 million. The decline stemmed directly from a compressed net interest margin, which dropped to 4.0 percent from 4.5 percent in the prior quarter, and a 26.8 percent year-over-year drop in fee income to ISK 1,416 million due to sluggish capital markets. While net interest income remained relatively stable year-over-year at ISK 3,013 million, it fell quarter-on-quarter as the positive inflation balance contribution diminished and a ISK 10.2 billion dividend payment impacted earnings. Additionally, net impairment provisions doubled to ISK 269 million, a cost efficiency charge added ISK 112 million to expenses, and the effective tax rate climbed to 34.8 percent from 24.4 percent following funding derivative closures.

Underlying cost management held firm, with administrative expenses decreasing 1.1 percent to ISK 2,947 million despite the charge, paving the way for ISK 300 to 400 million in annualized savings. The balance sheet grew steadily, with total assets at ISK 375.7 billion and customer loans expanding 7.6 percent to ISK 223.3 billion. Deposits advanced 16.5 percent year-over-year to ISK 201.2 billion, driving the loan-to-deposit ratio down to 111 percent. Credit quality stayed resilient, with impairment coverage stable at 1.2 percent of gross loans. Increased impaired exposures were isolated to one well-collateralized United Kingdom position rather than systemic weakness. Business segments demonstrated broad lending strength, while investment banking adapted to weaker transaction volumes.

Near-term profitability was intentionally moderated to fund strategic capital improvements that will bolster long-term returns. Distributing a special dividend, issuing Tier 1 instruments, and refinancing into cheaper senior foreign currency debt, which achieved an 85 basis point pricing improvement, temporarily reduce margins but will enhance return on equity and widen net interest margins as older liabilities retire in the second half of 2026. The bank maintains strong buffers, recording a 276 percent liquidity coverage ratio and a 23.0 percent capital adequacy ratio. Concurrent divestitures of Straumur and major United Kingdom assets, alongside executive succession planning, will streamline operations and sharpen strategic focus. These optimization steps position the institution to capitalize on improved funding economics and sustained loan growth as conditions stabilize.
