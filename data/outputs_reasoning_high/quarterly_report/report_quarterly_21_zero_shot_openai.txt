AS Tallinna Sadam’s revenue increased by EUR 1.4 million, or 2.4%, to EUR 59.3 million in the six months ended 30 June 2026. Growth was mainly supported by a higher number of charter days for the icebreaker Botnica, which increased charter-fee revenue by EUR 0.9 million, higher electricity sales and tariffs, and increased ferry-service revenue. Revenue rose in the Passenger harbours, Ferry and Other segments, but declined by 2.5% in Cargo harbours.

Despite higher revenue, profitability weakened significantly. Operating expenses increased by EUR 3.9 million, or 24.0%, to EUR 20.2 million. Fuel costs rose 47.6% to EUR 4.4 million due to higher diesel prices and more severe ice conditions, while electricity costs increased 28.8% to EUR 3.1 million. Technical maintenance and repair expenses rose 80.0% to EUR 2.8 million, reflecting more extensive repairs to Botnica, ferries and harbour infrastructure. Personnel expenses and depreciation, amortisation and impairment also increased by 4.7% and 6.0%, respectively. The comparative period additionally benefited from a EUR 0.9 million insurance indemnity related to Botnica and a EUR 0.9 million profit from the sale of land for the Rail Baltica project.

Operating profit fell EUR 4.7 million, or 25.7%, to EUR 13.6 million, reducing the operating margin from 31.6% to 22.9%. Adjusted EBITDA declined 13.5% to EUR 25.8 million, with the margin decreasing from 51.5% to 43.5%. Adjusted EBITDA decreased in every segment, most notably in Cargo harbours, down EUR 1.9 million, and Ferry, down EUR 1.8 million. Lower net finance costs partly offset the operating deterioration, but profit before tax still fell 27.3% to EUR 11.4 million. Profit for the period declined 41.7% to EUR 6.0 million, while earnings per share decreased from EUR 0.04 to EUR 0.02. The EUR 5.4 million income-tax expense was unchanged and related to the EUR 19.2 million dividend paid in May.

Operating volumes were generally weaker. Cargo volumes declined 5.0% to 6.5 million tonnes, mainly because liquid bulk fell 33.8%. Passenger numbers decreased 2.0% to 3.6 million, although cruise passengers increased 47.8%. Vessel calls fell 4.8%. Botnica’s charter days rose from 110 to 130, supported by project work in the North Sea.

Investments decreased 70.6% to EUR 3.5 million. Operating cash flow fell to EUR 18.2 million from EUR 26.3 million, and cash declined to EUR 27.6 million after dividend payments. At period-end, net debt was EUR 145.8 million, 1.5% higher than a year earlier. The Group also secured an unsecured loan facility of up to EUR 32 million. Management identified geopolitical tensions, higher energy costs and uncertainty over cargo flows as key risks.
