Hepsor AS reported consolidated revenue of 35.4 million euros for 2025, declining from 38.4 million euros in 2024. Fourth quarter revenue fell to 4.5 million euros compared to 10.5 million euros in the previous year. The group posted a full-year net profit of 1.0 million euros, down from 2.1 million euros, while the fourth quarter recorded a net loss of 0.5 million euros versus a net profit of 1.0 million euros in Q4 2024. Performance deterioration resulted from fewer apartment handovers totaling 141 units, a decrease from 194 units in 2024, alongside compressed gross margins on sold projects which dropped to 16.9 percent from 17.8 percent. Marketing expenses increased to 1.3 million euros from 0.9 million euros due to more intensive sales efforts required by a larger inventory of projects available for sale. Finance and personnel costs remained stable year-over-year.

Underlying market demand remained strong, with the group securing primary sales agreements for 185 homes worth 36.1 million euros, nearly doubling the 19.8 million euros achieved in 2024. The four-quarter moving average of initial sales rose 182 percent to 9 million euros in Q4 2025. Development activity accelerated, with residential construction increasing 223 percent to 428 homes under construction by year-end. Construction started on three projects in Riga comprising 262 homes, and in Tallinn, the Manufaktuuri Vabrik project reached a construction contract valued at 33.3 million euros. Commercial operations saw a partial occupancy permit granted for StokOfiss U34 in Riga, achieving 74 percent lease coverage for the building and 84 percent across the completed portfolio, supplemented by new long-term agreements with Maxima Eesti and GYM Eesti.

Financial position strengthened as total assets grew to 91.8 million euros. Total equity expanded 23 percent to 27.1 million euros, driven mainly by a non-cash property contribution to the voluntary reserve of joint venture Hepsor PHX5 OÜ. Debt burden eased with interest-bearing liabilities falling to 47.7 million euros, reducing the debt ratio to 52.0 percent from 61.6 percent. A key financing achievement occurred in November 2025 with an oversubscribed bond issuance raising 8 million euros at a fixed 9.50 percent interest rate. Operating cash flow generated 14.3 million euros. Canadian focus centered on urban planning, highlighted by a Toronto City Council decision granting rights for the Weston Road project, expanding the allowable building volume to 62,000 square metres.
