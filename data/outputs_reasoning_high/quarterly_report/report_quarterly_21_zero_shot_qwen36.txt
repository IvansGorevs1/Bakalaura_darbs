For the six-month period ended 30 June 2026, AS Tallinna Sadam generated total revenue of EUR 59.3 million, a 2.4 percent increase compared to EUR 57.9 million in the prior year period. Growth stemmed primarily from higher charter fees for the multifunctional icebreaker Botnica, increased electricity sales following new network tariffs and regulatory security fees, and modest ferry service revenue growth. These gains were offset by a 24.0 percent rise in operating expenses to EUR 20.2 million. Cost pressures arose mainly from elevated diesel prices and harsher ice conditions increasing fuel consumption, higher technical maintenance spending for the Botnica and fleet, rising electricity costs, and increased depreciation and impairment tied to newly commissioned quays.

Operating profit consequently declined by 25.7 percent to EUR 13.6 million, compressing the operating margin to 22.9 percent from 31.6 percent. Adjusted EBITDA fell by 13.5 percent to EUR 25.8 million, with margins dropping to 43.5 percent. Profit for the period decreased by 41.7 percent to EUR 6.0 million, yielding earnings per share of EUR 0.02 compared to EUR 0.04 previously. In the second quarter alone, revenue reached EUR 31.1 million, up 5.4 percent year-on-year, while quarterly profit fell to EUR 1.4 million, a 59.0 percent decline. This sharp deterioration contrasts with 2025, which included non-recurring gains from a Rail Baltica land sale and an icebreaker repair indemnity. Lower average debt balances partially cushioned the impact by reducing net finance costs.

Operational metrics indicated shifting trade and travel patterns. Total cargo volumes fell by 5.0 percent to 6.5 million tonnes, heavily impacted by a 33.8 percent contraction in liquid bulk amid altered regional trade flows and sanctions-related routing adjustments. Vessel calls dropped 4.8 percent to 3,376. Total passenger traffic decreased by 2.0 percent to 3.6 million, though cruise passengers grew 47.8 percent due to added cruise schedules, while temporary ferry dry-dockings limited mainline route figures. Capital expenditure contracted sharply by 70.6 percent to EUR 3.5 million, allocating funds to ferry engine replacements, quay modernization, and terminal design studies. Management also finalized a EUR 32 million unsecured credit facility, maintained full covenant compliance, and distributed a EUR 19.2 million dividend. Overall, the reporting period highlighted resilient revenue diversification alongside noticeably tighter profitability driven by energy and maintenance inflation, normalization of one-off commercial gains, and structural cargo throughput reductions.
