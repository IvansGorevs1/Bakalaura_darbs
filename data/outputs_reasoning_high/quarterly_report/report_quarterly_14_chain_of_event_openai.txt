AS Merko Ehitus generated revenue of EUR 149.9 million in the first six months of 2026, down 10.7% from EUR 167.9 million a year earlier. Second-quarter revenue increased to EUR 92.7 million from EUR 82.6 million. Profitability declined substantially for the half-year: operating profit fell to EUR 8.4 million from EUR 18.6 million, EBITDA to EUR 10.3 million from EUR 20.1 million, and pre-tax profit to EUR 10.8 million from EUR 23.5 million. Net profit attributable to shareholders decreased to EUR 12.8 million from EUR 21.7 million. The pre-tax margin fell to 7.2% from 14.0% and the net profit margin to 8.6% from 12.9%. Second-quarter net profit was EUR 8.5 million, compared with EUR 11.2 million in the prior-year quarter.

The construction services segment reported relatively stable six-month revenue of EUR 118.3 million, down 1.5%, but operating profit dropped 65.5% to EUR 3.0 million and its margin declined to 2.6% from 7.3%. Management attributed the weaker margin primarily to the expiration of successful contracts from previous periods and a return to more typical margins in a highly competitive environment, with half-year results also affected by first-quarter seasonality. The real estate development segment’s revenue fell 33.9% to EUR 31.6 million and operating profit declined 40.8% to EUR 7.0 million. Apartment sales decreased to 120 from 222, while apartment-sale revenue fell to EUR 27.6 million from EUR 44.9 million. Conditions improved sequentially in the second quarter, when 84 apartments were sold and development revenue reached EUR 22.6 million, compared with EUR 21.6 million a year earlier.

The strongest positive development was the secured order book, which reached a record EUR 835.7 million at 30 June, up approximately 88% from EUR 443.8 million a year earlier. New contracts signed during the half-year totaled EUR 486.4 million, compared with EUR 223.1 million in 2025. Major awards included the Krulli Quarter commercial building, event centre and infrastructure works in Tallinn. The Rūdninkai defence campus project also entered full-scale construction in early July; its two phases have total construction costs exceeding EUR 370 million and are expected to contribute significantly to revenue in coming quarters.

Management reported increased activity in both segments during the second quarter, but also rising construction material and equipment prices, with no expected easing of price pressures. Public-sector, defence and Rail Baltica projects continued to support demand while private-sector customers remained cautious and price-sensitive. Cash and cash equivalents were EUR 39.6 million versus EUR 25.9 million, but operating cash flow worsened to negative EUR 31.6 million, mainly reflecting higher inventories and construction-contract balances. Additional development borrowing raised net debt to EUR 29.6 million and the debt ratio to 15.8% from 6.1%. The company paid EUR 22.1 million in dividends, or EUR 1.25 per share.
