Kvika banki hf. reported a pre-tax profit of ISK 1,545 million for Q2 2026, decreasing by 23.7 percent year-on-year and 14.6 percent quarter-on-quarter, driven by lower net operating income and a higher effective tax rate of 34.8 percent. After-tax profit declined to ISK 903 million. Net interest income rose 1.7 percent to ISK 3,013 million against the prior year, bolstered by steady customer lending growth. However, it fell from Q1 2026 due to reduced inflation balance contributions and the impact of an ISK 10.2 billion dividend payout. The net interest margin contracted to 4.0 percent from 4.5 percent in the first quarter. Net fee and commission income dropped 26.8 percent to ISK 1,416 million, reflecting sustained low activity in equity and fixed-income markets. Administrative expenses totaled ISK 2,947 million, a 1.1 percent year-on-year reduction despite an ISK 112 million one-off restructuring charge aimed at achieving ISK 300 to 400 million in annualized savings. Net impairment provisions increased to ISK 269 million from ISK 122 million in the comparable period, though overall credit quality remains sound with Stage three loans averaging a 52.2 percent loan-to-value ratio and total provisioning held at 1.2 percent of the gross loan book.

Total assets grew 9.5 percent to ISK 375.7 billion, underpinned by a 7.6 percent increase in customer loans to ISK 223.3 billion compared to year-end 2025. Deposit funding strengthened considerably, exceeding ISK 200 billion and rising 16.5 percent from December 2025, which lowered the loan-to-deposit ratio to 111 percent. Capital adequacy remained robust, with a common equity tier one ratio of 17.9 percent and a total capital adequacy ratio of 23.0 percent, maintaining a 530 basis point buffer above regulatory minimums. Liquidity was secure with a coverage ratio of 276 percent and a stable funding ratio of 137 percent. Strategic funding optimization progressed through recent capital and senior unsecured bond issuances, notably securing an 85 basis point pricing improvement over prior year European deals. The quarter also saw the distribution of a special dividend and the authorization for covered bond issuance to diversify long-term funding. Operational challenges from restrictive market conditions were mitigated by disciplined cost control and structural improvements. Key corporate developments include the ongoing divestment of Straumur greiðslumiðlun hf, preparations to exit specific principal UK investments, and the planned departure of CEO Ármann Þorvaldsson by year-end 2026. Management indicated that near-term earnings pressure stems from deliberate balance sheet and cost restructuring intended to permanently enhance return on tangible equity and support accelerated lending expansion.
