Liven AS reported mixed financial results for the first half of 2026, characterized by significantly stronger sales pipeline activity but lower current-period recognition due to the cyclical nature of residential development. During the six-month period, the group signed 111 sales contracts, nearly doubling the 56 contracted in the same period of 2025, while its presale portfolio expanded by EUR 14.9 million year-to-date to EUR 48.6 million. The weekly sales ratio strengthened substantially, surpassing the long-term normal range to reach nearly 3.1 percent in the second quarter, reflecting robust market demand and securing an estimated over eight percent market share in Greater Tallinn. Despite this momentum, revenue for the first half of 2026 amounted to EUR 11,439 thousand, a clear increase from EUR 9,319 thousand in 2025, primarily because fewer homes were handed over to buyers. Only 26 units were delivered in the first half compared to 41 in the prior year, highlighting the typical lag between contract signing and revenue recognition. Consequently, profit before tax declined to EUR 186 thousand from EUR 296 thousand, resulting in a net loss of EUR 198 thousand compared to a net profit of EUR 268 thousand previously. The second-quarter net loss of EUR 618 thousand was notably impacted by one-off expenses related to the company’s initial public offering and income taxes on distributed dividends. Operationally, EBITDA decreased to EUR 2,425 thousand with a margin of 21.2 percent, down from EUR 2,777 thousand and 29.8 percent in the first half of 2025. Total assets grew to EUR 100,186 thousand, supported by rising inventories and construction activity, while total borrowings reached EUR 57,341 thousand. Financial position was bolstered by the successful IPO conducted in April and May 2026, which raised EUR 4,932 thousand through the issuance of over one million shares traded on the Nasdaq Tallinn Main List since mid-May. Shareholders received dividends of EUR 1,362 thousand. The expansion continued with the acquisition of two residential sites in Berlin and a major development property in central Tallinn post-reporting period. Management indicated that economic conditions remain supportive, with household purchasing power recovering and wage growth outpacing inflation, although borrowing costs eased modestly amid stable monetary policy expectations. Looking ahead, the group expects to translate its strong order backlog into higher revenue and profitability in the second half of 2026, targeting near-total realization of its EUR 59 million annual revenue forecast. Construction loan volumes are anticipated to peak in the third quarter before declining toward year-end as project completions accelerate, supporting a return to positive earnings and an expected return on equity exceeding the targeted 20 percent threshold.
