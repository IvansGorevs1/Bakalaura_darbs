Kvika banki’s Q2 2026 pre-tax profit was ISK 1,545 million, down ISK 480 million, or 23.7%, from Q2 2025 and 14.6% from Q1 2026. After-tax profit fell to ISK 903 million from ISK 1,439 million a year earlier. Net operating income declined 7.3% year on year to ISK 4,756 million, mainly reflecting weaker fee income and higher impairment charges.

Net interest income remained resilient, increasing 1.7% year on year to ISK 3,013 million, although it fell ISK 253 million from Q1. The quarter-on-quarter decline was mainly attributed to lower inflation reducing the contribution from Kvika’s positive ISK 6.1 billion inflation balance, the ISK 10.2 billion dividend paid in June, and the recent senior preferred and AT1 bond issuances. Net interest margin was 4.0%, compared with 4.5% in Q1. Net fee and commission income decreased 26.8% year on year to ISK 1,416 million as subdued capital-market activity, lower transaction volumes and softer client activity weighed on fees. Other net operating income increased ISK 96 million to ISK 327 million.

Administrative expenses fell 1.1% year on year to ISK 2,947 million despite ISK 112 million of restructuring costs recognised in June. Efficiency measures and organisational changes reduced the employee count to 232 and are expected to lower the annual cost base by ISK 300–400 million. Net impairment increased to ISK 269 million from ISK 122 million, reflecting a more cautious loan-book review amid economic uncertainty, including ISK 103 million of write-downs. Management reported that underlying credit quality remained strong, with impairment levels at 1.2% of gross loans and the increase in Stage 3 exposures primarily related to one well-collateralised UK exposure.

For the first six months, pre-tax profit from continuing operations rose 23.0% to ISK 3,354 million. Net operating income increased 0.7% to ISK 9,643 million, supported by 6.8% growth in net interest income and higher other operating income, while fees declined 16.0% and administrative expenses fell 1.8%. Profit for the period decreased to ISK 2,277 million from ISK 3,525 million because 6M 2025 included ISK 1,901 million of profit from discontinued operations.

Loan growth continued, with customer loans rising 7.6% from year-end 2025 to ISK 223.3 billion, while deposits increased 16.5% to ISK 201.2 billion. The bank maintained a strong capital and liquidity position, with a 23.0% capital adequacy ratio, a 276% LCR and a 137% NSFR. Straumur’s sale process is underway and expected to close before year-end, while CEO Ármann Þorvaldsson plans to step down by the end of 2026.
