Kvika banki hf. reported its financial results for the second quarter of 2026, showing lower profitability compared with the same period last year alongside ongoing strategic and balance sheet developments. Profit before tax for the second quarter declined by 23.7% year-on-year to ISK 1,545 million, down from ISK 2,025 million in the second quarter of 2025, and decreased by 14.6% compared with the first quarter of 2026. After-tax profit for the quarter fell to ISK 903 million from ISK 1,439 million in the prior-year period. Conversely, for the first six months of 2026, pre-tax profit from continuing operations increased by 23.0% year-on-year to reach ISK 3,354 million, supported by solid performance in the earlier part of the year.

Net operating income for the second quarter of 2026 amounted to ISK 4,756 million, representing a 7.3% decrease from ISK 5,128 million in the second quarter of 2025. Net interest income demonstrated resilience, rising by 1.7% year-on-year to ISK 3,013 million, although it declined compared with the first quarter of 2026 due to lower inflation affecting Kvika's positive inflation balance and the impact of a large dividend payment in June. Net fee and commission income fell significantly by 26.8% year-on-year to ISK 1,416 million, driven by continued subdued activity in capital markets and lower transaction volumes. Other net operating income improved to ISK 327 million compared to ISK 231 million in the second quarter of the previous year. Administrative expenses decreased slightly by 1.1% year-on-year to ISK 2,947 million, reflecting continued cost discipline that successfully absorbed a one-off restructuring charge of ISK 112 million incurred in June for efficiency measures. Net impairment losses rose to ISK 269 million from ISK 122 million in the second quarter of 2025, while the effective income tax rate increased to 34.8% due to higher special taxes on financial activities.

Total assets expanded by 9.5% from year-end 2025 to reach ISK 375.7 billion at the end of the second quarter of 2026. Loans to customers grew by 7.6% to ISK 223.3 billion, driven by broad-based momentum across domestic corporate banking, vehicle, and equipment lending. Total deposits surpassed ISK 200 billion for the first time, climbing 16.5% from year-end 2025 to ISK 201.2 billion, supported primarily by retail and public entities. Market funding increased following senior unsecured and additional tier 1 bond issuances completed during the first half of the year. The bank maintained robust liquidity and capital metrics, reporting a liquidity coverage ratio of 276% and a capital adequacy ratio of 23.0%, which adjusted following a major dividend distribution and a reduction in Pillar II requirements. In corporate developments, the bank noted that the sales process for Straumur greiðslumiðlun hf. was underway and expected to close before year-end. Additionally, Chief Executive Officer Ármann Þorvaldsson announced his intention to step down by the end of 2026 after nearly ten years in the role, with the board actively initiating the recruitment of a successor.
