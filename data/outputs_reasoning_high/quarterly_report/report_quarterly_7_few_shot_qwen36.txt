The company reported modest top-line growth during the first half of 2026, with consolidated revenue increasing by 0.6% to 28,590 thousand EUR compared to the same period last year. This slight expansion was primarily driven by a strong recovery in the retail segment, which grew by 13.6% to account for 47.4% of total sales, offsetting a 9.1% contraction in wholesale revenue. Geographic contributions shifted notably, with Belarus emerging as a key growth driver while traditional markets faced headwinds from local currency depreciation and operational adjustments.

Profitability metrics presented a mixed picture despite improved sales quality. Gross profit rose by 5.3% to 15,892 thousand EUR, supported by a 4.8% reduction in the cost of goods sold and an expanded gross margin of 55.6%, up from 53.1%. However, operating profit contracted by 3.5% to 5,751 thousand EUR, as elevated distribution and administrative expenses compressed operating margins to 20.1%. Consolidated EBITDA declined marginally by 0.8% to 7,508 thousand EUR. Reported net profit attributable to equity holders fell sharply by 31.5% to 3,961 thousand EUR, reducing earnings per share to 0.11 EUR from 0.16 EUR in the prior year.

Despite the decline in accounting profits, cash generation improved substantially. Operating cash flow nearly doubled, climbing 96.6% to 6,842 thousand EUR, largely fueled by effective working capital management and a 1,535 thousand EUR drawdown in inventory levels. The financial position remained solid, with total assets growing to 98,603 thousand EUR and equity attributable to parent shareholders increasing by 6.1% to 81,479 thousand EUR. Liquid resources strengthened considerably, with cash and short-term deposits totaling 51,150 thousand EUR. Liquidity ratios improved accordingly, with the current ratio rising to 10.3 and the quick ratio reaching 6.7.

Regional performance diverged across the Group’s primary markets. Sales in Russia decreased slightly by 0.6% in EUR terms, while local currency revenues fell by 7.8%, leading management to prioritize retail profitability and reduce owned store locations to 67. Belarus delivered resilient growth, with sales up 6.4% in EUR terms alongside positive local currency movements. Declines were recorded in the Baltics and other international territories. To reinforce its balance sheet, the shareholder meeting resolved to retain the entire 2025 net profit within retained earnings rather than distribute dividends. Overall, the reporting period reflected a strategic transition toward retail operations, disciplined cost control, and strengthened liquidity amidst fluctuating macroeconomic conditions in core markets.
