AS Trigon Property Development reported a significant deterioration in financial performance for the twelve months ended 31 December 2025 compared with the prior year. The company recorded a net loss of 99,507 euros, reversing a net profit of 167,409 euros in 2024. Earnings per share turned negative at 0.02212 euros, down from 0.03721 euros. The downturn stemmed from zero property sales and a sharp rise in administrative expenses. Gross profit fell to zero from 220,299 euros the previous year, as no land parcels were sold. Operating costs increased to 108,021 euros from 63,650 euros, mainly due to newly introduced board remuneration of 41,452 euros and higher professional service fees. This resulted in an operating loss of 108,021 euros versus a 161,649 euro operating profit in 2024.

Key financial ratios weakened considerably. Return on assets declined to negative 5.51 percent from 8.93 percent, while return on equity dropped to negative 5.67 percent from 9.03 percent. Total assets contracted to 1,804,656 euros from 1,873,680 euros, and equity fell to 1,755,267 euros from 1,854,774 euros. Inventory grew by 91,262 euros to 1,562,500 euros following capital injections into the Pärnu development site. Cash reserves shrank by 142,945 euros to 227,911 euros, aligning with full-year operating cash outflows. Payables more than doubled to 49,389 euros, pushing the debt ratio up from 1.01 percent to 2.74 percent. Shareholder value showed limited movement, with the closing price rising marginally to 0.634 euros from 0.620 euros and market capitalisation reaching 2,852,405 euros.

Operations remained focused on real estate development and land disposal without strategic shifts. After small parcel sales in late 2024, the company secured an amended zoning approval in April 2024 consolidating transport zones with commercial plots to enable future subdivisions. No further disposals occurred during 2025, and executive compensation was formally established this year. Management confirms going concern status and IFRS compliance. Overall, the period reflects a transitional phase characterized by halted revenue, elevated overhead costs, and ongoing project investments, temporarily pressuring profitability and liquidity.
