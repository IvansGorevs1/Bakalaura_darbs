AS LHV Group reported consolidated net profit of EUR 24.7 million in the second quarter of 2026, representing a EUR 5 million or 25 percent increase compared to the first quarter, but a EUR 6.1 million or 20 percent decline relative to the same period in 2025. Earnings per share totaled EUR 0.07, while consolidated net income reached EUR 79.0 million, growing 7.3 percent quarter-over-quarter and 7 percent year-over-year. The return on equity improved to 13.1 percent from 10.7 percent in the prior quarter, though it remained below the 17.4 percent recorded in the second quarter of 2025. Net interest income expanded by 8 percent year-over-year to EUR 62.5 million, reflecting supportive market interest rates and European Central Bank policy adjustments. Conversely, net service fee income decreased by 6 percent to EUR 14.6 million, which management attributed to lower client activity.

Operating expenses amounted to EUR 43.6 million, decreasing slightly from the first quarter but rising 8 percent compared to the second quarter of 2025, resulting in a cost-to-income ratio deterioration to 61.9 percent. A primary factor affecting quarterly profitability was the significant increase in loan and bond provisions to EUR 5.8 million, up from EUR 1.3 million in the first quarter and reversing the negative provision seen in the prior year. Management explicitly linked this rise to model-based, forward-looking impairment charges applied to specific borrowers, alongside a EUR 63 million increase in risk-weighted assets driven by regulatory compliance updates and organic portfolio expansion. Despite these higher provisions, operating profit climbed to EUR 35.4 million from EUR 28.1 million in the preceding quarter.

The loan portfolio grew 15 percent year-over-year to EUR 5,768 million, sustained by corporate and residential mortgage lending. Customer deposits contracted by EUR 245 million quarter-over-quarter to EUR 7,557 million, a deliberate strategic reduction focusing on platform and financial intermediary accounts that successfully contained interest expenses. The group maintained a capital adequacy ratio of 20.8 percent, exceeding internal targets. During the reporting period, LHV Pank finalized the migration of its core banking infrastructure to a cloud-based architecture and transitioned to a product-oriented management structure. The institution also obtained Estonia’s first regulated cryptocurrency service license under the MiCA framework and advanced plans for an English name change and European Economic Area cross-border licensing to facilitate international scaling. While business volumes aligned with expectations, slightly elevated operational costs and increased impairment allowances positioned actual results marginally below initial forecasts, though management highlighted continued optimism given stronger-than-planned interest income performance.
