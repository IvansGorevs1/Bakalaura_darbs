AS Tallinna Sadam’s revenue increased 2.4% year on year to EUR 59.3 million in the six months ended 30 June 2026, but profitability declined substantially. Operating profit fell 25.7% to EUR 13.6 million, reducing the operating margin from 31.6% to 22.9%. Adjusted EBITDA decreased 13.5% to EUR 25.8 million, with its margin declining from 51.5% to 43.5%. Profit before income tax fell 27.3% to EUR 11.4 million, while profit for the period declined 41.7% to EUR 6.0 million, or EUR 0.02 per share compared with EUR 0.04 a year earlier.

Revenue growth was mainly supported by a EUR 0.9 million increase in charter-fee revenue, as icebreaker Botnica recorded 130 charter days versus 110 in the comparative period, including a 20-day North Sea project in June. Electricity sales rose EUR 0.6 million, helped by higher sales volumes, market prices, new network tariffs introduced in March and a security-of-supply fee resulting from a regulatory change. Ferry-service revenue increased EUR 0.3 million due to tariff indexation and compensation for additional Regula trips.

These gains were outweighed by a 24.0% increase in operating expenses to EUR 20.2 million. Fuel costs rose 47.6% to EUR 4.4 million because of higher diesel prices and more severe ice conditions, which increased ferry fuel consumption. Electricity costs increased 28.8% to EUR 3.1 million, while technical maintenance and repair expenses rose 80.0% to EUR 2.8 million. The latter increase reflected more extensive repairs to Botnica, ferries and harbour infrastructure, while the previous year included a EUR 0.9 million insurance indemnity for Botnica repairs. Depreciation, amortisation and impairment increased 6.0% to EUR 12.9 million, partly due to the commissioning of a new quay and capitalised ferry dry-docking projects.

Operating volumes weakened: cargo declined 5.0% to 6.5 million tonnes, mainly because liquid bulk fell 33.8%, while passengers decreased 2.0% to 3.6 million and vessel calls fell 4.8%. Helsinki passenger traffic was affected by the scheduled dry-docking of Eckerö Line’s Finlandia. The report also links lower liquid-bulk volumes and higher fuel costs to geopolitical conditions, including Russia’s war in Ukraine and the US-Iran conflict and Strait of Hormuz blockade.

The comparison was further weakened by EUR 0.9 million of profit from the 2025 Rail Baltica land sale and other prior-year benefits. Lower net finance costs, down 11.2% to EUR 2.3 million, partly mitigated the operating decline. The Group paid EUR 19.2 million in dividends, resulting in a EUR 4.4 million reduction in cash to EUR 27.6 million and operating cash flow of EUR 18.2 million, down from EUR 26.3 million.
