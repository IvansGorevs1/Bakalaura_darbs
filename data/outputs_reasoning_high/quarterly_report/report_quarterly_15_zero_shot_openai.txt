Nordecon’s revenue for 2025 was €208.3 million, approximately 7% below €223.9 million in 2024. The decline was driven primarily by a roughly 10% reduction in Buildings revenue to €168.3 million, while Infrastructure revenue increased by 10% to €39.9 million. Buildings accounted for 81% of total revenue and Infrastructure for 19%. Within Buildings, commercial buildings revenue increased by approximately 71%, but public buildings revenue fell by approximately 32%. Revenue from own property development increased to €9.75 million from €7.69 million, mainly reflecting apartment sales in the Seileri Kvartal and Tammepärja Kodu developments.

Profitability weakened. Gross profit declined to €13.5 million from €16.8 million, reducing the gross margin to 6.5% from 7.5%. Operating profit fell to €5.65 million from €8.06 million, and EBITDA decreased to €8.25 million from €11.03 million, with the EBITDA margin declining to 4.0% from 4.9%. Administrative expenses decreased by approximately 14% to €6.81 million, mainly because weaker financial performance resulted in lower performance-based pay. Net profit fell to €2.55 million from €5.17 million, while profit attributable to Nordecon’s owners decreased to €1.44 million from €3.83 million; earnings per share declined to €0.05 from €0.12. Profit was affected by net finance costs of €2.97 million, including a €0.75 million foreign-exchange loss caused by the Ukrainian hryvnia weakening by around 12% against the euro.

Fourth-quarter revenue increased to €60.6 million from €45.2 million, but gross margin fell to 5.9% from 9.0%. Operating profit was €0.90 million, compared with €1.09 million, and the group reported a quarterly loss of €0.04 million versus a profit of €0.62 million in the fourth quarter of 2024.

The Estonian construction market stabilised and activity improved somewhat in the second half of the year, while the construction price index rose 1.5%, primarily due to higher material prices. Competition remained strong and public investment in buildings and roads continued to influence demand. Nordecon’s order book nevertheless increased approximately 30% to €273.1 million at year-end. New contracts totalled €235.0 million, supported by major Rail Baltica and road-construction contracts. Infrastructure represented 42% of the backlog, up from 18%, while Buildings represented 58%, down from 82%. Management expects business volumes to increase in 2026.

Operating cash flow was €1.20 million, compared with €1.08 million in 2024. Cash decreased to €5.27 million from €8.20 million, partly reflecting €2.90 million placed in long-term deposits, and the current ratio was 0.93.
