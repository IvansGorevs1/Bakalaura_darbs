AS Amber Latvijas balzams reported a severe deterioration in financial performance for the first three months of 2026, driven by compounding external shocks and broad market headwinds. The company recorded unaudited net turnover of EUR 11.1 million, a 29.6% decrease compared to the same period in 2025. Sales volume fell sharply by 36.1% to 517,600 nine-liter cases, down from 810,079 cases previously. This volume decline, combined with product mix shifts, pricing methodology adjustments, and a high comparative baseline that included a prior year revenue contract adjustment, directly compressed profitability. Gross profit contracted by 66.5% to EUR 1.2 million, as moderate production cost reductions failed to offset the adverse impacts of lower output and sales activity. Consequently, the operating result shifted from an EUR 0.6 million profit to a EUR 1.6 million loss, reducing the operating margin from 3.7% to negative 14.5%. The unaudited net result also turned into a loss of EUR 1.4 million, reversing the EUR 0.9 million profit reported in 2025.

These financial declines were fundamentally tied to several critical business developments. In January 2026, a trustee was appointed for the affiliated Stoli Group following U.S. Chapter 11 proceedings, removing company control over its largest customer and severely disrupting cash flow. Simultaneously, Russian authorities confiscated the group-owned Talvis distillery after the parent company publicly supported Ukraine. Broader industry challenges exacerbated the situation, with analysts citing shifting consumer preferences, constrained discretionary spending, and distributor inventory corrections causing sustained demand weakness. The resulting cash flow strain delayed excise tax payments, prompting the State Revenue Service to freeze bank accounts and necessitating a legal protection application. On February 5, 2026, the court initiated proceedings to facilitate liability restructuring while allowing continued operations, with a mandatory action plan deadline in October and a creditor moratorium through August. The filing automatically triggered default events under the principal shareholder bond agreements and banking facilities. Subsequently, the shareholder obtained authorization for judicial reorganization in Luxembourg, securing a four-month payment moratorium until late August to negotiate with secured creditors.

Throughout the period, the company contributed EUR 8.4 million in taxes to the state budget, including EUR 6.4 million in excise duties. At the reporting date, current assets exceeded current liabilities by EUR 82.9 million, providing a structural liquidity buffer despite the sharp quarterly losses and a reduced order fulfillment rate of 70.3%. Management continues to implement efficiency initiatives, internal process audits, and cost optimization measures aimed at restoring financial stability within the structured legal protection framework while navigating the ongoing macroeconomic and geopolitical challenges.
