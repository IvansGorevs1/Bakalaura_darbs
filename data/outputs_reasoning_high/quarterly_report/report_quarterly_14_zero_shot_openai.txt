AS Merko Ehitus reported revenue of EUR 149.9 million for the first six months of 2026, down 10.7% from EUR 167.9 million a year earlier. Second-quarter revenue increased to EUR 92.7 million from EUR 82.6 million, as activity increased in both construction services and real estate development and several large projects entered more active construction phases.

Profitability declined substantially in the first half. Gross profit was EUR 17.4 million, compared with EUR 29.1 million in the comparable period, while operating profit fell to EUR 8.4 million from EUR 18.6 million. EBITDA decreased to EUR 10.3 million from EUR 20.1 million. Pre-tax profit was EUR 10.8 million, versus EUR 23.5 million, and the pre-tax margin declined to 7.2% from 14.0%. Net profit attributable to shareholders was EUR 12.8 million, compared with EUR 21.7 million, reducing the net profit margin to 8.6% from 12.9%. Second-quarter net profit was EUR 8.5 million, down from EUR 11.2 million.

Construction services generated EUR 118.3 million of six-month revenue, broadly level with the prior year, but operating profit declined 65.5% to EUR 3.0 million and the margin fell to 2.6% from 7.3%. The report attributes the weaker profitability to the expiration of successful contracts from earlier periods, a return to more typical margins in a highly competitive environment and first-quarter seasonality. Real estate development revenue decreased 33.9% to EUR 31.6 million, while operating profit fell 40.8% to EUR 7.0 million. Apartment sales declined to 120 from 222, although second-quarter apartment revenue was EUR 20.6 million, slightly above the prior-year quarter.

The main positive development was the construction pipeline. The secured order book reached a record EUR 835.7 million at 30 June 2026, approximately 88% above the prior-year level of EUR 443.8 million. New contracts signed during the first half totaled EUR 486.4 million, including approximately EUR 80 million in the second quarter. Major projects included Rail Baltica, defence facilities, wind-farm infrastructure and the Krulli Quarter developments in Tallinn. The Rūdninkai defence campus contract also entered full force after the reporting date, with total construction costs exceeding EUR 370 million.

Market conditions remained competitive, with private-sector clients cautious and customers price-sensitive. Construction input prices increased during the second quarter, with continued inflationary and geopolitical pressures expected. The group sold 120 apartments and five commercial units, launched 204 apartments and invested EUR 42.6 million in development projects. Operating cash flow was negative at EUR 31.6 million, mainly reflecting increased inventories, while cash and cash equivalents stood at EUR 39.6 million. Net debt increased to EUR 29.6 million, but equity remained strong at EUR 251.1 million, representing 57.4% of total assets.
