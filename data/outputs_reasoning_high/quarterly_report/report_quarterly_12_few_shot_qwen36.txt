The company reported a net profit of EUR 24.7 million in the second quarter of 2026, reflecting a EUR 5 million improvement from the first quarter but a EUR 6.1 million contraction relative to the second quarter of 2025. Attributable profit to shareholders stood at EUR 24.1 million, yielding earnings per share of EUR 0.07. Return on equity rose to 13.1 percent, advancing from 10.7 percent in the prior quarter yet remaining well below the 17.4 percent recorded a year earlier. Consolidated net income climbed by 7.3 percent quarter-on-quarter to EUR 79.0 million, supported chiefly by an 8 percent annual expansion in net interest income to EUR 62.5 million. Net fee and commission income stabilised at EUR 14.6 million, marking a 6 percent year-on-year decline. Operating expenses amounted to EUR 43.6 million, down slightly from the first quarter but up 8 percent compared to the second quarter of 2025. Profitability was materially tempered by EUR 5.8 million in additional loan and bond provisions, a sharp reversal from the EUR 4.2 million provision release posted in the same period last year.

Balance sheet activity featured continued credit expansion alongside funding adjustments. The gross loan portfolio grew by 3 percent to EUR 5,768 million, driven by residential and corporate lending, while total customer deposits fell by EUR 245 million to EUR 7,557 million. Platform and financial intermediary deposits contributed to the withdrawal, whereas retail client balances rose by EUR 105 million. The group maintained a robust solvency posture, with total own funds reaching EUR 869.6 million and a capital adequacy ratio of 20.8 percent. Liquidity coverage held at 170.5 percent, and the loan-to-deposit ratio stood at 72 percent. Across segments, the core banking unit delivered EUR 24.3 million in profit, asset management generated EUR 0.4 million, insurance operations turned a EUR 0.8 million profit, and the UK subsidiary reported EUR 1.4 million, contrasted by minor losses at the parent entity and payment technology division.

Management characterised the quarter as slightly weaker than forecast due to elevated operating costs and softer client-driven fee revenues, which were partially offset by better-than-expected interest earnings and bond portfolio revaluations. Asset quality remained sound, with forward-looking impairment adjustments applied to select accounts despite historically low delinquency rates. Operationally, the quarter marked significant structural modernisation, including the successful migration of core banking systems to a cloud infrastructure, a transition to a product-led governance model, and the acquisition of a regulated crypto-asset service licence under European framework rules. The bank also advanced preparations for cross-border licensing across the European Economic Area to support international scaling. Against a backdrop of lingering geopolitical uncertainty and shifting monetary policy environments, the company highlighted resilient domestic economic activity and a positive trajectory for interest margins, projecting continued earnings stability as cost optimisation and digital transformation initiatives take full effect.
