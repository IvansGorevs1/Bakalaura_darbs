AS Merko Ehitus reported mixed financial results for the first half of 2026, characterized by a record-high secured order book alongside compressed profitability and a cyclical pullback in residential sales. Group revenue declined by 10.7% to EUR 149.9 million compared to EUR 167.9 million in the same period of 2025, although second-quarter revenue grew by 12.2% to EUR 92.7 million against EUR 82.6 million. Net profit attributable to shareholders fell to EUR 12.8 million from EUR 21.7 million a year earlier, resulting in a net profit margin decrease from 12.9% to 8.6%. The construction services segment experienced a sharp contraction in operating profitability, with operating profit dropping 65.5% to EUR 3.0 million and the operating margin narrowing to 2.6% from 7.3%. This deterioration was explicitly attributed to the expiration of previously successful high-margin contracts, a return to more typical industry margins amid intense competition, and rising material and labor costs. Real estate development revenue decreased by 33.9% to EUR 31.6 million, driven by a significant drop in apartment sales volume to 120 units from 222 units in the prior year half. However, the segment demonstrated operational resilience in the second quarter, delivering more than twice as many apartments as the first quarter and posting a 4.5% revenue increase alongside a slight rise in operating profit margin to 23.0%.

The primary driver behind the company’s positive forward outlook is a substantial expansion in the secured order book, which reached an all-time high of EUR 835.7 million, representing an 88% annual increase. New contracts worth EUR 486.4 million were secured in the first half, largely reflecting a strategic shift toward public-sector and defense-related infrastructure projects such as the Rail Baltica network and the Rūdninkai military campus, as private-sector demand remained constrained by economic uncertainty. Geopolitical tensions, particularly the escalating conflict involving Iran and elevated energy prices, have sustained upward pressure on construction input costs, with management indicating that price increases will persist through the remainder of the year. These cost dynamics, combined with the cash-intensive nature of scaling apartment construction, resulted in a negative operating cash flow of EUR 31.6 million and an increase in inventories to EUR 245.9 million. Despite these temporary liquidity pressures, the group maintained a robust financial position with total equity standing at EUR 251.1 million and a moderate net debt level of EUR 29.6 million. The reporting period underscores a transitional phase where strong contractual backlogs and Q2 operational improvements are temporarily overshadowed by macroeconomic inflation and cyclically adjusted real estate market conditions, while dividend distributions were reduced to EUR 22.1 million to preserve capital for ongoing development projects.
