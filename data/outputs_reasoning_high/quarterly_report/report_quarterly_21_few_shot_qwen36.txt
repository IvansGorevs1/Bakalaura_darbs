AS Tallinna Sadam reported a decline in profitability during the first half of 2026, driven by rising operating expenses and weaker operational volumes, despite a modest increase in revenue. The Group achieved revenue of EUR 59.3 million, representing a 2.4% growth compared to EUR 57.9 million in the same period of the previous year. This increase was primarily supported by higher charter revenues from the icebreaker Botnica, expanded revenue from electricity sales due to volume growth, tariff adjustments, and a regulatory security fee, as well as improved ferry service tariffs. However, operating profit contracted sharply by 25.7% to EUR 13.6 million, while profit for the period fell more steeply by 41.7% to EUR 6.0 million. Adjusted EBITDA also declined by 13.5% to EUR 25.8 million.

The deterioration in financial results stemmed largely from a significant surge in operating expenses, which rose by 24.0% to EUR 20.2 million. The largest expense drivers included a 47.6% increase in fuel costs, attributed to higher diesel prices and increased consumption caused by more severe ice conditions, as well as an 80.0% rise in technical maintenance and repair expenses. Repair costs escalated due to extensive work on the icebreaker Botnica, city harbour quays, and ferries, and were compounded by the absence of a substantial insurance indemnity received in the prior year for similar repairs. Additionally, depreciation and amortisation increased by 6.0%, reflecting the commissioning of the new quay at Paldiski South Harbour and capitalised ferry dry-docking projects, while personnel expenses grew by 4.7% due to salary increases across the Group. Other income decreased by EUR 0.7 million, mainly because the comparative period benefited from a one-off gain of EUR 0.9 million from the disposal of land for the Rail Baltica project.

Operational metrics reflected reduced activity in core areas. Cargo volumes handled by the Group's harbours decreased by 5.0% to 6.48 million tonnes, with the most notable decline in liquid bulk, which fell by 33.8%. Dry bulk volumes also dropped by 4.8%. In passenger transport, total passenger numbers declined by 2.0% to 3.59 million. While cruise passenger traffic surged by nearly 48% due to increased ship calls, this growth was offset by a decrease in traditional ferry passengers, particularly on the Tallinn–Helsinki route where volumes fell by 2.6%, impacted by a scheduled vessel dry-docking and a high baseline from major music events in the prior year. Vessel calls declined by 4.8%, and the average number of employees stood at 420, a reduction of 1.0%.

The Group maintained a robust balance sheet with equity at EUR 367.7 million at period-end, though total assets decreased slightly by 1.1% to EUR 615.0 million. Net debt increased marginally by 1.5% to EUR 145.8 million. Cash flows from operating activities amounted to EUR 18.2 million, down from EUR 26.3 million in the prior year, constrained by higher operating costs and working capital movements. Following investment expenditures and dividend payments of EUR 19.2 million, the Group recorded a net cash outflow of EUR 4.4 million. During the period, the company secured a new unsecured loan facility of up to EUR 32 million and maintained its dividend policy by paying EUR 0.073 per share. Segment analysis revealed that while revenue grew in the Passenger harbours, Ferry, and Other segments, all business units saw declines in adjusted EBITDA, with the Cargo harbours and Ferry segments experiencing the most significant drops due to lower volumes and elevated energy and maintenance costs.
