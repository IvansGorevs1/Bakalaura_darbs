The financial year 2025 presented a challenging operating environment for AS Tallink Grupp, primarily driven by constrained consumer and business confidence in its core markets, particularly Finland, alongside heightened geopolitical tensions. Despite these headwinds, internal adjustments helped stabilize results. The Group’s consolidated revenue for the full year declined slightly to EUR 765.3 million from EUR 785.8 million in 2024, while EBITDA fell more sharply to EUR 130.1 million from EUR 175.2 million. Net profit decreased to EUR 17.3 million, or EUR 0.023 per share, down from EUR 40.3 million in the prior year. Passenger volumes remained relatively stable at 5.53 million travelers, but cargo transport experienced a notable contraction, dropping 19.2 percent to 245,004 units. Road vehicle traffic also dipped by 2.2 percent to 760,473. At the segment level, revenue from core route operations decreased by EUR 13.7 million to EUR 625.2 million, with the segment result falling by EUR 11.0 million to EUR 70.5 million. The Estonia-Finland route generated the highest segment result despite a decline, while the Finland-Sweden and Estonia-Sweden segments saw reduced revenues and profits, with the latter posting an operating loss. Profitability was partially cushioned by a EUR 18.0 million reduction in depreciation and amortization expenses, resulting from the retrospective alignment of cruise and passenger vessel useful lives to 45 years and the disposal of three surplus vessels. Net finance costs also improved, decreasing by EUR 6.8 million to EUR 21.4 million, owing to a shrinking loan balance and favorable interest rate movements. In the fourth quarter, financial conditions stabilized significantly, with revenue rising 2.4 percent to EUR 188.0 million, EBITDA increasing 7.3 percent to EUR 27.6 million, and the company returning to profitability with a net gain of EUR 12.2 million compared to a net loss in the same quarter of 2024. This quarterly recovery reflected lower depreciation charges, strategic asset sales, and reduced financing outflows. The Group maintained a disciplined capital structure, repaying EUR 113.5 million in loans and interest throughout the year, which kept net debt steady at EUR 432.4 million and the net debt-to-EBITDA ratio at 3.3. Operating adjustments included chartering out multiple vessels to optimize utilization and managing substantial maintenance periods. Regulatory and fiscal pressures remained prominent, as increased VAT and income tax rates in Estonia and Finland, combined with mandatory CO2 allowance purchases, compressed margins. Conversely, plans to transition shuttle vessels to biomethane fuel aim to mitigate future environmental compliance costs. Management reaffirmed its commitment to shareholder returns, paying EUR 44.6 million in dividends for 2025 and proposing an identical EUR 0.06 per share distribution for 2026. Overall, while top-line metrics and cash generation weakened amid macroeconomic uncertainty, the company successfully navigated operational constraints, preserved liquidity, and demonstrated resilience through fleet optimization and cost management.
