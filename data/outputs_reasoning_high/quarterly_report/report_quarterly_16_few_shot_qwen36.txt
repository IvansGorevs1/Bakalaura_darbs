Nordic Fibreboard AS posted a modest top line expansion during the first quarter of 2026, yet profitability deteriorated sharply due to elevated energy costs and temporary production disruptions. Consolidated net revenue reached €1.86 million, representing a 7.3 percent increase compared with €1.74 million in the same period of 2025. The growth was primarily driven by a €241 thousand rise in orders from Finnish DIY retailers. Despite stronger sales, the company recorded a negative EBITDA of €395 thousand, a significant widening from an €85 thousand deficit in the prior year quarter, compressing the EBITDA margin to negative 21 percent. Operating profit fell to negative €530 thousand against negative €217 thousand a year earlier, resulting in a consolidated net loss of €570 thousand compared to €228 thousand previously. The decline in margins was heavily influenced by a jump in cost of goods sold to €1.966 million from €1.608 million, largely stemming from surging utility expenses. Electricity, heat, and water costs more than doubled to €1.048 million, exacerbated by an early January factory shutdown required for the calibration and start up of a new gas boiler house. Furthermore, regional geopolitical developments in late February triggered a near doubling of spot gas prices, which remained approximately 50 percent above January levels through the end of the quarter. Unusually cold weather further increased baseline energy consumption, compounding the pressure on operational costs.

Financial positioning and cash generation showed mixed but stabilizing trends alongside structural adjustments. Net cash generated from operating activities turned positive at €13 thousand, improving substantially from a €528 thousand outflow in the comparable prior year period, as expanded trade payables helped absorb working capital draws in receivables and finished goods inventory. Total assets grew to €9.7 million while total liabilities rose to €5.8 million, although gross borrowings declined slightly to €3.3 million from €3.8 million. Equity contracted to €3.89 million following the accumulated quarterly loss. Beyond core manufacturing, the property management segment now contributes immaterial revenue after historic lease terminations in late 2024, prompting a corporate restructuring that merged the holding and development entities. Management advanced the Pärnu residential development by completing architectural design, securing all municipal building permits, and signing a preliminary sale agreement for ten adjacent parcels, with final transfers scheduled for no later than June 2026. Workforce reduction continued, with headcount falling to 57 employees from 69 a year ago, driving labour expenses down to €0.41 million. Looking forward, the company expects stable fibreboard demand and plans to emphasize market expansion and interior panel product development, relying on the newly operational boiler infrastructure to improve long term production efficiency and energy reliability amid persistent macroeconomic and commodity price volatility.
