Nordecon’s financial performance weakened in 2025 despite a significant improvement in its order book. Revenue declined 7% to €208.3 million from €223.9 million in 2024, mainly because Buildings segment revenue fell approximately 10% to €168.3 million. Infrastructure revenue increased 10% to €39.9 million, supported by road construction, maintenance and Rail Baltica-related work. Buildings still represented the majority of revenue at 81%, while Infrastructure accounted for 19%.

Profitability declined year on year. Gross profit decreased to €13.5 million from €16.8 million, and the gross margin fell to 6.5% from 7.5%. Operating profit was €5.7 million, compared with €8.1 million in 2024, reducing the operating margin to 2.7% from 3.6%. EBITDA declined to €8.3 million from €11.0 million, while the EBITDA margin fell to 4.0% from 4.9%. Administrative expenses decreased by approximately 14% to €6.8 million, primarily because weaker financial performance resulted in lower performance-based pay, but this reduction did not offset the decline in gross profit. Net profit fell to €2.5 million from €5.2 million, and profit attributable to Nordecon’s owners decreased to €1.4 million from €3.8 million. Earnings per share declined to €0.05 from €0.12.

Finance costs increased to €3.5 million from €3.0 million, mainly because the weakening of the Ukrainian hryvnia against the euro generated a €750 thousand foreign-exchange loss, compared with €258 thousand in 2024. In the fourth quarter, revenue rose to €60.6 million from €45.2 million, but gross margin fell to 5.9% from 9.0%, and the group recorded a €43 thousand net loss compared with a €618 thousand profit in the prior-year quarter.

The construction market in Estonia stabilised during the second half of the year, although economic growth was modest at an estimated 1%, construction prices rose 1.5%, and competition remained intense. Nordecon secured €235.0 million of new contracts, compared with €181.4 million in 2024. Its order book reached €273.1 million at year-end, up 30%, with approximately 69% of the backlog scheduled for 2026. Infrastructure’s share rose to 42% from 18%, driven largely by major Rail Baltica and road contracts, while the Buildings backlog declined 7%. Management expects business volumes to increase in 2026.

Operating cash inflow was €1.2 million, while year-end cash declined to €5.3 million from €8.2 million, partly reflecting €2.9 million placed in long-term deposits. Liquidity remained tight, with a current ratio of 0.93, and management identified liquidity exposure as higher than usual. No dividends were paid in 2025.
