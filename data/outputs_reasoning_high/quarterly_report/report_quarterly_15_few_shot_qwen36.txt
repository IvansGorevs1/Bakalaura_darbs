Nordecon AS reported weakened financial performance for the twelve months ended December 31, 2025, characterized by a contraction in revenue and a notable decline in profitability relative to the prior year. The group achieved total revenue of €208 million, representing a decrease of approximately 7% from €224 million in 2024. This top-line reduction was primarily driven by a roughly 10% drop in the Buildings segment, which generated €168 million. Within this segment, commercial building revenues expanded significantly by about 71%, partially counterbalancing a substantial decline of approximately 32% in public building projects. Conversely, the Infrastructure segment delivered resilient results, with revenue growing by 10% to €40 million, supported by a strong order intake and execution of major public works. Amidst the revenue decline, the group implemented effective cost containment measures; administrative expenses fell by around 14% to €6.8 million, largely due to reduced staff costs and lower accruals for performance-based compensation.

Profitability margins narrowed across the board. The gross margin decreased by one percentage point to 6.5%, down from 7.5% in 2024, as the group faced upward pressure from rising material prices, evidenced by a 1.5% increase in the construction price index, alongside escalating labor and machinery operating costs. The operating margin similarly contracted to 2.7% from 3.6%. Foreign exchange fluctuations further pressured bottom-line results; the depreciation of the Ukrainian hryvnia against the euro by approximately 12% generated a translation loss of €0.8 million on intercompany loans. Consequently, net profit declined sharply to €2.5 million from €5.2 million in the previous year, with net profit attributable to parent shareholders falling to €1.4 million from €3.8 million. Earnings per share dropped to €0.05. Despite these headwinds, EBITDA remained relatively stable at €8.3 million, yielding a margin of 4.0%.

A pivotal development for the company was the substantial growth in its order book, which expanded by 30% to €273 million, compared to €209 million at the end of 2024. New contracts secured totaled €235 million, a marked increase over the prior period's total. This backlog accumulation was largely fueled by a nearly threefold surge in Infrastructure contracts, securing the company's position on critical national projects, including the Selja–Tootsi section of the Rail Baltica main line and the Päädeva–Orgita road section of the Tallinn–Pärnu–Ikla highway. Approximately 69% of the order book is slated for completion in 2026. Management indicated that the strengthened pipeline sets a favorable foundation for revenue growth in the coming year, supported by optimistic economic forecasts for Estonia and anticipated recovery in construction activity.

Operational efficiency improved in terms of cost discipline, though labor productivity metrics showed a decline linked to the lower revenue base. Meanwhile, nominal labor cost efficiency rose as staff cost reductions outpaced the drop in sales. Liquidity positions required careful management; cash and cash equivalents decreased to €5.3 million from €8.2 million, driven by investing outflows of €2.8 million, predominantly related to funds placed in long-term deposits to secure guarantee limits. The group reported a current ratio of 0.93, slightly below the previous year, reflecting a reclassification of loans to a Ukrainian associate as non-current due to extended timelines for asset realization amidst geopolitical instability. No dividends were paid in 2025, whereas €1.3 million had been distributed in 2024, allowing the group to retain capital for ongoing obligations and future project execution.
