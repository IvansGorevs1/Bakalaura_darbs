Kvika Banki reported a 23.7% year-over-year decline in pre-tax profit to ISK 1,545 million during the second quarter of 2026, with after-tax profit falling to ISK 903 million. This contraction stemmed primarily from a 26.8% drop in net fee and commission income to ISK 1,416 million, reflecting sustained weakness in capital markets and subdued transaction activity. Net operating income decreased 7.3% to ISK 4,756 million. Although net interest income marginally increased by 1.7% to ISK 3,013 million, it declined quarter-over-quarter due to lower inflation reducing the bank's positive inflation balance and the impact of a substantial ISK 10.2 billion dividend payment. The effective income tax rate rose to 34.8% from 24.4% a year earlier, largely attributable to higher special bank taxes linked to derivative closures.

Despite a one-time restructuring charge of ISK 112 million in June, administrative expenses decreased 1.1% year-over-year to ISK 2,947 million. Underlying costs continued trending downward, supported by workforce adjustments that reduced headcount to 232 and efficiency measures projected to yield annualized savings of ISK 300 million to ISK 400 million. The balance sheet expanded by 9.5% to ISK 375.7 billion, driven by robust loan growth. Customer loans reached ISK 223.3 billion, up 7.6% from year-end 2025. Deposit funding strengthened significantly, surpassing ISK 200 billion at ISK 201.2 billion, a 16.5% year-over-year increase. The liquidity coverage ratio remained elevated at 276%, while the capital adequacy ratio stood at 23.0%, tempered by the special dividend distribution.

Quarterly results were further shaped by deliberate balance sheet optimization and strategic asset divestitures aimed at improving longer-term returns. Recent capital raising initiatives, including senior unsecured and additional tier 1 issuances, temporarily compressed net interest margins but are positioned to reduce wholesale funding costs and enhance capital efficiency as deployment progresses. Credit quality remained stable, with impairment levels holding at 1.2% of the gross loan book. The ongoing sale of Straumur greiðslumiðlun hf. entered final due diligence ahead of an expected year-end close, while leadership transitions were confirmed with Chief Executive Officer Ármann Þorvaldsson announcing his step-down at the end of 2026.

Overall, the reporting period characterized a deliberate transition where disciplined cost control, conservative credit assessment, and structural funding adjustments moderated near-term headline profitability. These initiatives, coupled with consistent asset expansion and resilient deposit inflows, have established a leaner operational model and optimized funding profile. Management anticipates that recently implemented efficiencies, refinancing of legacy debt, and progression of invested assets will drive sustained improvements in core earnings and return metrics going forward.
