Kvika banki reported a weaker second quarter but a stronger first half of 2026. Q2 pre-tax profit was ISK 1,545 million, down ISK 480 million, or 23.7%, from Q2 2025 and 14.6% from Q1 2026. After-tax profit was ISK 903 million, compared with ISK 1,439 million a year earlier. Net operating income decreased 7.3% year on year to ISK 4,756 million.

Net interest income remained resilient at ISK 3,013 million, increasing 1.7% from Q2 2025, as higher income from customer lending offset a smaller liquidity portfolio and higher funding costs. It fell from ISK 3,266 million in Q1, however, and the net interest margin declined to 4.0% from 4.5%. The quarter was affected by lower inflation, which reduced the benefit from Kvika’s positive ISK 6.1 billion inflation balance, the ISK 10.2 billion dividend paid in June, and recent senior preferred and AT1 bond issuances. Net fee and commission income fell 26.8% year on year to ISK 1,416 million amid subdued capital-market activity. Equity-market turnover declined 50% and fixed-income turnover 12% from Q2 2025. Other net operating income increased to ISK 327 million from ISK 231 million.

Administrative expenses decreased 1.1% to ISK 2,947 million despite a one-off ISK 112 million restructuring charge. Employee numbers fell to 232 following organisational changes, and the efficiency programme is expected to reduce the annual cost base by ISK 300–400 million. Net impairment increased to ISK 269 million from ISK 122 million, reflecting a more conservative loan-book review and heightened economic uncertainty. The effective income-tax rate rose to 34.8%, mainly because of higher special taxes related to the closure of funding derivatives.

For the six months, net operating income increased 0.7% to ISK 9,643 million, while pre-tax profit from continuing operations rose 23.0% to ISK 3,354 million. After-tax profit from continuing operations was ISK 2,277 million, compared with ISK 1,625 million in the prior-year period. Reported profit for the period fell to ISK 2,277 million from ISK 3,525 million because 6M 2025 included ISK 1,901 million from discontinued operations.

Business activity remained positive. Loans grew 7.6% from year-end 2025 to ISK 223.3 billion, deposits rose 16.5% to ISK 201.2 billion, and total assets increased 9.5% to ISK 375.7 billion. The Commercial Banking loan and deposit books expanded, UK lending reached a record level, and asset management assets were broadly stable at ISK 448.6 billion excluding a liquidated fund. Liquidity and capital remained strong, with an LCR of 276%, NSFR of 137% and CAR of 23.0%. The sale of Straumur is progressing, while the CEO plans to step down by year-end 2026.
