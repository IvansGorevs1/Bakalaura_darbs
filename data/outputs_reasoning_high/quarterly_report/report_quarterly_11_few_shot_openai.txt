Kvika banki’s Q2 2026 results reflected resilient interest income and disciplined cost control, but weaker fee income, higher impairment charges and increased bank taxes reduced profitability. Profit before tax was ISK 1,545 million, down ISK 480 million, or 23.7%, from Q2 2025 and 14.6% from Q1 2026. After-tax profit fell to ISK 903 million from ISK 1,439 million a year earlier. Net operating income declined 7.3% year on year to ISK 4,756 million.

Net interest income increased modestly by 1.7% year on year to ISK 3,013 million, as higher interest income from customer lending offset the effects of a smaller liquidity portfolio and higher funding costs. It declined from ISK 3,266 million in Q1, primarily because lower inflation reduced the contribution from the bank’s positive ISK 6.1 billion inflation balance, while a dividend payment of ISK 10.2 billion and recent bond issuances also affected quarterly income. The net interest margin fell to 4.0% from 4.5% in Q1. Net fee and commission income decreased 26.8% year on year to ISK 1,416 million as equity-market turnover and fixed-income activity weakened, although the report described underlying fee income as broadly resilient. Other net operating income increased to ISK 327 million from ISK 231 million.

Administrative expenses declined 1.1% to ISK 2,947 million despite a one-off ISK 112 million restructuring charge. Employee numbers fell to 232, and efficiency measures are expected to reduce the annual cost base by ISK 300–400 million. Net impairment charges rose to ISK 269 million from ISK 122 million as the bank applied a more conservative loan-book review. The effective income tax rate increased to 34.8% from 24.4%, mainly because of higher special tax on financial activities related to the closure of funding derivatives.

For the first six months, continuing-operations pre-tax profit increased 23.0% to ISK 3,354 million. Net operating income rose 0.7% to ISK 9,643 million, supported by 6.8% growth in net interest income and a ISK 219 million increase in other operating income, while fees declined 16.0% and administrative expenses fell 1.8%. After-tax profit from continuing operations increased to ISK 2,277 million from ISK 1,625 million. However, profit for the period declined to ISK 2,277 million from ISK 3,525 million because the prior-year figure included ISK 1,901 million from discontinued operations.

The balance sheet expanded alongside continued lending and deposit growth. Loans to customers rose 7.6% from year-end 2025 to ISK 223.3 billion, while deposits increased 16.5% to ISK 201.2 billion. Liquidity remained strong, with an LCR of 276% and NSFR of 137%, and the capital adequacy ratio stood at 23.0%, 530 basis points above the total capital requirement. Strategic developments included the ongoing sale process for Straumur greiðslumiðlun, expected to close before year-end, and the CEO’s planned departure by the end of 2026.
