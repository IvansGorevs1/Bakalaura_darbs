Nordecon AS reported consolidated revenue of €208.3 million for the twelve months ended 31 December 2025, marking a seven percent decline from €223.9 million in the prior year. This reduction was mainly attributable to a ten percent drop in the Buildings segment, which comprised eighty-one percent of total revenue, partially offset by a ten percent increase in the Infrastructure segment. Gross profit contracted to €13.5 million, pushing the gross margin down to 6.5 percent from 7.5 percent. Operating profit fell to €5.7 million, resulting in an operating margin of 2.7 percent compared to 3.6 percent previously. The group generated EBITDA of €8.3 million with a 4.0 percent margin. Net profit decreased to €2.5 million, with €1.4 million attributable to parent shareholders, down from €5.2 million and €3.8 million in 2024. Earnings per share stood at €0.05.

The moderated profitability reflected multiple headwinds. Estonia’s construction price index rose 1.5 percent year-on-year, driven largely by escalating material costs, while intense public-sector bidding constrained contract pricing. Furthermore, a €750 thousand foreign exchange loss significantly pressured the bottom line. The Ukrainian hryvnia weakened approximately twelve percent against the euro, translating euro-denominated intercompany loans into substantial translation losses recognized in finance costs. Conversely, administrative expenses improved, declining fourteen percent to €6.8 million as weaker financial results triggered a smaller recognition of performance-based pay liabilities. The company paid no dividends during the period.

Looking forward, Nordecon reinforced its revenue pipeline. The order book expanded by thirty percent to €273.1 million by year-end, up from €209.5 million, fueled by a nearly threefold surge in infrastructure backlog. Major awards included Rail Baltica infrastructure works and national highway upgrades. Approximately sixty-nine percent of the current backlog is allocated to 2026, with the remainder spread through 2027 and 2028. During the year, the group secured new contracts worth €235.0 million. While the Estonian construction market stabilized with modest economic growth, management highlighted persistent risks from material price volatility, competitive contracting environments, and skilled labor shortages. Despite near-term margin compression and currency-related earnings drag, the substantially enlarged order book positions the company for volume growth in 2026, assuming prudent risk management and steady market conditions.
