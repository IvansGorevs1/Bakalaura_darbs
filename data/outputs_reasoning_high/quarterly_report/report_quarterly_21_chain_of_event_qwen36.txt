For the six-month period ended 30 June 2026, AS Tallinna Sadam reported total revenue of EUR 59.3 million, representing a 2.4 percent increase compared to the same period last year. Despite this top-line growth, the group experienced a substantial decline in profitability. Operating profit fell by 25.7 percent to EUR 13.6 million, while profit for the period dropped by 41.7 percent to EUR 6.0 million. Adjusted EBITDA decreased by 13.5 percent to EUR 25.8 million. Year-over-year earnings per share declined from EUR 0.04 to EUR 0.02. These profit reductions occurred against a comparative period that benefited from non-recurring items, including an EUR 0.9 million profit from a land disposal for the Rail Baltica project and an EUR 0.9 million insurance indemnity for repair works on the icebreaker Botnica.

The deterioration in financial performance was primarily driven by a 24.0 percent increase in operating expenses, totaling EUR 20.2 million. Fuel costs surged by 47.6 percent to EUR 4.4 million due to elevated diesel prices and more severe winter ice conditions increasing consumption. Electricity costs rose by 28.8 percent to EUR 3.1 million, reflecting higher market prices, increased purchase volumes, and the introduction of a new security of supply fee. Expenses for technical maintenance and repair grew by 80.0 percent to EUR 2.8 million, as the Group did not receive the comparable insurance payout and incurred higher repair costs for the Botnica, Old City Harbour quays, and ferries. Additionally, depreciation, amortisation, and impairment charges increased by 6.0 percent to EUR 12.9 million following the commissioning of a new quay at Paldiski South Harbour and capitalised ferry dry-docking projects.

Operational volumes contracted across key segments, contributing to margin pressure. Cargo handling fell by 5.0 percent to 6.5 million tonnes, weighed down by a 33.8 percent drop in liquid bulk and a 4.8 percent decline in dry bulk, influenced by ongoing geopolitical tensions disrupting European shipping routes. Passenger traffic decreased by 2.0 percent to 3.6 million, with traditional ferry routes experiencing declines due to vessel maintenance and competitive events, while cruise passenger numbers grew nearly 48 percent amid higher ship calls. Vessel calls overall dropped by 4.8 percent.

To partially offset these challenges, revenue growth was anchored by an 18.2 percent increase in charter fees for the icebreaker Botnica, which recorded 20 more contractual days than the prior year, and a 25.0 percent rise in electricity sales revenue. Ferry service revenue also expanded by 1.9 percent thanks to tariff indexation aligned with fuel, labour, and consumer price indices. Meanwhile, net finance costs declined by 11.2 percent due to a 5.7 percent reduction in total loans and borrowings, aiding the bottom line despite the sharp fall in operating margins. Capital expenditure decreased significantly by 70.6 percent to EUR 3.5 million, focused on ferry engine replacements, quay upgrades, and IT investments. Total assets at period-end stood at EUR 615.0 million, with equity decreasing marginally by 0.3 percent to EUR 367.7 million. The financial results reflect a period of higher input costs and structural cargo adjustments, which compressed profitability despite strong utilisation of support assets and regulated utility operations.
