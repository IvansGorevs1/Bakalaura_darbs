AS Merko Ehitus reported weaker financial performance during the first half of 2026 compared with the same period last year, driven by lower real estate development sales and compressed margins in its construction service segment. Consolidated revenue decreased by 10.7% to EUR 149.9 million, while net profit attributable to shareholders fell to EUR 12.8 million from EUR 21.7 million. The net profit margin contracted to 8.6%, down from 12.9%, and pre-tax profit declined to EUR 10.8 million from EUR 23.5 million. Despite a quarterly revenue rebound in the second quarter, bringing sales to EUR 92.7 million, half-year totals reflected seasonal cyclical effects and unfavorable volume shifts.

Operating results were uneven across reporting segments. The construction service segment generated EUR 118.3 million in revenue, a slight decline of 1.5% year-over-year, but operating profit dropped significantly to EUR 3.0 million, with the margin falling from 7.3% to 2.6%. Management attributed the profitability compression mainly to the expiration of highly successful contracts from previous periods, heightened competition, and rising input costs. The real estate development segment faced a sharper top-line contraction, with revenue declining 33.9% to EUR 31.6 million as the company delivered fewer units. Only 120 apartments were sold compared with 222 in the prior year, though operating margins remained relatively resilient at 22.2%. Across both divisions, persistent inflationary pressures on materials, labor, and energy have limited pricing power, particularly as customers across Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania increasingly favor more affordable housing options.

Financial positioning remains supportive of continued operations despite the earnings slowdown. The group’s secured order book reached an all-time high of EUR 835.7 million, reflecting EUR 486.4 million in new contracts signed during the period. Growth in the pipeline was largely fueled by public-sector assignments, including Rail Baltica infrastructure and defense-related facilities, while private clients maintained a cautious stance. On the balance sheet, cash and cash equivalents rose to EUR 39.6 million, and equity stood at EUR 251.1 million, representing 57.4% of total assets. Net debt increased to EUR 29.6 million to fund ongoing residential development projects, yet the overall debt ratio remained moderate at 15.8%. Operating cash flow was negatively impacted by working capital requirements tied to construction execution and inventory accumulation. Overall, the company navigated a challenging macroeconomic environment marked by elevated cost structures and shifting consumer demand, yet preserved strong forward visibility through record order bookings and a well-capitalized balance sheet.
