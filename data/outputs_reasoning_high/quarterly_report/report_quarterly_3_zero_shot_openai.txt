AS Amber Latvijas balzams reported a significant deterioration in performance in the first three months of 2026 compared with the same period of 2025. Sales volume fell 36.1%, from 810,079 to 517,600 9-liter cases. Net turnover decreased 29.6% to EUR 11.1 million from EUR 15.8 million, mainly because of lower sales volumes and a high comparison base in 2025, when revenue had benefited from an adjustment recognized under contractual terms.

Gross profit declined 66.5% to EUR 1.2 million from EUR 3.5 million. Management attributed the reduction primarily to changes in product mix and pricing methodology; although cost reductions were achieved, their positive effect was largely offset. The company moved from an operating profit of EUR 0.6 million in the first quarter of 2025 to an operating loss of EUR 1.6 million in 2026, reducing the operating margin from 3.7% to -14.5%. Net finance income decreased to EUR 0.2 million from EUR 0.3 million, while the company reported a net loss of EUR 1.4 million compared with a profit of EUR 0.9 million previously. Earnings per share consequently fell to a loss of EUR 0.19 from earnings of EUR 0.11.

The results were affected by several external and group-related developments. In January 2026, a trustee was appointed for related company Stoli Group (USA), LLC following Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceedings, causing the group to lose control of its U.S. operations and directly affecting the solvency of the company’s largest customer. The group also reported aggressive actions by Russian authorities, including the designation of related entities as extremist and the confiscation of the group-owned Talvis distillery. In addition, the global spirits market experienced a downturn, with declining volumes, weaker discretionary spending and inventory adjustments in distribution channels.

These pressures created substantial cash-flow difficulties and delayed excise-tax payments. After the State Revenue Service froze the company’s bank accounts, Amber Latvijas balzams applied for legal protection proceedings on 30 January 2026; the Riga City Court approved the application on 5 February. The proceedings are intended to restructure liabilities and stabilize operations. Operating activities used EUR 1.8 million of cash, and cash and cash equivalents fell from EUR 1.0 million at year-end 2025 to EUR 38,000 at 31 March 2026, although current assets exceeded current liabilities by EUR 82.9 million. Subsequent to the period, the principal shareholder entered Luxembourg judicial reorganization proceedings with a moratorium until 24 August 2026. Management is focusing on efficiency, cost optimization, stronger controls and improved financial stability.
