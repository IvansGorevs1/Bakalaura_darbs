Nordecon’s revenue in 2025 was €208.3 million, down 7% from €223.9 million in 2024. The decline was primarily driven by a roughly 10% reduction in Buildings revenue, to €168.3 million, while Infrastructure revenue increased 10% to €39.9 million. Buildings accounted for 81% of group revenue and Infrastructure for 19%. Revenue from commercial buildings rose approximately 71%, but public buildings revenue fell around 32%. Own property development revenue increased to €9.8 million from €7.7 million, mainly reflecting apartment sales in the Seileri Kvartal and Tammepärja Kodu developments.

Profitability weakened. Gross profit fell to €13.5 million from €16.8 million, and the gross margin declined to 6.5% from 7.5%. Operating profit decreased to €5.7 million from €8.1 million, reducing the operating margin to 2.7% from 3.6%. EBITDA was €8.3 million, compared with €11.0 million in 2024, while the EBITDA margin fell to 4.0% from 4.9%. Net profit declined to €2.5 million from €5.2 million, and profit attributable to Nordecon’s owners fell to €1.4 million from €3.8 million. Earnings per share decreased to €0.05 from €0.12.

Net finance costs increased to €3.0 million from €2.3 million, mainly because the Ukrainian hryvnia weakened approximately 12% against the euro. Translation of euro-denominated loans to Ukrainian subsidiaries produced a foreign exchange loss of €0.75 million, compared with €0.26 million in 2024. Administrative expenses fell 14% to €6.8 million, mainly because weaker financial performance resulted in lower performance-based pay. Credit losses nevertheless increased to €354 thousand from €20 thousand.

The fourth quarter showed a similar deterioration in profitability despite higher revenue. Revenue rose to €60.6 million from €45.2 million, but gross margin fell to 5.9% from 9.0%, operating profit declined to €0.9 million from €1.1 million, and the group recorded a €43 thousand loss compared with a €618 thousand profit in the fourth quarter of 2024.

The main positive development was the order book, which increased 30% to €273.1 million at year-end. New contracts secured rose to €235.0 million from €181.4 million. Infrastructure’s share of the backlog increased to 42% from 18%, supported by major Rail Baltica and road construction contracts, while the Buildings backlog declined 7%. Approximately 69% of the backlog is scheduled for 2026. Management expects higher business volumes in 2026 as Estonia’s construction market stabilises and public investment, particularly in defence and infrastructure, supports demand. Operating cash flow remained positive at €1.2 million, but cash and cash equivalents fell to €5.3 million from €8.2 million.
