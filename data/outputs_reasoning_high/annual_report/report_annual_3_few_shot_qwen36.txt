Coop Pank reported a net profit of €28.7 million for 2025, an 11% decline compared to the previous year. The reduction was primarily attributable to a challenging interest rate environment that compressed net interest income to €73.3 million, partially offsetting strong volume expansion. Total net operating income decreased modestly to €79.4 million, while net fee and commission income rose slightly to €4.5 million. Return on equity fell to 12.9% from 16.2% in 2024, and the cost-to-income ratio increased to 52.3%. Despite these profitability pressures, the bank executed its growth strategy vigorously, expanding its loan portfolio by €340 million, or 19%, to €2.11 billion. Corporate lending drove the majority of this growth at 27%, followed by a 17% increase in mortgage financing. Customer deposits also grew steadily by 9% to €2.05 billion, supported by a 11% rise in demand deposits as the bank added 19,000 new clients to reach a total of 227,000. 

The financial outcome was shaped by macroeconomic tailwinds and deliberate strategic investments. Estonia’s economy recorded 0.7% real GDP growth in 2025 after years of contraction, while ECB policy rate cuts and falling EURIBOR rates gradually restored borrowing confidence across the market. To sustain expansion and optimize funding costs, Coop Pank issued €250 million in four-year covered bonds on the Irish stock exchange, securing its first tranche of international debt under a €750 million program. Simultaneously, the bank concluded a synthetic securitization agreement with the European Investment Bank Group, transferring credit risk on a designated loan portfolio to unlock capital relief and enable up to €249 million in targeted lending for environmental and social projects. Leadership transitions included the appointment of Arko Kurtmann as chairman of the management board, while product innovation expanded through a sector-first monthly cashback loyalty program, fully virtual card offerings, and physical branch network enhancements. Cooperating closely with its domestic cooperative ownership, the bank maintained a strong capital buffer with a total capital adequacy ratio of 18.54% and distributed €7.2 million in dividends aligned with its historical payout policy. Overall, the reporting period characterized a deliberate trade-off between aggressive market penetration and near-term margin compression, positioning the bank to capture improved earnings once interest rates stabilize and funded asset growth matures into recurring income streams.
