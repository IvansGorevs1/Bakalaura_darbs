Heimstaden reported improved financial performance in 2025, supported by stronger operating margins, property revaluations, realised gains from disposals, and foreign-exchange gains. Total revenue increased slightly to SEK 18,033 million from SEK 17,979 million, while rental income remained broadly unchanged at SEK 15,916 million compared with SEK 15,919 million in 2024. Like-for-like rental income growth was 4.9%, supported mainly by indexation, tenant improvements, occupancy improvements, and rent reversion. Real economic occupancy remained high at 98.5%.

Net operating income increased 2.6% to SEK 11,475 million, and the margin improved to 72.1% from 70.3%. Property expenses declined 6.1% to SEK 4,378 million, reflecting cost controls, digitalisation, efficiency measures, and the smaller portfolio following property disposals. These improvements offset higher utility costs, which increased because municipal price increases exceeded core CPI.

Profit before tax rose to SEK 16,012 million from SEK 10,403 million. The result benefited from a SEK 9,498 million fair-value gain on investment properties, compared with SEK 8,554 million in 2024, realised disposal gains of SEK 2,207 million, and foreign-exchange gains of SEK 3,996 million versus a loss of SEK 2,038 million a year earlier. However, the company recognised a SEK 3,707 million goodwill impairment, primarily relating to Germany and Sweden, as higher discount rates reduced the recoverable value of Akelius-related goodwill. Net profit increased to SEK 13,056 million from SEK 7,621 million, although total comprehensive income declined to SEK 4,199 million from SEK 11,082 million because currency translation losses reached SEK 8,857 million.

The property portfolio generated a 3.0% fair-value increase during the year, but total investment property value declined to SEK 324,429 million from SEK 335,422 million after disposals and currency effects. Denmark and Czechia recorded particularly strong value growth. Privatisation sales amounted to SEK 10,664 million, covering 2,466 residential units sold at a 28.9% premium to book value. No new development or construction commitments were undertaken.

The balance sheet and credit position strengthened. Net interest-bearing liabilities fell to SEK 170,212 million from SEK 190,381 million, reducing net loan-to-value to 52.2% from 56.4% and net debt to total assets to 48.0% from 51.0%. Equity increased to SEK 150,557 million, and the equity ratio improved to 42.4%. Interest expense was stable at SEK 6,536 million, while the interest coverage ratio improved to 2.0x from 1.9x. Operating cash flow was SEK 2,742 million, and year-end cash was SEK 4,179 million, supplemented by SEK 23,449 million in unused credit facilities. The group completed approximately SEK 59.5 billion of financing transactions, while Fitch maintained a B- rating but revised the outlook to negative. A potential one-off accounting loss of approximately SEK 3 billion is expected in 2026 when the Kojamo investment is reclassified.
