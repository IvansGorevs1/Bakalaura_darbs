Nordic Fibreboard AS reported consolidated net sales of €7.33 million for the year ended 31 December 2025, a 3.4 percent decrease from €7.59 million in 2024. The company recorded a net loss of €1.15 million, widening from a €0.79 million loss in the prior year, while consolidated EBITDA deteriorated to negative €0.42 million compared to negative €0.03 million in 2024, representing a six percentage point decline in EBITDA margin to negative six percent. Operating loss reached €0.94 million against €0.55 million previously. Despite these operating declines, total equity increased to €4.46 million from €3.62 million, primarily driven by a €2.0 million share capital increase executed through a public offering in October 2025. This equity raise generated a €1.44 million cash inflow, partially counteracting a €0.50 million negative operating cash flow and stabilizing the asset base at €9.3 million.

The deterioration in profitability was explicitly linked to recurrent production disruptions and structural cost pressures. Both 2025 and 2024 saw December factory shutdowns that halted revenue generation while personnel salaries, ongoing maintenance, and repair expenses continued to accrue, collectively eroding roughly €0.2 million from segment EBITDA in 2025 alone. In 2025, the shutdown was a planned transition to a new gas boiler house, whereas 2024 experienced an unplanned suspension due to weather-related structural damage. Furthermore, underutilized manufacturing capacity increased internal unit production costs. Sales to the European Union remained stable at 92 percent of total revenue, but shipments to Africa and Asia contracted sharply. These commercial pressures unfolded within a challenging macroeconomic environment characterized by modest Estonian economic growth of 0.6 percent, inflation near 6 percent fueled by tax hikes, and weakened domestic demand in construction and business sectors.

Parallel to core operations, the Group streamlined its property portfolio by merging the subsidiary Pärnu Riverside Development OÜ into the parent company in mid-2025. Rental income collapsed to €3 thousand after lease agreements were terminated in August 2024 due to unsustainable utility costs, prompting a strategic pivot toward development and eventual disposal of the Admirali Quarter site. A preliminary sale agreement for ten divided properties was executed in January 2026. While long-term borrowings decreased to €3.2 million, trade payables surged to €1.3 million, contributing to a negative working capital position of €2.7 million. The combination of temporary production halts, elevated fixed costs during idle periods, and a subdued industrial market fundamentally drove the widened losses, while the timely capital raise and asset monetization efforts formed the primary responses to preserve liquidity and position the company for post-boiler-production efficiency gains.
