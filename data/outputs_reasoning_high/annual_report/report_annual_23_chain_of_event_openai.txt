TKM Grupp AS reported a weaker financial performance in 2025 amid a challenging Estonian retail and automotive market. Revenue decreased 2.6% to €919.6 million from €944.6 million in 2024. Profit before tax fell 31.4% to €24.3 million, while net profit declined 36.1% to €17.5 million. Earnings per share decreased from €0.67 to €0.43. EBITDA fell 14.7% to €77.4 million and operating profit declined 26.6% to €34.9 million, reducing the operating margin from 5% to 4% and the net margin from 3% to 2%. Gross margin, however, increased slightly from 27.5% to 27.7%.

The largest pressure came from the car trade segment, where revenue declined 11.9% to €176.9 million, profit before tax fell 48.5% to €5.7 million and net profit decreased 54.4% to €4.3 million. The report explicitly links this deterioration to Estonia’s motor vehicle tax, uncertainty before its introduction and a resulting sharp decline in demand. New passenger-car sales in Estonia fell 48.6% for the year and 62.4% in the fourth quarter, although stronger markets in Latvia and Lithuania partially offset the decline. The supermarkets segment increased revenue 0.3% to €611.9 million, but net profit fell 38.6% to €9.5 million as sales volumes declined, promotional activity increased and Jõgeva Selver’s renovation caused one-off costs and temporarily disrupted sales. Department-store revenue fell 1.1% to €103.0 million and its net loss widened to €0.5 million, partly reflecting consumer caution, high price sensitivity and weak demand for winter goods during unusually warm fourth-quarter weather. Security revenue declined 8.1% and the segment recorded a small loss, while real-estate revenue rose 5.1% to €7.7 million and net profit increased 20.5% to €6.3 million.

Cost control partly mitigated the revenue decline. Staff costs rose 3.7%, but other operating expenses increased only 0.2%. Lower borrowing interest reduced finance costs by €1.5 million, and income-tax expense decreased by €1.2 million. Nevertheless, 2024 included €4.0 million of one-off gains from investment-property revaluation and property disposals, making the comparison less favourable.

Operating cash flow decreased to €65.6 million from €73.1 million. Total investments were €25.5 million, down from €36.6 million, focused on store refurbishments, digital systems, car dealerships and real estate. Cash fell to €29.5 million, and working capital became negative at €14.2 million, largely because €32.1 million of loans maturing in 2026 were classified as current; refinancing was planned and management reported no liquidity issues. A €11.2 million property revaluation gain increased total comprehensive income to €28.7 million. After year-end, the Group agreed to acquire Škoda-related businesses in Estonia, subject to completion conditions, supporting its strategy to expand the automotive segment.
