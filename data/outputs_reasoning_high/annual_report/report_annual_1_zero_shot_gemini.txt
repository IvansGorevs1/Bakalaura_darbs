During the 2025 reporting period, AAC Clyde Space experienced a decline in overall financial performance compared to the previous year, driven by project delays and lower delivery volumes in its manufacturing segment, while continuing to advance its strategic transition toward recurring data services. Net sales for the full year decreased by sixteen percent to SEK 295.3 million, down from SEK 352.9 million in 2024. Total revenue reached SEK 337.8 million compared with SEK 408.8 million in the prior year. Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization declined to SEK 17.5 million from SEK 46.7 million, marking the company’s third consecutive year of positive EBITDA. The operating result worsened to a loss of SEK 40.0 million from a loss of SEK 4.0 million in 2024. The net loss after tax expanded to SEK 46.6 million compared with a loss of SEK 5.7 million previously, resulting in basic and diluted earnings per share of negative SEK 7.63 compared to negative SEK 1.00 in 2024. Operating cash flow dropped to negative SEK 72.6 million from positive SEK 56.8 million in 2024. Cash and cash equivalents at year-end stood at SEK 30.1 million down from SEK 49.7 million, while the equity ratio improved to eighty percent from seventy-one percent. The order backlog at the end of the year was SEK 352 million compared to SEK 641 million in 2024. Segment performance showed a clear divergence. The Data & Services segment demonstrated strong growth, with net sales increasing to SEK 80.1 million from SEK 44.9 million and EBITDA rising to SEK 27.8 million from SEK 21.1 million, supported by the operational rollout of the Sedna satellite constellation. Conversely, the Products & Missions segment recorded a decline, with net sales falling to SEK 245.0 million from SEK 346.0 million and EBITDA decreasing to SEK 16.8 million from SEK 51.6 million. The company reported that overall results and cash flows were materially affected by programme delays. Specifically, technical discussions between an end customer and a subcontractor delayed milestone recognition, shifting approximately SEK 32 million of revenue into 2026, while political and procurement developments postponed the EPS-Sterna project initiation. Additionally, commissioning of Sedna satellites took longer than planned, and launch rescheduling by providers delayed the deployment of initial VIREON satellites. Despite these setbacks, the company achieved key operational milestones, including signing the INFLECION Phase one contract with the European Space Agency, delivering initial CubeCAT laser communication terminals, and securing strategic capital through directed share issues, including an investment agreement with Bonnier Capital. Overall, the company maintained profitability at the EBITDA level and strengthened its long-term market position through expanded constellation capacity and major institutional agreements, even as short-term revenues and cash flows faced headwinds from scheduling and timing deferrals.
