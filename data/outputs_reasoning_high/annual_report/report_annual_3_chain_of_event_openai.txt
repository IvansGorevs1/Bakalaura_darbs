Coop Pank delivered strong business growth in 2025, but profitability declined as falling interest rates reduced income. Consolidated net profit was €28.7 million, down 11% from €32.2 million in 2024. Total net operating income decreased 3% to €79.4 million, mainly because net interest income fell 6% to €73.3 million and the net interest margin narrowed from 3.8% to 3.0%. The bank stated that the lower-interest environment was the main reason profit declined, as the 19% expansion in business volumes did not fully offset the pressure on interest income. Net fee and commission income increased 4% to €4.5 million, while net other income rose from near zero to €1.5 million.

Operating expenses increased 2% to €41.5 million, causing the cost-to-income ratio to deteriorate from 49.6% to 52.3%. Credit loss allowances improved, declining to €3.3 million from €4.6 million, while the effective tax rate rose to 17% from 12%, partly reflecting the increase in the advance income tax rate for credit institutions from 14% to 18%. Return on equity fell from 16.2% to 12.9% and return on assets from 1.6% to 1.2%.

Business volumes expanded substantially amid the early recovery of the Estonian economy, declining Euribor rates and stronger confidence among businesses and households. The customer base grew by 19,000, or 9%, to 227,000. The net loan portfolio increased by €340 million, or 19%, to €2.11 billion, exceeding €2 billion for the first time. Business loans grew 27% to €986 million and home loans increased 17% to €863 million. Customer deposits rose 9% to €2.05 billion, with demand deposits up 11% and term deposits up 8%. Management reported that loan quality remained at a low-risk level; Stage 3 gross exposures declined to €14.0 million from €14.8 million despite portfolio growth.

Funding was strengthened through the first €250 million covered-bond issuance under a €750 million programme, providing longer-term financing and allowing the bank to reduce more expensive term and foreign deposits. An agreement with the European Investment Bank Group is expected to provide capital relief and enable up to €249 million of new business lending, although it was not yet effective and had no impact on 2025 results.

Equity rose to €234.5 million from €211.6 million. Capital ratios declined as risk-weighted assets grew, with the CET1 ratio at 14.15% and total capital ratio at 18.54%, but all regulatory requirements were met. The bank paid €7.2 million in dividends for 2024 and proposed a 2025 dividend of €0.065 per share, totaling €6.7 million.
