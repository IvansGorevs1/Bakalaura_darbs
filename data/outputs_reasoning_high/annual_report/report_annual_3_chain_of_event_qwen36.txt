Coop Pank reported net profit of 28.7 million euros for the full year 2025, representing an eleven percent decline from the 32.2 million euros recorded in 2024. This reduction in profitability occurred alongside substantial balance sheet expansion, as the loan portfolio grew nineteen percent year over year to reach 2.11 billion euros. The increase was driven primarily by a twenty seven percent rise in business loans and a seventeen percent increase in home loans, while consumer lending and leasing showed more moderate growth. Concurrently, the customer base expanded by nineteen thousand accounts, a nine percent gain that brought total clients to 227,000. Customer deposits also rose, reaching 2.05 billion euros, reflecting an eleven percent increase in demand deposits and an eight percent growth in term deposits.

Despite these volume gains, profitability and efficiency metrics weakened. Net interest income decreased to 73.3 million euros from 77.6 million euros, lowering the net interest margin to three percent from three point eight percent. Return on equity fell to twelve point nine percent from sixteen point two percent, and the cost to income ratio rose to fifty two point three percent from forty nine point six percent. Operating expenses reached 41.5 million euros against total net operating income of 79.4 million euros. Credit loss allowances declined to 3.3 million euros from 4.6 million euros, confirming stable asset quality and a consistently low risk portfolio. Income tax expense was 5.8 million euros compared to 4.5 million euros the prior year.

The primary driver behind the earnings contraction was explicitly identified as the broader macroeconomic environment characterized by steadily declining interest rates. Following multiple rate cuts by the European Central Bank, which lowered its deposit facility rate to two percent, market benchmarks such as the six month Euribor dropped significantly throughout the year. This compressive rate dynamic reduced the banks net interest income faster than the expanding loan book could offset. To mitigate funding pressures and support continued lending, the institution issued two hundred fifty million euros in covered bonds during the first quarter, securing long term stable capital. Additional strategic developments included signing a European Investment Bank guarantee for synthetic securitization, obtaining a five million euro subordinated green loan, and elevating its partnership with Coop Estonia through a widespread cashback loyalty program. Leadership transitions saw Arko Kurtmann assume the role of Chairman of the Management Board, while Moody s affirmed the bank deposit rating at Baa2 with a positive outlook. Capitalization remained robust, with a common equity tier one ratio of fourteen point one five percent and total capital adequacy at eighteen point five four percent, comfortably satisfying regulatory requirements. The combination of disciplined volume growth, enhanced funding diversification, and maintained asset quality positions the bank for its stated objective of returning to net profit expansion in subsequent periods once favorable rate dynamics align with its scaled operations.
