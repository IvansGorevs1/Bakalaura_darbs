Tallinna Vesi reported improved financial performance in 2025, with growth in revenue, operating profit and profit before tax. Group sales increased by 11.4% to €72.48 million, supported by a 6.9% increase in water-services revenue to €62.31 million and a 62.0% rise in construction-services revenue to €8.46 million. Revenue from private customers in the main service area grew 18.3% to €31.84 million, partly due to higher consumption and tariff changes, while business-customer revenue declined 2.7% to €16.76 million following reductions in business tariffs as prices were harmonised. Revenue from customers outside the main service area increased 13.8% to €7.71 million.

Gross profit rose 10.5% to €29.52 million, although the gross margin eased slightly to 40.73% from 41.04%. Operating profit increased 11.8% to €21.22 million and EBITDA rose to €30.95 million, lifting the EBITDA margin to 42.70%. Cost of goods and services sold increased 12.0% to €42.96 million, mainly because construction-services costs rose 74.5%, staff costs increased 6.8% and depreciation and amortisation rose 11.9%. Direct production costs were broadly unchanged, as lower electricity purchases offset higher chemical and pollution-tax costs. The combined heat and power plant at the Paljassaare wastewater treatment plant reduced purchased electricity and was a key contributor to operating-profit growth.

Net financial expense declined to €3.78 million from €4.20 million, mainly because of lower Euribor rates. Profit before tax increased 18.0% to €17.43 million, while net profit rose 7.2% to €14.24 million. Net profit growth was constrained by the higher income-tax rate on dividend payments. Earnings per share increased to €0.71 from €0.66, while the net profit margin decreased to 19.69% from 20.41%.

The company continued its substantial investment programme, investing €55.74 million in fixed assets, up from €49.53 million in 2024. Investments focused on pipe networks, stormwater systems, treatment facilities, smart meters and service continuity. Operating cash flow increased to €30.83 million, but investment cash outflows reached €45.15 million. New loans of €45 million helped finance the programme, and year-end cash increased to €11.29 million from €3.59 million. Borrowings rose to €161.4 million, increasing the debt-to-assets ratio to 66.02% from 61.71%.

The company paid €10.6 million, or €0.53 per share, in dividends from 2024 profits; a dividend for 2025 had not yet been declared. A €6.02 million provision remained for tariff-related third-party claims totalling €14.67 million. Watercom also recorded a successful year, reporting record turnover and profit amid recovering construction activity, although competitive pressure and rising input costs continued to limit construction margins. For 2026, investments of up to €60 million are planned, alongside continued network and treatment-plant modernisation.
