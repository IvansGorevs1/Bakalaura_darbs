Coop Pank delivered strong business growth in 2025, although profitability declined as falling interest rates reduced margins. Net profit was €28.7 million, down 11% from €32.2 million in 2024. Profit before tax decreased to €34.6 million from €36.7 million, while return on equity fell from 16.2% to 12.9% and return on assets from 1.6% to 1.2%. Net interest income declined 6% to €73.3 million, and net interest margin narrowed from 3.8% to 3.0%. This reflected the lower-interest-rate environment, which was not fully offset by the 19% expansion in business volumes. Net fee and commission income increased 4% to €4.5 million, while net other income improved to €1.5 million from approximately zero in 2024.

Operating expenses rose 2% to €41.5 million, resulting in a higher cost-to-income ratio of 52.3%, compared with 49.6% a year earlier. Credit loss allowance decreased to €3.3 million from €4.6 million, and management reported that portfolio quality remained at a low-risk level, with low overdue debt and limited demand for payment holidays. Income tax expense increased to €5.8 million from €4.5 million, with the effective tax rate rising from 12% to 17%.

The bank’s loan portfolio grew by €340 million, or 19%, to €2.11 billion, exceeding €2 billion for the first time. Business lending increased 27%, or €207 million, and the home-loan portfolio rose 17%, or €125 million. Customer deposits increased 9% to €2.05 billion, with demand deposits up 11% and term deposits up 8%. The customer base expanded by 19,000, or 9%, to 227,000. Total assets increased to €2.70 billion from €2.19 billion, while shareholders’ equity rose to €234.5 million from €211.6 million.

Performance benefited from the beginning of Estonia’s economic recovery, stronger loan demand and greater investment activity. Estonia’s GDP grew 0.7% in 2025, while declining inflation and interest rates supported confidence among businesses and households. However, the six-month Euribor fell to 2.1% by year-end, contributing to pressure on interest income.

Coop Pank diversified funding by issuing €250 million of four-year covered bonds, the first tranche of a €750 million programme. It also signed an EIB Group guarantee agreement enabling up to €249 million of new business lending; the transaction had not become effective and had no impact on 2025 results. Moody’s affirmed the bank’s Baa2 deposit rating with a positive outlook. Capital requirements were met, although the CET1 ratio declined to 14.15% from 14.28% and total capital adequacy to 18.54% from 19.41%. The bank paid €7.2 million in dividends for 2024 and proposed a €0.065-per-share dividend from 2025 profit.
