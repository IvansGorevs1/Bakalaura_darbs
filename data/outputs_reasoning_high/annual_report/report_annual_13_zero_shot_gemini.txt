During the financial year 2024, AS LHV Group achieved the highest business volumes and net profit in its history, supported by robust loan portfolio expansion, strong deposit growth, and efficient operations. Total consolidated assets reached EUR 8.7 billion, representing a 23 percent increase compared to EUR 7.09 billion in 2023. Total customer deposits rose by 21 percent year-over-year to EUR 6.9 billion. The net loan portfolio expanded by 28 percent to EUR 4.55 billion, driven by a 21 percent increase in Estonian corporate and retail lending alongside rapid growth in United Kingdom SME loans, which surged by 338 percent to reach EUR 348 million following LHV Bank's full year under a banking license.

Financial results demonstrated strong top-line performance. Net interest income grew by 8 percent to EUR 273.3 million, while net fee and commission income increased by 24 percent to EUR 60.3 million. Total net income rose by 11 percent to EUR 338.3 million. Operating expenses increased by 14 percent to EUR 146.9 million, primarily driven by higher personnel numbers. Expected credit losses amounted to EUR 16.3 million, compared to EUR 11.5 million in the prior year. Pre-tax profit totalled EUR 175.1 million. Net profit reached a record EUR 150.3 million, a 7 percent increase from EUR 140.9 million in 2023. Net profit attributable to owners of the parent was EUR 149.0 million, resulting in basic earnings per share of EUR 0.46.

Profitability indicators remained strong. Return on equity stood at 24.5 percent, down from 29.0 percent in 2023, while return on assets was 1.9 percent. The cost-to-income ratio was 43.4 percent. Capitalisation remained robust with a total capital adequacy ratio of 20.68 percent and a Common Equity Tier 1 ratio of 16.89 percent, comfortably exceeding regulatory requirements. Assets under management by LHV Varahaldus increased by 3 percent to EUR 1.56 billion, and LHV Kindlustus generated gross written premiums of EUR 38.0 million, marking a 21 percent increase and its second consecutive profitable year.

The operating environment was shaped by negative GDP growth in Estonia and shifting monetary policies. The European Central Bank implemented four interest rate cuts in the second half of the year, bringing the deposit facility rate to 3 percent by year-end. Despite intense competition for deposits and lending, LHV successfully fulfilled all financial targets, expanded its active customer base in LHV Pank by 38,000 to 456,000 customers, and entered 2025 with a solid operational foundation.
