During the financial year ended December 31, 2025, Coop Pank reported a moderation in profitability amid a shifting macroeconomic environment characterized by receding inflation and declining interest rates in the euro area and Estonia. Total net operating income slightly decreased to 79.4 million euros from 81.9 million euros in the previous year. Consequently, the bank reported a net profit of 28.7 million euros, representing an 11 percent decline compared to 32.2 million euros in 2024. Management noted that this decrease was primarily driven by the lower-interest rate environment, which the 19 percent growth in business volumes could not fully offset. Return on equity stood at 12.9 percent, down from 16.2 percent in the prior year, while return on assets was 1.2 percent compared to 1.6 percent.

Net interest income declined to 73.3 million euros from 77.6 million euros, reflecting narrower interest margins as market rates dropped, with the six-month Euribor falling to 2.1 percent by year-end. Conversely, net fee and commission income rose slightly to 4.5 million euros from 4.3 million euros. Operating expenses increased to 41.5 million euros compared to 40.6 million euros in 2024, raising the cost-to-income ratio to 52.3 percent from 49.6 percent. Despite economic uncertainties, credit quality remained robust, allowing the bank to reduce credit loss allowances to 3.3 million euros from 4.6 million euros in the previous period. Income tax expenses increased to 5.8 million euros from 4.5 million euros following an adjustment to credit institution tax rates.

Despite the lower net profit, the bank experienced robust operational expansion. The total customer base grew by 19,000 clients, or 9 percent, reaching 227,000 customers by the end of 2025. For the first time in its history, the net loan portfolio exceeded two billion euros, expanding by 19 percent to reach 2.11 billion euros compared to 1.77 billion euros at the end of 2024. Growth was propelled by all financing lines, led by a 27 percent increase in business loans amounting to 207 million euros and a 17 percent increase in home loans totaling 125 million euros. Customer deposits grew by 9 percent to reach 2.05 billion euros, driven by an 11 percent increase in demand deposits and an 8 percent increase in term deposits.

A significant strategic milestone during the period was the issuance of 250 million euros in covered bonds on the Irish stock exchange under a new 750 million euro program, providing long-term stable funding that enabled the bank to intentionally reduce reliance on expensive term deposits. Additionally, the bank concluded a synthetic securitization transaction with the European Investment Bank Group to optimize regulatory capital and facilitate up to 249 million euros in new sustainable business loans. Shareholders equity increased to 235 million euros from 212 million euros. In line with its dividend policy, the bank distributed 7.2 million euros in net dividends, translating to 0.07 euros per share, and proposed a dividend of 0.065 euros per share for the 2025 financial year.
