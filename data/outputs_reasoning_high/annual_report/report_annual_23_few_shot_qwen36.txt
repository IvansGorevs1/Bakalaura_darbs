TKM Grupp AS reported a decline in both revenue and profitability during the 2025 financial year, driven primarily by challenging macroeconomic conditions and sector-specific regulatory changes. Consolidated sales revenue fell to 919.6 million euros, representing a 2.6 percent decrease compared to 944.6 million euros in 2024. The Group’s net profit dropped to 17.5 million euros, marking a 36.1 percent year-on-year reduction from 27.5 million euros, largely due to a weakened automotive market and higher comparative non-recurring income in the prior year. Profit before tax contracted to 24.3 million euros, down 31.4 percent, with earnings per share declining to 0.43 euros from 0.67 euros.

Performance varied significantly across business segments. The supermarket division maintained top-line stability with revenue rising marginally to 611.9 million euros, though net profit declined to 9.5 million euros amid rising input costs and shifting consumer purchasing patterns toward promotional items. The department stores segment recorded a slight revenue dip to 103.0 million euros and operated at a minor loss before tax. The most substantial contraction occurred in the car trade segment, where revenue fell 11.9 percent to 176.9 million euros as Estonia’s new motor vehicle tax suppressed domestic new vehicle demand by nearly half. Partially offsetting these declines, the real estate segment posted stronger results, with rental and operating income pushing revenue up 5.1 percent to 7.7 million euros and net profit increasing to 6.3 million euros. Security services revenues contracted by 8.1 percent to 20.1 million euros.

Operating discipline remained central to the Group’s financial outcomes. Labour costs rose modestly by 3.7 percent, while other operating expenses grew just 0.2 percent, helping to cushion the impact of lower sales. Interest expenses provided a positive variance, saving approximately 1.5 million euros compared to the prior period. The Group maintained an investment posture of 25.5 million euros, directing capital toward store modernisations, digital platform upgrades, and the opening of a multi-brand automotive facility in Lithuania. At year-end, cash reserves decreased to 29.5 million euros, and working capital turned negative at minus 14.2 million euros due to the reclassification of maturing long-term loans as current liabilities. Management confirmed that ongoing operating cash flows and available credit facilities are sufficient to manage near-term obligations, with refinancing activities already initiated for 2026 maturities. Looking ahead, the Group announced post-balance-sheet negotiations to acquire two established automotive dealer networks in Estonia, positioning its automotive operations for cross-border synergies and expanded market coverage as the broader economic environment gradually stabilises.
