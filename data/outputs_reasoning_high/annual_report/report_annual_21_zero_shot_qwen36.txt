AS Tallinna Sadam Group delivered a resilient financial performance in the year ended 31 December 2025, driven by operational efficiency gains and strong recovery in its core port segments despite a marginal top-line contraction. Consolidated revenue slightly declined by 0.8% to EUR 118.7 million from EUR 119.6 million in 2024, primarily due to a sharp drop in icebreaker Botnica charter fees following a 35% reduction in utilization. However, this decline was more than offset by stronger volumes and indexed pricing across the passenger and cargo harbours, as well as domestic ferry services. Operating profit rose 12.2% to EUR 32.7 million, lifting the operating margin to 27.6% from 24.4%. Adjusted EBITDA grew 6.4% to EUR 56.5 million, with the adjusted EBITDA margin expanding to 47.6% from 44.4%, reflecting disciplined expense management. Net profit increased 17.3% to EUR 22.5 million, yielding an earnings per share of EUR 0.09.

Operational throughput continued its post-pandemic recovery trajectory. Cargo volumes reached 13.8 million tonnes, a 5.1% year-on-year increase led by a robust 47% surge in liquid bulk and steady growth in dry bulk. The Port of Tallinn maintained its position as the second-largest passenger harbour in the Baltic Sea, handling 8.3 million passengers, a 1.0% increase. Domestic ferry traffic carried 2.5 million passengers across 23,050 trips, with service revenues growing due to contractual fee indexing tied to inflation indices, even as voyage counts dipped slightly by 0.6%. Cruise tourism also rebounded, with passenger numbers rising 25% and ship calls increasing.

Market conditions and strategic developments shaped these results. While geopolitical tensions and sanctions impacted traditional liquid bulk flows, operators successfully adapted by sourcing alternative cargoes, supporting the segment’s recovery. The absence of St Petersburg from Baltic cruise routes prompted lines to extend seasons and increase off-season calls, benefiting Old City Harbour. On the cost side, operating expenses fell 10% to EUR 36.4 million, largely thanks to lower technical maintenance costs for the icebreaker following a one-time insurance reimbursement, reduced fuel consumption through optimized routing, and lower lease expenses. Strategic investments of EUR 33.2 million supported infrastructure upgrades, including the completion of a new multifunctional quay at Paldiski South Harbour, while future capital expenditure will focus on shore power installations aligned with EU environmental regulations. The Group maintained a solid balance sheet, with net debt decreasing 15.5% to EUR 141.7 million and total equity rising 0.9% to EUR 380.9 million. Following its established dividend policy, the Board proposed distributing EUR 19.2 million, equivalent to 100% of the prior year’s profit, ensuring steady shareholder returns amid ongoing sustainability and digitalization initiatives.
