Novaturas AB reported consolidated revenue of EUR 200.9 million for the year ended 31 December 2024, a 3.6 percent decline from EUR 208.3 million in 2023. The group served 239 thousand passengers, down 7.6 percent. Gross profit contracted to EUR 20.7 million, a 20.9 percent decrease, resulting in a gross margin of 10.3 percent compared to 12.6 percent previously. This compression stemmed from intense price competition and an oversupplied travel market in the Baltic States, particularly affecting package tours to Turkey and Greece. 

Profitability deteriorated sharply across the board. The group posted a net loss of EUR 7.6 million, reversing a EUR 3.4 million profit in 2023. Total operating expenses rose 25 percent to EUR 27.5 million, heavily impacted by a one-off goodwill impairment of EUR 5.7 million allocated to the Estonia cash-generating unit. Without this charge, operating expenses actually fell 1 percent to EUR 21.8 million. Accordingly, EBITDA swung to a negative EUR 6.1 million from a positive EUR 5.2 million, and adjusted EBITDA registered a minor deficit of EUR 0.45 million versus EUR 5.2 million. The impairment reflected revised macroeconomic assumptions, specifically a projected traveler volume compound annual growth rate decline from 1.3 percent to minus 5.0 percent, alongside a higher pre-tax discount rate of 12.72 percent.

Operational initiatives introduced during 2024 began delivering results from the third quarter onward. Strategy adjustments included optimizing destination portfolios, reducing low-margin last-minute bookings, and improving flight utilization. These measures raised the fourth-quarter load factor to 98.9 percent, up from 96.7 percent, and drove strong passenger growth to long-haul markets like Spain, Vietnam, and Thailand. However, the accumulated losses strained balance sheet metrics, lowering the equity-to-assets ratio to 20.3 percent and triggering breach of certain bank covenants, which lenders subsequently waived. To address liquidity and fund recovery, the company secured additional credit lines shortly after year-end and finalized a strategic investment agreement granting a Turkish tourism executive a 23.2 percent ownership stake, with further capital injections planned to support operational restructuring and future growth.
