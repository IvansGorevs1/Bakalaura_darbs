Heimstaden reported stronger operating profitability in 2025 despite broadly unchanged rental income. Rental income was SEK 15,916 million, essentially flat versus SEK 15,919 million in 2024. Indexation and tenant improvement investments supported income, while foreign-exchange effects, privatisations, and portfolio sales offset those gains. Annualised like-for-like rental growth was 4.9%, down from 5.6%, while real economic occupancy remained stable at 98.5%. Fourth-quarter rental income declined 3.4% to SEK 3,945 million, although quarterly occupancy improved to 98.7%.

Net operating income increased to SEK 11,475 million from SEK 11,188 million, and the NOI margin rose to 72.1% from 70.3%. The improvement reflected a 6.1% reduction in property expenses to SEK 4,378 million, attributed to cost-control measures, digitalisation, operating efficiencies, and the smaller portfolio following property sales. Utilities were an exception, increasing 5.7% because municipal price increases exceeded core inflation. Capital expenditures were SEK 4,439 million, while tenant-improvement investments generated SEK 94 million of additional rental income at an average yield on cost of 10.8%.

The group’s reported profit before tax rose to SEK 16,012 million from SEK 10,403 million, and profit for the year increased to SEK 13,056 million from SEK 7,621 million. The result benefited from a SEK 9,498 million fair-value gain on investment properties, compared with SEK 8,554 million in 2024, and realised property-divestment gains of SEK 2,207 million versus SEK 1,687 million. A SEK 3,996 million foreign-exchange gain also compared with a SEK 2,038 million loss in 2024. These gains were partly offset by a SEK 3,707 million goodwill impairment, primarily relating to Germany and Sweden, following higher discount rates in the Akelius goodwill impairment test. Total comprehensive income fell to SEK 4,199 million from SEK 11,082 million because currency translation differences shifted to a SEK 8,857 million loss from a SEK 3,461 million gain.

The privatisation programme remained central to capital allocation: 2,466 units were sold for SEK 10,664 million at a 28.9% premium to book value. Divestments reduced rental income by SEK 538 million but generated proceeds used to reduce debt. Interest-bearing liabilities declined to SEK 175,227 million from SEK 195,816 million, reducing net LTV to 52.2% from 56.4% and improving ICR to 2.0x from 1.9x. Financing activity remained substantial, including SEK 59,500 million raised or refinanced by Heimstaden Bostad and SEK 13,767 million of outstanding green bonds. Fitch affirmed Heimstaden’s B- rating but revised the outlook to Negative. No dividend was proposed for any share class.
