Aktsiaselts Infortar reported strong operational growth for the 2025 financial year, with consolidated revenue rising 33.9 percent to EUR 1,837 million compared to EUR 1,372 million in 2024. EBITDA increased by 56.9 percent to EUR 227.9 million, expanding the EBITDA margin to 12.4 percent from 10.6 percent in the prior year. Operating profit grew to EUR 109.0 million from EUR 77.0 million. Despite these top-line and operating improvements, net profit attributable to parent company owners declined by 63.1 percent to EUR 70.6 million from EUR 191.3 million in 2024. The report explicitly attributes this decrease to non-recurring one-off effects related to the initial consolidation of Tallink Grupp in 2024, which included substantial gains from bargain purchase and fair value adjustments that do not reappear in the current period.

The substantial growth in revenue and EBITDA is directly explained by the full twelve-month consolidation of Tallink Grupp, whereas its results were only partially consolidated from August onwards in 2024. Additionally, the Elenger energy segment contributed an approximate EUR 12 million increase in EBITDA, supported by expanded infrastructure operations and new acquisitions in Poland and Latvia. Infortar consolidated results also benefited from the inclusion of agriculture operations through the acquisition of Estonia Farmid and Halinga in 2025. Strategic diversification across maritime transport, energy, real estate, and supporting sectors has yielded more stable cash flows, as highlighted in the CEO letter.

Financial position strengthened further, with total equity rising slightly to EUR 1,175 million and the equity ratio improving to 45 percent from 43 percent. Interest-bearing liabilities relative to equity decreased from 104.9 percent to 91 percent, while the net debt to EBITDA ratio improved from 4.0 to 3.7, reflecting active loan repayments and disciplined capital management. Macroeconomic conditions, including geopolitical tensions, trade policy uncertainties, and volatile European gas prices driven by weather patterns and storage levels, created a challenging operating environment. Nevertheless, regulatory frameworks such as the EU Emissions Trading System and FuelEU Maritime continue to influence cost structures, particularly in the maritime segment. The Board proposed a dividend of EUR 3 per share, amounting to approximately EUR 62 million, maintaining the company commitment to consistent shareholder returns. Overall, 2025 demonstrated robust operational scaling and strategic portfolio integration, offsetting high one-time gains recorded during the transformative Tallink acquisition in the prior year.
