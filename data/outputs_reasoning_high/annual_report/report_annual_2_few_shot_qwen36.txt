The Infortar Group reported significantly higher revenue and earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization for the year ended 31 December 2025, driven primarily by the full-year consolidation of its maritime transport subsidiary, Tallink Grupp, which gained controlling influence in August 2024. Consolidated revenue reached EUR 1,837.0 million, up from EUR 1,371.8 million in 2024, while EBITDA increased substantially to EUR 227.9 million compared with EUR 145.3 million the previous year. The operating profit grew to EUR 109.0 million from EUR 77.0 million. However, net profit declined to EUR 69.9 million from EUR 193.7 million, a reduction the company attributes mainly to the absence of non-recurring one-off gains related to the initial Tallink acquisition recorded in 2024, alongside higher operational costs from comprehensive consolidation.

The group total assets stood at EUR 2,596.6 million, with total equity rising marginally to EUR 1,175.4 million and the equity ratio improving to 45 percent. Interest-bearing liabilities were reduced, bringing the net debt-to-EBITDA ratio down to 3.7 times. Operating cash flows remained robust, supported by strong liquidity management and centralized cash pooling across the group European operations. 

Performance across business segments reflected both market dynamics and strategic developments. The maritime transport segment benefited from vessel chartering arrangements that mitigated seasonal fluctuations, though passenger volumes saw minor declines amid broader economic uncertainty and geopolitical pressures affecting demand. The energy division faced a 9 percent drop in regional gas consumption and continued commodity price volatility, yet successfully expanded its renewable portfolio through new solar generation facilities, battery storage investments, and biomethane production initiatives, including the acquisition of agricultural assets to integrate circular bioeconomy practices. Real estate activities delivered steady rental income while progressing major infrastructure projects, such as logistics centres and rail network expansions.

Throughout the reporting period, the company maintained a disciplined capital allocation strategy, proposing a dividend of EUR 3 per share totaling EUR 62 million. Management emphasized a shift toward building a diversified, crisis-resilient portfolio capable of generating stable cash flows despite moderate macroeconomic growth, persistent trade uncertainties, and evolving regulatory environments regarding emissions and energy transitions. The overall financial trajectory demonstrates improved top-line momentum and stronger asset coverage, even as bottom-line results normalized after an acquisition-heavy prior year.
