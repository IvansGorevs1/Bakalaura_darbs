Nordic Fibreboard AS’s financial performance weakened in 2025. Consolidated revenue declined 3.4% to €7.328 million from €7.592 million in 2024, with virtually all revenue generated by fibreboard production and sales. Sales to the European Union, which represented 92% of total revenue, remained relatively stable, while sales to Africa, Asia and the Middle East decreased. The company reported that weak domestic demand, limited investment activity and continued uncertainty in the construction and industrial markets constrained performance.

Profitability deteriorated significantly. EBITDA fell to a loss of €420 thousand from a loss of €25 thousand, reducing the EBITDA margin to negative 6% from 0%. The fibreboard segment moved from positive EBITDA of €26 thousand to negative €296 thousand, primarily because production capacity was underutilized and production costs increased. The planned factory shutdown in December to transition to a new gas boiler house also reduced EBITDA by approximately €0.2 million, as personnel, maintenance and repair costs continued during the stoppage. Operating loss increased to €940 thousand from €547 thousand, while net loss widened to €1.154 million from €786 thousand. The operating margin declined to negative 13% from negative 7%, and the net margin to negative 16% from negative 10%.

Although cost of goods sold decreased to €6.725 million from €6.820 million, gross profit fell to €603 thousand from €771 thousand. Distribution costs increased to €982 thousand from €935 thousand and administrative expenses rose to €502 thousand from €381 thousand. Labour expenses increased to €1.931 million from €1.630 million despite the workforce decreasing to 60 employees from 68 at year-end 2024. Net financial costs were €214 thousand, compared with €238 thousand previously.

The Group’s total assets increased to €9.323 million from €8.252 million, supported by higher inventories, fixed assets and investment property. Capital expenditure was substantial, with €1.367 million invested in production assets, mainly the new gas boiler house, and €161 thousand invested in the Admirali Quarter property project. Equity increased to €4.462 million from €3.616 million following a €2 million share issue, partly offset by the annual loss. Borrowings declined to €3.168 million from €3.724 million, but payables rose sharply to €1.553 million from €788 thousand. Cash fell to €5 thousand from €53 thousand.

Operating cash flow was negative €503 thousand, compared with negative €174 thousand in 2024. Investing activities used €989 thousand, while financing activities generated €1.444 million, including the share issue and related-party financing. The Group ended the year with negative working capital of €2.7 million and all three loan covenants unfulfilled, resulting in €604 thousand of long-term Coop Pank debt being classified as current. Management plans to address working-capital pressure through property sales. A preliminary agreement to sell ten Admirali Quarter properties was signed in January 2026, while the gas boiler project was completed later that month.
