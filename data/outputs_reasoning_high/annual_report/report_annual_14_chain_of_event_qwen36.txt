Lyko Group reported net revenue of 3,963 million Swedish kronor for 2025, an 11.3 percent increase from 3,562 million in 2024. While sales expanded, profitability indicators declined. Gross profit reached 1,659 million, but the gross margin contracted from 44.0 percent to 41.9 percent. Operating profit fell from 117.4 million to 78.2 million, reducing the EBIT margin from 3.3 percent to 2.0 percent. This resulted in a net loss of 3.0 million compared to a 21.0 million profit previously, lowering earnings per share to minus 0.20 SEK. Conversely, cash flow from operating activities surged to 527.5 million from 209.7 million, driven by efficient working capital management within the dominant Nordic segment, which comprised 93.4 percent of revenue. The company reported net debt of 292.5 million excluding IFRS 16 leases and maintained an equity ratio of 14.8 percent.

These financial movements stem directly from strategic infrastructure projects and prevailing market dynamics. Lyko commissioned its new automated warehouse in Vansbro, a 500 million SEK investment designed to handle 100,000 orders daily. Management explicitly states that implementing this automation impacted results during the year. Simultaneously, the company opened its first Fab Store in Täby, alongside locations in Norway and Finland, while increasing depreciation and amortization to 174.1 million. Financing these expansions required 270.3 million in new loans, elevating financial expenses to 82.7 million and pressuring the bottom line. The beauty sector faced intensifying competition and structural shifts, leading Lyko to initiate a late-year reorganization centralizing European operations to lower overhead.

To navigate temporary margin compression, Lyko deferred dividends to prioritize reinvestment, aligning with its medium-term target of 15 to 20 percent annual revenue growth and a long-term 10 percent EBITDA margin before IFRS 16. The firm bolstered its own brand portfolio through initiatives like the Make Up Store relaunch to improve future margins. Post-reporting, Lyko announced a cost savings program targeting 100 million SEK annually across approximately 70 positions, backed by 28 million SEK in committed facility extensions until 2028. The reporting period thus reflects a deliberate scaling phase where elevated capital expenditure and financing costs temporarily suppressed accounting profitability while securing the operational capacity and positive cash generation required to sustain competitive advantage and pursue targeted efficiency improvements.
