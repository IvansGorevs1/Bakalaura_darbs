AS Merko Ehitus’ performance in 2025 was affected by the completion of several large construction projects and continued uncertainty in the Baltic economies. Consolidated revenue declined 42.3% to EUR 310.9 million from EUR 539.0 million in 2024. Operating profit fell 52.6% to EUR 34.3 million, while EBITDA decreased to EUR 37.5 million from EUR 75.7 million. The operating profit margin narrowed to 11.0% from 13.4%, reflecting the completion of particularly successful contracts and a return to more typical sector margins in a highly competitive market.

Gross profit decreased to EUR 55.9 million, but the gross margin improved slightly to 18.0% from 17.8%. Profit before tax declined to EUR 44.8 million from EUR 76.4 million. Net profit attributable to the parent’s shareholders was EUR 39.9 million, compared with EUR 64.7 million in 2024, while the net margin increased to 12.8% from 12.0%, partly reflecting lower finance costs and income tax expense. Profit from joint ventures increased to EUR 10.4 million from EUR 10.0 million; the Connecto investment contributed approximately one quarter of consolidated net profit.

Construction services remained the main business, but revenue fell 49.9% to EUR 237.6 million and operating profit declined 56.8% to EUR 25.7 million. Its share of group revenue decreased to 76.4% from 88.0%, and the operating margin fell to 10.8% from 12.5%. Private-sector demand remained weak, while public-sector spending increasingly focused on defence and infrastructure. Renewable-energy activity declined compared with the previous period, although Lithuania and Latvia recorded stronger market activity than Estonia.

Real estate development provided some offset. Segment revenue increased 13.8% to EUR 73.4 million, including EUR 67.8 million from apartment sales, as 358 apartments were sold versus 323 in 2024. However, operating profit fell 18.8% to EUR 13.4 million and the margin declined to 18.2% from 25.5%, including the effect of a write-down of a Norwegian property. The group invested EUR 56.2 million in development projects, up from EUR 41.6 million, and began construction of more than three times as many new apartments as in 2024.

New construction contracts signed rose to EUR 362.8 million from EUR 338.0 million, lifting the secured order book 37.1% to EUR 466.9 million at year-end. Major awards included Rail Baltica, defence, hotel, office and renewable-energy projects. Cash flow was affected by development investment and inventory growth: operating cash flow was negative EUR 1.7 million and cash and cash equivalents fell to EUR 41.4 million. Nevertheless, equity increased to EUR 260.6 million and the equity ratio strengthened to 62.8% from 56.9%, with interest-bearing liabilities remaining moderate at 8.0% of assets. The board proposed a EUR 1.25-per-share dividend, totalling EUR 22.1 million, equivalent to a 55% payout ratio.
