AS Tallinna Sadam Group’s 2025 performance improved overall despite a slight decline in revenue. Consolidated revenue decreased 0.8% to EUR 118.7 million from EUR 119.6 million in 2024, mainly because icebreaker Botnica charter revenue fell by EUR 4.0 million, or 34%, to EUR 7.9 million. This reflected 42 fewer charter days and a lower project-based charter rate. Growth in vessel dues, lease income, passenger fees, cargo charges and ferry-service revenue partly offset the decline.

Operating expenses fell by EUR 4.0 million, or 10%, to EUR 36.4 million, primarily due to lower technical maintenance and repair costs, including reduced Botnica-related costs and EUR 0.9 million of insurance indemnity. Fuel, electricity and lease expenses also decreased, while personnel expenses increased 3% to EUR 26.6 million despite the average number of employees falling from 438 to 425. As expenses declined more than revenue, operating profit increased 12.2% to EUR 32.7 million, and the operating margin rose from 24.4% to 27.6%. Adjusted EBITDA grew 6.4% to EUR 56.5 million, lifting the margin from 44.4% to 47.6%.

Profit before income tax increased 25% to EUR 27.9 million. Financial costs fell 30% to EUR 5.8 million because of lower interest rates and reduced borrowings. However, the associate Green Marine moved from a EUR 0.5 million profit to a EUR 26 thousand loss, and higher dividend taxation contributed to a smaller increase in net profit. Profit for the year rose 17.3% to EUR 22.5 million, while earnings per share increased from EUR 0.07 to EUR 0.09.

Operating volumes generally recovered. Cargo volume increased 5.1% to 13.8 million tonnes, driven primarily by a 47% increase in liquid bulk and higher dry-bulk volumes; ro-ro and container volumes declined slightly. Passenger numbers rose 1.0% to 8.3 million, supported by growth on the Tallinn–Helsinki route and a 25% increase in cruise passengers. Ferry operations served 2.5 million passengers and 1.2 million vehicles, although voyages declined 0.6%. The cargo recovery occurred amid continuing effects from sanctions on Russia, while cruise traffic remained affected by the absence of St Petersburg as a Baltic destination.

The Passenger and Cargo harbours segments strengthened, with adjusted EBITDA rising 5% and 19%, respectively. Ferry EBITDA declined 2%, while the Other segment remained broadly stable at EBITDA level despite lower Botnica revenue. The Group invested EUR 33.2 million, down 14% year on year, including completion of the new multifunctional quay at Paldiski South Harbour. Operating cash flow increased to EUR 53.7 million, cash ended at EUR 32.0 million, and net debt fell 15.5% to EUR 141.7 million. Shareholders received EUR 19.2 million in dividends, or EUR 0.073 per share.
