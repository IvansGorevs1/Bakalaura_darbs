TKM Grupp AS reported weaker financial performance in 2025 amid a challenging economic environment. Revenue declined 2.6% to €919.6 million from €944.6 million, while profit before tax fell 31.4% to €24.3 million. Net profit decreased 36.1% to €17.5 million, reducing earnings per share from €0.67 to €0.43. Operating profit declined from €47.6 million to €34.9 million, and EBITDA fell 14.7% to €77.4 million. The EBITDA margin decreased to 8.4% from 9.6%, while the net margin fell to 2% from 3%. Gross profit declined to €254.6 million, although the gross margin improved slightly to 27.7%.

The main pressure came from the car trade segment. Revenue fell 11.9% to €176.9 million, EBITDA declined 40.3% to €8.1 million, and net profit dropped 54.4% to €4.3 million. The introduction of Estonia’s motor vehicle tax, weaker consumer confidence and demand brought forward into late 2024 contributed to a 48.6% decline in new passenger car sales in Estonia. More stable markets in Latvia and Lithuania only partially offset the weakness. The supermarket segment delivered broadly stable revenue of €611.9 million, up 0.3%, but its net profit declined 38.6% to €9.5 million as lower sales volumes, promotional pricing, wage pressure and refurbishment costs affected margins. Department stores recorded a €0.5 million net loss, while security services recorded a €0.04 million loss. Real estate revenue increased 5.1% to €7.7 million and net profit rose 20.5% to €6.3 million, although the prior year benefited from €4.0 million of one-off gains from property revaluations and disposals.

Cost control partly mitigated the decline in sales. Staff costs increased 3.7%, while other operating expenses rose only 0.2%. Lower borrowing interest reduced finance costs by €1.5 million, and income tax expense fell by €1.2 million. Operating cash flow remained strong at €65.6 million, compared with €73.1 million in 2024. Total investments decreased to €25.5 million from €36.6 million, mainly funding real estate projects, new car facilities, store refurbishments and digital systems. Financing outflows, including dividends, debt repayments and lease payments, resulted in a €15.9 million decline in cash, leaving €29.5 million at year-end.

Net debt increased to €291.0 million from €278.9 million, while equity rose modestly to €263.8 million, supported partly by an €11.2 million property revaluation. Current liabilities increased because €32.1 million of loans maturing in 2026 were reclassified as current; refinancing had begun, and management reported no liquidity issues. The Group paid €26.5 million in dividends, or €0.65 per share, and proposed a 2025 dividend of €24.4 million, or €0.60 per share. In January 2026, it agreed to acquire three companies involved in Škoda retail and servicing, subject to conditions, with completion expected between February and June 2026. PwC issued an unqualified audit opinion on the consolidated financial statements.
