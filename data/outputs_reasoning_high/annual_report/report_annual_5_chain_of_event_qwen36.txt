Eleving Group delivered its strongest annual performance to date in 2025, achieving record revenue of EUR 250.0 million, representing a 15.5 percent year-over-year increase. The net loan portfolio expanded by 20.2 percent to EUR 446.3 million, while adjusted EBITDA reached a twelve-month high of EUR 101.9 million, up 13.5 percent. This growth was primarily driven by robust demand for flexible vehicle financing, which grew 25.9 percent, and consumer lending, which increased 18.0 percent. The introduction of new product lines further diversified revenue streams, with smartphone financing generating EUR 7.9 million upon launch in Kenya and Uganda, and installment loans contributing positively across European markets. Conversely, traditional vehicle financing declined by 6.0 percent. Total loan issuances surged by 24.3 percent to EUR 458.0 million, reflecting strengthened customer acquisition and effective use of digital channels and partner networks.

Despite improvements in operating metrics, total net profit remained stable at EUR 29.2 million, largely due to substantial foreign exchange translation losses amounting to EUR 11.7 million, which contrasted sharply with a EUR 3.7 million gain in the prior year. Elevated write-offs of long-overdue balances increased impairment expenses, though the group maintained disciplined underwriting standards and controlled default rates relative to portfolio growth. On the capital markets front, the company successfully executed two major bond transactions, including a record EUR 275 million issuance that refinanced maturing obligations and funded expansion. These activities increased total borrowings and resulted in a net leverage ratio of 3.8 times, compared to 3.3 times in 2024, while the capitalization ratio decreased to 23.7 percent. Nevertheless, the interest coverage ratio remained adequate at 2.3 times, and all financial covenants were met. The group also maintained a progressive dividend policy, distributing EUR 19.65 million during the year, equivalent to a 10 percent cash yield based on the IPO price. Geographic diversification continued to support resilience, with operations spanning seventeen countries across Europe, Africa, and Asia, while strategic investments in automation, including pilot deployments of artificial intelligence voice agents at call centers, laid the groundwork for improved operational efficiency and sustained profitable growth in the coming year.
