AAC Clyde Space reported a decline in top-line revenue and profitability during the 2025 financial year, although underlying structural adjustments and strong growth in its data services division partially offset the negative developments. Consolidated net sales fell by 16% to SEK 295.3 million, primarily driven by lower deliveries in the Products & Missions segment, which dropped to SEK 245 million from SEK 346 million in the previous year. Conversely, the newly established Data & Services segment achieved an 80% increase in net sales to SEK 80 million, reflecting the company strategic pivot toward recurring satellite data revenues. Operating earnings weakened significantly, with EBITDA declining to SEK 17.5 million from SEK 46.7 million, and the group recorded an operating loss of SEK 40.0 million alongside a net loss of SEK 46.6 million. Negative exchange-rate movements and elevated depreciation charges further weighed on the bottom line, resulting in earnings per share of minus 7.63 SEK.

The company reorganized its operations into two reporting segments in the second quarter to better align hardware development and data service delivery. However, execution faced headwinds as technical discussions and procurement postponements in key government and commercial programmes shifted approximately SEK 32 million of recognized revenue into 2026, creating notable pressure on working capital and reducing operating cash flow to a negative SEK 73 million. To defend its financial flexibility, management completed two directed share issues throughout the year, including a major strategic investment from Bonnier Capital that strengthened institutional ownership and secured funding for upcoming constellation launches. This capital infusion helped raise the equity ratio to 80% from 71%, ensuring adequate liquidity to sustain ongoing R&D and mission delivery without reliance on drawn credit facilities.

Operational milestones remained consistent with long-term ambitions, highlighted by the full commissioning of the Sedna maritime satellites, successful deliveries of CubeCAT optical terminals, and the commencement of VIREON Earth Observation satellite production and launch preparations. Significantly, post-year-end developments substantially improved the outlook, with the award of a EUR 76.3 million instrument contract for the EPS-Sterna weather programme and the completion of Phase 1 of the ESA-backed INFLECION maritime intelligence initiative. Collectively, these wins expanded the order backlog to approximately SEK 1.1 billion as of early 2026. While the 2025 period was characterized by schedule-driven revenue deferrals and temporary cash flow constraints, the strengthened balance sheet, expanding service portfolio, and robust pipeline of institutional contracts position the company for pronounced revenue recognition growth in the coming years.
