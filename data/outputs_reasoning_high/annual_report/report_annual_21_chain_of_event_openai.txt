AS Tallinna Sadam Group’s 2025 performance improved materially despite slightly lower revenue. Revenue declined 0.8% to EUR 118.7 million, mainly because icebreaker Botnica charter revenue fell EUR 4.0 million, or 34%, to EUR 7.9 million. Botnica’s charter days decreased by 42, and its utilisation rate fell to 49%, as Baffinland Iron Mines Corporation did not exercise its charter option. However, revenue increased in all other segments, supported by higher vessel dues, indexed fees, stronger passenger activity and recovering cargo volumes.

Operating profit rose 12.2% to EUR 32.7 million, while adjusted EBITDA increased 6.4% to EUR 56.5 million. The adjusted EBITDA margin improved from 44.4% to 47.6%, as operating expenses related to the main operations fell by EUR 4.0 million, or 10%. Lower technical maintenance and repair costs, reduced fuel and electricity costs, lower lease expenses, and a EUR 0.8 million reduction in impairment losses on financial assets contributed to the improvement. Botnica’s costs also declined because of fewer repairs and charter days, and the Group received EUR 0.9 million of insurance compensation related to a prior technical failure.

Profit increased 17.3% to EUR 22.5 million, or EUR 0.09 per share. Profit before tax rose 25% to EUR 27.9 million, helped by a 30% reduction in net finance costs to EUR 4.8 million due to lower Euribor rates and reduced borrowings. The increase in net profit was partly limited by higher dividend income tax following Estonia’s increase in the tax rate and abolition of the lower rate for regular dividends. The associate Green Marine recorded a loss of EUR 26 thousand, compared with a EUR 0.5 million profit in 2024.

Passenger numbers increased 1.0% to 8.3 million, with growth on the Tallinn–Helsinki route and a 25% increase in cruise passengers. Cargo volume grew 5.1% to 13.8 million tonnes, led by a 47% increase in liquid bulk, particularly ligroin and gasoline, and by moderate dry-bulk growth. The report links this recovery to changed market conditions following the impact of sanctions against Russia and the broader economic downturn. Ferry traffic also grew in passengers and vehicles, although the Ferry segment’s adjusted EBITDA declined 2% because higher personnel, consulting and development costs outweighed lower fuel, maintenance and electricity expenses.

The Group invested EUR 33.2 million, 14.4% less than in 2024, primarily completing the new multifunctional quay and backup area at Paldiski South Harbour. Net debt fell 15.5% to EUR 141.7 million, while operating cash flow increased to EUR 53.7 million. EUR 19.2 million was paid in dividends, and the same EUR 0.073-per-share dividend was proposed for 2025. Management expects moderate volume growth in 2026 but warns that higher input and financing costs, together with the absence of 2025 one-off gains, may reduce operating and net profit. PwC issued an unmodified audit opinion.
