Gigasun reported strong operational expansion and improved profitability for 2025, posting a net loss narrowed significantly from the prior year despite currency headwinds and regional tariff adjustments. Total revenue increased by 9.8 percent to SEK 239.7 million, while net revenue rose 9.9 percent to SEK 203.2 million, driven by a 30 percent increase in electricity production to 328 million kilowatt hours. The company installed 55.7 megawatts of new capacity, bringing total revenue-generating assets to 395.8 megawatts across 216 sites. Gross profit grew to SEK 195.8 million, sustaining an 82 percent margin. Operating profit rose by 21.1 percent to SEK 73.5 million, lifting the EBIT margin to 31 percent.

The net loss contracted to SEK minus 7.2 million from SEK minus 32.9 million in 2024, reflecting stronger core earnings and active debt management. Interest expenses declined to SEK 76.9 million from SEK 84.2 million, explicitly resulting from a continuing refinancing program with Agricultural Bank Financial Leasing Co., Ltd. The new facilities carry a 3.60 percent interest rate and feature a ten-year amortization schedule replacing shorter-term leaseback agreements. This structural shift reduced borrowing costs and strengthened cash flow from current operations to SEK 101.1 million, despite record investments of SEK 169.9 million in new solar projects. Cash and bank balances fell to SEK 45.1 million as proceeds were deployed toward portfolio growth and loan repayments.

Top-line growth was tempered by external pricing and valuation pressures. Translation differences due to a weaker Swedish krona reduced revenue by approximately SEK 18.0 million and impacted the bottom line by SEK minus 3.8 million. Additionally, regulatory tariff revisions implemented by provincial authorities in Jiangsu and Anhui lowered average electricity prices by roughly 8 percent in the second half of the year. Management assesses the net impact on the existing portfolio at approximately a 3 to 4 percent revenue decline. Operating expenses grew 5.5 percent to SEK 166.2 million, primarily due to higher depreciation and maintenance obligations linked to the enlarged asset base, partially offset by the same currency translation benefits.

The group maintains its strategic objective of reaching 1,000 megawatts of installed capacity in the medium term. Declining hardware installation costs and cheaper long-term financing are projected to neutralize anticipated pricing compressions. Year-end signed orders totaled 16.4 megawatts, securing near-term project visibility. Overall, Gigasun demonstrated robust scaling capabilities and executed decisive financial restructuring that substantially improved underlying earnings strength ahead of continued market expansion in China.
