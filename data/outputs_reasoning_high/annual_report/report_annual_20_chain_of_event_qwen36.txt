In the financial year 2025, AS Tallink Grupp reported consolidated revenue of EUR 765.3 million, a decrease of 2.6 percent from EUR 785.8 million in the prior year. Core route operations revenue fell by EUR 13.7 million to EUR 625.2 million. The group carried 5.5 million passengers, marking a slight 0.9 percent decline, while transported cargo units dropped sharply by 19.2 percent to 245,004 and passenger vehicles decreased by 2.2 percent. These volume reductions contributed to a contracted gross profit of EUR 143.2 million, down from EUR 153.6 million, and a significantly lower EBITDA of EUR 130.1 million compared to EUR 175.2 million in 2024. Audited net profit declined by 57.1 percent to EUR 17.3 million, yielding earnings per share of EUR 0.023.

The deterioration in profitability was primarily driven by challenging macroeconomic conditions across the Baltic Sea region, including low consumer and business confidence, elevated inflation, and fiscal policy adjustments such as increased VAT and corporate income taxes. Weaker demand specifically impacted cargo transport and onboard retail and restaurant sales, where cost of goods sold fell to EUR 160.5 million due to reduced passenger footfall and constrained leisure spending. Personnel expenses rose to EUR 201.0 million, reflecting wage adjustments under the Estonian maritime collective agreement. Maintenance work lasting 68 days during the first quarter also negatively affected the Finland-Sweden segment results.

Several strategic actions and accounting adjustments partially mitigated the downward pressure on earnings. The group aligned the estimated useful lives of its cruise and passenger vessels to 45 years, which reduced depreciation and amortisation expenses by EUR 18.0 million to EUR 79.8 million. Additionally, the sale of three idle vessels throughout the year eliminated associated depreciation charges and generated disposal proceeds. The group maintained continuous vessel chartering, resulting in EUR 54.9 million in charter income, and implemented ongoing cost efficiency measures. On the financing side, total loan repayments and interest payments reached EUR 113.5 million, leading to a substantial reduction in interest-bearing liabilities to EUR 445.9 million. Consequently, net finance costs decreased by EUR 6.8 million to EUR 21.4 million, and net debt fell to EUR 432.4 million from EUR 537.7 million the previous year. Shareholder distributions were maintained at EUR 0.06 per share, totaling EUR 44.6 million plus related taxes of EUR 11.3 million. Capital expenditure amounted to EUR 33.0 million, primarily directed toward vessel maintenance and technical upgrades. The group secured a liquidity buffer of EUR 110.4 million at year-end, and the equity ratio improved to 56.4 percent. Looking forward, management emphasized continued fleet optimisation, sustained margin discipline, and preparations for transitioning shuttle vessels to liquified biomethane fuel in 2026 to address rising regulatory and energy cost pressures.
