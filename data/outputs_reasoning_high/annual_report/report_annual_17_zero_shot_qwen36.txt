Novaturas AB reported a financially challenging year for 2024, recording revenue of EUR 200.9 million, a 3.6 percent decline from EUR 208.3 million in 2023. The group served 239.5 thousand travelers across Lithuania, Latvia, and Estonia, reflecting a 7.6 percent drop in customer volume. Gross profit contracted by 20.9 percent to EUR 20.7 million, driving the gross profit margin down to 10.3 percent from 12.6 percent in the prior year. Operating expenses totalled EUR 27.5 million, largely due to a one-off, non-cash goodwill impairment of EUR 5.7 million allocated to the Estonian cash-generating unit. Excluding this exceptional charge, operating expenses decreased by 1 percent to EUR 21.8 million. As a result, the company shifted from a EUR 3.4 million net profit in 2023 to a net loss of EUR 7.6 million in 2024. Adjusted EBITDA turned negative at EUR (0.449) million compared to a positive EUR 5.2 million previously, while earnings per share fell to EUR (0.97) from EUR 0.43. Profitability ratios deteriorated consistently, with EBITDA and net profit margins slipping to (3.1) percent and (3.8) percent respectively. Financial leverage also strengthened significantly, as the debt-to-equity ratio climbed to 121.1 percent from 58.0 percent, and return on assets plunged to (19.3) percent from 7.1 percent.

The adverse financial performance was primarily caused by an intensified competitive environment and a regional oversupply of travel packages in the Baltic States, which triggered aggressive price wars, particularly for Turkish and Greek destinations. To counter these headwinds, management restructured the destination portfolio mid-year, deliberately phasing out unprofitable last-minute bookings and emphasizing higher-yield long-haul and corporate workation trips. These strategic adjustments accelerated a clear operational turnaround from September onward. Fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA recovered to EUR 2.3 million, and quarterly load factors improved to 98.9 percent compared to 96.7 percent in late 2023. Product mix shifts drove strong demand, highlighted by a 149 percent surge in flights to Spanish resorts, an 8 percent overall increase in long-haul passengers, and record participation in group and workation programmes.

Looking forward, Novaturas addressed liquidity constraints post-year-end by securing covenant waivers and arranging additional financing, including a EUR 2.5 million credit facility and multiple loans. Strategic realignment is further supported by the entry of Turkish investor Neset Kockar, who acquired 23.2 percent of shares in April 2025 with plans to reach 33.19 percent ownership by autumn. Leveraging his industry network and existing supplier contracts, management intends to optimize costs and expand digital direct-to-consumer channels. Based on current projections, the company expects to return to sustainable profitability in 2025, forecasting adjusted EBITDA in the range of EUR 2.0 million to EUR 3.3 million.
