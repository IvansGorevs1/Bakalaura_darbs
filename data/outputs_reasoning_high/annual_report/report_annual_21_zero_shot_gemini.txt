During the financial year ended 31 December 2025, AS Tallinna Sadam Group demonstrated resilient financial and operational performance driven by a recovery in cargo volumes and passenger numbers, despite a complex geopolitical and economic environment. Consolidated revenue was EUR 118.7 million, representing a minor decrease of 0.8% or EUR 0.9 million compared to 2024, primarily due to lower charter fees for the icebreaker Botnica resulting from fewer project charter days. Conversely, adjusted EBITDA grew by 6.4% to EUR 56.5 million, raising the adjusted EBITDA margin from 44.4% to 47.6%. Operating profit increased by 12.2% to EUR 32.7 million, and profit for the period rose by 17.3% to EUR 22.5 million. The growth in profitability was supported by a 10% reduction in operating expenses, down by EUR 4.0 million to EUR 36.4 million, achieved through lower asset repair costs following technical failures in the previous year and the receipt of EUR 0.9 million in insurance indemnities. Furthermore, financial expenses decreased by 30% to EUR 5.8 million due to falling Euribor rates and lower loan balances. Income tax expense increased by 73.3% to EUR 5.4 million due to a higher dividend tax rate. Capital investments totaled EUR 33.2 million, marking a 14.4% decrease from the prior year, with major expenditures directed toward the completion of the new multifunctional quay and back-up area at Paldiski South Harbour, as well as infrastructure updates at Muuga and Old City Harbour. Net debt improved significantly, decreasing by 15.5% to EUR 141.7 million, while total equity stood at EUR 380.9 million. Operational indicators showed positive momentum across core segments. Total cargo volume grew by 5.1% to 13.8 million tonnes, bolstered by a strong recovery in liquid bulk volumes which surged 47%, alongside moderate growth in dry bulk and a 75% increase in transit goods. The number of passengers across the Group harbours increased by 1.0% to nearly 8.3 million, supported by growth on the Tallinn to Helsinki route and a 25% surge in cruise passengers. The ferry subsidiary TS Laevad performed 23,050 trips, serving 2.5 million passengers and 1.2 million vehicles. Conversely, the icebreaker Botnica experienced a lower utilisation rate of 49% and 179 charter days compared to 221 days in 2024, though it successfully participated in a cargo ship rescue operation in Northern Canada. In line with its dividend policy, the management proposed a dividend distribution of EUR 0.073 per share, totaling EUR 19.199 million, matching the prior year's total payout.
