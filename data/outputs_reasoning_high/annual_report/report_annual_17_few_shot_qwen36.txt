Novaturas AB reported a significant deterioration in financial performance for the year ended 31 December 2024, recording a net loss of EUR 7.6 million compared to a net profit of EUR 3.4 million in the prior year. Revenue declined by 3.6% to EUR 200.9 million, while gross profit contracted sharply by 20.9% to EUR 20.7 million, reducing the gross profit margin to 10.3% from 12.6%. Operating expenses totaled EUR 27.5 million, heavily impacted by a EUR 5.7 million non-cash goodwill impairment in the Estonian cash-generating unit, driven by revised forecasts projecting a 5% annual decline in traveler volumes and a higher discount rate. Excluding this item, operating expenses decreased by 1% to EUR 21.8 million. Adjusted EBITDA turned negative at EUR 449 thousand against a positive EUR 5.2 million in 2023, reflecting sustained margin compression across core operations.

The results were primarily shaped by an oversupplied and highly competitive Baltic travel market, which triggered intense price reductions, particularly for Turkey and Greek island destinations. Total passenger traffic across Lithuania, Latvia, and Estonia fell by 7.6% to 239,000 customers. In response, management executed targeted optimizations late in the reporting period, successfully raising the fourth-quarter seat load factor to 98.9% and significantly reducing lower-margin last-minute sales. Demand patterns shifted notably toward Spain, where flight package sales surged by 149% compared to 2022, while long-haul routes expanded steadily, led by strong growth to Vietnam and Thailand. Distribution channels also realigned, with traditional travel agencies gaining market share at the expense of direct digital sales.

To stabilize capital structure and fund future operations, the company engaged a strategic investor, Turkish tourism businessman Neset Kockar, who initially acquired 23.2% of shares in April 2025 with plans to increase his holding to 33.19% after regulatory approval. Concurrently, Novaturas secured additional financing, including a EUR 2.5 million facility from SME Bank and EUR 2 million in shareholder loans, while obtaining necessary covenant waivers from existing lenders. Management anticipates a steady recovery in 2025, expecting adjusted EBITDA to recover into the EUR 2 million to EUR 3.3 million range as new partnerships drive operational synergies and efficiency improvements gain traction.
