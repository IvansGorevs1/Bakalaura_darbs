For the reporting year ended 31 December 2025, AS Tallinna Vesi delivered robust financial results across all key performance indicators. Group sales reached €72.48 million, representing an eleven-point-four percent increase compared to 2024, primarily driven by higher consumption volumes and sequential tariff adjustments that took effect throughout 2024 and early 2025. Water services revenue grew by six-point-nine percent to €62.31 million, while construction services revenue surged sixty-two percent to €8.46 million following increased contract turnover. The group posted gross profit of €29.52 million and operating profit of €21.22 million, both reflecting double-digit year-on-year growth of ten-point-five and eleven-point-eight percent respectively. Profit before taxes rose eighteen percent to €17.43 million, and net profit increased seven-point-two percent to €14.24 million. Earnings per share climbed to €0.71, up from €0.66 in the prior year. Despite the positive trajectory, the net profit margin experienced a slight contraction to nineteen-point-sixty-nine percent from twenty-point-four-one percent, mainly attributed to a higher income tax rate applied to dividend distributions.

Cost of goods and services sold increased twelve percent to €42.96 million. Total direct production costs remained largely stable, benefiting from a six-point-nine percent reduction in electricity expenses, which resulted from the combined heat and power plant at the Paljassaare Wastewater Treatment Plant reaching full operational capacity. This facility now supplies all required heating and over half of the electricity for treatment processes, directly lowering purchased power volumes. Conversely, staff costs rose six-point-eight percent and construction service costs jumped seventy-four-point-five percent, contributing to the overall expense growth. Depreciation and amortisation also increased by eleven-point-nine percent in line with expanded asset bases. Net financial expenses slightly declined to €3.78 million due to lower prevailing Euribor reference rates.

The company’s strategic expansion continued with heavy capital expenditure amounting to €55.74 million in fixed assets, funded in part by a syndicated loan drawdown and a new loan agreement with the Nordic Investment Bank. Total outstanding loans reached €160 million, pushing the debt to total assets ratio to sixty-six-point-zero-two percent from sixty-one-point-seven-one percent at year-end. Despite higher leverage, liquidity remained solid with cash balances growing to €11.29 million and trade receivable collectability maintaining a high ninety-nine-point-sixty-eight percent rate. A statutory obligation to harmonize water prices for private and business customers drove an eighteen-point-three percent surge in private customer sales, offsetting a two-point-seven percent decline in commercial billing. Additionally, a regulatory pricing reform redirected combined sewer revenues into wastewater collection lines, structurally altering segment reporting. Throughout the period, the organization maintained uninterrupted service delivery, successfully managing infrastructure upgrades and meeting established operational continuity targets without material disruptions.
