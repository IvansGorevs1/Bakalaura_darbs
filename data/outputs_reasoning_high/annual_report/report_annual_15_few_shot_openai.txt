AS Merko Ehitus reported a weaker consolidated operating performance in 2025, primarily because several large construction projects completed during the year and were not immediately replaced by projects of comparable scale. Revenue declined 42.3% to EUR 310.9 million from EUR 539.0 million in 2024. Gross profit fell to EUR 55.9 million from EUR 95.9 million, although the gross margin improved slightly to 18.0% from 17.8%. Operating profit decreased to EUR 34.3 million from EUR 72.5 million, and the operating margin narrowed to 11.0% from 13.4%, reflecting the completion of particularly successful contracts and a return to more typical sector margins in a competitive market.

Profit before tax was EUR 44.8 million, compared with EUR 76.4 million in 2024, while net profit attributable to the parent’s shareholders fell to EUR 39.9 million from EUR 64.7 million. Despite the lower earnings, the pre-tax margin increased to 14.4% from 14.2% and the net margin to 12.8% from 12.0%, supported by lower finance costs, lower income tax expense and EUR 10.4 million of profit from joint ventures. The Connecto investment was a significant contributor, accounting for approximately one quarter of consolidated net profit according to management. Earnings per share declined to EUR 2.26 from EUR 3.65.

Construction services remained the main business, but revenue fell 49.9% to EUR 237.6 million and operating profit declined 56.8% to EUR 25.7 million. The segment’s operating margin decreased to 10.8% from 12.5%. Real estate development performed better in terms of sales: revenue rose 13.8% to EUR 73.4 million, including EUR 67.8 million from apartment sales, and 358 apartments were sold compared with 323 in 2024. However, operating profit decreased to EUR 13.4 million from EUR 16.5 million, with the margin falling to 18.2% from 25.5%, partly due to a write-down of property in Norway.

The group’s financial position remained strong. Equity increased to EUR 260.6 million, while the equity ratio improved to 62.8% from 56.9%. Interest-bearing liabilities were broadly stable at EUR 33.1 million, and the debt ratio remained low at 8.0%. Cash and cash equivalents fell to EUR 41.4 million from EUR 91.9 million, reflecting negative operating cash flow of EUR 1.7 million, investment outflows, higher development inventories and EUR 33.6 million of dividends paid. Inventories increased to EUR 219.8 million as apartment construction expanded.

Future workload improved materially. The secured order book rose to EUR 466.9 million from EUR 340.6 million, while new construction contracts signed increased to EUR 362.8 million. Major Rail Baltica, defence, hotel, office and renewable-energy contracts are expected to support revenue in future periods. Management also expects the Rūdninkai defence-campus PPP project, signed in early 2026, to provide a stable workload from 2026 onward. The board proposes a EUR 1.25-per-share dividend, representing a 55% payout ratio.
