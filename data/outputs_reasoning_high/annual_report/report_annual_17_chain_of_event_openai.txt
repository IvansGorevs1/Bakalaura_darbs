Novaturas Group’s audited 2024 revenue was EUR 200.9 million, down 3.6% from the restated 2023 figure of EUR 208.3 million, while passengers served decreased 7.6% to 239.5 thousand. Flight-package revenue, the Group’s main product, fell to EUR 170.7 million from EUR 182.8 million, partly offset by a 21.3% increase in other sales to EUR 25.4 million. Gross profit declined 20.9% to EUR 20.7 million, reducing the gross margin to 10.3% from 12.6%.

Profitability deteriorated substantially. EBITDA declined from a positive EUR 5.2 million in 2023 to negative EUR 6.1 million, while adjusted EBITDA, excluding the goodwill impairment, fell from positive EUR 5.2 million to negative EUR 449 thousand. The Group reported an operating loss of EUR 6.6 million and a net loss of EUR 7.6 million, compared with a restated 2023 net profit of EUR 3.4 million. Basic loss per share was EUR 0.98, compared with earnings of EUR 0.43 in 2023. The result included a one-off, non-cash goodwill impairment charge of EUR 5.7 million relating to the Estonian cash-generating unit; excluding this item, the net loss was EUR 1.9 million. Operating expenses rose to EUR 27.5 million, primarily because of the impairment; excluding it, expenses were EUR 21.8 million, approximately 1% below 2023. Net finance expenses also increased to EUR 1.2 million from EUR 0.5 million.

Management attributed the annual deterioration mainly to increased competition and oversupply of travel capacity in the Baltic States, which caused intense price competition, particularly in Turkey and the Greek islands. In response, the Group optimized its travel program and adjusted its offering to customer preferences. This contributed to a marked improvement in the latter part of the year: adjusted EBITDA was negative EUR 1.6 million in January–June but positive EUR 2.3 million in September–December. Fourth-quarter load factor increased to 98.9% from 96.7%, and lower reliance on last-minute sales supported profitable fourth-quarter operations. Growth in Spain, long-haul travel and group and workation trips also broadened demand.

Financial pressure remained significant. Operating cash flow turned negative at EUR 1.6 million, compared with positive EUR 3.0 million in 2023, and cash fell to EUR 394 thousand from EUR 3.3 million. Current liabilities exceeded current assets by EUR 12.5 million; the Group’s liquidity ratio declined to 0.51 from 0.65, while its debt-to-equity ratio rose to 121.1% from 58.0%. The Group breached financing covenants at year-end but received waivers. Management concluded that going concern remained appropriate, supported by planned operational improvements, expected 2025 EBITDA of EUR 2–3.3 million, new financing, and the post-year-end acquisition of a 23.2% stake by Turkish tourism investor Neset Kockar, who also provided EUR 2 million in loans.
