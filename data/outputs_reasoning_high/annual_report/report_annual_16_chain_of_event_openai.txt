Nordic Fibreboard AS reported a difficult 2025, with revenue declining 3.4% to €7.328 million from €7.592 million in 2024. Fibreboard production and sales generated €7.325 million, down from €7.567 million, while real-estate management revenue fell to €3 thousand from €25 thousand following the termination of rental agreements in August 2024. Sales to the European Union, representing 92% of revenue, remained relatively stable, but sales to Africa, Asia and the Middle East declined.

Profitability deteriorated substantially. EBITDA fell to a loss of €420 thousand from a loss of €25 thousand, reducing the EBITDA margin from 0% to negative 6%. The fibreboard segment moved from EBITDA of €26 thousand to negative €296 thousand, while the real-estate segment’s EBITDA loss widened from €48 thousand to €86 thousand. Gross profit declined to €603 thousand from €771 thousand, despite cost of goods sold decreasing modestly to €6.725 million from €6.820 million. Distribution costs increased to €982 thousand from €935 thousand and administrative expenses to €502 thousand from €381 thousand. Other operating expenses also rose to €78 thousand from €11 thousand, including €40 thousand of penalties and €18 thousand from fixed-asset write-offs. Consequently, the operating loss increased to €940 thousand from €547 thousand, and the net loss widened to €1.154 million from €786 thousand. Loss per share improved to €0.14 from €0.17 because the share count increased from 4.499 million to 8.499 million.

Management attributed the negative EBITDA primarily to underutilized production capacity, which increased the cost of internally produced goods, and to the December factory shutdown. The 2025 shutdown was planned to allow the transition to a new gas boiler house, whereas the 2024 shutdown resulted from weather-related structural damage. Personnel, maintenance and repair costs continued during the shutdown and had an approximately €0.2 million negative impact on fibreboard EBITDA. The company also reported weak domestic demand and limited willingness by households and businesses to invest amid modest Estonian economic growth, inflation of around 6%, and challenging construction-market conditions.

Operating cash flow deteriorated to a €503 thousand outflow from a €174 thousand outflow in 2024. Investing cash outflow increased to €989 thousand, including €1.367 million invested in production assets and €161 thousand in real estate. Financing generated €1.444 million, supported by a €2 million share issue completed in October and related-party financing. Cash nevertheless declined to €5 thousand from €53 thousand. Negative working capital increased to €2.7 million from €0.7 million; management plans to address this through the proposed sale of ten Admirali Quarter properties, for which a preliminary agreement was signed in January 2026. The Admirali Quarter was transferred to the parent through a 2025 merger, while permits and design work for the development were completed.
