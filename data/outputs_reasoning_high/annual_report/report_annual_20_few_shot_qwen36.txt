The company reported a decline in both top-line revenue and profitability during the 2025 financial year compared with the previous period. Consolidated revenue amounted to EUR 765.3 million, representing a 2.6% decrease from EUR 785.8 million in 2024, driven primarily by a contraction in core route operations where revenue fell by EUR 13.7 million to EUR 625.2 million. Operational volumes reflected market softness, with total passengers carried dropping 0.9% to 5.5 million and cargo units declining sharply by 19.2% to 245,004 units. Earnings metrics weakened accordingly, as EBITDA fell to EUR 130.1 million from EUR 175.2 million, and net profit contracted significantly to EUR 17.3 million from EUR 40.3 million, resulting in an earnings per share of EUR 0.02. Margin compression was evident across key indicators, with gross margin narrowing to 18.7% and net profit margin settling at 2.3%.

The deterioration in financial performance was largely attributable to subdued consumer and business confidence amid ongoing geopolitical tensions and macroeconomic uncertainty in the Group’s core Baltic markets. These conditions restrained leisure spending and reduced onboard retail and restaurant sales, while weaker freight activity lowered cargo transport revenues. Despite these headwinds, the Group benefited from accounting adjustments that positively impacted operating results. The depreciation and amortisation charge decreased by EUR 18.0 million to EUR 79.8 million following the extension of estimated useful lives for cruise and passenger vessels to 45 years and the disposal of three older vessels. Additionally, net finance costs declined by EUR 6.8 million to EUR 21.4 million thanks to lower outstanding loan balances and falling interest rates. Personnel expenses rose modestly to EUR 201.0 million, reflecting collective agreement-driven wage increases, while fuel and emission costs remained relatively stable at EUR 96.5 million.

Financial positioning and liquidity management strengthened during the reporting period. Proceeds from the sale of three vessels contributed EUR 78.8 million to investing cash flows, supporting a substantial reduction in net debt to EUR 432.4 million from EUR 537.7 million a year earlier. The equity ratio improved to 56.4% from 53.4%, while the total liquidity buffer stood at EUR 110.4 million. Operating cash flow remained positive at EUR 127.1 million, though it was partially offset by dividend distributions of EUR 44.6 million and related income taxes of EUR 11.3 million, alongside EUR 113.5 million in loan principal and interest repayments. Strategic initiatives included optimising fleet utilisation through additional vessel charters, maintaining strict cost discipline, and advancing environmental compliance by preparing to transition shuttle vessels from liquefied natural gas to certified liquefied biomethane. Looking ahead, management anticipates continued volatility linked to geopolitical developments, fluctuating energy prices, and restrictive monetary conditions, while reaffirming a commitment to operational resilience, asset efficiency, and sustainable long-term competitiveness.
