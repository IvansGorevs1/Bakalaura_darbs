The Group reported resilient financial performance during the 2025 financial year, characterized by stabilized revenues alongside notable improvements in profitability and operational efficiency. Consolidated revenue reached EUR 118.7 million, representing a marginal decline of 0.8 percent compared to the previous year. This slight contraction was primarily driven by a significant drop in charter fees from the multifunctional icebreaker Botnica, which saw its utilization rate fall to 49 percent, outweighing revenue growth across the port operations and domestic ferry segments. Despite the top-line dip, adjusted EBITDA expanded by 6.4 percent to EUR 56.5 million, reflecting an improved margin of 47.6 percent against 44.4 percent a year earlier. Operating profit surged by 12.2 percent to EUR 32.7 million, driven by a 10 percent reduction in operating expenses that more than offset the modest revenue decline. Cost savings were largely attributable to lower maintenance and repair expenditures following a technical failure experienced in 2024, as well as decreased fuel and lease expenses related to reduced icebreaker activity. These efficiencies, combined with a 30 percent decline in finance costs due to falling interest rates and debt repayment, supported a strong bottom line. Net profit grew by 17.3 percent to EUR 22.5 million, although the absolute increase was moderated by the abolition of the preferential dividend income tax rate.

Operational volumes demonstrated steady recovery across core business lines. Cargo throughput increased by 5.1 percent to 13.8 million tonnes, bolstered by robust growth in liquid bulk handling and a 5 percent rise in dry bulk, which reflected successful adaptation to shifting trade routes and sanctions-related market adjustments. Conversely, roll-on-roll-off and container volumes experienced minor declines. In the passenger segment, total footfall reached 8.3 million visitors, up 1.0 percent, supported by a 25 percent surge in cruise passengers and continued strength on the Tallinn Helsinki ferry corridor. Domestic ferry services maintained stable connectivity, serving 2.5 million passengers and 1.2 million vehicles across the Estonian mainland and islands. Revenue contributions strengthened in both the passenger and cargo harbour segments, benefiting from indexed fee structures, higher vessel dues from larger-tonnage ships, and increased commercial lease income.

Strategically, the Group advanced several infrastructure and sustainability initiatives. Construction of a new multifunctional quay at Paldiski South Harbour was completed, positioning the terminal for future offshore wind farm logistics and military dual-use capabilities. Design contracts for the Rail Baltica freight station connection at Muuga Harbour were secured, reinforcing long-term trade corridor prospects. Environmental targets progressed meaningfully, with greenhouse gas emissions declining by 11 percent and renewable energy accounting for 77 percent of total consumption. Capital expenditures were scaled back to EUR 33.2 million, aligning with the completion of major construction phases, while net debt decreased by 15.5 percent to EUR 141.7 million and equity strengthened to EUR 380.9 million. Shareholder returns remained consistent, with dividends of EUR 0.073 per share paid out, representing full distribution of the prior year earnings. Overall, the reporting period highlighted the Group capability to navigate macroeconomic headwinds through disciplined cost management, diversified revenue streams, and targeted infrastructure investments.
