InCoax Networks reported net sales of SEK 48,055,007 for 2025, representing a thirty-five percent decline from SEK 74,497,775 in the prior year. The drop in revenue was primarily driven by timing shifts in customer orders, delayed project implementations, and a cautious market environment that slowed the commercial ramp-up within established partnerships. Despite lower top-line figures, the gross margin expanded to sixty-two percent from fifty-seven percent, although the widening operating loss reached SEK -32,380,175 compared to SEK -19,378,406 in 2024. The net loss after tax totaled SEK -32,559,663, translating to a loss per share of SEK -0.25 against SEK -0.18 previously. Capitalised development expenditures remained consistent at SEK 25,269,745, reflecting sustained investment in next-generation products during the transition from the development phase to commercial execution.

Operational performance was offset by substantial financing activities that delivered a positive total cash flow of SEK 6,516,117, a marked improvement from a negative SEK -12,758,133 in 2024. The positive figure stemmed entirely from capital raising, which included a directed share issue of approximately SEK 33.5 million, new borrowings of SEK 20 million, and outstanding convertible instruments. These funds were allocated to manage quarterly cash flow variations, support ongoing product development, and sustain operations until profitability is achieved. Meanwhile, operating cash flow improved to minus SEK 20 million from minus SEK 27.1 million, yet remained negative as the company navigates its transition. To bridge the gap between technical milestones and commercial success, management implemented structural cost reductions aimed at lowering the recurring expense base by over forty percent, or approximately SEK 25 million annually, effective from 2026. This measure focuses on cutting development overhead, streamlining operations, and redirecting resources toward direct sales conversion.

Strategically, the year marked the conclusion of the company’s most intensive product development period, culminating in the launch of the D2501 unit fully integrated into Nokia’s Gigabit Connect platform and the release of the D2508 model for smaller deployments. Initial installations via Nokia commenced in North America, signaling the start of scaled commercial rollouts. The broader macroeconomic and industry context supports this pivot, as global broadband infrastructure reaches maturity and operators seek faster, lower-cost methods to activate subscribers in multi-dwelling buildings where existing coaxial networks can be leveraged instead of installing new fiber. Regulatory initiatives in Europe and the United States further reinforce demand for such cost-effective in-building extension technologies. Nevertheless, the audit report explicitly notes a material uncertainty regarding the company’s ability to continue as a going concern, highlighting dependence on the future exercise of warrants for additional capital and the successful conversion of its technology pipeline into sustained commercial revenue. Management emphasizes strict financial discipline, prioritizing capital allocation toward initiatives with clear payback periods and accelerated sales generation to achieve cash flow neutrality within the coming twelve months.
