LHV Group reported a record 2024, with consolidated net profit increasing 7% year on year to EUR 150.3 million, including EUR 149.0 million attributable to the owners of the parent. Profit before tax rose to EUR 175.1 million from EUR 164.6 million, while net income increased 11% to EUR 338.3 million. Earnings per share increased to EUR 0.46 from EUR 0.44. Return on equity remained high at 24.5%, although it declined from 29.0% in 2023, and return on assets fell to 1.9% from 2.1%.

Growth in lending and customer activity was the main feature of the year. Net loans increased by EUR 990 million, or 28%, to EUR 4.6 billion. Estonian corporate and retail loan portfolios each grew by 21%, while UK SME lending increased 338% to EUR 348 million. Customer deposits rose 21% to EUR 6.9 billion, including a 14% increase in regular LHV Pank deposits. Consolidated assets reached EUR 8.7 billion, up 23%, and equity increased to EUR 678.7 million from EUR 556.4 million.

Net interest income grew 8% to EUR 273.3 million and net fee and commission income rose 24% to EUR 60.3 million, supported by asset growth and higher customer activity. These gains were partly offset by a 14% increase in operating expenses to EUR 146.9 million, primarily reflecting higher employee numbers, and by higher expected credit losses, which increased from EUR 11.5 million to EUR 16.3 million. The cost-to-income ratio was 43.4%, compared with 42.3% in 2023, while net interest margin declined from 3.88% to 3.48%.

LHV Pank remained the dominant contributor, generating EUR 140.5 million of net profit, 1% below 2023. Its income increased 6%, but expenses rose 12%, credit losses increased, and deposit repricing weighed on interest income. LHV Bank’s profit rose to EUR 5.8 million from EUR 5.3 million as it expanded UK lending and deposits and launched retail banking. LHV Kindlustus increased net profit to EUR 1.2 million from EUR 0.3 million, with gross written premiums up 21% to EUR 38.0 million. LHV Paytech’s profit rose 40% to EUR 0.7 million, while LHV Varahaldus’ profit declined slightly to EUR 1.6 million; assets under management increased 3% to EUR 1.56 billion.

Results were achieved despite Estonia’s continuing recession, sharply increased competition in lending and deposits, and falling interest rates during the second half. The Group strengthened its UK platform, issued EUR 300 million of MREL bonds and EUR 20 million of Tier 2 bonds, and implemented cost-control measures. Capital remained strong, with a CET1 ratio of 16.89% and total capital adequacy of 20.68%, both above requirements. The share price declined 7% during the year, and management proposed a 2024 dividend of EUR 0.09 per share.
