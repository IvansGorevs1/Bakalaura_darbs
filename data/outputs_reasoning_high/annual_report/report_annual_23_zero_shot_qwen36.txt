TKM Grupp AS recorded consolidated revenue of 919.6 million euros for the 2025 financial year, a decrease of 2.6 percent from 944.6 million euros in 2024. Net profit amounted to 17.5 million euros, declining by 36.2 percent compared to the 27.5 million euro profit reported in the prior year. The primary driver of this earnings reduction was the car trade segment, where revenue contracted by 11.9 percent to 176.9 million euros and net profit fell by 54.4 percent to 4.3 million euros. This pronounced weakness stemmed directly from the introduction of a motor vehicle tax in Estonia during the reporting period, which triggered a 48.6 percent drop in domestic new passenger car sales. Conversely, the supermarket segment demonstrated resilience, with revenue rising 0.3 percent to 611.9 million euros, though net profit decreased to 9.5 million euros due to heightened promotional discounting and shifting consumer purchasing patterns toward lower-priced product tiers. The department stores and security segments both reported minor revenue declines, while the real estate segment generated 7.7 million euros in revenue, up 5.1 percent, aided by initial leasing income from a newly completed logistics centre. Prior year results were inflated by one-off gains totaling six million euros from property revaluations and non-core asset sales.

Financial discipline remained a focal point, as the Group successfully constrained total operating expenses to a 0.2 percent annual increase and reduced finance costs by 1.5 million euros thanks to lower interest rates linked to Euribor. Cash and cash equivalents stood at 29.5 million euros at year end, while working capital turned negative at minus 14.2 million euros due to the reclassification of maturing long-term loans as current liabilities. Capital expenditures totaled 25.5 million euros, funding comprehensive refurbishments across key retail locations, IT infrastructure enhancements, and the opening of a combined KIA and Škoda multi-brand dealership in Vilnius. On a group level, annual average employment decreased slightly to 4,743 staff, loyal Partner Card holders surpassed 750,000, and earnings per share declined to 0.43 euros from 0.67 euros. In early 2026, the Group announced a definitive agreement to acquire Škoda distribution rights in Tallinn and Harju County, reinforcing its long-term automotive expansion strategy. Additionally, a corporate demerger in Lithuania successfully separated the KIA and Škoda operations into distinct entities. Although headline profitability weakened against a buoyant comparative base, TKM Grupp maintained steady operational execution, optimized its supply chain, and preserved liquidity through active debt refinancing preparations, positioning the organization for measured recovery as local consumer sentiment gradually stabilizes.
