Lyko Group reported net revenue of SEK 3,962.6 million for the full year 2025, an 11.3 percent increase from SEK 3,561.7 million in 2024. Growth was concentrated in the Nordics segment, where sales rose 12.0 percent to SEK 3,699.8 million, representing 93.4 percent of total group revenue. European online sales decreased 1.3 percent to SEK 130.7 million. Gross profit reached SEK 1,658.7 million, though the gross profit margin contracted to 41.9 percent from 44.0 percent. Operating profit (EBIT) declined to SEK 78.2 million with a 2.0 percent margin, down from SEK 117.4 million and a 3.3 percent margin in 2024. Depreciation and amortization increased to SEK 174.1 million. Higher net financial expenses, combined with operating results, led to a pre-tax loss of SEK 0.9 million compared to a pre-tax profit of SEK 37.6 million previously. The group recorded a net loss of SEK 3.0 million, translating to earnings per share of negative SEK 0.20, versus a net profit of SEK 21.0 million and earnings per share of SEK 1.37 in 2024.

Despite reduced profitability indicators, cash flow from operating activities improved substantially to SEK 527.5 million from SEK 209.7 million, driven by strong increases in operating liabilities. Cash flow used in investing activities totaled negative SEK 351.2 million, primarily covering the commissioning of the new warehouse automation system and new retail locations. The company financed its investment cycle through new borrowings of SEK 270.3 million, bringing total interest-bearing liabilities to SEK 1,416.3 million. Net debt excluding lease liabilities stood at SEK 292.5 million, down from SEK 333.1 million, while the equity ratio declined to 14.8 percent from 20.3 percent due to balance sheet expansion and increased borrowing.

The reporting period was defined by large-scale infrastructure investments and retail format innovation. Lyko advanced the ramp-up of a fully automated warehouse in Vansbro, expanding daily processing capacity to approximately 100,000 orders to support long-term scalability. The retailer debuted its standardized Fab Store concept in Täby Centrum and extended its physical footprint with new locations in Norway and Finland, bringing the total number of stores to 34. The company continued building its own brands portfolio, including the relaunch of Make Up Store and the introduction of PLY Skin and Scandinavian Soap Factory. In Europe, a segment reorganization began, closing local offices in Germany and the Netherlands to centralize operations and lower overhead. Management highlighted that despite intensified sector competition and widespread profit warnings elsewhere in the beauty industry, Lyko maintained above-market growth and reinforced brand awareness. Following the reporting date, the board announced a SEK 100 million annual cost savings program and secured a three-year extension of revolving credit facilities, signaling a strategic shift toward stabilizing profitability while preserving execution capacity for continued expansion.
