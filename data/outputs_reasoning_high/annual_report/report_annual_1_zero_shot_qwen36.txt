AAC Clyde Space reported net sales of SEK 295 million in 2025, representing a 16 percent decline from SEK 353 million in 2024. The Group recorded an EBITDA of SEK 17 million, down from SEK 47 million previously, while the operating result widened to a loss of SEK 40 million compared to SEK 4 million in 2024. Earnings per share deteriorated to minus SEK 7.63 from minus SEK 1.00. Operating cash flow fell to negative SEK 73 million from positive SEK 57 million, and cash reserves decreased to SEK 30 million from SEK 50 million. The equity ratio strengthened to 80 percent from 71 percent. The order backlog contracted to SEK 352 million at year end from SEK 641 million in 2024. Operations were formally restructured into two segments in the second quarter. Data and Services demonstrated robust growth, with net sales rising 80 percent to SEK 80 million and EBITDA increasing to SEK 28 million from SEK 21 million, reflecting expanding recurring maritime and Earth observation data revenues supported by the operational Sedna constellation. Conversely, Products and Missions net sales declined to SEK 245 million from SEK 346 million, and EBITDA decreased to SEK 17 million from SEK 52 million. The consolidated revenue shortfall and cash burn were directly attributable to program execution delays. Technical discussions between a customer and subcontractor shifted approximately SEK 32 million in milestone revenue into 2026. Procurement and political developments postponed the EPS Sterna weather satellite program initiation, while additional resources were required to commission Sedna satellites and reschedule VIREON launch windows. To sustain constellation investments and cover working capital needs, the company executed two directed share issues during the year, raising approximately SEK 115 million before transaction costs and securing a strategic investment from Bonnier Capital. Looking ahead, commercial momentum accelerated significantly after year end. The Group finalized phase one of the European Space Agency INFLECION maritime intelligence program and advanced to negotiations for subsequent phases. An extraordinary general meeting approved a follow-on capital raise, and the company received a major EUR 76.3 million order from OHB Sweden for twenty microwave radiometers. This contract expanded the prospective backlog to approximately SEK 1.1 billion as of March 2026. Concurrently, the first two VIREON earth observation satellites successfully launched and entered commissioning, broadening the groups proprietary data offerings. Management characterizes the underlying trajectory as improved, with diversified institutional backing, a revitalized contract pipeline, and a deliberate structural pivot toward scalable space data services positioning the company for sustained revenue growth in defense, security, and environmental monitoring sectors.
