Nordic Fibreboard AS reported a consolidated net loss of €1.15 million for the year ended 31 December 2025, increasing from the €0.79 million loss recorded in 2024. Consolidated net sales declined by 3.4% to €7.33 million, down from €7.59 million in the prior year. Sales within the European Union remained stable at 92% of total revenue, while exports to Africa, Asia, and other regions experienced notable reductions. Real estate management revenue decreased significantly to €3 thousand from €25 thousand in 2024 following the termination of rental agreements. The group’s EBITDA deteriorated sharply to negative €0.42 million, compared to a negligible negative €0.03 million in 2024, pushing the EBITDA margin down 6 percentage points to negative 6%. This decline was primarily driven by a planned December factory shutdown to transition to a new gas boiler system, which mirrored an unplanned weather-related shutdown in December 2024. During both periods, ongoing personnel, maintenance, and repair expenses continued to accumulate without corresponding production output, creating an approximate €0.2 million adverse impact on EBITDA in each year. Additionally, underutilized production capacity increased unit manufacturing costs.

The operating environment remained challenging, with Estonia’s economic growth reaching a modest 0.6% amid high inflation of approximately 6%, largely fueled by tax increases that constrained domestic consumption. Although the wood processing sector saw early-year recovery, overall industrial demand stayed weak. To address these pressures and secure liquidity, the group executed a €2.0 million share issuance in October 2025, increasing equity to €4.46 million and improving the debt-to-equity ratio to 52% from 56% in 2024. Total assets grew to €9.3 million, while working capital remained deeply negative at €2.7 million. Cash flows reflected operating outflows of €503 thousand, offset partially by €1,444 thousand from financing activities, primarily the share issue and related-party borrowings.

Strategically, the group streamlined its corporate structure by merging the real estate subsidiary Pärnu Riverside Development OÜ and fully consolidated the Admirali Quarter property development project in Pärnu. Design work concluded and construction permits were secured for multiple residential buildings and utility networks. A preliminary agreement to sell ten properties from the project was signed in January 2026. The workforce contracted to 60 employees from 68, while total labour costs rose to €1.9 million from €1.6 million. Management expects fibreboard demand to remain stable and plans to continue expanding into Nordic markets while closely monitoring cash flow to navigate the difficult construction materials market.
