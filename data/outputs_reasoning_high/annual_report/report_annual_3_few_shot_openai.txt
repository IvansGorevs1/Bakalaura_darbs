Coop Pank’s financial performance weakened in 2025 despite continued expansion in lending and customer numbers. Net profit declined by 11% to €28.7 million from €32.2 million in 2024. The main pressure came from the lower-interest-rate environment, which reduced interest income and margins faster than the bank’s business volumes increased. Net interest income fell to €73.3 million from €77.6 million, while net interest margin narrowed from 3.8% to 3.0%. Total net operating income decreased to €79.4 million from €81.9 million.

Fee and commission income increased modestly, with net fee and commission income rising to €4.5 million from €4.3 million. Net other income also improved to €1.5 million from near zero in 2024. These gains only partly offset higher operating costs, which increased to €41.5 million from €40.6 million. Payroll expenses rose as the average number of employees increased to approximately 452 from 433, while information-system and legal expenses also grew. The cost-to-income ratio consequently increased to 52.3% from 49.6%. Credit loss allowances declined to €3.3 million from €4.6 million, reflecting the continued low-risk quality of the loan portfolio. However, the effective tax rate increased to 17% from 12%, and income tax expense rose to €5.8 million.

Business volumes grew strongly. The net loan portfolio increased by 19% to €2.114 billion, exceeding €2 billion for the first time. Business lending grew by 27% to €986 million, while mortgage and other private loans increased by 17% to €863 million. Customer deposits rose by 9% to €2.047 billion, supported by an 11% increase in demand deposits and an 8% increase in term deposits. The customer base expanded by 19,000, or 9%, to 227,000. Loan quality remained at a low-risk level, although total loan-loss allowances increased slightly to €19.4 million from €18.6 million.

The bank strengthened and diversified its funding during the year by issuing €250 million of four-year covered bonds, the first tranche under a €750 million programme. It also entered into an agreement with the European Investment Bank Group that is expected to enable up to €249 million of new business lending through capital relief, and signed a €5 million subordinated loan agreement with the European Energy Efficiency Fund.

Profitability ratios declined alongside earnings: return on equity fell to 12.9% from 16.2%, and return on assets to 1.2% from 1.6%. Capital ratios also decreased moderately, but the Group remained compliant with all regulatory requirements. The bank paid €7.2 million in dividends for 2024 and proposed a 2025 dividend of €0.065 per share, totaling €6.7 million. Overall, 2025 combined strong balance-sheet growth and improved funding capacity with weaker earnings caused primarily by lower interest rates and reduced margins.
