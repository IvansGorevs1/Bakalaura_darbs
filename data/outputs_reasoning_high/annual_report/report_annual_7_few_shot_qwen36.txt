Heimstaden reported mixed top-line results but improved overall profitability during the 2025 reporting period. Annual rental income remained virtually unchanged at SEK 15,916 million, compared with SEK 15,919 million in 2024, as positive contributions from contract indexations and tenant improvement investments were largely offset by foreign currency translation effects and portfolio reductions from divestments. Despite flat top-line growth, net operating income expanded to SEK 11,475 million from SEK 11,188 million, driven by a 6.1 percent reduction in property expenses to SEK 4,378 million. The decline in operating costs reflected sustained efficiency measures, digitalisation initiatives, and the shrinking footprint following active sales programmes. Consequently, the net operating income margin improved to 72.1 percent, up from 70.3 percent in the prior year.

Property valuations showed resilience, with the fair value of investment properties increasing by 3 percent over the year to SEK 324,429 million. The valuation uplift of SEK 9,498 million was supported by strong rental performance across multiple markets, value-accretive capital expenditure, and favorable ownership housing dynamics in several regions. The company continued its strategic privatisation programme, selling 2,466 residential units throughout the year at an average premium of 28.9 percent to book value. Full-year realised gains from property divestments reached SEK 2,207 million, contributing significantly to the bottom line. Reported profit before tax rose to SEK 16,012 million from SEK 10,403 million, primarily reflecting the combined effect of higher net operating income, substantial unrealised property revaluations, and realised divestment gains, partially offset by a SEK 3,707 million goodwill impairment charge related to the Akelius acquisition.

On the capital structure front, the group successfully continued its deleveraging trajectory. The net loan-to-value ratio decreased to 52.2 percent from 56.4 percent, supported by the deployment of privatisation proceeds toward debt repayment. Net debt to total assets fell to 48.0 percent, and the interest coverage ratio strengthened to 2.0 times. Financing activities remained robust, with the subsidiary executing approximately SEK 59,500 million in refinancing and new funding transactions across both capital markets and banking facilities. Average borrowing costs stabilised at 3.43 percent, and unutilised credit facilities amounted to SEK 23,449 million, providing ample liquidity headroom. 

Operational developments included a final arbitration award in December 2025 ruling in favour of the company in a dispute with Alecta regarding the Shareholders Agreement. Credit ratings received a negative outlook revision in January 2025, while the company maintained a negligible environmental, social, and governance risk rating. The board proposed carrying forward all accumulated profits with no interim dividend distribution, directing capital toward further balance sheet strengthening and portfolio optimisation. Overall, the reporting period was characterised by disciplined execution, margin expansion, and successful asset monetisation, reinforcing the company’s focus on sustainable returns and financial flexibility.
