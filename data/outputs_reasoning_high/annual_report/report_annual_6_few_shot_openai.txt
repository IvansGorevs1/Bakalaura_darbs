Gigasun reported continued operational growth in 2025, with higher electricity production, expanded solar capacity and improved operating profitability. Group revenue increased 9.8% to SEK 239.7 million from SEK 218.3 million, while net revenue from electricity sales rose 9.9% to SEK 203.2 million. Revenue growth was supported by higher production, although it was partly offset by lower average prices from a greater share of electricity sold to the grid, revised tariffs in the second half of the year and currency effects of SEK -18.0 million. Subsidies contributed SEK 32.7 million of other operating income.

Gross profit increased to SEK 195.8 million from SEK 179.2 million, with the gross margin unchanged at 82%. EBITDA rose to SEK 163.7 million from SEK 145.8 million, lifting the EBITDA margin from 67% to 68%. Operating expenses increased 5.5% to SEK 166.2 million, mainly because the larger installed base resulted in higher depreciation and maintenance costs. Despite this increase, EBIT grew 21.1% to SEK 73.5 million, and the EBIT margin improved from 28% to 31%.

The net loss narrowed substantially to SEK 7.2 million from SEK 32.9 million, and earnings per share improved to negative SEK 0.13 from negative SEK 0.58. Financial expenses and similar items declined to SEK 82.0 million from SEK 88.7 million, reflecting the refinancing of Chinese debt. Results were also affected by a negative currency difference of SEK 3.8 million and a one-off cost of SEK 3.5 million. Excluding these items, management reported profit of approximately SEK 0.1 million, compared with a loss of SEK 34.7 million in 2024.

Operationally, Gigasun installed 55.7 MW of new rooftop solar capacity, bringing total revenue-generating capacity to 395.8 MW from 340.1 MW, an increase of approximately 16%. Electricity production increased 30% to 327.8 million kWh, while 17 contracts were signed compared with 15 in the previous year. Electricity tariffs in Jiangsu and Anhui reduced realized prices per kWh by approximately 8% in the second half, with an estimated 3–4% impact on revenue from the existing portfolio. The company expects lower financing and construction costs to offset this pressure. New regulatory requirements for self-consumption and, in some cases, energy storage may increase project requirements while creating opportunities for expanded energy-storage offerings.

Cash flow was negative SEK 34.9 million, compared with positive SEK 68.3 million in 2024, as operating cash flow of SEK 101.1 million was more than offset by SEK 208.1 million of investment outflows. Interest-bearing debt declined to SEK 1.38 billion, but cash fell to SEK 45.1 million and the equity ratio weakened to 21% from 23%. Refinancing of approximately SEK 1.24 billion at 3.60% over ten years is underway and is expected to continue in 2026. However, the SEK 70.4 million SOLT4 bond, carrying 14.25% interest, is due in November 2026, and management states that failure to secure additional financing would create significant uncertainty regarding continued operations.
