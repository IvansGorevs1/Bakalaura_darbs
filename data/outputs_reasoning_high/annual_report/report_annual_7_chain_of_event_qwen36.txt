Heimstaden AB reported flat revenue alongside improved profitability and a stronger balance sheet for the year ended 31 December 2025. Rental income remained steady at SEK 15,916 million, matching the prior year as indexation gains and tenant improvements were exactly offset by foreign currency headwinds and active divestments. Despite static revenues, net operating income climbed to SEK 11,475 million from SEK 11,188 million, lifting the net operating income margin to 72.1 percent from 70.3 percent. Management explicitly credits this expansion to a 6.1 percent decline in property expenses to SEK 4,378 million, resulting from digitalisation-led cost controls and a shrinking portfolio following privatisation sales. Like-for-like rental growth moderated to 4.9 percent from 5.6 percent, while real economic occupancy held constant at 98.5 percent.

Fair value of investment properties increased by 3 percent, adding SEK 9,498 million to reach SEK 324,429 million. The report attributes this appreciation to solid operating performance, value-accretive capital expenditure on maintenance and sustainability upgrades, and strong local ownership markets. The privatisation programme progressed significantly, selling 2,466 units for SEK 10,664 million at a 28.9 percent premium to book value and generating SEK 2,207 million in realised gains. Proceeds from these asset sales were strategically deployed to pay down debt, which successfully reduced the net loan-to-value ratio from 56.4 percent to 52.2 percent and raised the interest coverage ratio to 2.0 times. The funding strategy was complemented by liability management exercises, including new senior unsecured bond issuances, maintaining an interest rate hedge ratio of 87 percent.

Profit before tax more than doubled to SEK 16,012 million from SEK 10,403 million, primarily reflecting higher unrealised property valuation adjustments and operational leverage. This gain was partially counterbalanced by a SEK 3,707 million goodwill impairment linked to the Akelius acquisition. Management identifies rising levered betas and consequent increases in the weighted average cost of capital as the direct catalysts for this write-down. Total equity grew to SEK 150,557 million, though comprehensive income suffered from SEK 8,857 million in adverse currency translation differences across foreign operations. Overall, the period demonstrated resilient operational execution, effective asset monetisation, and deliberate deleveraging, effectively managing the interplay between stabilising rental metrics, declining leverage, and external market pressures affecting valuations and foreign exchange exposures.
