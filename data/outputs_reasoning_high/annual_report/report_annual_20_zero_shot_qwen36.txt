AS Tallink Grupp reported consolidated revenue of EUR 765.3 million for the year ended 31 December 2025, a 2.6 percent decrease from EUR 785.8 million in 2024. Passengers carried fell 0.9 percent to 5.5 million, cargo units declined 19.2 percent to 245,004, and passenger vehicles dropped 2.2 percent. Core route revenue fell by EUR 13.7 million to EUR 625.2 million. Gross profit contracted by EUR 10.3 million to EUR 143.2 million, while EBITDA dropped by EUR 45.1 million to EUR 130.1 million, yielding an EBITDA margin of 17.0 percent versus 22.3 percent in the prior year. Net profit totaled EUR 17.3 million, or EUR 0.023 per share, compared to EUR 40.3 million, or EUR 0.054 per share, in 2024.

Profitability deterioration stemmed from weak consumer and business confidence, ongoing economic headwinds in core markets, and geopolitical uncertainty suppressing travel demand. Operational adjustments also shaped results, including the sale of three vessels which generated EUR 78.8 million in proceeds and reduced annual depreciation by EUR 18.0 million to EUR 79.8 million. Management simultaneously extended the estimated useful lives of cruise and passenger vessels to forty-five years. Personnel expenses rose 4.3 percent to EUR 201.0 million due to maritime collective agreements, while fuel costs increased marginally to EUR 96.5 million. Route performance diverged; Estonia-Finland revenue remained resilient at EUR 314.5 million despite volume pressures, whereas Estonia-Sweden and Finland-Sweden routes faced steeper declines driven by falling cargo volumes and sixty-eight days of scheduled dry-docking maintenance in early 2025.

Financial discipline helped improve the balance sheet, with interest-bearing liabilities falling to EUR 445.9 million and net debt decreasing to EUR 432.4 million from EUR 537.7 million. The net debt to EBITDA ratio rose slightly to 3.3 times from 3.1 times. Operating cash flow remained robust at EUR 127.1 million, supporting total liquidity of EUR 110.4 million after distributing EUR 44.6 million in dividends plus EUR 11.3 million in associated withholding tax. Management maintained market leadership through route optimization, strategic vessel charters, and persistent cost efficiency measures. The Group also advanced sustainability initiatives, planning to convert shuttle vessels to liquefied biomethane by 2026, while announcing a leadership transition with Peep Jalakas appointed as new Chairman of the Management Board effective April 2026. Overall, Tallink delivered lower profits amid volatile demand but preserved strong cash generation and strengthened its capital structure through fleet rationalization and disciplined execution.
