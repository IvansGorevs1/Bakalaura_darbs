For the year ended 31 December 2025, Tallinna Sadam Group reported consolidated revenue of EUR 118.7 million, down 0.8 per cent or EUR 0.9 million from 2024. Despite this slight top-line decline, operating profit increased 12.2 per cent to EUR 32.7 million and adjusted EBITDA rose 6.4 per cent to EUR 56.5 million. Net profit grew 17.3 per cent to EUR 22.5 million, producing earnings per share of EUR 0.09. The adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 47.6 per cent from 44.4 per cent, underscoring improved operational efficiency. Cargo volumes recovered by 5.1 per cent to 13.8 million tonnes, led by a 47 per cent jump in liquid bulk at Paldiski South Harbour and record steel and grain flows at Muuga. Passenger numbers edged up 1.0 per cent to 8.3 million, buoyed by the Tallinn-Helsinki route and eighteen additional cruise ship calls that expanded winter programming. In contrast, icebreaker Botnica utilisation dropped to 49 per cent, cutting charter revenue by EUR 4.0 million or 34 per cent due to fewer booked days and lower spot rates.

This revenue shortfall was more than neutralised by a EUR 4.0 million or 10.0 per cent reduction in operating expenses. Lower technical maintenance costs accounted for a EUR 2.6 million decline, as 2024 bore heavy repair burdens from a Botnica technical breakdown that were partially offset by a EUR 0.9 million insurance payout in 2025. Reduced vessel charter durations further lowered equipment leasing and fuel costs by EUR 0.8 million and EUR 0.6 million respectively. Pricing adjustments tied to consumer inflation indexes, alongside larger average vessel sizes, lifted vessel dues by EUR 2.5 million or 8.0 per cent and strengthened lease and cargo charge income. Although financing expenses fell 30.0 per cent to EUR 5.8 million owing to declining Euribor rates and principal repayments, net profit expansion was tempered by a 73.3 per cent surge in dividend income tax expense to EUR 5.4 million and an equity-method loss from associate Green Marine.

Capital investments fell 14.4 per cent to EUR 33.2 million, primarily because construction of the multifunctional quay at Paldiski South Harbour concluded early. Future spending is expected to pivot toward terminal shore power infrastructure, which may increase near-term operating costs. Consistent with its corporate policy, the Group distributed dividends of EUR 0.073 per share, totalling EUR 19.2 million. The reporting period highlighted a pronounced recovery in core maritime operations and rigorous cost discipline, effectively translating steady cargo and passenger growth into enhanced profitability while executing major infrastructure milestones.
