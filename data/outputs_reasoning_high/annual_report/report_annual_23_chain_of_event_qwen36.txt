TKM Grupp AS reported consolidated revenue of 919.6 million euros for 2025, a decrease of 2.6 percent from the prior year’s 944.6 million euros. Net profit amounted to 17.5 million euros, representing a 36.1 percent decline compared to 27.5 million euros in 2024. EBITDA contracted by 14.7 percent to 77.4 million euros, operating profit fell 26.6 percent to 34.9 million euros, and earnings per share dropped to 0.43 euros from 0.67 euros. Financial performance deteriorated across most segments, with only real estate revenue showing marginal growth.

The decline was primarily driven by adverse market conditions and specific corporate events. Automotive segment revenue contracted sharply by 11.9 percent to 176.9 million euros, a direct consequence of Estonia’s newly introduced motor vehicle tax. This legislation suppressed consumer confidence and delayed vehicle purchases, causing national new car sales to fall 48.6 percent year-on-year. Furthermore, the comparative prior year included four million euros in one-off real estate gains from property sales and fair value adjustments, artificially elevating the previous year’s baseline. In retail, the supermarket segment achieved resilient top-line growth of 0.3 percent to 611.9 million euros, yet profitability weakened as promotional pricing, increased theft incidents reflecting cautious spending, and one-off renovation costs at the Jõgeva store weighed on margins. Department store revenue dipped 1.1 percent, negatively impacted by unseasonably warm winter weather that reduced seasonal apparel demand. Security services revenue declined 8.1 percent amid intense competition and rising input costs, driving segment profit to near zero.

To mitigate these headwinds, management executed strict cost discipline, limiting non-labour operating expense growth to 0.2 percent annually while labour costs rose 3.7 percent. The profit decline was partially offset by a 1.5 million euro reduction in finance costs, a 1.2 million euro decrease in tax expense from deferred tax recalculations, and geographic diversification. While the Estonian auto market collapsed, subsidiaries in Latvia and Lithuania expanded their presence and benefited from stronger regional car sales growth of 31.4 percent and 39.3 percent respectively. Total strategic investments reached 25.5 million euros, focused on physical store modernizations, IT infrastructure upgrades, and the development of a new multi-brand dealership in Vilnius. Following the reporting period, management secured an agreement to acquire Škoda distribution rights in Tallinn and Harju County to strengthen brand synergy and market coverage. Against this backdrop, the board proposes a final dividend of 0.60 euros per share, aligning distribution with a cautiously optimistic outlook supported by expected wage growth and moderating inflation.
