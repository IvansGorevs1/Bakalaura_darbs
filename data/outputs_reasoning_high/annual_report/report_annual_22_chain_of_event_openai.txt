Tallinna Vesi reported stronger operating results in 2025. Group revenue increased 11.4% to €72.48 million, supported by a 6.9% increase in water-services revenue to €62.31 million and a 62.0% increase in construction-services revenue to €8.46 million. Revenue from private customers in the main service area rose 18.3% to €31.84 million, driven by higher consumption and tariff changes. Business-customer revenue declined 2.7% to €16.76 million because business tariffs were reduced as part of the statutory harmonisation of private and business prices. Revenue from customers outside the main service area rose 13.8% to €7.71 million.

Construction growth reflected higher pipe-construction and external road-construction activity. Watercom described 2025 as a record year for turnover and profit, supported by the recovering economy and construction sector and projects won during late 2024 and 2025. However, intense competition and rising input prices continued to pressure construction margins. The cost of goods and services sold increased 12.0% to €42.96 million, mainly because construction-services costs rose 74.5% and staff costs rose 6.8%. Direct production costs were broadly unchanged at €9.90 million: higher chemical and pollution-tax costs were largely offset by lower electricity costs.

Gross profit increased 10.5% to €29.52 million, EBITDA rose to €30.95 million from €27.51 million, and operating profit grew 11.8% to €21.22 million. Management attributed the operating-profit improvement mainly to electricity-cost optimisation following the commissioning of the combined heat and power plant at Paljassaare. The plant produced 9,987 MWh of electricity and 6,933 MWh of heat in 2025; its electricity production covered 43.8% of the annual consumption of the wastewater-treatment plant and main pumping station. Net financial expense decreased to €3.78 million from €4.20 million, mainly because of lower Euribor rates.

Profit before tax rose 18.0% to €17.43 million, while net profit increased 7.2% to €14.24 million. The smaller increase in net profit reflected the higher income-tax rate on dividend payments. Earnings per share rose to €0.71 from €0.66, although the net-profit margin declined from 20.41% to 19.69%. The 2025 dividend had not been declared when the report was issued; €10.60 million, or €0.53 per share, was paid from 2024 profit.

Investment accelerated, with €55.74 million invested in fixed assets versus €49.53 million in 2024. The expansion was financed partly through additional borrowing: outstanding loans increased to €160 million from €118 million, while the debt-to-assets ratio rose to 66.02% from 61.71%. Cash increased to €11.29 million from €3.59 million, supported by €45 million of new loans and operating cash flow of €30.83 million. The group retained a €6.02 million provision for tariff-related claims, against claims totalling €14.67 million, and no final judgment had been issued. PwC issued an unqualified opinion on the consolidated financial statements.
