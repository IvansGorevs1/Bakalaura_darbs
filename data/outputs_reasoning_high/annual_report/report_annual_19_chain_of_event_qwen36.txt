For the period ending December 31, 2025, Talkpool reported net sales of EUR 17.756 million, representing a 12.5 percent increase compared to EUR 15.782 million in the prior year. EBITDA reached EUR 1.828 million with a margin of 10.3 percent, while EBIT stood at EUR 1.620 million and an EBIT margin of 9.1 percent. Both operating profit measures declined slightly relative to the previous year, as management noted that near-term margin compression resulted from upfront investments in personnel and technology required to support expansion. Net earnings after tax amounted to EUR 862 thousand, reflecting a 42 percent improvement and an earnings margin of 4.8 percent. The company simultaneously repaired its capital structure, strengthening equity to EUR 2.1 million and moving to a positive net cash position of EUR 0.3 million, with auditors confirming no going concern uncertainties. These financial outcomes followed the conclusion of a multi-year restructuring phase and the April launch of the Vision 2030 Strategic Growth Plan, which targets EUR 40 million in revenues, a 14 percent EBITDA margin, and zero net debt by 2030. Revenue growth accelerated organically across regions, marking the first double-digit expansion since consolidation began. The Americas remained the largest market, delivering consistent growth in the Caribbean and Jamaica despite first-half currency headwinds that saw the US dollar weaken by 11 percent and the Pakistani rupee drop by 14 percent, both of which reduced euro-reported top-line figures. Regional dynamics varied elsewhere, with Germany providing steady liquidity even as temporary planning volume flattening occurred due to shifting client rollout priorities. Pakistan solidified its position as the primary production base, achieving record local-currency revenues and improved gross profitability following successful commercial renegotiations and the deployment of AI-powered site monitoring tools. To capture higher-margin opportunities, the company strategically discontinued proprietary software and hardware development to operate instead as an integrator and service provider for network technology vendors. This pivot toward AI-enabled remote delivery and tech solutions drives current investment spending, explaining the modest margin dip, though management expects capitalized investments to translate into enhanced profitability and cash flow within twelve months. Re-entry into the US market commenced through a targeted service contract, with scaled expansion reserved for the coming year to align with broader capacity building. Overall, the period demonstrates a successful transition from aggressive cost containment to disciplined, technology-focused growth, underpinned by stabilized profitability, balance sheet normalization, and clear long-term commercial targets.
