During the financial year ended December 31, 2025, Coop Pank AS reported a consolidated net profit of 28.7 million euros, representing an 11 percent decrease compared to 32.2 million euros in 2024. The decline in net profit was primarily driven by the lower-interest economic environment, which reduced net interest income to 73.3 million euros from 77.6 million euros in the previous year, an impact that the expansion in business volumes could not fully offset. Total net operating income decreased from 81.9 million euros to 79.4 million euros. Net fee and commission income rose slightly to 4.5 million euros from 4.3 million euros. Operating expenses increased to 41.5 million euros compared to 40.6 million euros in 2024, while credit loss allowances decreased to 3.3 million euros from 4.6 million euros. Income tax expense increased to 5.8 million euros from 4.5 million euros. Despite the reduction in net profit, the bank experienced robust growth across its core business volumes. The customer base expanded by 19,000 customers, or 9 percent, reaching 227,000 customers by the end of 2025. For the first time in the bank's history, the net loan portfolio exceeded 2 billion euros, reaching 2.11 billion euros and growing by 19 percent year-on-year. This expansion was supported across all business lines, led by a 27 percent increase in the business loan portfolio to 968.6 million euros and a 17 percent increase in the home loan portfolio. Customer deposits grew by 9 percent to reach 2.05 billion euros, driven by an 11 percent increase in demand deposits and an 8 percent increase in term deposits. Return on equity stood at 12.9 percent compared to 16.2 percent in 2024, and the cost-to-income ratio was 52.3 percent compared to 49.6 percent in the previous year. Capital adequacy at the end of 2025 was 18.54 percent, and the Common Equity Tier 1 capital ratio was 14.15 percent, with both ratios complying with regulatory requirements. The operating environment in Estonia during 2025 was characterized by modest economic growth with a real GDP growth of 0.7 percent, receding inflation, and declining central bank interest rates that brought Euribor down. Strategic developments during the period included the bank issuing covered bonds amounting to 250 million euros on the Irish stock exchange for the first time under a 750 million euro program, which provided a stable long-term funding source and allowed the bank to intentionally reduce higher-cost term deposits. Additionally, the bank entered into a guarantee agreement with the European Investment Bank Group for capital relief to support new lending and secured a 5 million euro subordinated loan from the European Energy Efficiency Fund for energy efficiency projects. In line with its dividend policy, the bank paid out 7.2 million euros in dividends during 2025, translating to 0.07 euros per share from 2024 earnings, and the management board has proposed a dividend of 0.065 euros per share for the 2025 financial year.
