Gigasun reported stronger operating performance in 2025, although the Group remained loss-making after financial items and faced ongoing financing risks. Total revenue increased 9.8% to SEK 239.7 million from SEK 218.3 million, while net revenue rose 9.9% to SEK 203.2 million. The increase was driven by higher electricity production, partly offset by lower average prices from a higher share of electricity sold to the grid, revised tariffs during the second half of the year and a weaker currency. Reported revenue included negative currency effects of SEK 18.0 million, compared with SEK 4.5 million in 2024. Subsidies contributed SEK 32.7 million and represented part of other operating income of SEK 36.5 million.

Operational profitability improved. Gross profit increased to SEK 195.8 million from SEK 179.2 million, while the gross margin remained 82%. EBITDA rose 12.3% to SEK 163.7 million and the EBITDA margin improved from 67% to 68%. EBIT increased 21.1% to SEK 73.5 million, raising the EBIT margin from 28% to 31%. Operating expenses increased 5.5% to SEK 166.2 million, primarily because the larger installed base generated higher depreciation and maintenance costs. The report attributes the improvement in operating profit to stable operations and improved underlying profitability.

The Group’s net loss narrowed substantially to SEK 7.2 million from SEK 32.9 million, and earnings per share improved to negative SEK 0.13 from negative SEK 0.58. However, the result was affected by a negative non-cash currency effect of SEK 3.8 million and a one-off cost of SEK 3.5 million. Adjusted for these items, management reported profit of SEK 0.1 million, compared with an adjusted loss of SEK 34.7 million in 2024. Interest expenses and similar charges declined to SEK 82.0 million from SEK 88.7 million, as the Chinese refinancing began reducing financing costs.

The solar portfolio expanded during the year. Installed capacity increased 16% to 395.8 MW across 216 installations, following the addition of 55.7 MW. Electricity production rose 30% to 327.8 million kWh, reducing CO2-related emissions by approximately 161,000 tonnes. The company signed 17 contracts totaling approximately 41.8 MW, but signed orders at year-end fell to 18.9 MW from 38.8 MW.

Financing remained central. Gigasun secured additional refinancing of approximately SEK 372 million at a 3.60% interest rate and extended the SOLT4 bond’s maturity to 8 November 2026. Interest-bearing debt declined to SEK 1.383 billion from SEK 1.483 billion, but the equity ratio weakened to 21% from 23%, cash fell to SEK 45.1 million from SEK 89.9 million, and total cash flow turned negative at SEK 34.9 million. Management stated that further financing during 2026 is important to execute the business plan and that failure to secure it would create significant uncertainty regarding continued operations.
