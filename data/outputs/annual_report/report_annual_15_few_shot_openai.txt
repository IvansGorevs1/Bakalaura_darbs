AS Merko Ehitus reported a weaker top-line and operating performance in 2025 as several large construction projects completed and new contract signing was slower during 2024 and the first half of 2025. Revenue declined 42.3% to EUR 310.9 million from EUR 539.0 million, while gross profit fell to EUR 55.9 million from EUR 95.9 million. Despite the lower volume, gross margin improved slightly to 18.0% from 17.8%. Operating profit decreased 52.6% to EUR 34.3 million, and the operating margin narrowed to 11.0% from 13.4%, reflecting the completion of highly profitable contracts and a return to more typical sector margins amid strong competition. EBITDA fell to EUR 37.5 million from EUR 75.7 million.

Profit before tax declined to EUR 44.8 million from EUR 76.4 million, while net profit attributable to shareholders decreased to EUR 39.9 million from EUR 64.7 million. However, the net margin improved to 12.8% from 12.0%, supported partly by lower finance costs and a EUR 10.4 million contribution from joint ventures. Earnings per share fell to EUR 2.26 from EUR 3.65.

Construction services remained the main business, but revenue fell 49.9% to EUR 237.6 million and operating profit declined 56.8% to EUR 25.7 million. Real estate development revenue increased 13.8% to EUR 73.4 million, supported by the sale of 358 apartments compared with 323 in 2024, although segment operating profit fell 18.8% to EUR 13.4 million as margins declined and a property in Norway was written down. The group also started more than three times as many new apartments as in the prior year.

Market conditions remained uncertain, with weak private investment, subdued construction activity in Estonia and reduced renewable-energy investment, although defence and infrastructure spending supported demand. New construction contracts worth EUR 362.8 million were signed, lifting the secured order book 37.1% to EUR 466.9 million. Major contracts included Rail Baltica, defence-related projects, a Pärnu hotel and conference centre, and wind-farm infrastructure. The largest contracts in Merko’s history for two phases of the Rūdninkai defence campus were signed early in 2026 and are expected to contribute from 2026 onward.

Cash flow weakened materially: operating cash flow was negative at EUR 1.7 million compared with positive cash flow of EUR 57.8 million in 2024, mainly because of higher inventories and construction-related working-capital movements. Cash and cash equivalents declined to EUR 41.4 million from EUR 91.9 million after EUR 33.6 million of dividends were paid. Nevertheless, equity increased to EUR 260.6 million, the equity ratio strengthened to 62.8% from 56.9%, and interest-bearing liabilities remained moderate at 8.0% of assets. Management proposed a EUR 1.25-per-share dividend, totaling EUR 22.1 million, equivalent to a 55% payout ratio.
