AS Tallinna Sadam Group reported improved profitability in 2025 despite slightly lower revenue. Revenue decreased 0.8% to EUR 118.7 million, mainly because charter income from the icebreaker Botnica fell by EUR 4.0 million, or 34%, to EUR 7.9 million. Botnica’s utilisation declined to 49% from 60%, as charter days fell by 42 and Baffinland Iron Mines did not exercise its annual charter option. This decline was partly offset by higher vessel dues, passenger fees, lease income, cargo charges and ferry-service revenue.

Operating profit increased 12.2% to EUR 32.7 million, while adjusted EBITDA rose 6.4% to EUR 56.5 million. The adjusted EBITDA margin improved from 44.4% to 47.6%, reflecting a EUR 4.0 million, or 10%, reduction in operating expenses. Lower maintenance and repair costs, reduced lease and fuel expenses, lower impairment losses on financial assets, and EUR 0.9 million of insurance compensation related to Botnica’s 2024 technical failure contributed to the cost reduction. Personnel expenses nevertheless increased 3% despite the average workforce declining from 438 to 425 employees.

Net profit rose 17.3% to EUR 22.5 million, and earnings per share increased from EUR 0.07 to EUR 0.09. Profit before tax grew 25% to EUR 27.9 million, supported by a 30% reduction in net finance costs to EUR 4.8 million as interest rates and borrowings decreased. The increase in income tax expense, to EUR 5.4 million from EUR 3.1 million, due mainly to changes in Estonia’s dividend taxation, moderated the growth in net profit. The associate Green Marine moved from a EUR 0.5 million profit to a EUR 26 thousand loss.

Operating activity recovered in the principal port businesses. Cargo volume increased 5.1% to 13.8 million tonnes, driven especially by a 47% recovery in liquid bulk, while passenger numbers rose 1.0% to 8.3 million, including a 25% increase in cruise passengers. Cargo-harbour adjusted EBITDA grew 19% to EUR 16.9 million and passenger-harbour EBITDA increased 5% to EUR 20.8 million. Ferry EBITDA declined 2% because higher personnel, consulting and development costs outweighed lower fuel, maintenance and electricity costs.

Capital investment decreased 14.4% to EUR 33.2 million, following major infrastructure spending in 2024. The new multifunctional quay at Paldiski South Harbour was completed, supporting cargo diversification, offshore wind projects and military uses. Operating cash flow increased to EUR 53.7 million from EUR 48.7 million, and net debt fell 15.5% to EUR 141.7 million. The Group paid EUR 19.2 million in dividends, or EUR 0.073 per share, and proposed the same dividend for 2025.
