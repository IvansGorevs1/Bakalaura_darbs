Heimstaden reported stronger underlying performance in 2025, although rental income remained broadly unchanged because portfolio disposals, privatisations and foreign-exchange effects offset growth from the standing portfolio. Rental income was SEK 15,916 million, compared with SEK 15,919 million in 2024, while annualised like-for-like rental growth remained solid at 4.9%, supported mainly by indexation, tenant improvements and occupancy gains. Real economic occupancy was stable at 98.5% for the year and reached 98.7% in the fourth quarter.

Profitability improved as cost controls and a smaller portfolio reduced operating expenses. Property expenses declined 6.1% to SEK 4,378 million, despite utilities increasing 5.7% because supplier price increases exceeded core inflation. Net operating income rose to SEK 11,475 million from SEK 11,188 million, and the NOI margin increased to 72.1% from 70.3%. Capital expenditure was SEK 4,439 million, including SEK 3,893 million on investment and improvement projects, with higher spending on maintenance and sustainability. Tenant improvements generated SEK 94 million of additional rental income at an average yield on cost of 10.8%.

Property values increased 3.0%, or SEK 9,498 million, during the year, supported by operating performance, value-accretive capital expenditure and stronger housing markets, particularly in Denmark and Czechia. The fair value of investment properties at year-end was SEK 324,429 million, down from SEK 335,422 million after divestments and currency effects. Privatisation activity remained significant: SEK 10,664 million of sales were completed, covering 2,466 units at a 28.9% premium to book value. Total realised gains from property divestments increased to SEK 2,207 million from SEK 1,687 million.

Profit before tax increased to SEK 16,012 million from SEK 10,403 million, supported by higher property gains, fair-value gains and a SEK 3,996 million foreign-exchange gain, compared with a loss in 2024. However, the group recorded a SEK 3,707 million goodwill impairment, primarily related to Germany and Sweden. Profit for the year consequently rose to SEK 13,056 million from SEK 7,621 million. Total equity increased modestly to SEK 150,557 million, while currency translation losses of SEK 8,857 million limited comprehensive income.

The balance sheet strengthened through debt reduction and asset sales. Net loan-to-value improved to 52.2% from 56.4%, net debt to total assets fell to 48.0% from 51.0%, and the interest coverage ratio increased to 2.0x from 1.9x. Interest expense was broadly stable at SEK 6,536 million, although the average interest rate increased to 3.43% from 3.01%. Heimstaden Bostad completed approximately SEK 59.5 billion of financing and ended the year with SEK 23.4 billion of unused credit facilities. Operating cash flow was SEK 2,742 million, while year-end cash stood at SEK 4,179 million.

Sustainability investments continued to support operational efficiency and financing access. Weather-corrected energy intensity declined 10%, and the group reported 14.2% progress against its emissions-reduction baseline. Green assets totalled approximately SEK 75.9 billion and outstanding green bonds reached SEK 13.8 billion. Subsequent to year-end, Heimstaden expects an accounting loss of approximately SEK 3 billion when its Kojamo ownership falls below 20%. The company proposed carrying forward all available earnings and paying no dividends.
