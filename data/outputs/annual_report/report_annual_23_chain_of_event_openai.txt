TKM Grupp AS reported a weaker financial performance in 2025 amid difficult economic conditions and intense retail competition. Revenue declined 2.6% to €919.6 million from €944.6 million in 2024, while profit before tax fell 31.4% to €24.3 million. Net profit decreased 36.1% to €17.5 million, reducing earnings per share from €0.67 to €0.43. EBITDA declined 14.7% to €77.4 million and operating profit fell from €47.6 million to €34.9 million, reducing the operating margin from 5% to 4% and the net margin from 3% to 2%. Gross margin nevertheless improved slightly from 27.5% to 27.7%.

The largest deterioration came from car trade. Segment revenue fell 11.9% to €176.9 million, EBITDA declined 40.3% to €8.1 million and net profit fell 54.4% to €4.3 million. The report explicitly links this decline to Estonia’s motor vehicle tax, uncertainty before its introduction and reduced consumer confidence. New passenger-car sales in Estonia fell 48.6% for the year and 62.4% in the fourth quarter, although stronger markets in Latvia and Lithuania partly offset the decline.

Supermarket revenue was broadly stable, increasing 0.3% to €611.9 million, but net profit fell 38.6% to €9.5 million. Lower sales volumes, increased promotional activity, weaker purchasing power and additional costs associated with the Jõgeva Selver renovation affected profitability. Department-store revenue declined 1.1% to €103.0 million and the segment’s net loss widened to €0.5 million, while security revenue fell 8.1% to €20.1 million and the segment recorded a small loss. Real estate was the main positive contributor: revenue increased 5.1% to €7.7 million and net profit rose 20.5% to €6.3 million, supported by logistics-centre rental income and lower borrowing costs, although the prior year included €4.0 million of one-off gains.

Cost control limited the impact of weaker sales. Labour costs increased 3.7%, while other operating expenses rose only 0.2%. Finance costs declined by €1.5 million, and income-tax expense fell by €1.2 million, supporting net profit. Operating cash flow remained strong at €65.6 million, but cash declined to €29.5 million from €45.5 million after €25.5 million of investments and €26.5 million of dividends paid.

Strategic investments included new KIA and Škoda facilities, store refurbishments and digital-system upgrades. In January 2026, the Group agreed to acquire Rohe Auto, SKO Motors and a related real-estate company, subject to conditions, to expand its Škoda operations and strengthen the car segment. The proposed 2025 dividend is €0.60 per share, below the €0.65 paid for 2024.
