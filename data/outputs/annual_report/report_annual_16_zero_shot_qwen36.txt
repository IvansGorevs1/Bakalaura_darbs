Nordic Fibreboard AS reported a challenging financial year in 2025, characterized by a decline in profitability and negative operational cash flows despite stable top-line revenue. Consolidated net sales decreased by 3.4% to €7.33 million from €7.59 million in 2024. This slight reduction was primarily driven by a drop in sales to non-EU regions, particularly Africa and Asia, while sales within the European Union, which constitute 92% of total revenue, remained relatively flat. Revenue from real estate management also fell significantly to €3 thousand from €25 thousand in the prior year, reflecting the termination of rental agreements in 2024.

The company’s operational performance deteriorated, resulting in a consolidated EBITDA of negative €0.42 million, a sharp decline from the negative €0.03 million reported in 2024. The EBITDA margin contracted by 6 percentage points to negative 6%. This negative result was largely attributable to a planned factory shutdown in December 2025 to transition to a new gas boiler house, which incurred ongoing personnel and maintenance costs without generating production output. In 2024, a similar shutdown occurred due to force majeure weather damage. Additionally, underutilized production capacity increased the per-unit cost of goods, further pressuring margins. Consequently, the operating loss widened to €0.94 million from €0.55 million in 2024, and the net loss increased to €1.15 million from €0.79 million.

Total assets grew to €9.3 million from €8.3 million in 2024, driven by investments in property, plant, and equipment, as well as increases in inventories and receivables. However, the group’s liquidity position remained under pressure, with working capital turning negative by €2.7 million compared to €0.7 million in the previous year. Operating cash flow was negative €0.5 million, reflecting the operational losses and changes in working capital. To address liquidity needs and fund investments, the company executed a share issue in October 2025, raising €2.0 million. This capital increase boosted equity to €4.46 million and reduced the debt-to-equity ratio from 56% to 52%. Total borrowings decreased to €3.2 million from €3.7 million, although the loan from Coop Pank AS was classified as short-term due to covenant non-fulfillment.

Strategically, the group simplified its structure by merging its subsidiary Pärnu Riverside Development OÜ into the parent company in June 2025. The real estate segment continues to focus on the Admirali Quarter development project in Pärnu, where design works are complete and building permits have been issued. Following the balance sheet date, the company entered into a preliminary agreement to sell ten properties related to this project, aiming to liquidate negative working capital and fund future obligations. The workforce was reduced to 60 employees from 68 in 2024, while labor costs increased to €1.9 million due to wage adjustments. The company anticipates stable demand for fibreboards in the near term and continues to invest in energy-efficient production technologies.
