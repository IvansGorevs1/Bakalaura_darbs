Tallinna Vesi reported improved financial performance in 2025, with growth in revenue, operating profit and profit before tax. Group sales increased 11.4% to €72.48 million, supported by a 6.9% increase in water-services revenue to €62.31 million and a 62.0% increase in construction-services revenue to €8.46 million. Private-customer sales in the main service area rose 18.3% to €31.84 million, partly reflecting higher consumption and tariff changes. Business-customer sales declined 2.7% to €16.76 million following reductions in business tariffs as prices were harmonised with private-customer rates. Revenue from customers outside the main service area increased 13.8% to €7.71 million.

Costs increased broadly in line with revenue. Cost of goods and services sold rose 12.0% to €42.96 million, mainly because construction-services costs increased 74.5% and staff costs rose 6.8%. Depreciation and amortisation increased 11.9% to €9.08 million. Direct production costs were broadly unchanged at €9.90 million: higher chemical and pollution-tax costs were largely offset by lower electricity costs. Electricity expenses fell 6.9%, partly because the combined heat and power plant generated energy for the wastewater treatment process.

Gross profit rose 10.5% to €29.52 million, while operating profit increased 11.8% to €21.22 million. The EBITDA margin improved slightly to 42.70% from 42.27%, and the operating margin increased to 29.27% from 29.17%. Net financial expenses declined to €3.78 million from €4.20 million as Euribor rates fell. Consequently, profit before tax increased 18.0% to €17.43 million. Net profit rose 7.2% to €14.24 million, although the increase was moderated by a higher income-tax rate on dividend payments. Earnings per share increased from €0.66 to €0.71.

Investment activity expanded significantly. The Group invested €55.74 million in fixed assets, compared with €49.53 million in 2024, primarily to develop and renew water, wastewater and stormwater infrastructure. Operating cash flow increased to €30.83 million from €26.25 million, while year-end cash rose to €11.29 million from €3.59 million. Capital spending was financed partly through additional borrowing: outstanding loans increased to €160 million from €118 million, and the debt-to-assets ratio rose to 66.02% from 61.71%.

Operational developments included commissioning the combined heat and power plant, which produced 9,987 MWh of electricity and 6,933 MWh of heat and covered 43.8% of electricity consumption at the wastewater treatment plant and main pumping station. The company also laid or reconstructed 45.1 kilometres of pipes and continued smart-meter and stormwater-system investments. Overall, 2025 was characterised by sustained revenue and profit growth alongside substantially higher investment and leverage.
