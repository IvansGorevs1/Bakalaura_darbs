Coop Pank delivered strong business growth in 2025, although profitability declined as falling interest rates reduced income. Net profit decreased 11% year-on-year to €28.7 million, while earnings per share fell from €0.31 to €0.28 and return on equity declined from 16.2% to 12.9%. Total net operating income fell 3% to €79.4 million, mainly because net interest income decreased from €77.6 million to €73.3 million as the lower-interest-rate environment reduced margins. Net interest margin declined from 3.8% to 3.0%. Net fee and commission income increased 4% to €4.5 million, and net other income rose to €1.5 million from near zero in 2024. Operating expenses increased 2% to €41.5 million, causing the cost-to-income ratio to worsen from 49.6% to 52.3%. Credit loss allowance nevertheless improved to €3.3 million from €4.6 million, reflecting continued low credit losses and a low-risk loan portfolio.

Business volumes expanded substantially. The loan portfolio grew €340 million, or 19%, to €2.11 billion, exceeding €2 billion for the first time. Business loans increased 27% to €986 million, while home loans and other private loans rose 17% to €863 million. Customer deposits increased 9% to €2.05 billion, with demand deposits up 11% and term deposits up 8%. The customer base grew by 19,000, or 9%, to 227,000. Management attributed the strong loan growth to improved confidence among businesses and households, declining interest rates, stable energy prices, greater predictability of tax changes, and the beginning of postponed investments as Estonia entered a post-recession recovery phase.

The bank strengthened its funding and growth capacity during the year. It issued €250 million of four-year covered bonds in March 2025, its first international covered-bond issuance, providing a stable long-term funding source and allowing it to reduce more expensive term and foreign deposits. The bank also entered into an EIB Group guarantee arrangement expected to enable up to €249 million of new business lending, particularly for projects supporting gender equality and environmental sustainability. A €5 million subordinated loan agreement with the European Energy Efficiency Fund was signed in December and drawn in February 2026.

Capital remained compliant with all regulatory requirements, although capital ratios declined as risk-weighted assets expanded: the CET1 ratio fell from 14.28% to 14.15%, and the total capital ratio from 19.41% to 18.54%. The bank maintained its dividend policy, paying €7.2 million, or €0.07 per share, from 2024 profit, and proposed a 2025 dividend of €0.065 per share, totaling €6.7 million.
