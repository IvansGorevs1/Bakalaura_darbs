Heimstaden reported stronger underlying performance in 2025 despite flat headline rental income and continued portfolio disposals. Rental income was SEK 15,916 million, essentially unchanged from SEK 15,919 million in 2024. Indexation, tenant improvements and occupancy gains supported rental growth, but foreign exchange movements, privatisation and other portfolio sales offset these benefits. Like-for-like rental income growth remained robust at 4.9%, although it declined from 5.6% in 2024. Real economic occupancy was unchanged at 98.5% for the year and reached 98.7% in the fourth quarter.

Profitability improved through lower operating costs. Property expenses declined 6.1% to SEK 4,378 million, supported by cost controls, digitalisation, efficiency improvements and the smaller portfolio resulting from disposals. Consequently, net operating income increased 2.6% to SEK 11,475 million, while the NOI margin rose to 72.1% from 70.3%. Capital expenditure under the main investment pillars increased to SEK 3,756 million from SEK 3,449 million, particularly reflecting higher maintenance and sustainability spending. Tenant improvement investments generated SEK 94 million of additional rental income at an average yield on cost of 10.8%.

Property values increased by SEK 9,498 million, or 3.0%, compared with 2.6% in 2024. Growth was supported by NOI improvements and value-accretive capital expenditure, as well as strong ownership housing markets, particularly in Denmark. The year-end investment property value was SEK 324,429 million, down from SEK 335,422 million because of disposals and currency translation effects. Privatisation sales reached SEK 10,664 million, with 2,466 units sold at a 28.9% premium to book value. Realised gains from property disposals rose to SEK 2,207 million from SEK 1,687 million.

Profit before tax increased to SEK 16,012 million from SEK 10,403 million, driven by higher property-related earnings, fair value gains and a SEK 3,996 million foreign exchange gain compared with a SEK 2,038 million loss in 2024. However, the Group recorded a SEK 3,707 million goodwill impairment, primarily because higher discount rates reduced recoverable values for Akelius-related cash-generating units. Despite this charge, profit for the year rose to SEK 13,056 million from SEK 7,621 million.

The balance sheet strengthened through disposals and debt reduction. Net LTV improved to 52.2% from 56.4%, net debt to total assets fell to 48.0% from 51.0%, and ICR increased to 2.0x from 1.9x. Heimstaden Bostad completed approximately SEK 59.5 billion of financing transactions, while Heimstaden executed liability-management issues in euros and Swedish kronor. No preference-share dividend is proposed. A post-year-end Kojamo transaction is expected to reduce Heimstaden’s ownership below 20% and may result in an accounting loss of approximately SEK 3 billion.
