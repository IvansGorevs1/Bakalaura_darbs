Coop Pank reported weaker financial results for the 2025 financial year, primarily impacted by a lower-interest economic environment that offset strong business volume growth. Net profit for the period decreased by 11% to 28.7 million euros compared with 32.2 million euros in the previous year. Total net operating income also declined slightly by 3% to 79.4 million euros from 81.9 million euros, driven by a reduction in net interest income to 73.2 million euros from 77.5 million euros. Conversely, net fee and commission income rose to 4.5 million euros compared with 4.4 million euros, and net other income improved to 1.5 million euros from zero. Operating expenses increased to 41.5 million euros from 40.6 million euros, contributing to a higher cost-to-income ratio of 52.3% compared with 49.6% in 2024. Credit loss allowances improved, decreasing to 3.3 million euros from 4.6 million euros, while income tax expense increased to 5.8 million euros from 4.5 million euros. Return on equity stood at 12.9% compared with 16.2% in the prior year, and return on assets was 1.2% compared with 1.6%.

Despite the decline in net profit, the bank experienced robust growth across its core business volumes. The customer base expanded by 19,000 clients, or 9%, reaching 227,000 customers by the end of 2025. Total assets grew significantly to 2.70 billion euros compared with 2.19 billion euros at the end of 2024. For the first time in the bank's history, the net loan portfolio exceeded 2 billion euros, increasing by 19% to 2.11 billion euros from 1.77 billion euros. Loan growth was broad-based, led by business loans increasing by 207 million euros or 27%, and home loans growing by 125 million euros or 17%. Customer deposits also grew by 9% to 2.05 billion euros, with demand deposits increasing by 11% and term deposits growing by 8%. Shareholders' equity increased to 234.5 million euros from 211.6 million euros.

The reporting period featured several major funding and strategic developments. In the first quarter of 2025, Coop Pank issued its inaugural covered bonds on the Irish stock exchange amounting to 250 million euros with a four-year maturity under a newly established 750 million euro program, providing a stable long-term funding source that allowed the bank to intentionally reduce more expensive term deposits. Additionally, towards the end of the year, the bank entered into a synthetic securitization guarantee agreement with the European Investment Bank Group to optimize regulatory capital and enable up to 249 million euros in new loans for sustainable and gender-equality projects in Estonia. The bank also secured a 5 million euro subordinated loan from the European Energy Efficiency Fund.

Capital adequacy remained strong and fully compliant with regulatory requirements. As of December 31, 2025, the bank's Common Equity Tier 1 capital ratio was 14.15%, the Tier 1 capital ratio was 16.11%, and the total capital adequacy ratio was 18.54%. Following its dividend policy, the management proposed a dividend payment of 0.065 euros per share for the 2025 financial year, representing a total net payout of 6.7 million euros, consistent with returning value to shareholders while supporting ongoing business expansion and strategic targets aimed at reaching a 10% loan market share and 60 million euros in net profit by 2030.
