TKM Grupp AS reported a weaker financial performance in 2025 amid a challenging Estonian economic environment, low consumer confidence, intense retail competition and the introduction of Estonia’s motor vehicle tax. Revenue declined 2.6% to €919.6 million from €944.6 million in 2024. Profit before tax fell 31.4% to €24.3 million, while net profit decreased 36.1% to €17.5 million. Earnings per share declined from €0.67 to €0.43. EBITDA fell 14.7% to €77.4 million and operating profit dropped 26.6% to €34.9 million, reducing the operating margin from 5% to 4% and the net margin from 3% to 2%.

The car trade segment was the main drag on results. Its revenue decreased 11.9% to €176.9 million, EBITDA fell 40.3% to €8.1 million and net profit declined 54.4% to €4.3 million. Estonia’s new passenger-car market contracted 48.6%, with the motor vehicle tax and weakened consumer confidence cited as significant factors. More stable sales in Latvia and Lithuania partly offset the decline. The supermarkets segment generated modest revenue growth of 0.3% to €611.9 million, but net profit fell 38.6% to €9.5 million, reflecting lower sales volumes, increased promotional activity, wage-cost pressure and one-off costs associated with the Jõgeva Selver refurbishment. Department-store revenue declined 1.1% to €103.0 million and the segment recorded a €0.5 million net loss. Security revenue fell 8.1% to €20.1 million and the segment recorded a small loss. Real-estate revenue increased 5.1% to €7.7 million, while net profit rose 20.5% to €6.3 million; the comparison was affected by €4.0 million of one-off gains in 2024.

Cost control helped limit the impact of lower sales. Labour costs increased 3.7%, while other operating expenses rose only 0.2%. Lower borrowing costs reduced finance expenses by €1.5 million, and income tax expense fell by €1.2 million. Operating cash flow remained strong at €65.6 million, although cash and cash equivalents declined to €29.5 million from €45.5 million, largely reflecting €62.6 million of financing cash outflows, including dividends and debt-related payments.

The Group invested €25.5 million during the year, including new and refurbished stores, digital systems, and car dealerships in Estonia and Lithuania. A new multi-brand KIA and Škoda centre opened in Vilnius, while negotiations led to a January 2026 agreement to acquire Škoda dealerships in Tallinn and Harju County, subject to completion conditions. The Group’s loyalty programme exceeded 750,000 customers. Management described 2025 as a year of stable progress despite difficult conditions and maintained a cautiously positive outlook for 2026.
