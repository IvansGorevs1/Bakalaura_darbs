TKM Grupp AS reported weaker financial performance in 2025 amid low consumer confidence, intense retail competition, higher price sensitivity and the introduction of Estonia’s motor vehicle tax. Revenue declined 2.6% to €919.6 million from €944.6 million in 2024. Profit before tax fell 31.4% to €24.3 million, while net profit decreased 36.1% to €17.5 million. Earnings per share declined from €0.67 to €0.43, and the net margin fell from 3% to 2%. EBITDA decreased 14.7% to €77.4 million, reducing the EBITDA margin from 9.6% to 8.4%; operating profit fell from €47.6 million to €34.9 million.

The main pressure came from the car trade segment, where revenue declined 11.9% to €176.9 million and net profit fell 54.4% to €4.3 million. Estonia’s new passenger-car market contracted by 48.6%, with the decline worsening to 62.4% in the fourth quarter following demand being brought forward into 2024 ahead of the motor vehicle tax. More stable sales in Latvia and Lithuania partially offset the Estonian downturn. The Group sold 5,140 new vehicles during the year and opened a multi-brand KIA and Škoda dealership in Vilnius.

Supermarket revenue was broadly stable at €611.9 million, up 0.3%, but net profit declined 38.6% to €9.5 million. Results were affected by lower sales volumes, a greater share of promotional products, weaker purchasing power and additional costs related to the renovation of Jõgeva Selver. Department-store revenue decreased 1.1% to €103.0 million, and the segment remained loss-making, with net loss increasing to €0.5 million. Security revenue fell 8.1% to €20.1 million and the segment recorded a small loss. Real estate was comparatively resilient: revenue rose 5.1% to €7.7 million and net profit increased 20.5% to €6.3 million, although prior-year profit had included €4.0 million of one-off gains from property revaluations and disposals.

Cost control partly cushioned the revenue decline. Labour costs increased 3.7%, while other operating expenses grew only 0.2%; lower borrowing costs reduced finance expenses by €1.5 million. Operating cash flow remained strong at €65.6 million, although cash declined to €29.5 million after €25.5 million of investments, dividends and financing outflows. Total debt was €320.5 million, and working capital became negative at €14.2 million, largely because €32.1 million of loan maturities were classified as current and are planned for refinancing in 2026.

The Group continued investing in stores, digital systems, dealerships and real estate. A January 2026 agreement to acquire Rohe Auto, SKO Motors and a related property company is expected to expand the Škoda business in Tallinn and Harju County once completion conditions are met. Management proposed a 2025 dividend of €0.60 per share, compared with €0.65 paid for 2024.
