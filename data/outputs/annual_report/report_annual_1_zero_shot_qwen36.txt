AAC Clyde Space reported net sales of SEK 295.3 million for the full year 2025, representing a 16 percent decrease from SEK 352.9 million in 2024. This decline was primarily driven by a significant drop in the Products & Missions segment, where net sales fell by 29 percent to SEK 245.0 million from SEK 346.0 million in the prior year. Conversely, the Data & Services segment demonstrated strong growth, with net sales increasing by 78 percent to SEK 80.1 million from SEK 44.9 million in 2024. The company achieved positive EBITDA for the third consecutive year, reporting SEK 17.5 million in 2025 compared to SEK 46.7 million in 2024. The EBITDA margin narrowed considerably, reflecting the lower revenue volume in the hardware-focused Products & Missions business. The operating result after depreciation and impairment was negative at SEK -40.0 million, a substantial deterioration from the negative SEK -4.0 million recorded in 2024. The loss for the period attributable to parent company shareholders amounted to SEK -46.1 million, compared to a loss of SEK -5.6 million in the previous year. Earnings per share declined to negative SEK -7.63 from negative SEK -1.00. Operating cash flow turned negative at SEK -72.6 million, a sharp reversal from the positive SEK 56.8 million generated in 2024, largely due to timing effects on project deliveries and working capital changes. Cash and cash equivalents decreased to SEK 30.1 million from SEK 49.7 million at the end of 2024. The equity ratio improved to 80 percent from 71 percent, supported by two directed share issues that raised approximately SEK 115 million before transaction costs. These capital raises strengthened the balance sheet and funded ongoing investments in satellite constellations. Order backlog stood at SEK 352 million at year-end, down from SEK 641 million in 2024, though this figure is expected to rise significantly following post-year-end events. Key business developments included the operational launch of Sedna-1 and Sedna-2 satellites, which strengthened the maritime data network, and the beginning of assembly for the first VIREON Earth Observation satellites. The company also signed a Phase 1 contract with the European Space Agency for the INFLECION maritime intelligence programme. A major milestone occurred after the reporting period when AAC Clyde Space received a EUR 76.3 million order from OHB Sweden for microwave radiometers for the EPS-Sterna weather constellation, the largest single order in the company’s history. Additionally, the first two VIREON satellites were successfully launched in early 2026. The year was marked by programme-related delays, including postponed recognition of revenue in a customer project and delays in the EPS-Sterna programme initiation, which impacted 2025 revenues and cash flow. Despite these challenges, the company’s strategic shift toward recurring data revenues and the strengthening of its institutional shareholder base, including a significant investment from Bonnier Capital, position the company for long-term growth in the space data market.
