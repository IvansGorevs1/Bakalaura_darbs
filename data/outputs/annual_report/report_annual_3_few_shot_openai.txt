Coop Pank reported continued expansion in 2025, although lower interest rates reduced profitability. Net profit declined 11% to €28.7 million from €32.2 million in 2024. The decrease was primarily driven by lower net interest income, which fell to €73.3 million from €77.6 million as the interest-rate environment weakened. Net interest margin declined to 3.0% from 3.8%. This more than offset growth in business volumes and modest increases in fee and commission income, which rose 4% to €4.5 million, and net other income, which increased to €1.5 million from virtually zero.

Total net operating income decreased 3% to €79.4 million. Operating expenses increased 2% to €41.5 million, including higher payroll and information-system costs, while marketing expenses and contributions to the Deposit Guarantee Fund declined. The cost-to-income ratio consequently worsened to 52.3% from 49.6%. Credit loss allowances improved to €3.3 million from €4.6 million, reflecting continued low credit losses and a loan portfolio that management described as low risk. Income tax expense increased to €5.8 million from €4.5 million, with the effective tax rate rising to 17% from 12%.

Growth in lending remained strong. The net loan portfolio increased 19%, or €340 million, to €2.11 billion, exceeding €2 billion for the first time. Business loans grew 27% to €986 million, while mortgage and other private loans rose 17% to €863 million. Customer deposits increased 9% to €2.05 billion, supported by 11% growth in demand deposits and 8% growth in term deposits. The customer base expanded by 19,000, or 9%, to 227,000.

The operating environment improved as Estonia returned to modest GDP growth of 0.7%, inflation receded, and the six-month Euribor declined to 2.1% by year-end. These conditions supported renewed demand for household and business lending, although they also compressed interest income. The bank issued its first international covered bonds, raising €250 million through a four-year issue, thereby diversifying funding and supporting loan growth. It also entered into an agreement with the European Investment Bank Group that is expected to enable up to €249 million of new business lending.

Capital and liquidity remained solid, with a CET1 ratio of 14.15%, total capital adequacy of 18.54%, LCR of 202.3%, and NSFR of 119.6%. Shareholders’ equity increased to €234.5 million from €211.6 million. The bank paid €7.2 million in dividends for 2024 and proposed a 2025 dividend of €0.065 per share, totaling €6.7 million. Overall, 2025 was characterized by rapid balance-sheet growth, stable asset quality, and strong funding and capital positions, but weaker earnings and returns due to falling interest rates.
