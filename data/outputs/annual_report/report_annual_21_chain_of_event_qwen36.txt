Tallinna Sadam Group reported a profit of EUR 22.5 million for the financial year ended 31 December 2025, representing a 17.3% increase from the EUR 19.2 million recorded in 2024. Total revenue decreased slightly by 0.8% to EUR 118.7 million, primarily due to a significant drop in charter fees from the icebreaker Botnica in the Other segment. However, the Group’s financial performance improved substantially as operating expenses fell more sharply than revenues. Operating expenses decreased by EUR 3.8 million, or 4%, to EUR 88.0 million, driven largely by a EUR 4.0 million reduction in technical maintenance and repair costs. This decline in repairs was partly attributable to lower activity on the icebreaker Botnica and the receipt of an insurance indemnity for a technical failure that occurred in the previous year. Consequently, operating profit grew by 12.2% to EUR 32.7 million, and adjusted EBITDA increased by 6.4% to EUR 56.5 million, with the adjusted EBITDA margin expanding from 44.4% to 47.6%.

The cargo segment demonstrated strong recovery, with cargo volumes rising by 5.1% to 13.8 million tonnes. This growth was led by a substantial 47% increase in liquid bulk volumes, which recovered from previous declines, and a 5% rise in dry bulk. These operational improvements contributed to a 19% increase in the adjusted EBITDA of the Cargo harbours segment. The passenger harbours segment also performed well, with the number of passengers growing by 1.0% to 8.3 million. Revenue in this segment increased by 4%, supported by higher passenger numbers on the Tallinn–Helsinki route and an 18% increase in cruise ship calls, which reached a record number of winter cruise calls. The Ferry segment, providing domestic services to Estonian islands, saw revenue grow by 1% despite a 0.6% decrease in voyages, as fee indexing offset the volume decline. However, the segment’s adjusted EBITDA fell by 2% due to rising personnel and development costs that outpaced the revenue growth.

The Other segment, which includes the icebreaker Botnica, experienced a 35% decline in revenue to EUR 8.0 million, as the vessel’s utilization rate dropped to 49% and the number of charter days decreased by 42. Despite the revenue drop, operating profit in this segment improved due to significantly lower operating expenses, including reduced fuel costs and lease expenses. The Group’s net debt decreased by 15.5% to EUR 141.7 million, aided by lower interest rates and loan repayments. Financial expenses fell by 30% to EUR 5.8 million. The Group paid dividends of EUR 19.2 million, equivalent to 100% of the previous year’s profit. Capital investments totaled EUR 33.2 million, a 14.4% decrease from the prior year, with major spending directed toward infrastructure in Paldiski South Harbour and Muuga Harbour. The equity to assets ratio remained stable at 61%, and the share price increased by 22.7% to EUR 1.296 by year-end.
