AS Tallinna Sadam Group delivered improved profitability in 2025 despite a slight decline in revenue. Revenue decreased by EUR 0.9 million, or 0.8%, to EUR 118.7 million, mainly because lower utilisation of the icebreaker Botnica reduced charter-fee income. This was partly offset by higher vessel dues, passenger fees, lease income, ferry-service revenue and cargo charges. Vessel dues increased 8% to EUR 34.1 million, while Botnica charter fees fell 34% to EUR 7.9 million as charter days declined by 42.

Operating performance strengthened as expenses fell faster than revenue. Operating expenses decreased 10% to EUR 36.4 million, primarily because of lower maintenance and repair, lease and fuel costs, while an EUR 0.9 million insurance indemnity related to Botnica also supported results. Personnel expenses increased 3% to EUR 26.6 million despite the average workforce declining from 438 to 425 employees. Operating profit rose 12.2% to EUR 32.7 million, and the operating margin improved from 24.4% to 27.6%. Adjusted EBITDA increased 6.4% to EUR 56.5 million, raising the margin from 44.4% to 47.6%.

Net profit grew 17.3% to EUR 22.5 million, while earnings per share increased from EUR 0.07 to EUR 0.09. Profit before income tax rose 25% to EUR 27.9 million, supported by a 30% reduction in net finance costs to EUR 4.8 million as interest rates and borrowings declined. The increase in net profit was moderated by higher dividend-related income tax following the abolition of Estonia’s lower tax rate for regular dividends and by the associate Green Marine reporting a loss of EUR 0.05 million, compared with a EUR 0.5 million profit in 2024.

Business volumes generally recovered. Cargo throughput increased 5.1% to 13.8 million tonnes, driven mainly by a 47% rise in liquid bulk and moderate growth in dry bulk, although ro-ro volumes declined 2%. Passenger numbers increased 1.0% to 8.3 million, with growth on the Tallinn–Helsinki route and a 25% increase in cruise passengers. Domestic ferry services carried 2.5 million passengers and 1.2 million vehicles, despite 0.6% fewer voyages. In contrast, Botnica’s utilisation rate fell to 49%, contributing to weaker revenue in the Other segment.

The Group invested EUR 33.2 million, 14.4% less than in 2024, while operating cash flow increased to EUR 53.7 million. Cash and equivalents rose to EUR 32.0 million, and net debt declined 15.5% to EUR 141.7 million. Equity increased slightly to EUR 380.9 million, representing 61% of total assets. The Group paid EUR 19.2 million in dividends, or EUR 0.073 per share, and proposed the same dividend for 2025. Management expects moderate passenger and cargo growth in 2026 but anticipates pressure from higher input costs, financing needs and the absence of certain one-off benefits recorded in 2025.
