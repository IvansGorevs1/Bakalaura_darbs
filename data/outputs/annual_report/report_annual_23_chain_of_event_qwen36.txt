TKM Grupp AS reported a challenging financial year in 2025, characterized by a decline in revenue and profitability driven by a difficult economic environment in Estonia. Consolidated sales revenue decreased by 2.6% to 919.6 million euros from 944.6 million euros in 2024. This contraction was primarily attributable to the car trade segment, where revenue fell by 11.9% to 176.9 million euros. The sharp decline in the Estonian automotive market, which saw new passenger car sales drop by 48.6% year-on-year, was directly linked to the introduction of the motor vehicle tax and resulting consumer uncertainty. While the Group’s subsidiaries in Latvia and Lithuania experienced growth in the car market, this was insufficient to fully offset the significant downturn in the Estonian operations.

Net profit declined substantially by 36.1% to 17.5 million euros, compared to 27.5 million euros in the previous year. Profit before tax dropped by 31.4% to 24.3 million euros. The reduction in net profit was influenced by several factors. The supermarket segment, the Group’s largest revenue contributor, saw a slight revenue increase of 0.3% to 611.9 million euros, but net profit fell by 38.6% to 9.5 million euros. This profit decline was partly due to a comparative base that included one-off gains in 2024, such as a 4.0 million euro profit from the sale of non-core properties in Latvia and property revaluations. In 2025, the supermarket segment faced pressure from high price sensitivity, increased promotional activity, and lower sales volumes of everyday goods. The department stores segment recorded a net loss of 0.5 million euros, worsening from a small loss in 2024, due to warm winter weather reducing demand for seasonal clothing and aggressive pricing campaigns in the beauty retail sub-segment.

Despite the operational challenges, the Group maintained stable progress through strategic investments totaling 25.5 million euros. These investments focused on modernizing sales environments, including the refurbishment of the Jõgeva Selver store and the Kaubamaja Children’s World, as well as the construction of a new multi-brand car center in Lithuania and a bodywork workshop in Peetri, Estonia. The Group also advanced its strategic position in the car segment by negotiating the acquisition of Škoda distribution rights in Tallinn and Harju County, with the transaction expected to complete in early 2026.

Financially, the Group managed its liquidity and capital structure effectively. Cash and cash equivalents decreased to 29.5 million euros from 45.5 million euros in 2024, reflecting higher working capital requirements and debt repayments. The debt-to-equity ratio remained stable at 52%. Interest expenses decreased by 1.5 million euros due to lower borrowing costs, providing some relief to profitability. The Group’s loyalty program, Partner Card, grew to over 750,000 users, reinforcing customer retention. Earnings per share decreased to 0.43 euros from 0.67 euros in 2024. The Management Board proposed a dividend of 0.60 euros per share for the 2025 financial year. Overall, while 2025 results were impacted by specific tax-driven market contractions and one-off items in the prior year, the Group demonstrated resilience through cost control and strategic expansion in the Baltic region.
