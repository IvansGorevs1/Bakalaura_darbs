Gigasun reported continued operational growth and improved profitability in 2025, although the Group remained loss-making after financial items. Total revenue increased 9.8% to SEK 239.7 million, while net revenue from electricity sales rose 9.9% to SEK 203.2 million. Revenue growth was supported by higher electricity production and a larger installed base, but was partly offset by lower average prices caused by increased sales to the grid, revised electricity tariffs in the second half of the year and adverse currency effects. Currency movements reduced total revenue by SEK 18.0 million. Subsidies contributed SEK 32.7 million and represented a significant portion of other operating income.

Operational capacity expanded by 55.7 MW during the year, reaching 395.8 MW across 216 solar installations at year-end, compared with 340.1 MW across 200 installations in 2024. Electricity production increased 30% to 327.8 million kWh, contributing to an estimated reduction of approximately 161,000 tonnes of CO2-related emissions. The company signed 17 contracts representing 41.8 MW of capacity, although signed orders at year-end declined to 18.9 MW from 38.8 MW.

Profitability improved despite higher operating costs. Operating expenses increased 5.5% to SEK 166.2 million, primarily because the larger installed base resulted in higher depreciation and maintenance costs. EBITDA rose 12.3% to SEK 163.7 million, with the margin increasing from 67% to 68%. Operating profit increased 21.1% to SEK 73.5 million, and the EBIT margin improved from 28% to 31%. Interest expenses and similar financial charges declined to SEK 82.0 million from SEK 88.7 million as the refinancing of Chinese debt began to reduce financing costs. However, the result was affected by a SEK 3.8 million negative exchange-rate difference. Profit after tax improved to a loss of SEK 7.2 million, compared with a loss of SEK 32.9 million in 2024, while earnings per share improved to negative SEK 0.13 from negative SEK 0.58. Management stated that profit adjusted for the currency effect and a one-off cost was approximately SEK 0.1 million.

Cash flow from operations decreased to SEK 101.1 million from SEK 133.8 million, while investments in solar installations fell to SEK 169.9 million from SEK 301.7 million. Total cash flow was negative SEK 34.9 million, leaving cash of SEK 45.1 million at year-end. Interest-bearing debt declined to SEK 1,382.6 million, but the equity ratio weakened to 21% from 23%.

Financing remained a key issue. The company expanded its refinancing agreement with Agricultural Bank Financial Leasing by SEK 372 million, at an interest rate of 3.60% and with ten-year amortization, while the SOLT4 bond was extended to November 8, 2026. Management continued its strategic review and indicated that further financing in 2026 is important to support the business plan and maintain the going-concern assumption. Lower financing and construction costs are expected to offset the impact of reduced electricity tariffs and support gradually improved earnings.
