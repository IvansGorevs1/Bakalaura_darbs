AS Tallink Grupp reported a challenging financial year in 2025, characterized by a decline in revenue and profitability driven by subdued consumer confidence and geopolitical tensions in its core Baltic markets. The Group’s consolidated revenue decreased by 2.6% year-on-year to EUR 765.3 million, down from EUR 785.8 million in 2024. This contraction was primarily attributed to a drop in route operations revenue, which fell by EUR 13.7 million to EUR 625.2 million. Operational metrics reflected this weakness, with the number of passengers carried decreasing by 0.9% to 5.5 million and cargo units transported falling sharply by 19.2% to 245,004. Passenger vehicle numbers also declined by 2.2% to 760,473.

Profitability indicators showed a significant downturn compared to the previous period. EBITDA declined by EUR 45.1 million, or 25.7%, to EUR 130.1 million from EUR 175.2 million in 2024. Consequently, the audited net profit for the period dropped by 57.1% to EUR 17.3 million, compared to EUR 40.3 million in the prior year. Earnings per share fell to EUR 0.023 from EUR 0.054. The deterioration in results was largely influenced by lower onboard sales resulting from reduced passenger volumes and weak consumer spending. Additionally, personnel expenses increased by 4.3% to EUR 201.0 million, driven by collective agreement adjustments, while fuel and emission costs remained relatively stable at EUR 96.5 million.

Despite the operational headwinds, the Group’s financial position remained robust, aided by strategic asset management and cost efficiencies. A key positive factor was the alignment of the estimated useful lives of cruise and passenger vessels to 45 years, which reduced depreciation expense by EUR 18.0 million to EUR 79.8 million. The Group also completed the sale of three vessels, generating proceeds of EUR 78.8 million and helping to optimize the fleet. Net debt decreased substantially by EUR 105.3 million to EUR 432.4 million, and the net debt to EBITDA ratio stood at 3.3. Total liquidity at year-end was EUR 110.4 million, comprising cash, cash equivalents, and unused credit facilities.

The Group maintained its market-leading position on core routes, carrying approximately 48% of passengers and 41% of ro-ro cargo on the Tallinn-Helsinki route. The Estonia-Finland route saw a slight increase in passenger numbers (+1.8%) but a significant drop in cargo (-20.0%). In contrast, the Estonia-Sweden and Finland-Sweden routes experienced declines in both passenger and cargo volumes. The Group continued to focus on cost efficiency and optimized capacity, chartering out three vessels to generate additional revenue. Looking ahead, the Group faces continued macroeconomic uncertainty, including potential energy price shocks and geopolitical instability, but remains committed to long-term competitiveness and sustainability initiatives, such as the transition to liquefied biomethane for shuttle vessels.
