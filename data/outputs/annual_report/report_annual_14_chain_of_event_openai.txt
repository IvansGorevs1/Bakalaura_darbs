Lyko delivered continued top-line growth in 2025 but with weaker profitability. Net sales increased 11.3% to SEK 3,962.6 million from SEK 3,561.7 million in 2024, close to SEK 4 billion but below the company’s medium-term annual growth target of 15–20%. Growth was primarily driven by the Nordics, where revenue rose 12.0% to SEK 3,699.8 million and represented 93.4% of group sales. Europe declined 1.3% to SEK 130.7 million. Norway surpassed SEK 1 billion in annual revenue.

Gross profit increased to SEK 1,658.7 million from SEK 1,568.8 million, but the gross margin fell from 44.0% to 41.9%. EBIT declined from SEK 117.4 million to SEK 78.2 million, reducing the EBIT margin from 3.3% to 2.0%. EBITDA decreased from SEK 287.4 million to SEK 252.3 million. The group reported a loss of SEK 3.0 million, compared with a profit of SEK 21.0 million, and earnings per share fell from SEK 1.37 to negative SEK 0.20.

The report states that implementation of the new warehouse automation affected results during parts of the year. The Vansbro project, Lyko’s largest investment at approximately SEK 500 million, began ramp-up during the summer and autumn and is expected to be fully operational in early 2026. The system provides capacity of approximately 100,000 orders per day and is intended to support larger volumes and more cost-efficient logistics over time. Investments also included two new stores, the first Fab Store in Täby and continued digital development. The company expanded to 34 stores, including new locations in Finland and Norway, and reorganized its European operations by closing local offices in Germany and the Netherlands to reduce overhead costs.

Cash flow from operating activities improved substantially to SEK 527.5 million from SEK 209.7 million. However, investing cash flow increased to negative SEK 351.2 million, mainly due to warehouse automation, store openings and digital investments. Cash and cash equivalents rose to SEK 452.5 million from SEK 140.5 million, while net debt excluding IFRS 16 decreased to SEK 292.5 million from SEK 333.1 million. The equity ratio weakened from 20.3% to 14.8%, reflecting a larger balance sheet and increased borrowing.

The company operated in a market characterized by intense competition, structural shifts and profit warnings among industry players. Despite these conditions, Lyko reported market-share gains and increased brand awareness. Following year-end, Lyko announced a cost-saving programme expected to generate approximately SEK 100 million in annual savings, excluding restructuring costs. No dividend is proposed for 2025.
