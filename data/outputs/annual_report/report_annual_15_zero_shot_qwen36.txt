AS Merko Ehitus reported a significant decline in financial performance for the year ended 31 December 2025, driven primarily by the completion of large-scale construction projects in the prior year. Consolidated revenue decreased by 42.3% to EUR 310.9 million from EUR 539.0 million in 2024. This drop was largely attributable to a 68.8% reduction in revenue from Lithuania, where major defense and renewable energy contracts concluded, and a 24.0% decrease in Estonia. Conversely, revenue in Latvia increased by 68.9%. The construction services segment, which accounted for 76.4% of total revenue, saw its sales drop by 49.9% to EUR 237.6 million, while real estate development revenue rose slightly by 13.8% to EUR 73.4 million.

Profitability metrics reflected the lower revenue volume. Gross profit fell to EUR 55.9 million from EUR 95.9 million, though the gross margin remained stable at 18.0% compared to 17.8% in 2024. Operating profit decreased substantially to EUR 34.3 million from EUR 72.5 million, resulting in an operating profit margin of 11.0%, down from 13.4%. Net profit attributable to equity holders of the parent was EUR 39.9 million, a 38.3% decline from EUR 64.7 million in the previous year. The net margin improved slightly to 12.8% from 12.0%, supported by lower general and administrative expenses and reduced finance costs. EBITDA dropped to EUR 37.5 million from EUR 75.7 million.

Despite the revenue contraction, the company maintained a strong balance sheet. Total assets decreased to EUR 415.1 million from EUR 447.1 million, while equity increased to EUR 260.6 million from EUR 254.3 million. The equity ratio strengthened to 62.8% from 56.9%, and the debt ratio remained low at 8.0%. Cash and cash equivalents declined to EUR 41.4 million from EUR 91.9 million, primarily due to dividend payments of EUR 33.6 million and increased inventory investments for real estate development. The group’s secured order book grew significantly to EUR 466.9 million from EUR 340.6 million, indicating a robust pipeline for future revenue recognition, particularly from new contracts signed in the second half of 2025 and early 2026.

Key business developments included the successful completion of major projects such as the Rail Baltica Ülemiste terminal and various wind farm infrastructures. The group also secured its largest contracts in history for the Rūdninkai defense campus in Lithuania, which will contribute to revenue starting in 2026. Management highlighted that while global uncertainty and high interest rates restrained private investment, public sector demand in defense and infrastructure provided stability. The company fulfilled its long-term financial objectives, achieving a return on equity of 15.6% and maintaining an equity ratio above the 50% target. The Management Board proposed a dividend of EUR 1.25 per share for 2025, payable from retained earnings, reflecting a commitment to shareholder returns despite the cyclical downturn in construction volumes.
