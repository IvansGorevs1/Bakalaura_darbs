The company reported a significant decline in financial performance during the 2025 reporting period, primarily driven by the completion of major construction contracts in the previous year and a subsequent slowdown in new project commencement. Consolidated revenue decreased by 42.3% to EUR 310.9 million, down from EUR 539.0 million in 2024. This contraction was largely attributable to the construction services segment, where revenue fell by 49.9% to EUR 237.6 million, reflecting the natural cycle of large-scale project completions. In contrast, the real estate development segment showed resilience, with revenue increasing by 13.8% to EUR 73.4 million, supported by higher apartment sales volumes and improved market conditions in the Baltic states.

Profitability metrics also weakened considerably. Operating profit dropped by 52.7% to EUR 34.3 million, resulting in an operating margin of 11.0%, compared to 13.4% in the prior year. Net profit attributable to equity holders of the parent decreased by 38.3% to EUR 39.9 million, with a net margin of 12.8%. The decline in operating profit was primarily due to the expiration of highly profitable contracts from 2024 and a return to more typical sector margins in a competitive environment. However, the group maintained a healthy balance sheet, with equity increasing to EUR 260.6 million and the equity ratio rising to 62.8%, well above the long-term target of 50-70%. The debt ratio remained low at 8.0%, indicating a conservative capital structure.

Business developments were marked by strategic shifts in the order book and geographic revenue mix. The secured order book grew significantly to EUR 466.9 million from EUR 340.6 million, driven by new contracts in Estonia and Latvia, including major infrastructure and defense-related projects. Geographically, the revenue mix shifted, with Estonia becoming the largest contributor at 55% of total revenue, while Lithuania’s share decreased to 28%. The company also benefited from its joint venture investments, particularly in the Connecto infrastructure network, which contributed substantially to the net profit. Despite the revenue decline, the company initiated a new residential development pipeline, starting construction on more than three times as many new apartments as in 2024, positioning itself for potential growth in 2026 and beyond. The management proposed a dividend of EUR 1.25 per share for 2025, reflecting a 55% payout ratio from retained earnings, consistent with the group’s long-term financial objectives. Overall, the period was characterized by a transitional phase where high-volume projects concluded, leading to lower reported results, while the order book and real estate activities provided a foundation for future stability.
