AS Coop Pank reported its financial results for the 2025 financial year, showing a net profit of 28,724 thousand euros, which represents an 11% decrease compared to the 32,178 thousand euros reported in 2024. Total net operating income also declined slightly by 3% from 81,900 thousand euros in 2024 to 79,400 thousand euros in 2025. This decrease was primarily driven by a reduction in net interest income, which fell from 77,570 thousand euros in 2024 to 73,269 thousand euros in 2025, influenced by the lower-interest economic environment. Net fee and commission income increased slightly from 4,358 thousand euros to 4,545 thousand euros, while total operating expenses rose from 40,576 thousand euros to 41,491 thousand euros. Credit loss allowances decreased from 4,643 thousand euros in 2024 to 3,299 thousand euros in 2025, and income tax expense increased from 4,486 thousand euros to 5,842 thousand euros. Return on equity dropped from 16.2% in 2024 to 12.9% in 2025, and return on assets declined from 1.6% to 1.2%. Despite the lower net profit, the bank experienced robust growth in business volumes. The net loan portfolio grew by 19%, exceeding 2 billion euros for the first time to reach 2,114,091 thousand euros compared to 1,774,118 thousand euros in the previous year. This growth was supported by all financing business lines, with the business loan portfolio increasing by 207 million euros or 27% and the home loan portfolio growing by 125 million euros or 17%. Customer deposits and loans received increased by 9% to reach 2,047,459 thousand euros for customer deposits and 77,061 thousand euros for loans received. Total assets expanded by 24% to 2,702,994 thousand euros up from 2,188,614 thousand euros, and shareholders equity increased to 234,543 thousand euros compared to 211,637 thousand euros at the end of 2024. The customer base expanded by 19,000 customers or 9%, reaching 227,000 customers by the end of 2025. The financial performance was directly influenced by the broader macroeconomic environment characterized by receding inflation and declining central bank interest rates, which caused a compression in net interest margins despite strong underlying volume growth in loans and customer acquisition.
