AS Merko Ehitus reported a substantially smaller but still profitable year in 2025. Revenue fell 42.3% to EUR 310.9 million from EUR 539.0 million, primarily because several large construction contracts completed during the year and replacement projects were not yet contributing at the same scale. Gross profit decreased to EUR 55.9 million from EUR 95.9 million, while the gross margin improved slightly to 18.0% from 17.8%. Operating profit declined 52.6% to EUR 34.3 million, and the operating margin narrowed to 11.0% from 13.4%. EBITDA fell to EUR 37.5 million from EUR 75.7 million, with its margin declining to 12.0% from 14.0%.

Net profit attributable to shareholders decreased 38.3% to EUR 39.9 million from EUR 64.7 million, and earnings per share fell to EUR 2.26 from EUR 3.65. However, the net margin increased to 12.8% from 12.0%, supported by a lower tax expense and finance costs, as well as EUR 10.4 million of profit from joint ventures, compared with EUR 10.0 million in 2024. The Connecto-related joint venture investment contributed approximately one quarter of consolidated net profit, according to management.

Construction services accounted for 76.4% of revenue. Segment revenue declined 49.9% to EUR 237.6 million and operating profit fell 56.8% to EUR 25.7 million; the margin decreased to 10.8% from 12.5%. Management attributed the decline to the completion of highly successful contracts and a return to more typical margins in a competitive market. Private-sector investment remained weak amid geopolitical and economic uncertainty, while public-sector demand increasingly centred on defence and infrastructure.

Real estate development performed better in volume terms: revenue rose 13.8% to EUR 73.4 million, apartment sales increased to 358 units worth EUR 67.8 million from 323 units worth EUR 58.9 million, and more than three times as many new apartments were started as in 2024. Nevertheless, segment operating profit fell 18.8% to EUR 13.4 million and its margin declined to 18.2% from 25.5%, partly due to a EUR 1.0 million write-down, including a property in Norway.

The secured construction order book rose 37.1% to EUR 466.9 million, following EUR 362.8 million of new contracts. Major awards included Rail Baltica projects and defence-related work. Contracts for the Rūdninkai defence campus signed in early 2026 are expected to contribute gradually from 2026 onward.

Financial strength improved: equity rose to EUR 260.6 million, the equity ratio increased to 62.8% from 56.9%, and debt remained moderate at 8.0% of assets. Operating cash flow was negative EUR 1.7 million, mainly reflecting higher inventories and working-capital movements, while cash declined to EUR 41.4 million after EUR 33.6 million of dividends. The Board proposed a further EUR 22.1 million dividend, or EUR 1.25 per share.
