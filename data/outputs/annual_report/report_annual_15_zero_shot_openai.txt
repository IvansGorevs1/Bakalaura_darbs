AS Merko Ehitus reported a substantially lower level of activity in 2025 after several large construction projects completed during the year and new contract signing remained slow through 2024 and the first half of 2025. Revenue fell 42.3% to EUR 310.9 million from EUR 539.0 million in 2024. Construction services accounted for EUR 237.6 million, down 49.9%, while real estate development revenue increased 13.8% to EUR 73.4 million. Estonia generated 55% of revenue, Lithuania 28% and Latvia 17%, compared with a much larger Lithuanian contribution in 2024.

Gross profit decreased to EUR 55.9 million from EUR 95.9 million, although gross margin edged up to 18.0% from 17.8%. Operating profit fell 52.6% to EUR 34.3 million, and the operating margin declined to 11.0% from 13.4%, reflecting the completion of highly successful contracts and a return to more typical sector margins in a competitive market. EBITDA decreased to EUR 37.5 million from EUR 75.7 million, with the margin falling to 12.0% from 14.0%. Profit before tax declined to EUR 44.8 million from EUR 76.4 million, while net profit attributable to shareholders fell to EUR 39.9 million from EUR 64.7 million. However, the net margin improved to 12.8% from 12.0%, partly reflecting lower finance costs and income tax expense. Earnings per share decreased to EUR 2.26 from EUR 3.65.

Real estate development delivered higher revenue and apartment sales, with 358 apartments sold for EUR 67.8 million, compared with 323 apartments and EUR 58.9 million in 2024. Nevertheless, segment operating profit declined 18.8% to EUR 13.4 million and margin fell to 18.2% from 25.5%, including the effect of a write-down on a property in Norway. The group invested EUR 56.2 million in development projects and began construction of more than three times as many new apartments as in 2024.

The secured construction order book increased 37.1% to EUR 466.9 million, supported by EUR 362.8 million of new contracts, including major Rail Baltica, defence, hotel, office and renewable-energy projects. These awards are expected to contribute progressively from 2026 onward. Market conditions remained uncertain, with weak private investment, continued geopolitical pressure and increased public-sector focus on defence and infrastructure.

Cash and cash equivalents declined to EUR 41.4 million from EUR 91.9 million, and operating cash flow was negative at EUR 1.7 million versus positive EUR 57.8 million in 2024, mainly alongside increased development inventories and dividend payments. Despite this, equity rose to EUR 260.6 million, the equity ratio improved to 62.8% from 56.9%, and interest-bearing liabilities remained moderate at 8.0% of assets. The board proposes a EUR 1.25 per-share dividend, totalling EUR 22.1 million, equal to a 55% payout ratio.
