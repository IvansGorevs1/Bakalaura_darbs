Coop Pank reported a net profit of €28.7 million for the 2025 financial year, representing an 11% decline compared to the €32.2 million recorded in 2024. This reduction in profitability was primarily driven by a contracting interest rate environment, which negatively impacted net interest income despite significant growth in business volumes. The bank’s net interest income decreased by 5.5% to €73.3 million, falling from €77.6 million in the previous year. This decline occurred as the European Central Bank continued to lower interest rates throughout 2025, reducing the yield on the bank’s loan portfolio faster than it could reduce funding costs. Operating expenses increased moderately by 2.2% to €41.5 million, while credit loss allowances decreased from €4.6 million to €3.3 million, reflecting stable asset quality. Consequently, the return on equity dropped to 12.9% from 16.2%, and the cost-to-income ratio rose to 52.3% from 49.6%.

Despite the pressure on margins, the bank executed a robust growth strategy, expanding its loan portfolio by 19% to exceed €2 billion for the first time, reaching €2.11 billion. Business loans grew the fastest, increasing by €207 million or 27%, while home loans expanded by €125 million or 17%. The customer base also grew significantly, adding 19,000 clients to reach a total of 227,000. Customer deposits increased by 9% to €2.05 billion, with demand deposits rising by 11% and term deposits by 8%. The bank maintained a conservative risk profile, with non-performing loans remaining at a low level and the capital adequacy ratio standing at 18.54%, well above regulatory requirements.

Strategic developments in 2025 included the issuance of €250 million in covered bonds on the Irish stock exchange, marking the first tranche of a €750 million program. This move provided long-term, stable funding and allowed the bank to begin reducing reliance on more expensive term and foreign deposits. Additionally, Coop Pank entered into a synthetic securitization agreement with the European Investment Bank Group, transferring credit risk on part of its loan portfolio to secure capital relief, enabling the issuance of up to €249 million in new loans focused on sustainability and gender equality projects. The bank also signed a €5 million subordinated loan agreement with the European Energy Efficiency Fund.

Management changes occurred during the year, with Arko Kurtmann appointed as Chairman of the Management Board in the third quarter, succeeding Margus Rink. The bank continued to prioritize customer convenience and digital innovation, introducing features such as virtual cards and cashback rewards through its partnership with Coop Estonia. Although net profit declined, the bank fulfilled its dividend policy by paying €0.07 per share, totaling €7.2 million, and maintained its investment-grade credit rating from Moody’s. The overall performance in 2025 was characterized by strong volume growth and strategic funding optimization, which positioned the bank to return to profit growth as the economic recovery gains momentum in 2026.
