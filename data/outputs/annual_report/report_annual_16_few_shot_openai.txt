Nordic Fibreboard AS’s financial performance weakened in 2025. Consolidated revenue declined 3.4% to €7.33 million from €7.59 million in 2024, with fibreboard sales accounting for virtually all revenue. Sales within the European Union, which represented 92% of total revenue, remained relatively stable, while sales to Africa, Asia and the Middle East decreased. Real estate management revenue fell to €3 thousand from €25 thousand following the termination of rental agreements in 2024.

Profitability deteriorated substantially. EBITDA declined to a negative €420 thousand from negative €25 thousand, reducing the EBITDA margin to negative 6% from 0%. The fibreboard segment generated negative EBITDA of €296 thousand, compared with positive EBITDA of €26 thousand in 2024, while the real estate segment’s negative EBITDA increased to €86 thousand from €48 thousand. The production business was affected by underutilized capacity, which increased the cost of internally produced goods. In addition, a planned factory shutdown in December to transition to a new gas boiler house resulted in continuing personnel, maintenance and repair costs, with an estimated negative EBITDA impact of approximately €0.2 million. The boiler-system upgrade was completed in January 2026.

The Group’s operating loss increased to €940 thousand from €547 thousand, and net loss widened to €1.15 million from €786 thousand. The net margin therefore declined to negative 16% from negative 10%. Distribution costs increased to €982 thousand from €935 thousand, while administrative expenses rose to €502 thousand from €381 thousand. Labour expenses increased to €1.93 million from €1.63 million despite average employment declining from 65 to 60 people. Net financial costs remained significant at €214 thousand, compared with €238 thousand in 2024.

Total assets increased to €9.32 million from €8.25 million, mainly due to higher property, plant and equipment and inventories. Equity rose to €4.46 million from €3.62 million, supported by a €2 million share issue completed in October, although the annual loss reduced retained earnings. Total liabilities increased to €4.86 million from €4.64 million, and payables rose sharply to €1.55 million from €0.79 million. Borrowings and lease liabilities declined to €3.17 million from €3.72 million, but most borrowings were classified as current because lending-covenant requirements were not fulfilled and lender confirmation had not been obtained.

Cash flow remained under pressure. Operating activities used €503 thousand, compared with €174 thousand in 2024, while investing activities used €989 thousand, principally for production assets and real estate development. Financing generated €1.44 million, including the share issue, leaving cash at only €5 thousand at year-end. Working capital was negative €2.7 million, compared with negative €0.7 million in 2024. Management plans to address this position through the proposed sale of ten Admirali Quarter properties, supported by a preliminary agreement signed in January 2026.
