Coop Pank delivered strong business growth in 2025, although profitability declined as lower interest rates reduced income. Net profit fell 11% year on year to €28.7 million, from €32.2 million in 2024. Net interest income decreased 6% to €73.3 million, reflecting the lower-interest-rate environment, while net interest margin declined from 3.8% to 3.0%. Net fee and commission income increased 4% to €4.5 million, and net other income rose to €1.5 million from virtually zero. Total net operating income declined 3% to €79.4 million.

Operating expenses increased 2% to €41.5 million, resulting in a deterioration in the cost-to-income ratio from 49.6% to 52.3%. Credit loss allowance improved to €3.3 million from €4.6 million, supported by continued low credit losses and a loan portfolio described as low risk. Income tax expense rose to €5.8 million from €4.5 million, and the effective tax rate increased from 12% to 17%. Return on equity declined from 16.2% to 12.9%, while return on assets fell from 1.6% to 1.2%.

Business volumes expanded considerably. The net loan portfolio grew 19%, or €340 million, to €2.11 billion, exceeding €2 billion for the first time. Business loans increased 27% to €986 million, and home and other private loans grew 17% to €863 million. Customer deposits rose 9% to €2.05 billion, with demand deposits up 11% and term deposits up 8%. The customer base increased by 19,000, or 9%, to 227,000.

The operating environment improved as Estonia’s economy returned to modest growth, inflation receded and interest rates declined. Estonian home and business lending grew approximately 10% market-wide, supporting demand for Coop Pank’s financing products, but falling rates constrained the bank’s interest income.

The bank strengthened funding by issuing €250 million of four-year covered bonds, its first international covered-bond issuance, and began reducing more expensive term and foreign deposits. It also entered into an EIB Group guarantee arrangement expected to enable up to €249 million of new business lending, while a €5 million EEEF subordinated loan was drawn in February 2026. Moody’s affirmed the bank’s Baa2 deposit rating with a positive outlook.

Equity increased 11% to €234.5 million. Regulatory capital requirements were met, although the CET1 ratio declined slightly to 14.15% from 14.28% and total capital adequacy to 18.54% from 19.41%. The bank paid €7.2 million in dividends for 2024, or €0.07 per share, and proposed a 2025 dividend of €0.065 per share.
