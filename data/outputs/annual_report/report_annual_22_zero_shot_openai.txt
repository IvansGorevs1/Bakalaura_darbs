Tallinna Vesi reported stronger financial performance in 2025, with growth in revenue, profitability and operating cash flow. Group sales increased 11.4% to €72.48 million from €65.08 million in 2024. Water-services revenue rose 6.9% to €62.31 million, while construction-services revenue increased 62.0% to €8.46 million, supported by higher pipe and external road construction activity. Revenue from private customers in the main service area increased 18.3% to €31.84 million, while business-customer revenue declined 2.7% to €16.76 million following tariff reductions linked to the statutory harmonisation of private and business prices. Stormwater and fire-hydrant service revenue fell 25.5% to €4.35 million because of changes in regulated revenue classification.

Gross profit rose 10.5% to €29.52 million and operating profit increased 11.8% to €21.22 million. EBITDA was €30.95 million, compared with €27.51 million in 2024, and the EBITDA margin improved slightly to 42.70% from 42.27%. Net financial expense decreased to €3.78 million from €4.20 million, mainly due to lower Euribor rates. Profit before tax increased 18.0% to €17.43 million, while net profit rose 7.2% to €14.24 million. The smaller increase in net profit reflected the higher income tax rate on dividend payments. Earnings per share increased from €0.66 to €0.71.

The main operating cost increases were construction services, up 74.5% to €7.13 million, depreciation and amortisation, up 11.9% to €9.08 million, and staff costs, up 6.8% to €11.45 million. Total direct production costs were broadly unchanged at €9.90 million. Electricity costs fell 6.9% to €4.37 million, partly because the new combined heat and power plant generated electricity for internal consumption, supporting operating-profit growth.

Investment accelerated substantially. Fixed-asset investment reached €55.74 million, compared with €49.53 million in 2024, and operating cash flow increased to €30.83 million from €26.25 million. Cash at year-end rose to €11.29 million from €3.59 million after €45 million of new borrowing, although outstanding loans increased to €160 million from €118 million. The debt-to-assets ratio consequently rose to 66.02% from 61.71%.

Operationally, the company laid or reconstructed 45.1 kilometres of pipes, expanded smart-meter coverage to more than 80% of customers and commissioned the Paljassaare combined heat and power plant. It produced all required treatment-process heat and 43.8% of electricity used by the wastewater plant and main pumping station. Service indicators improved, with blockages down 18% and water emergencies down 23%. Water quality remained high, with 99.5% of drinking-water samples meeting standards, while treated effluent met all applicable requirements. The company’s share price increased 7.84% to €11.00 by year-end.
