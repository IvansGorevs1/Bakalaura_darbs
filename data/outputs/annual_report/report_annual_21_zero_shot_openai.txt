Tallinna Sadam reported a stronger overall performance in 2025 despite slightly lower revenue. Revenue decreased 0.8% to EUR 118.7 million, mainly because icebreaker Botnica charter fees fell by EUR 4.0 million, or 34%, as charter days declined by 42. This was partly offset by higher vessel dues, passenger fees, lease income, ferry-service revenue and cargo charges. Vessel dues increased 8% to EUR 34.1 million, while ferry-service revenue rose 1% to EUR 36.4 million.

Profitability improved materially as operating expenses related to the main operations declined by EUR 3.8 million, or 4%, to EUR 88.0 million. Operating expenses alone fell 10%, primarily due to lower maintenance and repair costs, reduced fuel and lease expenses, and a EUR 0.9 million insurance indemnity related to the 2024 Botnica technical failure. Adjusted EBITDA increased 6.4% to EUR 56.5 million, lifting the margin from 44.4% to 47.6%. Operating profit rose 12.2% to EUR 32.7 million, and the operating margin improved from 24.4% to 27.6%.

Profit before income tax increased 25% to EUR 27.9 million, supported by a EUR 2.5 million reduction in net finance costs as interest rates and debt declined. Net profit rose 17.3% to EUR 22.5 million, with earnings per share increasing from EUR 0.07 to EUR 0.09. The increase in income tax expense, up 73.3% to EUR 5.4 million following changes to dividend taxation, limited the growth in net profit. The associate Green Marine contributed a EUR 26 thousand loss, compared with a EUR 487 thousand profit in 2024.

Operating volumes generally recovered. Cargo volume increased 5.1% to 13.8 million tonnes, driven especially by a 47% increase in liquid bulk and moderate growth in dry bulk. Passenger numbers rose 1.0% to 8.3 million, including a 25% increase in cruise passengers; however, volumes remained approximately 78% of the pre-pandemic level. Domestic ferry traffic served 2.5 million passengers and 1.2 million vehicles, while Botnica’s utilisation rate fell to 49%.

Capital investment decreased 14.4% to EUR 33.2 million, with major spending on the new multifunctional quay at Paldiski South Harbour, Muuga quay reinforcement and ferry maintenance. Net debt declined 15.5% to EUR 141.7 million, and operating cash flow increased to EUR 53.7 million from EUR 48.7 million. The Group paid EUR 19.2 million in dividends, or EUR 0.073 per share. Management expects moderate passenger and cargo growth in 2026 but anticipates pressure from higher input costs, increased financing needs and the absence of certain one-off benefits, potentially reducing operating and net profit.
