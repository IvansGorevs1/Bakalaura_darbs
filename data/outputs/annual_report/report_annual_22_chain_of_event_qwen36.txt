Tallinna Vesi reported strong financial growth for the year ended 31 December 2025, driven by increased sales and improved operational efficiency. Total sales reached 72.48 million euros, an 11.4 percent increase from 65.08 million euros in 2024. This growth was primarily fueled by a 62.0 percent surge in construction services revenue, which rose to 8.46 million euros, and a 6.9 percent increase in water services sales to 62.31 million euros. The rise in water services revenue was largely attributable to tariff adjustments implemented in 2024 and 2025, which increased prices for private customers while harmonizing rates for business customers. Despite a 2.7 percent decline in business customer sales due to these tariff reductions, the overall revenue growth was robust.

The group’s gross profit increased by 10.5 percent to 29.52 million euros, reflecting a gross profit margin of 40.73 percent. Operating profit improved by 11.8 percent to 21.22 million euros, with an operating margin of 29.27 percent. This improvement was significantly supported by the optimization of electricity costs following the launch of the combined heat and power plant at the Paljassaare Wastewater Treatment Plant, which produced all the heat and more than half of the electricity required for treatment processes. Although staff costs rose by 6.8 percent to 11.45 million euros and construction service costs increased by 74.5 percent, these were offset by a 6.9 percent reduction in electricity expenses.

Profit before taxes grew by 18.0 percent to 17.43 million euros, and net profit increased by 7.2 percent to 14.24 million euros. Earnings per share rose to 0.71 euros from 0.66 euros in the previous year. The company maintained a consistent dividend policy, paying 0.53 euros per share, which represented 80 percent of 2024 earnings. The return on equity improved to 11.70 percent from 11.20 percent in 2024.

Investment activity remained high, with capital expenditures in fixed assets totaling 55.74 million euros, up from 49.53 million euros in 2024. These investments focused on infrastructure continuity, environmental protection, and the expansion of smart metering, with over 80 percent of customers now equipped with smart meters. The company’s debt position increased, with total outstanding loans rising to 160 million euros from 118 million euros, resulting in a debt-to-total-assets ratio of 66.02 percent. The share price closed at 11.00 euros at the end of 2025, up 7.84 percent from the beginning of the year. The company also maintained a high collection rate for trade receivables at 99.68 percent and successfully reduced water emergencies by 23 percent and blockages by 18 percent through targeted maintenance and technological upgrades.
