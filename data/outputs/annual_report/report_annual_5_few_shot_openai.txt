Eleving Group delivered record financial performance in 2025, supported by strong portfolio expansion, higher demand, and continued product and market development. Revenue increased 15.4% to EUR 250.0 million, while adjusted EBITDA rose 13.5% to EUR 101.9 million. Net profit from continuing operations increased to EUR 29.2 million from EUR 28.8 million, although total net profit was broadly stable at EUR 29.2 million compared with EUR 29.6 million in 2024. Net profit before foreign exchange effects and discontinued operations grew 25.6% to EUR 40.8 million.

The net portfolio expanded 20.2% to EUR 446.3 million, driven by higher lending volumes across Europe and Africa. Loan issuances reached an all-time high of EUR 458.0 million, up 24.3% from EUR 368.6 million in 2024. Vehicle and device finance issuances increased 34.0%, while consumer finance issuances rose 15.7%. Revenue growth was led by consumer lending, which contributed EUR 114.8 million, up 18.0%, and flexible vehicle financing, which contributed EUR 60.3 million, up 25.9%. Traditional vehicle financing revenue declined 6.0% to EUR 67.0 million. Device financing, launched in 2025, contributed EUR 7.9 million.

Growth was supported by installment-loan offerings introduced in Latvia, Estonia, Romania, and Armenia, as well as smartphone financing launched in Kenya and Uganda. The smartphone portfolio reached EUR 13.5 million by year-end, while Tanzania became the Group’s 17th market through the launch of motorcycle financing. Latvia, Romania, and Kenya were identified as major contributors to overall growth. The Group also piloted AI voice agents in call centers and plans broader deployment in 2026 to address cost pressures and improve efficiency.

Profitability benefited from stronger operating scale, lower foreign-exchange losses, and reduced administrative and selling expenses. However, impairment expense remained significant at EUR 42.1 million, while the Group wrote off EUR 49.5 million of long-overdue loans; the related release of previously recognized impairment allowances largely offset the income-statement impact. Foreign-exchange gains declined to EUR 3.7 million from EUR 11.7 million, limiting reported profit growth.

The Group strengthened its funding position through a EUR 40 million bond tap and a EUR 275 million bond issuance that refinanced EUR 150 million of earlier debt. Total borrowings reached EUR 441.6 million, while cash increased to EUR 39.1 million. The capitalization ratio declined to 23.7% from 29.3%, and net leverage rose to 3.8 from 3.3. Dividends paid to parent-company shareholders totaled EUR 19.65 million. Overall, the year combined robust growth and record operating results with increased leverage, continued credit-risk exposure, and substantial investment in future expansion.
