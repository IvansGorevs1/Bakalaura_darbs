Novaturas Group’s financial performance weakened materially in 2024. Revenue declined 3.6% to EUR 200.9 million from EUR 208.3 million in 2023, while the number of passengers served fell 7.6% to 239.5 thousand. The deterioration was driven by increased competition and oversupply in the Baltic travel market, which created intense price pressure, particularly in Turkey and the Greek islands. Flight package revenue, the Group’s main product category, decreased 6.6% to EUR 170.7 million, while other sales increased 21.3% to EUR 25.4 million.

Gross profit fell 20.9% to EUR 20.7 million, reducing the gross margin from 12.6% to 10.3%. Selling expenses remained broadly stable at EUR 17.1 million, but general and administrative expenses increased to EUR 10.4 million from EUR 4.4 million. This increase included a one-off, non-cash goodwill impairment charge of EUR 5.7 million related to the Estonian cash-generating unit. Excluding this impairment, operating expenses were EUR 21.8 million, approximately 1% below the prior year.

The Group reported negative EBITDA of EUR 6.1 million, compared with positive EBITDA of EUR 5.2 million in 2023. Adjusted EBITDA, excluding the goodwill impairment, was negative EUR 449 thousand, versus positive EUR 5.2 million previously. Operating loss amounted to EUR 6.6 million, compared with operating profit of EUR 4.3 million, while the net result changed from a EUR 3.4 million profit to a EUR 7.6 million loss. Excluding the goodwill impairment, the net loss was EUR 1.9 million. Earnings per share declined from EUR 0.43 to a loss of EUR 0.98.

Performance improved toward the end of the year following optimization of the travel program and adjustments to destinations and customer offerings. Adjusted EBITDA was negative EUR 1.6 million in the first half but positive EUR 2.3 million from September through December. Fourth-quarter load factor increased to 98.9% from 96.7% in the comparable period, while last-minute travel sales decreased. Demand grew strongly for Spain and long-haul destinations, including Vietnam, Phuket and Zanzibar.

Financial flexibility remained a key concern. Year-end cash and cash equivalents fell to EUR 394 thousand from EUR 3.3 million, operating cash flow was negative EUR 1.6 million, and current liabilities exceeded current assets by EUR 12.5 million. Debt-to-equity increased to 121.1% from 58.0%, and the Group breached financing covenants, although waivers were subsequently obtained. After year-end, the Group secured additional financing and a strategic investor, Neset Kockar, acquired 23.2% of the shares and provided EUR 2 million in loans. Management expects 2025 EBITDA of EUR 2–3.3 million, supported by cost optimization, revised destination planning, new financing and operational synergies.
