AS Tallink Grupp’s 2025 performance weakened compared with 2024 amid low consumer and business confidence, economic challenges in its core markets, intense competition, and geopolitical tensions. Passenger volumes declined 0.9% to 5.5 million, while cargo units fell 19.2% to 245,004 and passenger vehicles decreased 2.2% to 760,473. Consolidated revenue fell 2.6% to EUR 765.3 million, including a EUR 13.7 million decline in route-operations revenue to EUR 625.2 million. Restaurant and shop sales decreased to EUR 366.4 million, cargo revenue to EUR 77.9 million, and charter revenue to EUR 54.9 million.

Profitability declined substantially. Gross profit fell to EUR 143.2 million from EUR 153.6 million, EBITDA decreased 25.7% to EUR 130.1 million from EUR 175.2 million, and EBIT declined to EUR 50.3 million from EUR 77.4 million. Net profit fell 57.1% to EUR 17.3 million, reducing earnings per share to EUR 0.023 from EUR 0.054 and the net profit margin to 2.3% from 5.1%. Route-operations profit declined to EUR 70.5 million from EUR 81.5 million. The Estonia-Finland route carried 1.8% more passengers and generated slightly higher revenue, but its result fell by EUR 10.9 million to EUR 70.4 million. Finland-Sweden performance was affected by 68 days of vessel maintenance, while Estonia-Sweden revenue fell EUR 12.1 million and remained loss-making, although its result improved by EUR 1.4 million.

Costs were mixed. Personnel expenses increased 4.3% to EUR 201.0 million, mainly due to the collective agreement covering maritime workers, while fuel and emission costs were broadly stable at EUR 96.5 million. Depreciation and amortisation fell EUR 18.0 million to EUR 79.8 million, primarily because useful lives of most cruise and passenger vessels were extended to 45 years and three vessels were sold. This change reduced 2025 depreciation by EUR 16.7 million. Net finance costs also declined EUR 6.8 million to EUR 21.4 million as debt balances and interest rates decreased.

The Group sold Star I, Regal Star and Sailor, reducing its fleet to 11 vessels and eliminating vessel lay-ups by the third quarter. Debt reduction continued: interest-bearing liabilities fell 19.9% to EUR 445.9 million and net debt to EUR 432.4 million, although net debt to EBITDA increased to 3.3 from 3.1 because EBITDA declined. Operating cash flow remained positive at EUR 127.1 million, while investing cash flow benefited from EUR 78.8 million of vessel-sale proceeds. After EUR 44.6 million of dividends, related tax, and EUR 113.5 million of loan and interest payments, year-end liquidity increased to EUR 110.4 million through higher unused credit facilities. The Group will propose another EUR 0.06-per-share dividend for 2026.
