TKM Grupp AS reported a decline in financial performance during the 2025 reporting period, driven by a challenging economic environment and significant headwinds in its automotive segment. Consolidated revenue decreased by 2.6% to 919.6 million euros from 944.6 million euros in the previous year. This contraction was primarily attributable to the car trade segment, where sales revenue fell by 11.9% to 176.9 million euros. The sharp decline in the Estonian passenger car market, which dropped by approximately 50% year-on-year, was largely caused by the introduction of the motor vehicle tax and reduced consumer confidence. While the Group’s subsidiaries in Latvia and Lithuania experienced growth in the car market, this positive development was insufficient to fully offset the weakness in the Estonian operations.

Profitability metrics also weakened significantly. Net profit amounted to 17.5 million euros, representing a substantial decrease of 36.1% compared to 27.5 million euros in 2024. Profit before tax declined by 31.4% to 24.3 million euros. The reduction in net profit was influenced by lower margins in the car segment, where net profit fell by 54.4% to 4.3 million euros. The supermarket segment, the Group’s largest revenue contributor, saw its net profit drop by 38.6% to 9.5 million euros, despite revenue remaining relatively stable with a slight increase of 0.3% to 611.9 million euros. The department stores segment recorded a small loss of 0.5 million euros, while the security services segment also posted a loss. The real estate segment was the only area to show improved profitability, with net profit increasing by 20.5% to 6.3 million euros, although this was partly due to one-off gains in the comparative period from property sales and revaluations.

Operating expenses were managed effectively, with other operating expenses growing by only 0.2% and labor costs increasing by 3.7%, helping to contain the impact of lower revenues. Finance costs decreased by 1.5 million euros due to lower interest expenses on borrowings, providing some relief to the bottom line. The Group maintained a strong balance sheet with an equity ratio of 38%. Total investments amounted to 25.5 million euros, focused on store refurbishments, digital platform upgrades, and the expansion of the car dealership network in the Baltic region.

Looking ahead, the Group has initiated strategic moves to strengthen its market position, including an agreement to acquire Škoda distribution rights in Tallinn and Harju County. Management anticipates that improvements in the economic environment, such as tax changes increasing disposable income and lower interest rates, may support a recovery in consumer demand in 2026. Despite the current volatility, the Group remains focused on operational efficiency, customer loyalty, and long-term strategic investments to sustain its competitive position in the Estonian and Baltic markets.
