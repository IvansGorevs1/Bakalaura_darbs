Coop Pank reported a net profit of 28.7 million euros for the year 2025, representing an 11 percent decrease from the 32.2 million euros recorded in 2024. This decline in profitability occurred despite significant growth in business volumes, as the bank’s loan portfolio expanded by 19 percent to exceed 2 billion euros for the first time in its history. The primary driver of the reduced earnings was the lower-interest economic environment, which compressed net interest margins and could not be fully offset by the increased lending activity. The net interest income fell to 73.3 million euros from 77.6 million euros in the prior year, reflecting the impact of declining interest rates set by the European Central Bank throughout 2025.

The bank’s total assets grew substantially, with average total assets reaching 2.45 billion euros compared to 2.07 billion euros in 2024. The loan portfolio increased by 340 million euros, driven largely by a 27 percent surge in the business loan segment, which grew by 207 million euros. Home loans also saw robust growth, increasing by 125 million euros or 17 percent. Customer deposits rose by 9 percent to 2.05 billion euros, with demand deposits growing faster than term deposits. The bank’s customer base expanded by 19,000 clients, reaching a total of 227,000. Operational efficiency metrics shifted, with the cost-to-income ratio rising to 52.3 percent from 49.6 percent in 2024, while the return on equity declined to 12.9 percent from 16.2 percent.

Strategic developments in 2025 included the issuance of 250 million euros in covered bonds on the Irish stock exchange, marking the first tranche of a 750 million euro program designed to secure long-term, stable funding. This move allowed the bank to begin reducing reliance on more expensive term and foreign deposits. Additionally, Coop Pank entered into a guarantee agreement with the European Investment Bank Group, a synthetic securitization transaction that transfers part of the credit risk from its loan portfolio to the EIB Group. This agreement provides capital relief and enables the issuance of up to 249 million euros in new loans focused on gender equality and environmental sustainability projects. The bank also signed a 5 million euro subordinated loan with the European Energy Efficiency Fund to support energy efficiency initiatives.

Credit quality remained strong, with the loan portfolio maintaining a low-risk level and overdue debt staying at historically low levels. The capital adequacy ratio stood at 18.54 percent, well above regulatory requirements, supported by a Common Equity Tier 1 ratio of 14.15 percent. The bank continued its dividend policy by paying 0.07 euros per share, totaling 7.2 million euros to shareholders. Management expects to return to net profit growth in 2026 as the economic recovery gains momentum, although the immediate impact of new investments is anticipated to materialize in the second half of 2026 and early 2027. The credit rating agency Moody’s affirmed Coop Pank’s deposit rating at Baa2 with a positive outlook, citing strong profitability, solid capitalization, and a high-quality credit portfolio.
