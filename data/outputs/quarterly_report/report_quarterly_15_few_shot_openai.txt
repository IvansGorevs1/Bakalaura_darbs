Nordecon’s financial performance weakened in 2025 compared with the previous year, although the group strengthened its order book and expects business volumes to increase in 2026. Revenue declined by 7% to €208.3 million, primarily because Buildings segment revenue fell by approximately 10% to €168.3 million. Infrastructure revenue increased by 10% to €39.9 million, supported by road construction, maintenance and Rail Baltica-related work. Buildings still represented the majority of revenue, with an 81% share, while Infrastructure accounted for 19%.

Profitability deteriorated as gross profit decreased to €13.5 million from €16.8 million and gross margin fell to 6.5% from 7.5%. Operating profit declined by 30% to €5.7 million, reducing the operating margin to 2.7% from 3.6%, below the group’s target of consistently exceeding 3%. EBITDA fell to €8.3 million from €11.0 million, and the EBITDA margin declined to 4.0% from 4.9%. Lower administrative expenses, which decreased by 14% to €6.8 million due mainly to reduced performance-based pay, partly offset the pressure on operating results.

Net profit declined by 51% to €2.5 million, while profit attributable to Nordecon’s owners fell to €1.4 million from €3.8 million. Earnings per share decreased to €0.05 from €0.12. Results were significantly affected by higher net finance costs, which increased to €3.0 million from €2.3 million. The main factor was a €750 thousand foreign-exchange loss arising from the weakening of the Ukrainian hryvnia against the euro, compared with €258 thousand in 2024. Credit losses also increased to €354 thousand from €20 thousand.

Operating cash flow remained positive at €1.2 million, compared with €1.1 million in 2024. However, investing activities used €2.8 million, including €2.9 million placed in long-term deposits to secure guarantee limits, while financing activities used €1.2 million. Consequently, cash and cash equivalents declined to €5.3 million from €8.2 million. Liquidity remained relatively constrained, with the current ratio at 0.93, broadly unchanged from 0.94 at the end of 2024.

The principal positive development was the 30% increase in the order book to €273.1 million at year-end. New contracts secured during the year totalled €235.0 million, including major Rail Baltica, road, public-building, defence and commercial projects. Infrastructure’s share of the order book rose to 42% from 18%, while Buildings’ share declined to 58% from 82%. About 69% of the backlog is scheduled for execution in 2026. Estonia’s construction market stabilised during the year, with activity improving in the second half, although competition, rising material prices and public-investment levels remain important risks.
