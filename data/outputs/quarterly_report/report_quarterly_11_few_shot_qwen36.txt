Kvika banki hf. reported a decline in profitability for the second quarter of 2026, with pre-tax profit decreasing by 23.7% year-over-year to ISK 1,545 million. This reduction was primarily driven by a significant drop in net fee and commission income, which fell by 26.8% to ISK 1,416 million due to subdued capital market activity and lower transaction volumes. Additionally, the effective income tax rate increased to 34.8% from 24.4% in the previous year, largely reflecting higher special taxes on financial activities. Despite these headwinds, net interest income remained resilient, increasing by 1.7% to ISK 3,013 million, supported by strong loan book growth and a solid funding base. Administrative expenses decreased slightly by 1.1% to ISK 2,947 million, although this figure included a one-off restructuring charge of ISK 112 million related to efficiency measures.

The bank’s balance sheet expanded significantly, with total assets rising by 9.5% to ISK 375.7 billion compared to year-end 2025. Loans to customers grew by 7.6% to ISK 223.3 billion, driven by broad-based growth in domestic corporate banking and vehicle lending. The loan-to-value profile remained strong, with 65.0% of the portfolio below a 70% loan-to-value ratio. Credit quality remained stable, with impairment levels holding steady at 1.2% of the gross loan book. On the liability side, deposits surged by 16.5% to ISK 201.2 billion, surpassing the ISK 200 billion milestone for the first time and reinforcing the bank’s primary funding source. Market funding also increased, with debt issuance reaching ISK 86.1 billion following recent senior unsecured and Additional Tier 1 bond issuances.

Strategic initiatives undertaken during the quarter were designed to dampen near-term earnings in favor of long-term value creation. These included a large dividend payment, which lowered interest income but is expected to increase return on tangible equity by reducing equity tied up. The issuance of senior preferred and AT1 bonds temporarily compressed the net interest margin to 4.0% but is anticipated to widen margins in the future as higher-cost legacy debt is refinanced. Furthermore, the bank is proceeding with the sale of its payment intermediary, Straumur greiðlun hf., expected to close before year-end, which will simplify the business structure.

Looking ahead, Kvika expects continued loan book growth and improved net interest margins as funding costs decrease. The efficiency measures announced in June are projected to yield annualized cost savings of ISK 300–400 million. The bank maintains a strong capital position with a Capital Adequacy Ratio of 23.0% and a robust liquidity coverage ratio of 276%. Leadership changes are also underway, with CEO Ármann Þorvaldsson announcing his intention to step down by the end of 2026, while the board recruits his successor. Overall, the period was characterized by a strategic shift toward a leaner, more focused business model with improved long-term profitability prospects despite short-term earnings pressure.
