Kvika Banki hf. reported a pre-tax profit of ISK 1,545 million for the second quarter of 2026, representing a 23.7% decline from ISK 2,025 million in the same period of 2025 and a 14.6% decrease from the first quarter of 2026. The reduction in profitability was primarily driven by a significant drop in net fee and commission income, which fell by 26.8% year-over-year to ISK 1,416 million due to subdued market activity and lower transaction volumes in capital markets. Net interest income showed resilience, increasing slightly by 1.7% to ISK 3,013 million, supported by strong loan book growth. However, the net interest margin contracted to 4.0% from 4.5% in the first quarter, largely due to lower inflation adjustments and the impact of a large dividend payment in June. Administrative expenses decreased by 1.1% to ISK 2,947 million, despite recognizing a one-off restructuring charge of ISK 112 million. The effective income tax rate increased to 34.8% from 24.4% in the previous year, reflecting higher special taxes on financial activities.

The company’s balance sheet expanded, with total assets reaching ISK 375.7 billion, a 9.5% increase from year-end 2025. Loans to customers grew by 7.6% to ISK 223.3 billion, driven by strong performance in domestic corporate banking and vehicle lending. Deposits also expanded significantly, surpassing ISK 200 billion for the first time to reach ISK 201.2 billion, up 16.5% from year-end 2025, which helped lower the loan-to-deposit ratio to 111%. The bank maintained a strong liquidity position, with a liquidity coverage ratio of 276% and a net stable funding ratio of 137%. Capital adequacy remained robust, with a capital adequacy ratio of 23.0%, well above regulatory requirements, although it decreased slightly due to the distribution of a special dividend.

Strategic developments during the quarter included the initiation of a sales process for Straumur greiðslumiðlun hf., expected to close before the end of the year, and the onboarding of new institutional investors in asset management. The UK business continued to grow, with the loan book reaching an all-time high. Looking forward, the bank expects efficiency measures to reduce the annual cost base by ISK 300 to 400 million. Additionally, recent senior unsecured and AT1 bond issuances are anticipated to lower funding costs and improve net interest margins in subsequent quarters as older, higher-priced debt is refinanced. CEO Ármann Þorvaldsson announced his intention to step down by the end of 2026, while the bank continues to focus on loan book growth and operational efficiency to drive long-term shareholder value.
