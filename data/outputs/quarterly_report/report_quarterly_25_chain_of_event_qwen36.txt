VILVI Group reported consolidated revenue of EUR 86.97 million for the first quarter of 2026, representing a 22.4% increase compared to EUR 71.07 million in the same period of 2025. This growth was driven by a 72.9% surge in domestic sales to EUR 14.63 million and a 46.6% rise in exports to other countries to EUR 29.82 million. Sales within the European Union remained relatively stable at EUR 42.52 million, showing a modest 0.6% increase. The acquisition of Marijampolės pieno konservai UAB, completed in January 2026, contributed EUR 11.92 million to the top line, accounting for the new Canned milk segment which represents 14% of total revenue. Consequently, the composition of revenue shifted significantly, with Dry milk products growing by 126.9% to EUR 24.66 million and Fresh milk products increasing by 73.4% to EUR 12.75 million. In contrast, revenue from Cheese, cheese products, and other items declined by 28.8% to EUR 37.64 million, reflecting a structural change in the portfolio due to the reclassification of canned milk.

Despite the strong revenue growth, profitability metrics deteriorated. EBITDA fell sharply by 50.2% to EUR 3.00 million, with the EBITDA margin contracting to 3.5% from 8.5% in the prior year period. The report explicitly states that this EBITDA figure excludes a preliminary negative goodwill of EUR 5.5 million related to the acquisition of Marijampolės pieno konservai UAB. If this non-cash item were included, the operational performance would appear even weaker. However, net profit increased by 25.7% to EUR 5.09 million, resulting in a profit margin of 5.8% and earnings per share of EUR 0.43. This divergence between EBITDA and net profit is largely attributable to the accounting treatment of the acquisition-related negative goodwill, which boosted the bottom line despite higher operating costs.

The company’s balance sheet expanded significantly, with total assets rising to EUR 220.31 million from EUR 188.98 million at the end of 2025. Net financial debt nearly doubled, increasing by 89.7% to EUR 83.43 million, driven by borrowings used to fund the acquisition and capital expenditures. Property, plant, and equipment grew to EUR 141.87 million, reflecting the completion of the new cheese factory in Latvia and other infrastructure investments. Cash flows from operating activities were negative at EUR 9.63 million, primarily due to increases in inventories and receivables, as well as the acquisition costs. Investing activities also saw a net outflow of EUR 8.04 million, including EUR 7.48 million for the acquisition of the subsidiary. Financing activities provided a net inflow of EUR 15.84 million, stemming from new borrowings. Key operational developments included the commercial launch of the Baltic Dairy Board cheese factory in Latvia, which is expected to produce 18,000 tons of cheese annually, and the discontinuation of transparent plastic lids for VILVI yogurts as part of sustainability initiatives. The Group purchased 155,954 tons of basic milk, a 54.2% increase year-over-year, supporting the expanded production capacity.
