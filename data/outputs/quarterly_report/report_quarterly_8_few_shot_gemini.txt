AS Tallink Grupp reported lower financial results for the 2025 financial year, driven by economic uncertainty, subdued consumer confidence, and geopolitical tensions, particularly in its primary Finnish and regional markets. Total consolidated revenue for the twelve-month period decreased to EUR 765.3 million compared with EUR 785.8 million in 2024. Profitability also weakened across the board, with EBITDA falling to EUR 130.1 million from EUR 175.2 million in the previous year. Net profit for 2025 declined to EUR 17.3 million, or EUR 0.023 per share, compared to EUR 40.3 million, or EUR 0.054 per share, in 2024. Gross profit dropped to EUR 143.2 million from EUR 153.6 million. 

Operational volumes reflected the challenging environment. The group transported 5,531,132 passengers during 2025, representing a 0.9% decrease year-on-year. The number of transported cargo units fell more sharply by 19.2% to 245,004, while passenger vehicles declined by 2.2% to 760,473. Segment performance showed mixed results, with core route operations generating EUR 625.2 million in revenue and a segment result of EUR 70.5 million, down from EUR 81.5 million in 2024. The Estonia-Finland route saw a slight revenue increase to EUR 314.5 million, though segment profit decreased. Finland-Sweden and Estonia-Sweden routes experienced revenue declines and continued segment pressures, although management actively optimized operations by chartering out vessels and completing the sale of three ships during the year, including the passenger vessel Star I, the cargo vessel Regal Star, and the cargo vessel Sailor. 

Despite lower earnings, the company benefited from reduced depreciation and lower net finance costs. Amortisation and depreciation expenses decreased by EUR 18.0 million to EUR 79.8 million, primarily resulting from the alignment of the estimated useful lives of cruise and passenger vessels to 45 years and the vessel sales. Net finance costs declined to EUR 21.4 million from EUR 28.3 million due to a reduced outstanding loan balance and lower interest rates. Capital expenditures for the year totaled EUR 33.0 million, focused largely on maintenance and technical upgrades for vessels during dry dockings. 

The company maintained its focus on debt reduction and shareholder returns. Net debt stood at EUR 432.4 million at the end of December 2025, virtually unchanged from the end of the third quarter, with a net debt to EBITDA ratio of 3.3. Total cash and cash equivalents decreased to EUR 13.5 million, influenced by loan and interest payments totaling EUR 113.5 million, alongside dividend payments of EUR 44.6 million and related income tax of EUR 11.3 million. Reflecting management's commitment to remaining a stable dividend-paying company, the Management Board intends to propose a dividend payment of 6 euro cents per share at the upcoming Annual General Meeting. 

Performance in the final quarter of 2025 showed signs of stabilization. During the fourth quarter, revenue increased by 2.4% year-on-year to EUR 188.0 million, EBITDA rose to EUR 27.6 million from EUR 25.7 million, and net profit rebounded significantly to EUR 12.2 million compared to a net loss of EUR 5.2 million in the final quarter of 2024. Management enters 2026 with cautious optimism, supported by easing declines in cargo volumes and ongoing adaptability in managing fleet capacity and cost structures.
