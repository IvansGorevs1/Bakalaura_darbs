Kvika banki reported pre-tax profit of ISK 1,545 million in Q2 2026, down ISK 480 million, or 23.7%, from Q2 2025 and 14.6% from Q1 2026. After-tax profit was ISK 903 million, compared with ISK 1,439 million a year earlier and ISK 1,374 million in the previous quarter. The decline reflected lower operating income, higher impairment and special bank taxes.

Net operating income decreased 7.3% year on year to ISK 4,756 million. Net interest income remained resilient, increasing 1.7% year on year to ISK 3,013 million, although it fell ISK 253 million from Q1. The quarter-on-quarter decline was mainly associated with lower inflation reducing the benefit from Kvika’s positive CPI balance, the ISK 10.2 billion dividend payment in June and recent senior preferred and AT1 issuances. Net interest margin declined to 4.0% from 4.5% in Q1. Net fee and commission income fell 26.8% year on year to ISK 1,416 million, as subdued capital-markets activity and lower transaction volumes weighed on fees. Other net operating income increased ISK 96 million to ISK 327 million.

Administrative expenses decreased 1.1% year on year to ISK 2,947 million despite a one-off ISK 112 million restructuring charge. Efficiency measures reduced the employee count to 232 at quarter-end and are expected to lower the annual cost base by ISK 300–400 million. Net impairment rose to ISK 269 million from ISK 122 million, reflecting a more conservative loan-book review and increased caution amid economic uncertainty.

For the first six months of 2026, pre-tax profit from continuing operations increased 23.0% year on year to ISK 3,354 million. Net operating income rose 0.7% to ISK 9,643 million, supported by 6.8% growth in net interest income and higher other operating income, while fee income declined 16.0%. After-tax profit from continuing operations was ISK 2,277 million, compared with ISK 1,625 million in the first half of 2025.

The balance sheet expanded as lending and deposits grew. Loans to customers increased 7.6% from year-end 2025 to ISK 223.3 billion, while deposits rose 16.5% to ISK 201.2 billion, surpassing ISK 200 billion for the first time. Credit quality remained strong, with impairment levels at 1.2% of gross loans. Capital and liquidity remained well above requirements: CAR was 23.0%, LCR 276% and NSFR 137%.

Strategically, Kvika continued to grow lending, improve efficiency and optimise funding. The sale process for Straumur greiðslumiðlun was underway, the sale of the UK business’s largest principal investment was progressing, and CEO Ármann Þorvaldsson announced plans to step down by year-end 2026.
