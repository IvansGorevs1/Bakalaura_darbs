Amber Latvijas balzams’ financial performance deteriorated substantially in the first three months of 2026. Sales volume fell 36.1% to 517,600 nine-liter cases from 810,079 cases in the corresponding period of 2025. Net turnover decreased 29.6% to EUR 11.1 million, primarily because of lower sales volumes and a high comparison base in the prior year, when revenue benefited from a contractual revenue adjustment. Revenue from alcoholic beverage production declined to EUR 10.5 million from EUR 14.8 million.

Gross profit fell 66.5% to EUR 1.2 million from EUR 3.5 million. The decline reflected lower volumes, an adverse change in product mix and adjustments to the pricing methodology. Although the Company reduced certain costs, these savings largely failed to offset the negative impact of these factors. Operating expenses also declined overall, but the Company reported an operating loss of EUR 1.6 million, compared with an operating profit of EUR 0.6 million a year earlier. The operating margin consequently fell to negative 14.5% from positive 3.7%.

The Company recorded a net loss of EUR 1.4 million, compared with a net profit of EUR 0.9 million in the first quarter of 2025. Earnings per share declined to a loss of EUR 0.19 from earnings of EUR 0.11. Operating cash flow was negative at EUR 1.8 million, while cash and cash equivalents decreased from EUR 1.0 million at the end of 2025 to EUR 38,000 at 31 March 2026. Current assets continued to exceed current liabilities, but the surplus narrowed to EUR 82.9 million from EUR 92.0 million.

The results were affected by a difficult global spirits market, including declining demand, weaker export sales and inventory adjustments in distribution channels. The Company also faced major group-level and geopolitical pressures. In January, a U.S. trustee was appointed in the Chapter 11 proceedings of related company Stoli Group (USA), LLC, causing the Group to lose control of its U.S. operations and directly weakening the solvency of the Company’s largest customer. Separately, Russian authorities designated related entities as extremist and confiscated the Group-owned Talvis distillery.

These developments created significant cash-flow pressure and delayed excise tax payments. After the State Revenue Service froze the Company’s bank accounts, Amber Latvijas balzams filed for legal protection proceedings on 30 January 2026; the Riga City Court approved the application on 5 February. The Company is now restructuring its liabilities and working to stabilize operations. Its principal shareholder also entered Luxembourg judicial reorganization proceedings in April, adding further financial and refinancing uncertainty to the Group. Management is responding with efficiency, cost-reduction, governance and financial-control initiatives aimed at restoring profitability and financial stability.
