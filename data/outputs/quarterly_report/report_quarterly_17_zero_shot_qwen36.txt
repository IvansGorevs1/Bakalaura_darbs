AB Novaturas reported consolidated revenue of EUR 168.48 million for the twelve-month period ended 31 December 2025, representing a 16.1 percent decline compared to EUR 200.88 million in the prior year. The number of travelers served decreased by 20.3 percent to 190.9 thousand from 239.5 thousand in 2024. Despite the reduction in volume, the Group achieved positive profitability metrics, signaling a successful strategic shift toward efficiency and margin improvement. Adjusted EBITDA turned positive at EUR 420 thousand, a significant improvement from the negative EUR 449 thousand recorded in 2024. This adjustment excludes a one-off, non-cash impairment of goodwill amounting to EUR 5.7 million that was recorded in the previous year. The annual net loss narrowed by 39 percent to EUR 1.17 million, compared to a net loss of EUR 1.92 million in 2024, when excluding the aforementioned goodwill impairment.

The improvement in financial performance was driven by a deliberate reduction in volumes on loss-making routes and a focus on higher-margin destinations. This strategy resulted in an increase in the aircraft load factor by 1.5 percentage points to 97.2 percent in 2025, up from 95.7 percent in 2024. Load factors on long-haul destinations improved to 98.5 percent, while ski destinations saw load factors rise from 90.5 percent to 97.2 percent. Gross profit margin expanded by 1.3 percentage points to 11.66 percent, reflecting better operational discipline. However, the company faced headwinds in the second half of the year, including intense market competition, excess travel supply, and disruptions at Vilnius Airport due to illegal cross-border activity involving unmanned balloons, which reduced last-minute demand.

Segment analysis indicates that flight package tours remained the core revenue driver, accounting for EUR 145.12 million in sales, a 15.0 percent decrease year-on-year. While sales volume dropped, the segment’s sales profit increased by 18.0 percent to EUR 12.16 million, demonstrating improved unit economics. Other sales segments, including sightseeing tours and other products, also experienced declines in revenue, with other sales posting a gross loss of EUR 2.52 million. Geographic data shows significant drops in client numbers in Lithuania (-22.2 percent) and Latvia (-25.0 percent), while Estonia saw a smaller decline of 10.6 percent.

Looking forward, early bookings for the 2026 summer season were 59 percent higher than the previous year, providing visibility into future demand. The company plans to maintain a similar or slightly higher travel program for 2026, focusing on operational efficiency and expanding its hotel portfolio to enhance competitiveness. The company’s market capitalization stood at EUR 8.82 million at the end of the reporting period. Total borrowings increased to EUR 12.87 million from EUR 9.25 million in the prior year, with the debt-to-equity ratio rising to 191.42 percent. The company did not pay dividends in 2025.
