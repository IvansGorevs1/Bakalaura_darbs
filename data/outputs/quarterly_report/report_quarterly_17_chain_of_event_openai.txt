AB Novaturas’ 2025 performance reflected a deliberate shift toward profitability and efficiency despite substantially lower business volumes. The Group served 190.9 thousand travellers, down 20.3% from 239.5 thousand in 2024, while revenue declined 16.1% to EUR 168.5 million. Gross profit decreased 5.3% to EUR 19.7 million, but the gross profit margin improved from 10.33% to 11.66%, indicating better profitability on the reduced sales base.

Profitability improved materially compared with 2024. Adjusted EBITDA rose to EUR 420 thousand from negative EUR 449 thousand, and EBITDA increased from a EUR 6.1 million loss to positive EUR 420 thousand. Operating loss narrowed from EUR 6.6 million to EUR 274 thousand. The net loss decreased 84.6%, from EUR 7.6 million to EUR 1.2 million; excluding the EUR 5.7 million non-cash goodwill impairment recorded in 2024, the report states that the annual net loss decreased by 39%. Earnings per share improved from negative EUR 0.97 to negative EUR 0.15. EBITDA margin and net margin improved by 3.3 and 3.1 percentage points, respectively, although the Group remained loss-making.

The main driver identified by management was the intentional reduction of loss-making routes and tighter control over the travel programme, with greater emphasis on higher-margin destinations. Aircraft load factor increased by 1.5 percentage points to 97.2%, while long-haul load factor rose from 97.4% to 98.5% and ski-destination load factor from 90.5% to 97.2%. Flight package tours, the core segment, generated EUR 145.1 million in sales, down 15.0%, but segment sales profit increased 18.0% to EUR 12.2 million and its margin rose to 8%. Conversely, sightseeing tours by plane generated substantially lower segment profit, while other sales remained loss-making, with a EUR 2.5 million segment loss.

The operating environment remained difficult because of intense competition and excess travel supply. Increased market-wide supply during the autumn pressured prices, while disruptions at Vilnius Airport linked to illegal cross-border unmanned-balloon activity reduced last-minute demand. These factors limited the second-half result, although EBITDA was positive in both the second half and full year.

The Group ended the year with EUR 1.7 million in cash, up from EUR 394 thousand, supported by positive financing cash flow and EUR 4.5 million of new loans. However, borrowings increased to EUR 12.9 million, equity declined to EUR 6.9 million, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 191.42%. Early bookings for summer 2026 were 59% higher by December 2025, supporting management’s plan for a similar or slightly larger programme focused on continued efficiency and profitability growth.
