AS Tallinna Sadam reported a decline in profitability during the first six months of 2026, despite a modest increase in revenue. The Group generated revenue of EUR 59.3 million, representing a 2.4% increase compared to EUR 57.9 million in the same period of the previous year. This growth was primarily driven by higher charter fees from the multifunctional icebreaker Botnica, increased revenue from the sale of electricity, and a rise in ferry service sales. However, these gains were insufficient to offset a significant surge in operating expenses, which rose by 24.0% to EUR 20.2 million. The increase in costs was largely attributable to higher fuel prices, which pushed fuel expenses up by 47.6%, as well as increased electricity costs and higher expenditures on technical maintenance and repairs. Additionally, depreciation, amortization, and impairment charges grew by 6.0%, reflecting new infrastructure projects and scheduled vessel maintenance.

Consequently, operating profit decreased by 25.7% to EUR 13.6 million, down from EUR 18.3 million in the prior year period. The operating profit margin contracted from 31.6% to 22.9%. Adjusted EBITDA also declined, falling by 13.5% to EUR 25.8 million from EUR 29.8 million. The profit for the period was significantly impacted by the absence of one-off gains that had boosted the comparative period, specifically a EUR 0.9 million profit from the disposal of land for the Rail Baltica project and an insurance indemnity received for repair works on the icebreaker Botnica. As a result, net profit for the six-month period dropped by 41.7% to EUR 6.0 million, with earnings per share decreasing to EUR 0.02 from EUR 0.04.

Operational volumes reflected a challenging market environment. Total cargo handled decreased by 5.0% to 6.5 million tonnes, driven by a sharp 33.8% drop in liquid bulk volumes. Passenger numbers fell by 2.0% to 3.6 million, although cruise passenger traffic surged by 47.8% due to an increase in cruise ship calls. The number of vessel calls declined by 4.8%. In the second quarter, the negative trends continued, with profit for the period falling by 59.0% to EUR 1.4 million, partly due to the lack of the aforementioned one-off items from the previous year.

On the balance sheet, total assets decreased slightly by 1.1% to EUR 615.0 million, while equity declined by 0.3% to EUR 367.7 million. Net debt increased by 1.5% to EUR 145.8 million, although total loans and borrowings decreased by 5.7% to EUR 173.4 million due to principal repayments. The company maintained its dividend policy, paying EUR 19.2 million in dividends for the 2025 financial year. Looking forward, the Group faces risks from global economic instability and rising energy costs, which continue to pressure operating margins. The company also secured a new EUR 32 million loan facility to support general business purposes and ongoing investments.
