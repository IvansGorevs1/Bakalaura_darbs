AS Tallinna Sadam reported a decline in profitability for the six-month period ended 30 June 2026, despite a modest increase in revenue. Total revenue rose by 2.4% to EUR 59.3 million, driven primarily by higher charter fees from the icebreaker Botnica, increased electricity sales, and growth in ferry service revenue. However, operating profit fell sharply by 25.7% to EUR 13.6 million, and profit for the period decreased by 41.7% to EUR 6.0 million. The significant drop in earnings was largely attributable to rising operating expenses, which increased by 24.0% to EUR 20.2 million. Key cost drivers included higher fuel prices, which boosted fuel expenses by 47.6%, and increased electricity costs due to new regulatory fees and higher market prices. Additionally, technical maintenance and repair expenses surged by 80.0%, partly because the Group did not receive an insurance indemnity in 2026 that had been recorded in the previous year for repairs to the icebreaker Botnica. Depreciation, amortisation, and impairment charges also rose by 6.0% to EUR 12.9 million, reflecting new assets such as the Paldiski South Harbour quay and capitalised ferry dry-docking projects. Adjusted EBITDA declined by 13.5% to EUR 25.8 million, with margins contracting from 51.5% to 43.5%. Operational volumes showed mixed trends; cargo throughput decreased by 5.0% to 6.5 million tonnes, primarily due to a sharp 33.8% drop in liquid bulk volumes. Passenger numbers fell by 2.0% to 3.6 million, with declines on the Tallinn-Helsinki and Tallinn-Stockholm routes offset by a 47.8% increase in cruise passengers. The number of vessel calls dropped by 4.8%. On the balance sheet, total assets decreased slightly by 1.1% to EUR 615.0 million, while net debt increased by 1.5% to EUR 145.8 million. Equity remained stable at EUR 367.7 million. The Group invested EUR 3.5 million in capital expenditures, a significant decrease from EUR 12.0 million in the prior year period. Net finance costs decreased by 11.2% due to lower debt levels. The company paid a dividend of EUR 19.2 million in the second quarter, consistent with the previous year. Management noted that global economic instability and geopolitical conflicts pose risks, particularly regarding fuel costs and cargo flows, although the Group remains compliant with all sanctions and financial covenants.
