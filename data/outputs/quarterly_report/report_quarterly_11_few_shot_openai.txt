Kvika banki reported lower quarterly profitability but stronger first-half earnings from continuing operations. Q2 2026 profit before tax was ISK 1,545 million, down ISK 480 million, or 23.7%, from Q2 2025 and 14.6% from Q1 2026. After-tax profit fell to ISK 903 million from ISK 1,439 million a year earlier, mainly reflecting weaker operating income, higher impairment charges and increased special bank taxes.

Net operating income decreased 7.3% year on year to ISK 4,756 million. Net interest income was relatively resilient, rising 1.7% to ISK 3,013 million, as higher interest income from customer lending offset higher funding costs and the effect of a smaller liquidity portfolio. However, net interest income fell ISK 253 million from Q1, partly because lower inflation reduced the contribution from the bank’s positive ISK 6.1 billion inflation balance, while the ISK 10.2 billion dividend payment in June and recent bond issuances also weighed on the quarter. Net interest margin declined to 4.0% from 4.5% in Q1.

Net fee and commission income remained the main weakness, falling 26.8% year on year to ISK 1,416 million as subdued equity and fixed-income market activity reduced transaction volumes and client activity. Other net operating income increased to ISK 327 million from ISK 231 million, supported by stronger net financial income and other operating income.

Costs remained under control. Administrative expenses declined 1.1% to ISK 2,947 million despite a one-off ISK 112 million restructuring charge. Organisational changes reduced employee numbers to 232, and the efficiency programme is expected to lower the annual cost base by ISK 300–400 million. Net impairment increased to ISK 269 million from ISK 122 million, reflecting a more conservative loan-book review and heightened economic uncertainty, although management said underlying credit quality remained strong.

For the first six months, pre-tax profit from continuing operations increased 23.0% to ISK 3,354 million. Net operating income rose 0.7% to ISK 9,643 million, while administrative expenses fell 1.8%. After-tax profit from continuing operations increased to ISK 2,277 million, although total profit for the period declined to ISK 2,277 million from ISK 3,525 million because the prior-year figure included ISK 1,901 million from discontinued operations.

Growth continued across the balance sheet. Loans to customers increased 7.6% from year-end 2025 to ISK 223.3 billion, while deposits rose 16.5% to ISK 201.2 billion. Total assets increased 9.5% to ISK 375.7 billion. Capital and liquidity remained strong, with a 23.0% capital adequacy ratio, an LCR of 276% and an NSFR of 137%. Strategic initiatives include further loan growth, refinancing expensive legacy funding, new fund launches and the planned divestment of Straumur. CEO Ármann Þorvaldsson will step down by year-end 2026.
