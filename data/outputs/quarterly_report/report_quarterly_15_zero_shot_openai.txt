Nordecon AS reported weaker profitability but a substantially stronger order book in 2025. Revenue declined 7% year on year to €208.3 million, mainly because Buildings segment revenue fell approximately 10% to €168.3 million. Infrastructure revenue increased 10% to €39.9 million, supported by road construction, maintenance and Rail Baltica-related work. Buildings still generated 81% of total revenue, although its share decreased from 84% in 2024.

Gross profit fell 19% to €13.5 million, reducing the gross margin to 6.5% from 7.5%. Operating profit declined 30% to €5.7 million, while EBITDA decreased to €8.3 million from €11.0 million and the EBITDA margin fell to 4.0% from 4.9%. The operating margin was 2.7%, below the group’s target of consistently exceeding 3%. Administrative expenses decreased 14% to €6.8 million, primarily because weaker financial performance resulted in lower performance-based staff compensation.

Net profit fell 51% to €2.5 million, and profit attributable to Nordecon shareholders decreased to €1.4 million from €3.8 million. Earnings per share declined from €0.12 to €0.05. Profitability was adversely affected by higher net finance costs, which rose to €3.0 million from €2.3 million. This included a €750 thousand foreign-exchange loss caused by the approximately 12% weakening of the Ukrainian hryvnia against the euro, compared with a €258 thousand loss in 2024. Credit losses also increased to €354 thousand from €20 thousand.

Despite lower revenue and profits, the order book strengthened significantly. At 31 December 2025 it stood at €273.1 million, up approximately 30% from €209.5 million a year earlier. New contracts secured during the year totalled €235.0 million, compared with €181.4 million in 2024. Infrastructure accounted for 42% of the backlog, up from 18%, following a nearly threefold increase, while the Buildings backlog declined 7%. Major awards included Rail Baltica and Tallinn–Pärnu–Ikla road projects, as well as several public, defence and commercial building contracts. Approximately 69% of the backlog is scheduled for completion in 2026.

The Estonian construction market stabilised and showed modest recovery in the second half of the year, while Estonia’s economy grew approximately 1%. Construction prices increased 1.5%, mainly due to higher material prices. Ukraine remained a small contributor at around 2% of revenue, and Sweden generated no revenue because it had no projects in progress. Management expects business volumes to increase in 2026, supported by the enlarged order book, although intense competition, input-cost pressures and market uncertainty remain.
