During the first quarter of 2026, Nordic Fibreboard AS reported consolidated net revenue of €1.86 million, representing a 7.3% increase compared with €1.74 million in the same period of the previous year. Revenue growth was driven primarily by the fibreboard segment and specifically supported by increased order volumes from Finnish DIY stores, which rose by €241 thousand to €855 thousand. Geographically, European Union sales reached €1.78 million, compared with €1.64 million in the prior year period, while sales to Africa were €62 thousand and Asia accounted for €13 thousand. Property management revenue remained immaterial following the termination of lease agreements in August 2024.

Despite the growth in sales, profitability weakened significantly during the reporting period. The group recorded a gross loss of €105 thousand, compared with a gross profit of €127 thousand in the first quarter of 2025. Operating loss widened to €530 thousand from an operating loss of €217 thousand, and the operating margin worsened to negative 28% from negative 13%. Consolidated EBITDA was negative €395 thousand, resulting in an EBITDA margin of negative 21%, compared with negative €85 thousand and a negative 5% margin in the prior year period. Net loss for the first quarter of 2026 expanded to €570 thousand, compared with a net loss of €228 thousand in the first quarter of 2025. Basic and diluted earnings per share both stood at negative €0.07, compared with negative €0.05 in the prior year.

The deterioration in financial results was primarily driven by operational factors affecting production costs, notably the scheduled shutdown of the factory in early January to transition to a new boiler house, the subsequent two-month calibration and start-up period, and cold weather conditions that increased normal energy consumption. Furthermore, a sharp surge in global energy prices in late February following geopolitical tensions caused market gas prices to nearly double, leaving gas prices approximately 50% higher than at the beginning of the year. Cost of goods sold increased to €1.97 million from €1.61 million, largely due to higher electricity, heat, and water expenses, which rose to €1.05 million from €725 thousand. Distribution costs increased to €261 thousand from €228 thousand, and administrative expenses rose to €154 thousand from €111 thousand. Total labour expenses across the group decreased to €411 thousand from €471 thousand, alongside a reduction in average personnel to 59 employees compared with 69 in the previous year.

Financial expenses for the period were €41 thousand, down from €51 thousand in the first quarter of 2025, and consisted primarily of loan interest costs. Total assets as of March 31, 2026, stood at €9.69 million, compared with €9.32 million at the end of December 2025 and €8.72 million a year earlier. Liabilities increased to €5.80 million, comprising borrowings of €3.32 million and payables and prepayments of €2.35 million. Total equity was €3.89 million at the end of the reporting period. Cash flow from operating activities improved to a positive €13 thousand, compared with a negative cash flow of €528 thousand in the first quarter of 2025. Net cash used in investing activities was €169 thousand, driven by investments in production assets, while financing activities generated a positive cash inflow of €152 thousand, supported by loans received from related parties. Overall, cash and cash equivalents decreased by €4 thousand during the quarter, ending the period at €1 thousand.
