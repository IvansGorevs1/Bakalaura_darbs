Kvika banki hf. reported its financial results for the second quarter of 2026, showing a profit before tax of ISK 1,545 million, which represents a decrease of 23.7% compared to ISK 2,025 million in the second quarter of 2025. For the first six months of 2026, pre-tax profit from continuing operations reached ISK 3,354 million, up 23.0% from ISK 2,726 million in the corresponding period of the previous year. Net interest income for the second quarter stood at ISK 3,013 million, marking a 1.7% increase from ISK 2,962 million in the second quarter of 2025, driven by ongoing loan book growth and a favorable funding base, though it decreased by ISK 253 million compared to the first quarter of 2026 due to lower inflation and the impact of a large dividend payment. Net fee and commission income for the quarter declined by 26.8% year-on-year to ISK 1,416 million, down from ISK 1,935 million, reflecting subdued market activity and lower capital markets transaction volumes. Administrative expenses for the second quarter were ISK 2,947 million, showing a 1.1% decrease from ISK 2,981 million in the second quarter of 2025, despite including a one-off restructuring charge of ISK 112 million associated with cost efficiency measures implemented in June. Other net operating income improved to ISK 327 million from ISK 231 million in the second quarter of 2025, supported by stronger net financial income and higher other operating income. After-tax profit for the second quarter was ISK 903 million, compared to ISK 1,439 million in the second quarter of 2025. 

Total assets at the end of the second quarter of 2026 reached ISK 375.7 billion, representing an increase of 9.5% from year-end 2025. Loans to customers grew by ISK 15.8 billion or 7.6% from year-end 2025 to total ISK 223.3 billion, driven by broad-based growth across corporate banking, vehicle and equipment lending, and other categories. Total liabilities and equity also rose by 9.5% to ISK 375.7 billion. Customer deposits increased by 16.5% from year-end 2025 to surpass ISK 200 billion for the first time, reaching ISK 201.2 billion at quarter-end, bolstered by retail deposits and public entities. Market funding expanded to ISK 101.7 billion following senior unsecured and additional tier one issuances during the first half of the year, including a EUR 150 million senior preferred notes issuance in June that achieved an 85 basis point pricing improvement compared to the previous year. The bank maintained a strong capital and liquidity position, with a capital adequacy ratio of 23.0% and a common equity tier one ratio of 17.7%, both remaining well above regulatory requirements. The liquidity coverage ratio stood at 276% and the net stable funding ratio at 137%. Strategic and operational developments during the period included ongoing preparations for the divestment of Straumur greiðslumiðlun hf. and the announcement that CEO Ármann Þorvaldsson will step down by year-end 2026 after nearly ten years with the group.
