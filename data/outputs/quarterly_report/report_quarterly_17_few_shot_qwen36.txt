AB Novaturas reported a significant contraction in revenue and customer volume for the twelve-month period ended 31 December 2025, yet successfully shifted its strategic focus toward profitability and operational efficiency. The company served 191,000 travelers, a decrease of 20 percent compared to the previous year, while total revenue declined by 16.1 percent to EUR 168.5 million. This reduction in scale was a deliberate management decision aimed at optimizing the travel program and exiting loss-making routes, which ultimately improved margin performance despite the lower top-line figures.

The most notable financial development was the transition to positive profitability at the operating level. Adjusted EBITDA turned positive, amounting to EUR 420 thousand, a substantial improvement from the negative EUR 449 thousand recorded in 2024. This turnaround was driven by stricter supply discipline and enhanced operational efficiency, evidenced by an increase in aircraft load factors to 97.2 percent from 95.7 percent in the prior year. Load factors on long-haul destinations exceeded 99 percent in the second half of the year, and ski destination load factors rose sharply from 90.5 percent to 97.2 percent. Gross profit margin expanded by 1.3 percentage points to 11.66 percent, reflecting the success of the margin-focused strategy.

However, the company still reported a net loss for the period, which decreased by 39 percent to EUR 1.17 million. This figure excludes a one-off, non-cash goodwill impairment of EUR 5.7 million recorded in the previous year, which had significantly distorted prior comparatives. The current net loss was influenced by intense market competition, an excess supply of travel options that pressured prices, and disruptions at Vilnius Airport caused by illegal cross-border activity involving unmanned balloons, which reduced last-minute travel demand. Finance expenses remained high, contributing to the overall net loss despite the improvement in operating results.

Segment performance highlighted the dominance of flight package tours, which accounted for the majority of sales, though this segment saw a 15.0 percent decline in revenue. Other sales, including flight tickets and hotel bookings, also experienced a significant drop of 24.5 percent. Conversely, sightseeing tours by coach showed slight growth in both sales and profitability. Geographic analysis indicated that demand remained strong for Turkey, Egypt, and Greece, while interest in long-haul destinations such as Zanzibar and Vietnam increased.

Looking forward, the company noted a strong start to the next booking cycle, with early bookings for the 2026 summer season increasing by 59 percent compared to the previous year. The Group plans to maintain its travel program at a similar or slightly higher level for 2026, continuing to prioritize operational and cost efficiency. Management intends to expand its hotel portfolio with more attractively priced options to strengthen competitiveness. The company’s market capitalization stood at EUR 8.82 million at the end of the period, reflecting investor sentiment amid the strategic pivot from volume growth to sustainable profitability.
