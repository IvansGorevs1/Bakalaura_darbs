AS Tallinna Sadam’s revenue increased 2.4% year on year to EUR 59.3 million in the first six months of 2026, supported mainly by higher charter income from the icebreaker Botnica, increased electricity sales, and higher ferry-service revenue. However, profitability declined materially. Operating profit fell 25.7% to EUR 13.6 million, adjusted EBITDA decreased 13.5% to EUR 25.8 million, and profit for the period declined 41.7% to EUR 6.0 million. Operating margin fell from 31.6% to 22.9%, while adjusted EBITDA margin decreased from 51.5% to 43.5%. Earnings per share declined from EUR 0.04 to EUR 0.02.

The weaker results reflected a 24.0% increase in operating expenses to EUR 20.2 million. Fuel costs rose 47.6% to EUR 4.4 million because of higher diesel prices and more severe ice conditions, which increased ferry fuel consumption. Electricity costs increased 28.8% to EUR 3.1 million, partly due to higher market and network-service prices and a new security-of-supply fee. Technical maintenance and repair costs rose 80.0% to EUR 2.8 million, including more extensive repairs to Botnica, ferries, and harbour infrastructure. Depreciation, amortisation and impairment increased 6.0% to EUR 12.9 million, mainly following the commissioning of the Paldiski South Harbour quay, capitalised ferry dry-docking projects, and impairments of old engines.

Operating volumes were also weaker. Cargo volumes fell 5.0% to 6.5 million tonnes, primarily because liquid bulk decreased 33.8%; dry bulk declined 4.8%. Passenger numbers fell 2.0% to 3.6 million, with lower traffic on the Tallinn–Helsinki and Tallinn–Stockholm routes. The Tallinn–Helsinki decline was partly linked to the scheduled dry-docking of the vessel Finlandia. Cruise passengers increased 47.8% due to 12 additional cruise calls and higher passenger capacity. Vessel calls decreased 4.8% to 3,376.

Comparability was negatively affected by the prior-year EUR 0.9 million profit from the sale of land for the Rail Baltica project and a EUR 0.9 million insurance indemnity related to Botnica repairs; neither benefited 2026. These factors, together with higher expenses and depreciation, outweighed revenue growth. Lower net finance costs, down 11.2% to EUR 2.3 million due to lower debt, partly moderated the decline in pre-tax profit.

The Group invested EUR 3.5 million, 70.6% less than a year earlier, while operating cash flow fell from EUR 26.3 million to EUR 18.2 million. A EUR 19.2 million dividend was paid in May, equal to EUR 0.073 per share. At period-end, net debt was EUR 145.8 million, up 1.5% year on year, and equity was EUR 367.7 million. Management also identified risks from the US-Iran conflict, the Strait of Hormuz blockade, energy prices, and continuing effects of Russia’s war in Ukraine on liquid-bulk cargo flows.
