AS Merko Ehitus reported a mixed first half of 2026. Revenue declined 10.7% year on year to EUR 149.9 million, although second-quarter revenue increased 12.1% to EUR 92.7 million from EUR 82.6 million. Profitability weakened substantially in the six-month comparison: operating profit fell 55.1% to EUR 8.4 million, pre-tax profit dropped 54.1% to EUR 10.8 million, and net profit attributable to shareholders decreased 40.8% to EUR 12.8 million. Net profit margin fell to 8.6% from 12.9%, while earnings per share declined to EUR 0.73 from EUR 1.22. Second-quarter net profit was EUR 8.5 million, compared with EUR 11.2 million in the prior-year quarter.

The decline was primarily reflected in the construction service and real estate development segments. Construction service revenue decreased 1.5% to EUR 118.3 million, while operating profit fell 65.5% to EUR 3.0 million and its margin declined to 2.6% from 7.3%. The report attributes the lower margin to the expiration of successful contracts from earlier periods and a return to more typical margins in a highly competitive market, with first-half results also affected by first-quarter seasonality. Real estate development revenue fell 33.9% to EUR 31.6 million, and operating profit declined 40.8% to EUR 7.0 million. Apartment sales decreased to 120 from 222, while revenue from apartment sales fell to EUR 27.6 million from EUR 44.9 million. In the second quarter, however, apartment-sale revenue was broadly stable at EUR 20.6 million.

The strongest development was in the secured order book, which reached a record EUR 835.7 million at 30 June 2026, up approximately 88% from EUR 443.8 million a year earlier. Group companies signed contracts worth EUR 486.4 million in the first half, more than double the EUR 223.1 million signed in the comparable period. Large public-sector, Rail Baltica, defence-related and energy-infrastructure projects supported the order book, including the Rūdninkai defence campus, whose construction commenced at full scale after the reporting date.

Higher construction input prices, energy costs, inflationary pressure and continued competition affected margins. Residential markets remained price-sensitive, with demand concentrated in more affordable apartments, although activity improved in Riga.

Operating cash flow was negative at EUR 31.6 million, mainly due to increased inventories and construction-contract balances, while development investment rose to EUR 42.6 million from EUR 22.5 million. Cash and cash equivalents were EUR 39.6 million, up from EUR 25.9 million, but net debt increased to EUR 29.6 million. Equity remained strong at EUR 251.1 million, representing 57.4% of assets.
