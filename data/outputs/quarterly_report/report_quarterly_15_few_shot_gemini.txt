Nordecon AS reported weaker financial results for the twelve months ended 31 December 2025, driven by a contraction in revenue and profitability compared with the previous year. Group revenue decreased by 7% year-on-year to 208,281 thousand euros, down from 223,925 thousand euros in 2024. The decline was primarily caused by a roughly 10% decrease in revenue within the Buildings segment, which dropped to 168,302 thousand euros from 187,573 thousand euros. Conversely, revenue in the Infrastructure segment grew by 10% to 39,902 thousand euros compared with 36,299 thousand euros in the prior year. The Buildings segment continued to dominate the group’s total revenue breakdown, accounting for 81%, while the Infrastructure segment contributed 19%.

Profitability metrics weakened across the board during the reporting period. Gross profit fell to 13,535 thousand euros from 16,770 thousand euros, resulting in a gross margin of 6.5% compared with 7.5% in 2024. Operating profit declined to 5,651 thousand euros from 8,061 thousand euros, pushing the operating margin down to 2.7% from 3.6%. Administrative expenses decreased by about 14% to 6,814 thousand euros, largely due to lower staff costs associated with reduced liabilities for performance-based pay following weaker financial performance. EBITDA dropped to 8,250 thousand euros with an EBITDA margin of 4.0%, compared to 11,025 thousand euros and 4.9% in the previous year. 

Net finance costs increased significantly to 2,965 thousand euros from 2,333 thousand euros in 2024, heavily impacted by currency fluctuations. Specifically, the weakening of the Ukrainian hryvnia against the euro by approximately 12% resulted in a foreign exchange loss of 750 thousand euros on the translation of euro-denominated loans provided to Ukrainian subsidiaries. Consequently, net profit for the twelve-month period dropped by 51% to 2,545 thousand euros compared with 5,165 thousand euros in 2024. Net profit attributable to owners of the parent fell to 1,439 thousand euros from 3,827 thousand euros, bringing basic and diluted earnings per share down to 0.05 euros from 0.12 euros.

Despite the drop in annual revenue and earnings, the group achieved substantial growth in its order book, which expanded by 30% to 273,060 thousand euros as of 31 December 2025, compared with 209,489 thousand euros at the end of 2024. This growth was driven primarily by a nearly threefold increase in the Infrastructure segment order book, boosted by major contracts such as the Rail Baltica main line railway infrastructure project and road construction contracts with the Estonian Transport Administration. New contracts secured during the year totaled 234,994 thousand euros, compared with 181,437 thousand euros in 2024. 

Cash flow from operating activities showed a slight improvement, producing a net cash inflow of 1,204 thousand euros compared with 1,075 thousand euros in 2024. Investing activities resulted in a net cash outflow of 2,767 thousand euros, which included 2,898 thousand euros placed in long-term deposits to secure guarantee limits. Financing activities resulted in a net cash outflow of 1,194 thousand euros, reflecting loan repayments and lease payments. The group closed the period with 5,266 thousand euros in cash and cash equivalents, down from 8,195 thousand euros at the end of 2024, while maintaining a current ratio of 0.93. Backed by the expanded order book, management expects business volumes to increase in 2026 while maintaining strict cost control and risk management in a highly competitive market environment.
