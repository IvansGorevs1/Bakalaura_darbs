AS Merko Ehitus reported a decline in financial performance during the first half of 2026, with revenue and profitability decreasing compared to the same period in the previous year. The group generated revenue of EUR 149.9 million for the six-month period, representing a 10.7% decrease from EUR 167.9 million in the first half of 2025. This contraction was primarily driven by the real estate development segment, where revenue fell by 33.9% to EUR 31.6 million, largely due to a significant reduction in the number of apartments sold. In contrast, the construction services segment showed relative stability, with revenue declining slightly by 1.5% to EUR 118.3 million.

Profitability metrics weakened across the board. Net profit attributable to shareholders amounted to EUR 12.8 million for the first half of 2026, down from EUR 21.7 million in the prior year period. The net profit margin decreased to 8.6% from 12.9%. Pre-tax profit was EUR 10.8 million, compared to EUR 23.5 million in the first half of 2025. The operating profit margin also contracted to 5.6% from 11.1%. The construction services segment saw its operating profit margin drop sharply to 2.6% from 7.3%, impacted by the expiration of highly profitable contracts and a return to more typical market margins in a competitive environment. The real estate development segment maintained a higher operating profit margin of 22.2%, although the absolute operating profit fell to EUR 7.0 million from EUR 11.9 million.

Despite the lower current results, the company’s future outlook appears robust, highlighted by a record-high secured order book. As of June 30, 2026, the secured order book reached EUR 835.7 million, an increase of approximately 88% from EUR 443.8 million at the end of June 2025. New contracts signed in the first half of 2026 totaled EUR 486.4 million, significantly exceeding the EUR 223.1 million signed in the same period of the previous year. This growth in the order book is largely attributed to public sector projects, including defense-related facilities and Rail Baltica infrastructure, which accounted for a substantial portion of the new business. Private sector orders remained cautious, comprising approximately 25% of the total order book.

The company’s financial position remained solid, with equity increasing to EUR 251.1 million, representing 57.4% of total assets. Cash and cash equivalents rose to EUR 39.6 million from EUR 25.9 million in the previous year. However, the group moved into a net debt position of EUR 29.6 million, compared to a net cash position of EUR 1.1 million in the prior year, due to increased borrowing to support development projects. Operating cash flow was negative at EUR 31.6 million, influenced by higher inventory levels related to apartment construction and changes in receivables. The company paid dividends of EUR 22.1 million, equivalent to EUR 1.25 per share, reflecting a 55% payout ratio based on 2025 profits. Market capitalization decreased by 14.5% to EUR 474.4 million, and the share price closed at EUR 26.80. Management noted that rising input prices and geopolitical tensions continue to create inflationary pressures, but the strong order book provides visibility for future revenue generation.
