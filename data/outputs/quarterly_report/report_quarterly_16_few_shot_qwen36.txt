Nordic Fibreboard AS reported a deterioration in financial performance during the first quarter of 2026, characterized by a significant widening of losses despite a modest increase in revenue. Consolidated net revenue rose by 7.3% to €1.86 million, up from €1.74 million in the same period of the previous year. This growth was primarily driven by increased sales to Finnish DIY stores, where order volumes grew by €241 thousand. However, the company’s profitability metrics weakened considerably. The consolidated EBITDA turned negative at €395 thousand, compared to a loss of €85 thousand in Q1 2025, resulting in an EBITDA margin of negative 21%. The operating loss expanded to €530 thousand from €217 thousand in the prior year period.

The sharp decline in profitability was largely attributable to external market factors and operational disruptions within the fibreboard segment. A scheduled factory shutdown in early January for the transition to a new gas boiler house disrupted production for approximately two months. Furthermore, energy costs surged significantly due to geopolitical tensions, specifically a military attack on Iran in late February, which caused gas prices to nearly double. Although prices have since moderated, they remain approximately 50% higher than at the beginning of the year. These elevated energy costs, combined with cold weather conditions that increased normal energy consumption, drove the cost of goods sold to €1.97 million, exceeding revenue and resulting in a gross loss of €105 thousand.

On the balance sheet, total assets increased to €9.7 million from €8.7 million a year earlier, largely due to higher fixed assets and inventories. Total liabilities rose to €5.8 million, while equity decreased to €3.9 million from €3.4 million in Q1 2025, reflecting the accumulated net loss. The group employed 57 people at the end of the quarter, a reduction from 69 employees in the previous year, which contributed to lower labor expenses of €0.41 million. Cash flow from operating activities improved significantly, generating a net inflow of €13 thousand compared to an outflow of €528 thousand in the prior year, although investing activities consumed €169 thousand in production asset investments.

In addition to its core manufacturing business, the company continues to manage a real estate development project in Pärnu. Design works for the Admirali Quarter have been completed, and building permits have been issued. In January 2026, the company entered into a preliminary agreement to sell ten properties in the area, with final agreements expected by June 2026. Looking ahead, management anticipates stable demand for fibreboard and plans to focus on developing existing clients and expanding into the Nordic market. The company also expects to benefit from the newly installed energy-efficient boiler system, although it remains cautious about potential impacts from macroeconomic and geopolitical risks on consumption and exports.
