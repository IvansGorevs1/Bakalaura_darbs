AS Merko Ehitus reported a decline in profitability for the first half of 2026, with net profit attributable to shareholders falling to EUR 12.8 million from EUR 21.7 million in the same period of 2025. Revenue decreased by 10.7% to EUR 149.9 million, down from EUR 167.9 million in the prior year. The pre-tax profit margin contracted significantly to 7.2% from 14.0%, reflecting increased costs and lower sales volumes in specific segments. Despite the drop in absolute profit figures, the company maintained a positive net profit margin of 8.6%.

The construction service segment, which accounted for 78.9% of total revenue, saw a slight revenue decrease of 1.5% to EUR 118.3 million. However, operating profit in this segment dropped sharply by 65.5% to EUR 3.0 million, resulting in an operating margin of just 2.6% compared to 7.3% in the previous year. Management attributed this margin compression to the expiration of highly profitable contracts and a return to more typical industry margins in a competitive environment. Conversely, the real estate development segment experienced a 33.9% revenue decline to EUR 31.6 million, driven by a reduction in apartment sales from 222 units to 120 units. Nevertheless, this segment maintained a robust operating profit of EUR 7.0 million and a healthy operating margin of 22.2%.

A key positive development was the substantial growth in the secured order book, which reached an all-time high of EUR 835.7 million as of June 30, 2026, up from EUR 443.8 million in the prior year. This growth was supported by new contracts worth EUR 486.4 million signed in the first half of the year, including major public sector projects such as Rail Baltica infrastructure and defense-related facilities. The group also launched construction on more than 160 apartments in Riga and continued work on significant projects in Tallinn and Vilnius.

Market conditions remained challenging due to persistent inflationary pressures and rising input costs for materials and labor, driven by geopolitical conflicts and energy price increases. Consumer demand in the residential market shifted toward more affordable housing segments across the Baltics. Financially, the group’s equity increased to EUR 251.1 million, while net debt rose to EUR 29.6 million from a negative net debt position in the previous year. Cash and cash equivalents stood at EUR 39.6 million. Operating cash flow was negative at EUR 31.6 million, primarily due to increased inventory levels associated with apartment development projects. The company paid dividends of EUR 22.1 million, equivalent to EUR 1.25 per share, representing a 55% payout rate of 2025 profits. Overall, while current profitability weakened due to cost pressures and sales cycles, the company’s strong order book provides a solid foundation for future revenue generation.
