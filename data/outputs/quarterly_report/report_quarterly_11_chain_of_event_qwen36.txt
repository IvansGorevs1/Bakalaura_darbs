Kvika Banki hf. reported a pre-tax profit of ISK 1,545 million for the second quarter of 2026, representing a significant decline of 23.7% compared to ISK 2,025 million in the same period of the previous year. This decrease of ISK 480 million was primarily driven by a substantial drop in net fee and commission income, which fell by 26.8% to ISK 1,416 million. The reduction in fee income aligns with continued subdued market activity, characterized by a 50% decrease in equity market turnover and a 12% drop in fixed income markets. In contrast, net interest income showed resilience, increasing by 1.7% year-over-year to ISK 3,013 million. This growth was supported by a 7.6% expansion in the loan book to ISK 223.3 billion and strong deposit growth, which surpassed ISK 200 billion for the first time. However, the net interest margin contracted to 4.0% from 4.5% in the first quarter of 2026, a decline attributed to lower inflation reducing the benefit of the bank's positive inflation balance and the impact of a large ISK 10.2 billion dividend payment in June.

Administrative expenses decreased slightly by 1.1% to ISK 2,947 million, reflecting disciplined cost management despite the recognition of ISK 112 million in one-off restructuring charges related to efficiency measures announced in June. These measures are expected to lower the annual cost base by ISK 300 to 400 million. Net impairment provisions increased to ISK 269 million from ISK 122 million in the prior year, including ISK 103 million in write-downs from a conservative loan book review. The effective income tax rate rose to 34.8% from 24.4% in the previous year, largely due to higher special taxes on financial activities associated with the closure of funding derivatives.

The bank’s capital position remained robust, with a Capital Adequacy Ratio of 23.0%, well above regulatory requirements. The decrease in the ratio was primarily due to the distribution of a special dividend. Kvika continues to optimize its funding structure, having issued senior unsecured and AT1 bonds in the first half of 2026 to strengthen FX liquidity and prepare for the refinancing of higher-priced legacy debt. The bank also received authorization to issue covered bonds in July 2026. Liquidity metrics were strong, with a Liquidity Coverage Ratio of 276% and a Net Stable Funding Ratio of 137%.

Strategic developments included the ongoing sale process of Straumur greiððslumiðlun hf., expected to close before year-end, and the step-down of CEO Ármann Þorvaldsson, who will remain in position until a successor is recruited. The UK business maintained strong deal momentum, with the loan book reaching an all-time high, while the Asset Management segment focused on operational efficiency and new fund launches. Overall, while reported earnings were dampened by one-off costs and market conditions, the bank positioned itself for improved returns in future quarters through balance sheet optimization and cost discipline.
