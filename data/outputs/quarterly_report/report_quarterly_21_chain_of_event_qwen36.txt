AS Tallinna Sadam reported a significant decline in profitability for the first half of 2026, despite a modest increase in revenue. Total revenue rose by 2.4% to EUR 59.3 million, driven primarily by higher charter fees from the icebreaker Botnica, increased electricity sales, and higher ferry service revenues. However, operating profit fell sharply by 25.7% to EUR 13.6 million, and profit for the period decreased by 41.7% to EUR 6.0 million. Adjusted EBITDA also declined by 13.5% to EUR 25.8 million. The deterioration in financial performance was primarily caused by a substantial rise in operating expenses, which increased by EUR 3.9 million or 24.0%. Key cost drivers included higher fuel prices and increased consumption due to severe ice conditions, which pushed fuel costs up by 47.6%. Additionally, expenses for technical maintenance and repair of non-current assets rose by 80.0%, partly because the Group did not receive the insurance indemnity for icebreaker repairs that it had received in the prior year. Electricity costs also increased by 28.8% due to higher market prices, increased volumes, and a new security of supply fee. Depreciation, amortization, and impairment charges grew by 6.0% following the commissioning of new infrastructure and ferry dry-docking projects.

Operational volumes reflected a challenging market environment. Cargo volumes decreased by 5.0% to 6.5 million tonnes, with liquid bulk dropping significantly by 33.8% and dry bulk falling by 4.8%. This decline was attributed to the war in Ukraine affecting cargo flows and a search for alternative delivery routes by customers. The number of vessel calls fell by 4.8%, and passenger numbers decreased by 2.0% to 3.6 million. The passenger decline was largely concentrated on the Tallinn-Helsinki route, where traffic dropped by 2.6% due to the scheduled dry-docking of a competitor’s vessel and the absence of major music events that had boosted traffic in the previous year. Conversely, cruise passenger numbers surged by 47.8% as the number of cruise ship calls increased.

Segment performance varied, with the Cargo harbours segment seeing the largest drop in adjusted EBITDA due to lower cargo volumes and the absence of one-off gains from land sales recorded in 2025. The Ferry segment also experienced a decline in adjusted EBITDA despite revenue growth, as rising fuel and personnel costs outweighed tariff indexation benefits. The Other segment saw revenue growth due to increased icebreaker utilization, though adjusted EBITDA fell due to higher repair costs. Net finance costs decreased by 11.2% due to lower debt levels. The Group maintained a stable dividend policy, paying EUR 19.2 million, equivalent to 85% of the previous year's profit. Total assets decreased slightly to EUR 615.0 million, while net debt increased marginally to EUR 145.8 million. The company concluded the period with equity of EUR 367.7 million.
