Kvika banki hf. reported financial results for the second quarter and first six months of 2026, characterized by resilient core operations amidst a challenging macroeconomic environment. For the second quarter of 2026, the bank generated a pre-tax profit of ISK 1,545 million, representing a decrease of 23.7% compared with ISK 2,025 million in the second quarter of 2025. Profit after tax for the quarter stood at ISK 903 million, down from ISK 1,439 million in the corresponding period of the previous year. For the first six months of 2026, pre-tax profit from continuing operations increased by 23.0% year-on-year to reach ISK 3,354 million, compared with ISK 2,726 million in the first six months of 2025, while total profit for the six-month period was ISK 2,277 million.

Net operating income for the second quarter of 2026 reached ISK 4,756 million, marking a 7.3% decline from ISK 5,128 million in the second quarter of 2025. This was shaped by contrasting trends across revenue streams. Net interest income demonstrated resilience, rising by 1.7% year-on-year to ISK 3,013 million, supported by ongoing lending momentum. However, net interest income decreased compared with the first quarter of 2026, primarily due to lower inflation impacting the bank's positive inflation balance and the effect of an ISK 10.2 billion dividend payment in June. Net fee and commission income remained subdued at ISK 1,416 million, representing a 26.8% decrease from ISK 1,935 million in the second quarter of 2025, reflecting continued soft activity in capital markets and lower transaction volumes. Conversely, other net operating income improved to ISK 327 million from ISK 231 million in the same period of the previous year.

Operating expenses demonstrated continued cost discipline. Administrative expenses for the second quarter of 2026 were ISK 2,947 million, reflecting a 1.1% decrease year-on-year despite the inclusion of a one-off restructuring charge of ISK 112 million incurred in June to implement efficiency measures. The bank expects these measures to lower the annual cost base going forward. Net impairment losses on loans amounted to ISK 269 million in the second quarter, compared with ISK 122 million in the second quarter of 2025.

The balance sheet expanded during the period, with total assets reaching ISK 375.7 billion at the end of June 2026, representing a 9.5% increase from year-end 2025. Loans to customers grew by 7.6% over the same timeframe to stand at ISK 223.3 billion, driven by broad-based growth across domestic corporate banking, vehicle and equipment lending, and the UK business segment. Asset quality remained strong, with stage 3 loans accounting for a stable portion of the portfolio and robust collateral coverage. On the liability side, total deposits surpassed ISK 200 billion for the first time, reaching ISK 201.2 billion at the end of the quarter, up 16.5% from year-end 2025. Market funding increased to ISK 101.7 billion following senior unsecured bond and additional tier 1 capital issuances completed during the first half of the year.

Kvika maintained a robust capital and liquidity position at the close of the reporting period. The total capital adequacy ratio stood at 23.0%, comfortably above regulatory requirements, following the distribution of the special dividend in June and a reduction in the Pillar II requirement resulting from the 2026 SREP results. The liquidity coverage ratio remained strong at 276%, and the net stable funding ratio was 137%. Additionally, the bank announced corporate developments during the period, including the ongoing sales process for Straumur greiðslumiðlun hf. and the planned departure of Chief Executive Officer Ármann Þorvaldsson by year-end 2026.
