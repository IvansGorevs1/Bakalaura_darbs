Kvika banki hf. reported its financial results for the second quarter of 2026, showing a pre-tax profit of ISK 1,545 million, which represents a decrease of 23.7% compared to ISK 2,025 million in the second quarter of 2025. Profit after tax for Q2 2026 stood at ISK 903 million, down from ISK 1,439 million in the corresponding period of the previous year. For the first six months of 2026, pre-tax profit from continuing operations increased by 23.0% year-on-year to ISK 3,354 million, while total profit for the six-month period was ISK 2,277 million compared to ISK 3,525 million in 6M 2025, which had included profit from discontinued operations. 

Net interest income for Q2 2026 increased by 1.7% year-on-year to ISK 3,013 million, compared to ISK 2,962 million in Q2 2025, driven by ongoing loan book growth and a solid net interest margin, though it declined from ISK 3,266 million in Q1 2026 due to lower inflation and the impact of a large dividend payment in June. Net fee and commission income faced continued subdued market activity, decreasing by 26.8% year-on-year to ISK 1,416 million from ISK 1,935 million. Other net operating income improved to ISK 327 million from ISK 231 million in Q2 2025. Total net operating income for the quarter was ISK 4,756 million, down 7.3% from ISK 5,128 million in Q2 2025. 

Administrative expenses decreased by 1.1% year-on-year to ISK 2,947 million in Q2 2026 from ISK 2,981 million, despite including a one-off restructuring charge of ISK 112 million associated with cost efficiency measures implemented in June. The underlying cost base continued to trend lower, supported by a reduction in full-time employees to 232. Net impairments on loans amounted to ISK 269 million in Q2 2026, compared to ISK 122 million in Q2 2025. 

Total assets reached ISK 375.7 billion at the end of June 2026, representing a 9.5% increase from year-end 2025. Loans to customers grew by 7.6% during the same period to ISK 223.3 billion, driven by broad-based growth across corporate banking, vehicle, and equipment lending. Credit quality remained strong, with stage 3 loans accounting for 3.5% of the net loan book and overall impairment levels remaining stable at 1.2% of the gross loan book. 

On the liability side, customer deposits increased by 16.5% from year-end 2025 to surpass ISK 200 billion for the first time, reaching ISK 201.2 billion. Market funding rose to ISK 101.7 billion following senior unsecured and additional tier 1 bond issuances during the first half of the year. The bank maintained robust liquidity and capital positions, with a liquidity coverage ratio of 276%, a net stable funding ratio of 137%, and a capital adequacy ratio of 23.0%, which remains well above regulatory requirements. Additionally, the formal sales process for Straumur greiðslumiðlun hf. is in progress with due diligence underway, and CEO Ármann Þorvaldsson announced plans to step down by the end of 2026.
