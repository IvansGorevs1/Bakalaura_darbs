Amber Latvijas balzams reported a marked deterioration in financial performance in the first three months of 2026. Sales volume fell 36.1% year on year to 517,600 nine-liter cases, while net turnover declined 29.6% to EUR 11.1 million from EUR 15.8 million. The company attributed the revenue reduction primarily to lower sales volumes and a high comparison base in 2025, when contractual revenue adjustments had positively affected net sales.

Gross profit decreased 66.5% to EUR 1.2 million from EUR 3.5 million. The decline was mainly associated with changes in product mix and adjustments to pricing methodology; cost reductions during the period did not offset these effects. Operating performance moved from a EUR 0.6 million profit in the first quarter of 2025 to a EUR 1.6 million loss in 2026, reducing the operating margin from 3.7% to -14.5%. The company reported a net loss of EUR 1.4 million, compared with a EUR 0.9 million profit a year earlier, and earnings per share declined from EUR 0.11 to negative EUR 0.19.

The weaker results occurred amid a significant downturn in the global spirits market, with changing consumer habits, pressure on discretionary spending and distributor inventory adjustments contributing to declining industry sales, particularly in export markets. The company also faced major group-related and geopolitical disruptions. In January 2026, U.S. Chapter 11 proceedings involving Stoli Group (USA), LLC resulted in the appointment of a trustee and the loss of the group’s control over that company’s U.S. operations. This directly affected the solvency of the company’s largest customer and its ability to meet creditor obligations. Actions by Russian authorities against group entities, including the designation of related companies as “extremist” and confiscation of the Talvis distillery, added to the external challenges.

Cash-flow difficulties led to delayed excise tax payments, after which the State Revenue Service froze the company’s bank accounts. To maintain business continuity and protect creditors, the company applied for legal protection proceedings on 30 January 2026; the Riga City Court approved the application on 5 February. The proceedings provide a framework for restructuring liabilities, including tax debts. The parent company subsequently entered Luxembourg judicial reorganization proceedings, with a moratorium granted until 24 August 2026.

Management is reviewing operations, governance, internal controls and investment priorities while continuing efficiency and cost-reduction measures. Production quality improved, with RFT rising to 99.5% from 98.4%, but order fulfillment deteriorated substantially, with OTIF falling to 70.3% from 97.5%.
