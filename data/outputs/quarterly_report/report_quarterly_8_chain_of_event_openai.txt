AS Tallink Grupp’s 2025 performance weakened year on year amid economic uncertainty, particularly in Finland, subdued consumer and business confidence, geopolitical tensions, inflation, higher taxes and reduced cargo demand. Revenue declined 2.6% to EUR 765.3 million from EUR 785.8 million. Passengers decreased 0.9% to 5.53 million, cargo units fell 19.2% to 245,004, and passenger vehicles declined 2.2% to 760,473.

Profitability also deteriorated. EBITDA fell 25.7% to EUR 130.1 million from EUR 175.2 million, reducing the EBITDA margin from 22.3% to 17.0%. Gross profit decreased to EUR 143.2 million from EUR 153.6 million, while operating profit declined to EUR 50.3 million from EUR 77.4 million. Net profit fell 57.1% to EUR 17.3 million, or EUR 0.023 per share compared with EUR 0.054 in 2024. Despite the weaker operating performance, lower depreciation and amortisation and reduced finance costs provided support. Depreciation and amortisation decreased 18.4% to EUR 79.8 million, mainly because the estimated useful lives of most cruise and passenger vessels were aligned to 45 years and three vessels were sold. Net finance costs declined 24.2% to EUR 21.4 million due to lower outstanding debt and declining interest rates.

Route operations revenue decreased by EUR 13.7 million to EUR 625.2 million, with the segment result falling to EUR 70.5 million from EUR 81.5 million. Estonia–Finland passenger volumes increased 1.8%, but cargo volumes declined 20.0%; revenue rose to EUR 314.5 million while the segment result fell by EUR 10.9 million. Finland–Sweden revenue decreased to EUR 225.5 million and its result fell to EUR 1.3 million, partly reflecting 68 days of maintenance for Silja Serenade and Baltic Princess. Estonia–Sweden revenue declined to EUR 85.3 million, while its loss improved to EUR 1.2 million. The Other segment’s revenue fell to EUR 145.5 million, but its result remained broadly stable, supported by charter operations.

The Group sold Star I, Regal Star and Sailor, reduced vessels in lay-up to zero by the third quarter, and ended the year with three vessels chartered out. Capital expenditure increased to EUR 33.0 million from EUR 22.4 million, largely for vessel maintenance and refurbishment. Net debt remained broadly stable at EUR 432.4 million, while the net debt-to-EBITDA ratio was 3.3 versus 3.1 at year-end 2024. Cash declined to EUR 13.5 million, partly because EUR 44.6 million of dividends and EUR 11.3 million of related income tax were paid.

Fourth-quarter results improved: revenue rose 2.4% to EUR 188.0 million, EBITDA increased to EUR 27.6 million, and net profit recovered to EUR 12.2 million from a EUR 5.2 million loss. The Board intends to propose a 2026 dividend of EUR 0.06 per share.
