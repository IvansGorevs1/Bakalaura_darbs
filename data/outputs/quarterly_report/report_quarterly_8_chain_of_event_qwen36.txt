Tallink Grupp reported a challenging financial year for 2025, characterized by declining revenue and profitability against a backdrop of economic uncertainty and reduced consumer confidence in its core markets. The Group’s consolidated revenue decreased by 2.6% year-on-year to EUR 765.3 million from EUR 785.8 million in 2024. This decline was driven primarily by a significant drop in cargo volumes, which fell by 19.2% to 245,004 units, and a modest 0.9% decrease in passenger numbers to 5,531,132. The Estonia-Finland route, the Group’s largest segment, saw a 20.0% decline in cargo units, while the Finland-Sweden routes experienced a 22.8% drop in cargo and a 2.4% decline in passengers.

Operating performance weakened significantly, with EBITDA falling to EUR 130.1 million from EUR 175.2 million in the previous year, representing a decrease of approximately 25.8%. Consequently, the net profit for the period dropped to EUR 17.3 million, or EUR 0.023 per share, compared to EUR 40.3 million, or EUR 0.054 per share, in 2024. The erosion in profitability was largely attributable to lower demand and adverse market conditions. However, the Group managed to mitigate some of the operational cost pressures through strategic internal decisions. Notably, depreciation and amortization expenses decreased by EUR 18.0 million to EUR 79.8 million. This reduction was explicitly attributed to the alignment of estimated useful lives for cruise and passenger vessels to 45 years, as well as the sale of three vessels during the year: Star I, Regal Star, and Sailor.

The sale of surplus vessels and the chartering out of three ships, including the Silja Europa, contributed to optimizing the cost base and maintaining liquidity. Despite the operational headwinds, the Group continued to prioritize debt reduction. Total loan repayments and interest payments amounted to EUR 113.5 million, bringing net debt to EUR 432.4 million at year-end. The net debt to EBITDA ratio improved slightly to 3.3 from 3.4 in the previous quarter. Capital investments totaled EUR 33.0 million, focused on maintenance and refurbishment of key vessels.

In the fourth quarter of 2025, the Group showed signs of stabilization. Revenue increased by 2.4% to EUR 188.0 million, and EBITDA rose to EUR 27.6 million from EUR 25.7 million in the same period last year. Net profit turned positive at EUR 12.2 million, reversing a net loss of EUR 5.2 million in Q4 2024. This quarterly improvement was supported by reduced depreciation expenses due to the accounting change and the sale of the cargo vessel Sailor. The Group maintained its dividend policy, paying EUR 44.6 million in 2025, and proposed a similar dividend of 6 euro cents per share for 2026. Management indicated that while the environment remains strained, cargo volumes showed moderate growth at the beginning of the new year, suggesting a potential recovery.
