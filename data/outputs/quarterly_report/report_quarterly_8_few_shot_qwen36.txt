Tallink Grupp reported a challenging financial year in 2025, characterized by a decline in overall profitability and revenue, although the fourth quarter demonstrated a notable recovery in earnings. The Group’s consolidated revenue for the full year decreased by 2.6% to EUR 765.3 million, down from EUR 785.8 million in 2024. This contraction was driven by a 19.2% drop in cargo volumes to 245,004 units and a modest 0.9% decrease in passenger numbers to 5.53 million. The operating environment was constrained by low consumer and business confidence in core markets, particularly Finland, as well as heightened geopolitical tensions and rising tax burdens in Estonia and Finland.

Profitability metrics reflected the pressure on operations. Unaudited EBITDA fell significantly by 25.8% to EUR 130.1 million from EUR 175.2 million in the prior year, resulting in an EBITDA margin of 17.0% compared to 22.3% previously. The Group’s net profit for the period was EUR 17.3 million, or EUR 0.023 per share, a substantial reduction from the EUR 40.3 million reported in 2024. The decline in net profit was partly offset by a EUR 18.0 million decrease in depreciation and amortization expenses, which stemmed from the sale of three vessels and an alignment of estimated useful lives for cruise and passenger vessels to 45 years. Additionally, net finance costs declined by 24.2% to EUR 21.4 million due to reduced loan balances and lower interest rates.

Segment performance varied across routes. The Estonia-Finland route, the Group’s largest segment, saw revenue increase slightly by 0.5% to EUR 314.5 million, but its segment result dropped by 13.4% to EUR 70.4 million due to lower cargo volumes. The Finland-Sweden route experienced a 1.4% revenue decline to EUR 225.5 million and a 52.9% drop in segment result to EUR 1.3 million, impacted by extensive maintenance works. The Estonia-Sweden segment recorded a revenue decrease of 12.4% to EUR 85.3 million, though the segment loss narrowed significantly to EUR 1.2 million from a loss of EUR 2.6 million in 2024.

In the fourth quarter of 2025, financial performance improved markedly. Revenue grew by 2.4% to EUR 188.0 million, and EBITDA increased to EUR 27.6 million. Most notably, the Group returned to net profitability with EUR 12.2 million, reversing a net loss of EUR 5.2 million in the same period of 2024. This quarterly improvement was supported by higher route operation margins and the sale of surplus FuelEU Maritime compliance units.

Strategic actions included the sale of three vessels (Star I, Regal Star, and Sailor) to optimize the cost base and the extension of charter agreements for vessels such as Silja Europa, Galaxy I, and Romantika. The Group maintained a disciplined financial position, with net debt remaining stable at EUR 432.4 million and the net debt to EBITDA ratio improving slightly to 3.3. Despite the difficult macroeconomic conditions, Tallink continued its shareholder return policy, paying a total dividend of EUR 44.6 million (EUR 0.06 per share) in 2025 and proposing a similar dividend for 2026. The company entered 2026 with a focus on cost efficiencies, fleet optimization, and adapting to the evolving regulatory landscape, including the transition to biomethane fuel for shuttle vessels.
