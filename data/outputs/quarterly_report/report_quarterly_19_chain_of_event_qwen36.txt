AS Pro Kapital Grupp reported a significant turnaround in financial performance for the twelve months ended 31 December 2025, transitioning from a net loss in the previous year to a robust net profit. Total revenue for the period reached EUR 53.2 million, a substantial increase from EUR 18.2 million in 2024. This growth was primarily driven by the completion and handover of residential units in key developments, including Kalaranna and Uus-Kindrali in Tallinn, and City Villas in Vilnius. The Estonian segment dominated revenue generation, accounting for 75% of total sales, compared to just 27% in the prior year. Conversely, the Latvian segment’s contribution dropped to 0.3% as remaining inventory was sold, while Lithuania contributed 12% and Germany 11%.

Gross profit more than tripled to EUR 18.8 million from EUR 5.4 million in 2024, resulting in a gross profit margin of 35%, up from 30%. Operating profit also improved dramatically to EUR 14.7 million from a negligible EUR 0.1 million in 2024. The Group’s net profit for the year was EUR 12.0 million, compared to a net loss of EUR 3.9 million in the previous period. Earnings per share rose to EUR 0.22 from EUR -0.06. Cash generated from operating activities turned positive at EUR 10.8 million, reversing the EUR 10.4 million cash used in 2024.

The improvement in profitability was supported by a reduction in finance costs, which fell to EUR 2.6 million from EUR 4.3 million in 2024, partly due to the partial redemption of secured bonds. The Group’s debt-to-equity ratio improved to 0.97 from 1.32, and return on equity increased to 21.0% from -7.0%. Net assets per share grew to EUR 1.12 from EUR 0.89.

Several strategic events influenced these results. In August 2025, the Group partially repaid EUR 9.4 million of its senior secured bonds, reducing the outstanding nominal amount to EUR 10.5 million. The Group also acquired an additional 10% stake in its Italian subsidiary, Preatoni Nuda Proprietà S.r.l., bringing its ownership to 77.5%. However, the Italian operations faced a challenging market, and the Group recognized a full impairment loss of EUR 0.86 million on goodwill arising from the initial acquisition of this subsidiary. Hotel operations in Germany performed slightly below budget, with occupancy softening towards year-end, although food and beverage revenue saw a modest increase. The Group maintained a conservative financing policy, focusing on core residential development while deciding to sell commercial properties in Riga to streamline operations. Overall, the reporting period was characterized by strong execution in the Baltic residential markets, leading to improved liquidity and profitability.
