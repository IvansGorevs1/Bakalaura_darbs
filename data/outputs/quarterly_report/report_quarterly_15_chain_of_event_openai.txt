Nordecon’s revenue in 2025 decreased 7% year on year to €208.3 million, reflecting weaker activity in the Buildings segment. Buildings revenue fell approximately 10% to €168.3 million, while Infrastructure revenue increased 10% to €39.9 million. Buildings still generated 81% of group revenue, although its share declined from 84% in 2024. Within Buildings, commercial-building revenue rose 71%, while public-building revenue declined 32%; own property-development revenue increased to €9.75 million from €7.69 million, mainly due to apartment sales in developments in Pärnu and Tartu.

Profitability deteriorated. Gross profit fell to €13.5 million from €16.8 million, and gross margin declined to 6.5% from 7.5%. Operating profit decreased 30% to €5.65 million, reducing the operating margin to 2.7% from 3.6%, below the group’s target of consistently exceeding 3%. EBITDA fell to €8.25 million from €11.0 million, with the EBITDA margin declining to 4.0% from 4.9%. Administrative expenses decreased 14% to €6.8 million, largely because weaker performance resulted in lower performance-based pay, but this reduction did not offset the decline in gross profit.

Net profit fell to €2.55 million from €5.17 million, while profit attributable to Nordecon’s owners declined to €1.44 million from €3.83 million. Earnings per share decreased to €0.05 from €0.12. Net finance costs increased to €3.0 million from €2.3 million, primarily because the Ukrainian hryvnia weakened approximately 12% against the euro, producing a €750 thousand foreign-exchange loss on loans to Ukrainian subsidiaries, compared with €258 thousand in 2024. Credit losses also increased to €354 thousand from €20 thousand.

The Estonian construction market stabilised, with activity improving modestly in the second half of the year, while construction prices rose 1.5%, mainly because of higher material prices. Competition remained intense, public investment in buildings declined, and underfunding continued to constrain road construction. Against this backdrop, new contracts secured increased to €235.0 million from €181.4 million, lifting the order book 30% to €273.1 million at year-end. Infrastructure accounted for 42% of the backlog, up from 18%, supported by major Rail Baltica and road-construction contracts, while the Buildings backlog declined 7%. Management expects business volumes to increase in 2026.

Operating cash inflow was €1.2 million, slightly above 2024, but investing and financing outflows resulted in a €2.8 million net cash reduction. Year-end cash declined to €5.3 million from €8.2 million, and the current ratio remained weak at 0.93. No dividends were paid in 2025.
