VILVI Group reported a significant expansion in top-line performance during the first quarter of 2026, with consolidated revenue increasing by 22.4% to EUR 87.0 million compared to EUR 71.1 million in the same period of the previous year. This growth was driven by substantial volume increases across multiple segments, particularly in dry milk products and fresh milk products, as well as the consolidation of results from the newly acquired Marijampolės pieno konservai UAB. The company purchased 156,000 tons of basic indicators milk, representing a 54.2% year-over-year increase, which supported the higher production and sales volumes.

Despite the strong revenue growth, profitability metrics showed mixed results. EBITDA declined sharply by 50.2% to EUR 3.0 million, with the EBITDA margin contracting to 3.5% from 8.5% in Q1 2025. This deterioration was primarily attributable to a preliminary negative goodwill charge of EUR 5.5 million related to the acquisition of Marijampolės pieno konservai UAB, which was excluded from the adjusted EBITDA figure. Excluding this one-time item, the underlying operational performance would have been stronger, though cost pressures and integration activities likely impacted margins.

Net profit, however, improved by 25.7% to EUR 5.1 million, resulting in earnings per share of EUR 0.43, up from EUR 0.34 in the prior year. The increase in net profit despite the lower EBITDA was supported by lower income tax expenses and other operating income. The Group’s profit margin stood at 5.8%, a slight improvement of 0.1 percentage points compared to the previous year.

Strategically, the quarter was defined by major business developments. The acquisition of Marijampolės pieno konservai UAB, the only producer of canned milk products in the Baltic States, was completed in January 2026. This move allows VILVI Group to utilize economies of scale and operational synergies, with plans to expand capacity in the region’s largest whey processing line. Additionally, the new cheese factory operated by Baltic Dairy Board SIA in Bauska, Latvia, commenced commercial production. The facility, which won third place in the Latvian Construction Awards 2025, is expected to produce 18,000 tons of cheese annually for export to over 40 countries and create more than 100 jobs.

Financially, the Group’s balance sheet reflects the impact of these investments and acquisitions. Net financial debt rose significantly by 89.7% to EUR 83.4 million, driven by borrowing to fund the acquisition and capital expenditures. Total assets increased to EUR 220.3 million, with notable growth in property, plant, and equipment, as well as inventories. The company continues to focus on sustainability and efficiency, evidenced by the discontinuation of transparent plastic lids for yogurts and the ongoing integration of acquired entities into the VILVI Group structure.
